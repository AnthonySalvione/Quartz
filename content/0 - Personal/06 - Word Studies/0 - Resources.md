## Suggested Best Workflow for a Bible Word Study

#### 1. Establish the exact Greek or Hebrew text and note any textual variants.
#### 2. Parse the form and analyze its syntactical role.
#### 3. Read the complete paragraph before consulting definitions.
#### 4. Examine every occurrence by the same author.
#### 5. Examine occurrences throughout the same Testament.
#### 6. Examine Septuagint usage and Hebrew–Greek correspondences.
#### 7. Consult BDAG or HALOT and keep their distinct senses separate.
#### 8. Compare Louw–Nida, BrillDAG/LSJ or DCH.
#### 9. Read NIDNTTE/NIDOTTE and other theological dictionaries critically.
#### 10. Search non-biblical texts from the same historical period.
#### 11. Study the relevant cultural institution or social practice.

##### Consult a technical commentary

- **New International Greek Testament Commentary**
- **Word Biblical Commentary**
- **Baker Exegetical Commentary**
- **International Critical Commentary**
- **New International Commentary**
- **Hermeneia**
- **Anchor Yale Bible**
- **Socio-Rhetorical Commentary**

##### Consult a specialized background dictionary



#### 12. Read technical commentaries and journal articles.
#### 13.  Return to the original verse and determine which evidence actually pertains to that context.

The last step is decisive. The deepest word study is not the one that gathers the most fascinating information; it is the one that can distinguish the word’s entire history from what the word is actually doing in the passage under consideration.
## Bible Hub — Resource Base

It functions primarily as a **rapid-reference hub**, rather than as a structured course for learning Greek or Hebrew or a personal study workspace.

### Very fast access

The greatest strength of Bible Hub's is how quickly it moves from a passage to a collection of related information. Searching a given verse immediately provides you with links to:

- Multiple translations
- The Greek or Hebrew interlinear
- Bible commentaries and cross-references
- Original language of the words used
- Dictionaries and concordances
- Parallel passages

You could quickly receive answers for questions such as, "What does this verse say in other translations?" or "What Greek word is behind this English word?"

### Parallel-translation view

Vertically, multiple translations can be displayed on one page for easy comparison. This could be useful for identifying the following:

- If a phrase is difficult to read, you might find another possible rendering.
- To find where a translation has interpreted something rather than reproducing the wording.
- Whether an expression that seems unusual occurs in just one translation, or globally.
- To compare the differences between literal translations versus more readable translations.

### Access to Greek and Hebrew interlinear

Bible Hub's interlinear page displays the following:

- Original Greek or Hebrew text
- Transliterations
- A short English glossary
- Strong's number concordance
- Expressions of speech
- Morphological information such as tense, case, gender, and number

### Easy search for more biblical usage

This could help with questions such as:

- Where else does the apostle Paul use this word?
- Is this Greek word translated the same way or differently elsewhere?
- Which Hebrew word is translated "spirit," "breath," or "wind"?
- How frequent is this word used?


### Most common uses

- Looking up a verse quickly
- Comparing many English translations
- Identifying the underlying Greek or Hebrew word
- Checking a Strong’s number and basic definition
- Finding every occurrence of a word
- Consulting several older commentaries on one verse
- Locating cross-references and parallel passages
- Preparing a Bible study, message, article, or group discussion
- Checking whether a claim about “the original Greek” is plausible
- Obtaining a preliminary overview before using specialized resources


## STEP Bible — Scripture Tools for Every Person

The goal of the STEP project, developed by Tyndale House and Cambridge, is to expose serious Greek and Hebrew information in a form that remains usable by people who have not formally learned those languages.

I am going to compare this resource with "Bible Hub," and see what the principal benefits of this resource is. While Bible Hub resembles a large online reference shelf, STEP Bible functions more like a free original-language analysis program.


### Stronger word-relationship analysis

For example, STEP is particularly good at showing the relationship between things such as the following:

- An English word and the different original words translated by it
- A Greek or Hebrew word and all the English expressions used to translate it
- An inflected word in a verse and its underlying dictionary form
- Displaying words that share the same root.

STEP can move beyond merely finding an English word and show how the underlying Greek or Hebrew is rendered elsewhere. It also offers several levels of lexical information, allowing a beginner to see a short definition while a more advanced user accesses greater detail.
