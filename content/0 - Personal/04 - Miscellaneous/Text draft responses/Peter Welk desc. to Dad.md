When ==M. Gillenormand (A.K.A. Peter Welk)== lived in the Rue Servandoni (A.K.A. the PNW), he had ==frequented many very good and very aristocratic salons== (Peter often travels to meet with certain company). Although a bourgeois, M. Gillenormand was received in society. 

This is what I found **most accurate**: *As he had a double measure of wit, in the first place, that which was born with him, and secondly, that which was attributed to him, he was even sought out and made much of.* — (Anth's note - Peter does definitely have a double measure of wit... but it might be a result of undiagnosed ADHD unbeknownst to Peter. So Peter might not know any other way of functioning).

He never went anywhere except on condition of being the chief person there. — (Peter does this not intentionally, but as the description continues to point out, he functions only as the 'oracle everywhere').

*There are people who ==will have influence at any price==, and who ==will have other people busy themselves over them==*;  — (This is how I perceive Peter, and others, when they come to a gathering with their own burden and agenda already settled within them, so they end up talking '*at you*' most of the time instead of talking '*with you*'). 

*when they cannot be oracles, they turn wags.* (At a social event, if Peter is not able to win the attention of an audience, he typically does his rounds, turning into a *wag* who moves repeatedly in order to find someone to speak oracles. If unsuccessful in this—since he mainly can only give and not receive—he usually goes somewhere by himself and plays his guitar)

M. Gillenormand was not of this nature; ==**his domination**== in the Royalist salons which he frequented cost his self‑respect nothing. ==He was an oracle everywhere==. It had happened to him to hold his own against M. de Bonald, and even against M. Bengy‑Puy‑Vallée.
