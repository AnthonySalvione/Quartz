# Process

As we have seen already, God's creation is fully covered in chapter 1, verse 1. "In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth." Also, Satan's rebellion is covered in the first half of verse 2. "And the earth became waste and empty; and darkness was upon the surface of the deep." In verse 2a, there are five main points: the fact that the earth became something different from what it was originally; the waste and the emptiness; the darkness and the deep. Keep these five items in mind. After the earth was judged due to Satan's rebellion, it became waste and empty.

Darkness is another indication of judgment. Exodus 10:21-22 and Revelation 16:10 show that darkness is a result of God's judgment. There was darkness when God exercised His judgment upon Pharaoh, and there will be darkness when He exercises His judgment over the antichrist. Thus, the darkness in Genesis 1:2a points to God's judgment.

In addition, we know that light accompanies life and that darkness always signifies death. Where life is, there is light; where death is, there is darkness. The reverse is also true: where there is death, there is darkness. Thus, the darkness in Genesis 1:2 also signifies that the earth was under a condition of death.

# life-study draft

## 3

I saw in this message that Genesis 1 is not mainly a record of creation as a scientific or historical account, but primarily a picture of life. In verse 1, we see God's original creation, but Genesis 1:2 shows that the earth became waste, empty, dark, and under the deep waters because of God’s judgment after Satan’s rebellion. This condition signifies death. Into this situation God came in, not only to restore what had been damaged, but also to carry out further creation, especially for the purpose of producing life. The message points out that the whole Bible is a book of life, and Genesis 1 should be understood from this point of view. Even though the word “life” is not used at first, the chapter is full of the process by which life is generated.

I thought this was cool; that the first requirement for life is the coming of the Spirit. The Spirit of God brooded over the water. Brother Lee used the analogy like a hen brooding over eggs, showing that the Spirit’s work is gentle, living, and for the producing of life. This also matches our experience of salvation. Before we were saved, we were waste, empty, dark, and full of death inwardly. But the Spirit came to brood over us and begin His regenerating work.

Next the Word came. God spoke, “Let there be light,” and the Word brought in light. In our experience, after the Spirit begins working in us, the Lord’s living word shines within us. This light separates us from darkness and gives us discernment. As we go on with the Lord, there is also a further separation between heavenly things and earthly things, and eventually between life and death.

The burden I received from this is that God’s work in us is always toward life, and the focus is life. He wants Christ to rise up in us in resurrection so that life can be produced. This can apply to our personal life, our speaking, our family life, and our function in the church. It is not enough to be outwardly proper or free from obvious darkness. There must be life. The Lord wants to clear away darkness, limit death, and make Christ appear in us so that we become persons who minister life to others.

## 4

This message is continuing the view received from the previous message, that Genesis 1 is a chapter of life, showing not only the generating of life but also the growth of life. In message 3, the Spirit, the Word, and the light came in, and then there were three separations. The dry land appeared out of the death waters on the third day. Wow, this land surely typifies Christ in resurrection as the source that produces life. Just as plant life, animal life, and human life came out of the earth in Genesis, every kind of spiritual life comes out of Christ as the good land.

The first kind of life produced was the plant life: the grass, the herbs, and the fruit trees. This shows the stage of life at it's beginning. When we first receive Christ, we do truly have life, but that life isn't full in its expression. In our experience, we may be like grass, then grow into herbs that yield seed, and eventually become fruit trees that can feed others. This should encourage us, because growth in life is a process. We should not be discouraged by a low stage, but we also should not be satisfied to remain there.

On the fourth day, the stronger lights appeared: the sun, the moon, and the stars. The message called these the "fourth day lights." These are for the growth of life. The sun typifies Christ, the moon typifies the church reflecting Christ, and the stars signify the overcoming saints who shine in this dark age. This shows that if we want to grow, we need to stay under the shining of Christ, remain in the church life, and be helped by living, shining saints.

The message goes on to show the further stages of growth through the fish, the birds, and the cattle. As fish, we can live in the death waters of the world without being “salted” by them. As birds, we can transcend opposition, temptation, and hardship. But even this is not the highest stage. Eventually, we need to be like cattle, able to bear burdens, feed others, and carry out God’s will on the earth.

The burden of this message, I feel, is that the life we received from the Lord must grow. God does not desire for us merely to be saved or to avoid darkness, but to progress and grow. In my experience and function in the church, I need to contact Christ all the time, stay in the church’s shining, and receive help from the saints so that I can grow from a seed, to transcending, and finally to bearing others and ministering life for God’s purpose.
