# Signal vs. Noise Worksheet

## Part 1: Signal or Noise?

**1. You’re driving home and realize you left the stove on. You feel a jolt of anxiety.**  
**Signal.**  
This anxiety is pointing to a real, practical problem. The right response would be to safely turn around or contact someone who can check it.

**2. You sent a text twenty minutes ago. You’ve reread it three times wondering if the tone was off.**  
**Noise.**  
Pattern: _Was that right? Did I sound okay? Was my tone off?_  
The right response would be to stop rereading, let the uncertainty be there, and move on without trying to get certainty.

**3. You feel a tightness in your chest while sitting on the couch. You start wondering if something is wrong with your heart.**  
**Probably noise, depending on context.**  
Pattern: _What does this sensation mean? Is something wrong with my body?_  
The right response would be to notice the sensation without spiraling into checking/researching/reassurance. If there are severe, unusual, or clearly dangerous symptoms, then it could be signal and medical care would be appropriate.

**4. Your friend told you yesterday they felt hurt by something you said. You’re anxious about reaching out to talk it through.**  
**Signal.**  
There is a real relational issue to address. The right response would be to reach out sincerely and talk it through, even if it feels uncomfortable.

**5. You already apologized for being late. An hour later, you’re still wondering if the apology sounded sincere enough.**  
**Noise.**  
Pattern: _Did I do enough? Was that sincere enough? Was that right?_  
The right response would be to accept that you already did the reasonable action and not keep reviewing or trying to fix the feeling.

**6. You have a paper due in two days. You haven’t started. You feel anxious and want to begin tonight.**  
**Signal.**  
The anxiety is pointing to a real responsibility. The right response would be to begin in a practical way, maybe with a small first step instead of waiting until you feel ready.

==FLAG==
**7. You rested for an hour this afternoon. Now you feel guilty and worried you’re ==falling behind on life==.**  
**Noise.**  
Pattern: _Did I do enough? Am I falling behind? Am I wasting my life?_  
The right response would be to let the guilt be there and continue with the next reasonable thing, without trying to “make up” for resting compulsively.

**8. You had an intrusive sexual thought about someone. You’re now wondering what it means about who you are.**  
**Noise.**  
Pattern: _What does this thought mean? What does this say about me?_  
The right response would be to label it as an intrusive thought, not analyze it, and not treat it as evidence about your character.

**9. Your parents are coming over in 45 minutes. You feel anxious about not being in the “right headspace” before they arrive.**  
**Noise.**  
Pattern: _Am I in the right state? Do I feel okay enough? What if I’m not ready internally?_  
The right response would be to allow yourself to feel however you feel and still receive them or continue with the visit without trying to perfectly prepare your emotions.

**10. You notice your stretching routine has expanded from 15 minutes to 90 minutes and is exhausting you. You’re anxious about it taking over your day.**  
**Signal.**  
This seems like a real pattern that is starting to interfere with life. The right response would be to acknowledge it, possibly set a limit, and bring it up with your therapist.

## Part 2: What’s the Right Response?

**11. You’ve labeled something as noise. The urge to “just check one more time to be sure” shows up. What’s the move?**  
**B. Don’t check. Let the urge be there without acting on it.**

**12. You catch yourself asking “what evidence do I have that this is a real problem?” for the fifth time in ten minutes about the same worry. What’s happening?**  
**B. Reality-testing has turned into a compulsion.**

==FLAG== ==KNOWING YOUR OPPONENT==
**13. A noise thought sounds like: “I should really make sure I’m being a good enough friend.” It feels important and morally serious. What does that suggest?**  
**B. Noise can disguise itself as wisdom or conscientiousness.**

**14. You’ve identified something as noise. Which response is not part of the game plan?**  
**C. Reassuring yourself that the worry isn’t true.**

## Part 3: Apply It

**15. Rewrite this thought from a “looking for certainty” stance into a “willingness” stance:**

Original:

> “I need to know for sure that I didn’t say anything wrong at lunch today.”

Possible rewrite:

> “I’m willing to not know for sure whether I said everything perfectly at lunch. I can let the uncertainty be there and continue with my day without reviewing or checking.”

Another more natural version:

> “Maybe I said something wrong, maybe I didn’t. I don’t need to solve that right now. I’m willing to feel uncertain and keep moving forward.”
