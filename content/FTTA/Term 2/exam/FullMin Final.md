# Response 1—Why do we need to be renewed, and how does renewing take place?

We need to be renewed because, although we have been regenerated in our spirit, much of our mind, emotion, will, concepts, and daily living still remain in the old creation and are easily conformed to this age. Romans 12:2 shows that we should not be fashioned according to the age but should be transformed by the renewing of the mind. This renewing is necessary so that we may be saved from a living saturated with the element of the world and become in a practical way, God’s new creation for the Body life and ultimately for the New Jerusalem. Renewing takes place by the renewing Spirit mingled with our regenerated spirit spreading into our mind to renew our entire being. Practically, this requires us to set our mind on the spirit, walk in newness of life, put off the old man, put on the new man, and allow the Lord to use our environment and sufferings to consume our outer man so that, according to 2 Corinthians 4:16, although our outer man is decaying, our inner man may be renewed day by day.

## Response 2—What does it mean to be conformed to the image of the firstborn Son of God?

To be conformed to the image of the firstborn Son of God means that God is making the believers the same as Christ in life, nature, expression, and function, but not in the Godhead. Romans 8:29 says that God predestinated us to be conformed to the image of His Son, that He might be the Firstborn among many brothers. Christ is not only the only begotten Son of God in His divinity but also the firstborn Son of God in resurrection, the prototype, model, and pattern of many sons. We, the believers, are the many brothers who are being molded into His image through God’s organic salvation. This conformation is the issue and consummation of transformation: as Christ’s life grows in us, spreads in us, and transforms us, we are gradually saved from our natural expression and self-likeness to bear the image and expression of Christ. The goal is that God would have not merely many saved individuals, but many sons conformed to Christ for His corporate expression.

## Response 3—How is reigning in life related to grace, righteousness, the Body of Christ, and the New Jerusalem?

Reigning in life is related to grace, righteousness, the Body of Christ, and the New Jerusalem because it is the practical issue of God’s complete salvation. Romans 5:17 says that those who receive the abundance of grace and of the gift of righteousness will reign in life through Jesus Christ. The gift of righteousness is related to God’s judicial redemption: through Christ’s death, God has justified us and given us a firm standing before Him. The abundance of grace is related to God’s organic salvation: grace is the processed Triune God as our life supply, saving us in life and enabling us to overcome sin, death, the self, the flesh, and all negative things. As we receive this grace and righteousness, we do not reign independently or for ourselves; rather, we are brought into the practical Body life, presenting our bodies and living as members one of another. Ultimately, this reigning in life consummates in the New Jerusalem, where God’s redeemed people will reign forever and ever. Thus, reigning in life begins with grace and righteousness, is practiced in the Body of Christ, and consummates in the New Jerusalem.

## Response 4—What does it mean to say that Romans 8 is the center of the universe?

To say that Romans 8 is the center of the universe does not mean that it is the physical or astronomical center of creation. Rather, it means that Romans 8 reveals the central matter in God’s economy: the processed Triune God as the all-inclusive Spirit dispensing Himself as life into the tripartite man. In Romans 8 we see the Spirit of life, the law of the Spirit of life, the Spirit of God, the Spirit of Christ, and the Spirit of the One who raised Jesus from the dead. This Spirit is not merely outside of us but is joined to our spirit, witnesses with our spirit, gives life to our spirit, spreads into our mind, and even gives life to our mortal body. Therefore, Romans 8 brings together God, Christ, the Spirit, life, the believers, sonship, conformation, glorification, and the two spirits. Since God’s purpose in the universe is to dispense Himself into man so that man may become His many sons for His corporate expression, Romans 8 is the center because it unveils this purpose in experience.
