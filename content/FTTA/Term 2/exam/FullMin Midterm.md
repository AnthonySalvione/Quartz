# 1) What do you see concerning the divine-human incorporation as it is revealed in John 14?

What I see concerning the divine-human incorporation in John 14 is that it is a revelation of mutual indwelling, or coinherence, between the Triune God and the regenerated believers. This is much deeper than mere association or outward union. Brother Lee points out that “incorporation” is the proper word because it describes not only oneness in life and nature, but also persons indwelling one another. In John 14:20 and 17, this is revealed in a striking way: the Lord shows that He is in the Father, we are in Him, and He is in us. This is not a corporation, which is only a combination of persons, but an incorporation, an intimate mingling and mutual abiding. Through this divine and mystical coinherence, the consummated God and the regenerated believers are brought into one universal, divine-human incorporation. John 14 therefore unveils an organic, living, personal union in which God and man dwell in one another for the expression of God in humanity.

# 2) How is the divine-human incorporation related to Christ’s being glorified by the Father with the divine glory?

The divine-human incorporation is related to Christ’s being glorified by the Father with the divine glory because Christ’s glorification issues in a corporate expression, not merely an individual 'exaltation'.

In John 14, the Lord’s glorification opens the way for the believers to be brought into the very relationship that He has with the Father. His being glorified means that the divine life and glory are released and imparted so that the believers may enter into this mutual indwelling. Brother Ron handed out a supplemental outline titled 'The Glory of God' in the third class, which speaks about this topic:  God’s goal is not only that individuals would be saved, but that He would gain a corporate, glorious expression through many sons brought into glory. The glory given by the Father to the Son is transmitted to the believers so that they may be one in the divine-human incorporation, with “You in Me and I in you” as its reality. Thus, Christ’s glorification produces this incorporation by bringing the believers into coinherence with the Triune God for God’s corporate expression in the church and ultimately in the New Jerusalem.
# 3) What is the relationship between eating the hidden manna and living in the divine-human incorporation?

The relationship between eating the hidden manna and living in the divine-human incorporation is that eating the hidden manna is the practical, experiential way we enter into and remain in this incorporation. The notes show that the hidden manna is not merely Christ as outward supply, but Christ enjoyed in the Holy of Holies, in the deepest and most personal fellowship with God. To eat the hidden manna is to abide in the Triune God and to participate in the “four ins” in John 14: the Son in the Father, the Father in the Son, the Son in us, and the Spirit of reality in us. In this sense, eating is not just receiving nourishment but entering into mutual indwelling. As we enjoy Christ in this hidden way, we are incorporated into the Triune God, becoming His dwelling place, His sanctuary, and ultimately part of the New Jerusalem as the enlarged Holy of Holies. Thus, eating the hidden manna is the inward secret that sustains a life lived in the divine-human incorporation.

# 4) How is the truth concerning the divine-human incorporation related to your personal life with the Lord and to your church life?


What I see is that the truth of the divine-human incorporation is not just a high doctrine to me. It is very related to my daily personal life with the Lord and also to my church life. In my personal life, this matter becomes real when I come to the Lord in a hidden way to eat Him, open to Him, and let Him make His home in me. I realize more and more that the Lord does not want to stay in just one part of my being. He wants full access. He wants to spread from my spirit into my mind, emotion, and will. Many times, what helps me most is simply to turn my mind back to Him, contact Him in the Word, and let Him wash me and renew me inwardly. Then this matter of incorporation becomes very personal and experiential instead of merely objective.

In my church life, I see that the divine-human incorporation is not an individual matter only. It issues in the Body, in mutual shepherding, and in a real oneness that is more of Christ and less of the self. I have been helped to see that the Lord’s heavenly ministry today is a shepherding ministry, and if I am truly living in fellowship with Him, that should affect the way I am with the saints. I should not only want to enjoy Christ for myself, but also care for others with the same tender care I have received from Him. Also, this truth adjusts my view of the church life. The goal is not just outward activity or attendance, but that we would all be brought into more of the Lord Himself as His dwelling place and expression. Even in a practical way, when my mind is renewed and my disposition is touched by the Lord, I am more preserved in oneness, more open to the saints, and more able to function in a living way.

So to me, the divine-human incorporation is deeply related to both sides of my Christian life: personally, it is my hidden, affectionate, daily contact with the Lord; corporately, it is the church life becoming a genuine expression of Christ through love, shepherding, and oneness. The more I let the Lord work Himself into me, the more I can say this truth is not just something I understand, but something I want to live in.
