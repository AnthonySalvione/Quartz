# Testimonies

## Sean Lin (sent from Irvine)

I came to the U.S. in 2014 and graduated from Columbus College in Michigan in 2018. After that, I went to UT Dallas for grad school and graduated in 2021. Since then, I’ve been working—three years as a software engineer. I also went back to China for a year and a half before coming to this training.

I’m a commuter this term. I have a wife—praise the Lord, much grace. My wife’s name is Sarah, and we have a three-year-old son named Ethan. It is just God’s mercy that I can be here. We’ve had a lot of consecrations and a lot of enjoyment with the Lord since middle school, but I’ve never really touched the Lord as much as I have since this past December, during the Winter Training.

I want to share one point that was a turning point for me to be here. Brother Ron was sharing in Message 8—_the man-child, the warring in heaven, and the overcoming saints_. Under point 3B(3), there was this line:

> Satan does not fear when we try to reason with him, but he **does** fear when we proclaim the spiritual facts of Christ’s victory.

In the past years, I have been reasoning too much. Praise the Lord—this point made me realize Satan loves when we reason with him.

During college, the brothers encouraged me to go to the FTTA. I was like, “Yeah, I’ll go… but maybe later. Let’s wait.” After that, all kinds of things came—relationships, work—everything changed. The Lord within me was speaking continuously, but I just reasoned. Hallelujah—Satan does not fear our reasoning. We do not reason with him. Never reason again. No more reasoning.

Dear trainees: we just proclaim the victory—proclaim Christ’s victory. He is the victory.

In this training, I’m just telling the Lord to deal with me. Give me more training—more “conditions.” It’s not just outward; it’s inward. We need to be trained and regulated so that all we live is Him and no longer ourselves. From that point, I began to see more of my soul-life. Praise the Lord—we can enjoy Him to the point that we would not live our soul-life anymore—only Christ.

## Karen (sent from Seattle)

God is the best Gardener—He knows where to plant us, and how to cause the growth.

I grew up as a “church kid,” but I was very on-and-off. I didn’t attend meetings regularly, but whenever I did go, I knew the joy was real. I grew up in Princeton, New Jersey (a very academic town), and all I cared about was getting into a good college. I filled my time with extracurriculars instead of meetings.

At an 11th grade conference, the brothers told us we were at a very critical junction concerning our faith. Revelation 3 was brought up—lukewarmness. The verse was mentioned: not being hot or cold, but lukewarm. I remember being so shaken. Why is cold better than lukewarm? I prayed, “Lord, make me hot—or cold—preferably hot, but please not lukewarm.”

The Lord used my college application process to strip away what I thought I was. I valued myself based on the college I went to. I went to a wedding (I didn’t even know the couple), and they spoke on Ecclesiastes 3:11. That’s when I accepted the Lord’s timing and plan—something exceeding anything I could plan for myself.

After that, the Lord sovereignly arranged for me to go to L.A., where I saw many patterns of the humanity of Jesus. After college, I started working, and I was led to realize: _even if I may not be in the FTTA, “you are still my trainer.”_ The Lord showed me Hebrews 11:6—He rewards those who diligently seek Him. The more I opened to the Lord to be trained, the more He showed me the benefit of coming to the FTTA.

My being here wasn’t a sudden moment, but a gradual opening where He shifted my being. Praise the Lord—I’m here now.

## Idam (Laredo, Texas)

My name is Idam, from Laredo, Texas. I graduated in December 2025 and majored in kinesiology.

I grew up as a church kid (this can be most of our experience). I grew up not knowing if the Lord was _my_ God or just my parents’ God. I didn’t go to many meetings. I knew the Lord, but not in a deep way—until I went to college.

Praise the Lord—there was a campus club, but we had no full-timers, so I had to rely on my companions’ experience. I remember talking to a brother about how I had companions and worldly friends who would draw me away from the Lord. I prayed for the Lord to remove any natural relationships that drew me away.

The Lord caused me to realize there are two trees to spend our time on. Even though my friends weren’t “bad people,” they were living on the wrong tree. The Lord made me realize I need to be trained and regulated.

A verse that really stuck with me is 2 Timothy 2:22: _“Flee youthful lusts with those who call on the Lord out of a pure heart.”_ Now I’m here—being trained with companions.

## Julie Moon (sent from San Diego; grew up in Anaheim)

I grew up attending the church in Anaheim. As a church kid, you go to meetings and learn a lot—you get a lot of knowledge. I was so blessed to be in Anaheim where there were many trainees and serving ones who cared for me. But sometimes I would go to the meetings, get stirred up, and then go home—and things would just go back to how they always do. Sometimes I felt like I was performing.

_(tearing up)_ The Lord brought me to a point where I really had to pray: “Lord, if I do not have Your appearing and Your presence, I don’t know if I can take Your way.” So I prayed that He would appear to me.

Three years ago, my brother was diagnosed with cancer. He struggled with self-reliance before the Lord, and so did I—but I had to pray for my brother. The Lord even changed my concept of prayer during this time. I started praying for the Lord to heal him, but then I realized I needed to praise Him, because He is the victorious One on the throne.

Even on my brother’s deathbed: “Your kingdom come; Your will be done.” Wow.

He went to be with the Lord. During that time I was as low and hopeless as I had ever been. I felt like I was in death. I was so low, but I couldn’t deny that I had this life within me. That life became my lifeline.

The Lord has been so sovereign to put me in the right situations to see His ways and how much higher they are than mine. I’m so grateful to all the saints who shepherded and cared for me.

The Lord wasn’t sovereign because my environment got better and I feel happier now. The Lord was sovereign because He was able to gain more of me—and I gained more of Him. This term, I pray that nothing would be a performance, and everything would be an issue of my enjoyment of Him and His appearing.

## Kameron Fritz (sent from Fairborn, Ohio)

I’m Kameron Fritz, sent from Fairborn, Ohio. I graduated in May 2024 in business administration and worked full-time for a year. I was raised in the church life my whole life. Like many, I went to SST, YP conferences, high school conferences—but unfortunately, I took a lot of it for granted growing up. I was very blessed to receive the speaking of the Lord at that age, but honestly, 75% went in one ear and out the other.

When I got to college, it was easy to get distracted and pulled away from the church life by the world. That was my experience for my first two years—distracted and pulled away. I stopped meeting with the saints. After so long, I didn’t want to come back because it felt awkward, and I assumed everyone would ask where I’d been. So I stopped showing up.

But by the Lord’s mercy, I experienced the cherishing and nourishing of the saints. My parents hosted a college dinner and fellowship at our house for students from the local campus, and I had no excuse not to go. I remember feeling, “This is actually what I’ve been missing this whole time”—and I didn’t even know I was missing it.

I realized I needed the members of the Body. The Christian life is impossible on your own.

Since then, I got back into the church life, back into the Word, pursuing with the believers. Last spring, I short-termed in the training. It was a wonderful experience. I met a lot of the brothers here, and it changed my concept of the trainees. I thought they were “special Christians,” but they’re normal believers who want to be trained.

I still thought, “Maybe I won’t do the training.” But the Lord kept poking me throughout the summer. I couldn’t stop thinking about it. I wondered: “Am I too content where I am?” I had a job—but the Lord led me here. Here I am.

## Sarah (Lubbock, Texas; grew up in Anaheim)

I grew up in Anaheim and was very aware of the training growing up (we had children’s team back then). I always looked up to the training, and ever since I was a kid, I had it in my heart to go.

In high school, I was at a low point, but the trainees met me where I was, and I could be myself with them. Because of that, I went to college in Texas where they were from.

My first two years were a very routine church life. I would talk to the Lord, but most of my moments with Him were in conferences or times I felt inspired.

In my sophomore year, one of my close friends got “poisoned” (?), and she ended our friendship overnight. I wanted to know what happened, and I was clueless. I reached out to her, and she talked to me for three hours—one-sided. She was badmouthing the church life, the Recovery, and the training. At that point, I realized my roots weren’t deep, because I started doubting the church life and my own experiences.

I decided I wouldn’t go to the training—because everyone else was going and it felt like an expectation. After that, I struggled a lot inwardly and was discouraged.

Somehow, I decided to go on a Greece trip the next semester before starting school. Before that, I went to the semiannual training and also to a fellowship concerning the Lord’s move in Europe (Paris). The Lord spoke to me: “You have nothing holding you back—why don’t you go?” I was like, “Oh…” I wasn’t expecting that. I got the way to go, and I went to Greece. My life was changed. I realized my life was not my own.

I went to Paris after that. I was on a high—I was in love with the Lord. I was by myself for two whole weeks in Paris. Everyone else was resting from the gospel trips or vacation—it was just me and the Lord. Thousands of conversations became my reality. We walked all around the city/country (?), and I had long conversations with the Lord.

Eventually I met the full-timers there and new contacts. I was exposed: I realized I could not overflow to these contacts like the full-timers could. I realized I need to be trained if I want to speak and serve the Lord full-time.

Here I am. I’m so happy to be here—it’s a privilege.

## Johnny Epp (Windsor, Canada; grew up in Calgary, Canada)

I think my testimony can be described by Ecclesiastes 4:9–10: “Two are better than one… woe to the one who falls…”—companionship.

I grew up a very individualistic person, from a Mennonite background. For those who don’t know: two things describe that background—being secluded from the modern world, and having a lot of children. I had a large family, and I felt very neglected in it. On top of that, I grew up in a locality that was very large, but there were no brothers my age. I felt so alone.

That led me to find friends anywhere I could, but I realized they weren’t my true companions. The Lord sovereignly arranged for my family to move to a new province. That was a new chapter for me. I was no longer surrounded by worldly friends, but God-given companions—and there were lots of them.

Although seldom and brief, I actually had a few times in high school where I prayed for a companion. Later I found out those companions I was built up with had the _same exact prayer_. I would encourage you: a simple prayer like that can go a long way in the Lord’s eyes. I prayed, they prayed, and the Lord answered. Praise the Lord.

Being surrounded by these ones was a great help to me, and it strengthened me to come here.

## Jenny Kim (Vancouver, Canada)

Like many who shared, I grew up in the church life. But the God my parents loved was never _my_ God. We immigrated to Canada when I was young. I adopted English and had to adapt to many environments. Because of that, I was not very open—I had little capacity because I was learning another language. Meetings became mostly routine.

Eventually I stopped meeting in 11th grade because my family stopped meeting. When I got to college, my first thought was: “I’m going to get my job, degree, family… go through all of that, and just die. There must be more to my life.” But I didn’t know who Jesus was.

I went through a huge self-improvement era. No matter what I did or accomplished, the emptier I felt.

A lot happened, but on the last day of SST registration, my companion texted me out of nowhere (we both weren’t meeting): “Do you want to go to college training in Champaign—for a week?” I wanted to see Chicago, so I went, but NACT was actually life-changing for me.

That week started my pursuit of knowing Jesus. It changed the entire direction of my life. The emptiness I thought nothing could ever fill was filled with Jesus. I started pursuing with many young people, and many things happened.

Fast forward: after I graduated, I was working at an internship. I tasted working for the first time and I loved it. That stable life was staring me in the face, but I knew how much I lacked in my foundation. I realized that not gaining the Lord day by day was wasting my time. My days would all be wasted if I don’t learn how to gain God.

I didn’t have peace to work full-time yet. I decided to consecrate two years to be here.
