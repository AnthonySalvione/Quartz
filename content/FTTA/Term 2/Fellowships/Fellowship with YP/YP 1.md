This is an important point. I want to dwell on this word facilitate. your job is not to regurgitate, your job is to faciliate. In this young generation - Gen Z and Alpha, all of them (because of their social media contacts) are consuming lots of media and concepts. You also know how to consume. But then, when it comes to speaking something, MOSTLY you just regurgitate. You take whatever you heard and just respeak it. I bet you, a lot of the things you talk about are not your original thoughts, but just rehashing someone elses thought.

One thing that the young generation lacks is really taking that next step to consider and think about, ponder, question, look at the underlying assumptions... you kind of just accept it because it is on the paper so it must be true. Then, you pawn it off as knowledge without really considering. This is the generational thing.

This affects our grouptimes. The group times may be spent mainly recounting what the brother spoke, rather than actually getting into it yoursself. Facilitating things - keep in mind some things. Questions are really important. Not just that you ask questions, but the kind of questions you ask. Clarifying questions. Questions that are open ended. Things like "Why do you think this? How would you define this?" Don't as kquestions that have yes or no asnwers. Typically those quesions have "is or isn't" as the operating word. They can always be answered yes or no. "Isn't it the case that... ". Try to avoid those kinds of questions, and instead say, "what are some other explanatians?" 

Lets say you ask a question and they get it very wrong. Don't cut them off, right? Because once you do that it makes it harder to facilitate a response, because they are in the realm of right and wrong and they realize they gave a wrong answer. Yes, there is a yes and wrong concerning the truth, but there is no wrong answer when fellowshipping. There is value in all kinds of fellowship and consideration. You could say, "Oh, that is a very interesting thought, what are some other ways of viewing this?" Just opening some doors to steer away from that point. Then, at the end of the time, you can end up by looking at what the bible says. YOu can lead them down this path, and eventaully at the end, you might say something, "oh maybe the truth could be this way". Basically, you are facilitating in a way that does not make anybody feel wrong or rejected. Every wrong answer can be slightly turned.

They may say "salvation is not eternal" - dont say "that is wrong dont say that", but rahter, "Well, say, if our salvation is not secure how many times do we need to be saved to be secure?"
