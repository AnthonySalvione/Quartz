# INCORPORATION

In speaking of the issue of Christ being glorified by the Father with the divine glory,
Brother Lee uses a very particular word—incorporation. To the best of our knowledge, this
word has not previously been used in interpreting the holy Word.

We should note that Brother Lee speaks not of corporation but of incorporation. The two
words are closely related, but there is nonetheless a significant difference between them. A
corporation is a group of people combined or associated into one body, usually for the purposes
of business or government. An incorporation is a matter of union and intimate mingling. To
incorporate is to unite intimately, to blend, combine, or mingle thoroughly into a whole.
Incorporation denotes both an act of incorporating and the state of being incorporated.

In speaking of the issue of Christ's glorification, incorporation is a much better term than
corporation. Whereas union concerns our oneness in life with the Lord and mingling is related
to the divine and human natures, incorporation is a matter of persons in a relationship of
coinherence. "Union and mingling refer to our relationship with the Lord in our life and nature
but not in our person. Humanly speaking, no person can be in another person. But in the
divine and mystical realm, the consummated God and the regenerated believers, the persons,
indwell one another. This is an incorporation. In this universal, divine-human incorporation,
persons indwell one another, that is, they coinhere" (The Issue of Christ Being Glorified, p. 41).

The coinherence that makes possible the universal, divine-human incorporation is revealed in
John 14:20, 17. If we see this crucial matter of coinherence, we will realize why we must avoid
the word corporation, which cannot convey the thought of coinherence but only of combination
or association, and must instead use the word incorporation, which implies an intimate union
and which, in our usage, can convey the idea of coinherence and the incorporation produced by
it.
