# Reviewing ICSC message on Job

If you recall, I started that message on the Lord’s Day morning with this matter—that in Job’s days (I'm referring to my message on Job), he was a contemporary of Abraham—roughly the same era. At that time, although God scarcely revealed Himself to man, He spoke with Job. He had conversation with Job. He even “lectured” certain things in the way of a man-to-man discussion. In fact, Job was asking God questions, and God was asking Job questions—quite an intimate and very personal exchange. Many of you would have a hard time believing that this happened thousands of years ago—that man was so close to Jehovah God—unless it were written.

God came in to directly address Job and spoke to him. He told Job, “Get ready. I am going to ask you some questions now.” Then God began to talk to him about creation—the old creation—this universe. Then, in a very interesting way, He spent a bit of time talking about animals. You might say, as did I previously, “Animals? What does that have to do with God?” Well, He spoke about animals—interesting. Then He spoke about other things.

## God's Visitation has a Purpose

But even though Job had God’s personal appearing to man, every time God meets with a man—every time God has fellowship or intercourse with man—there is always a purpose. That purpose is to unveil something about Himself. Otherwise, God has no reason to meet with man.

Every time the Lord comes to you, or appears to you (of course, not physically—but I am talking about better than physically, which is in your spirit): within you is the true God, which is Christ today, who is mingled with your spirit as the Spirit. When He comes to us, when He gets our attention, when He visits us in this way, He wants to reveal something to you. That is why. So for a human being to have the visitation of God is not a small thing.

After all, after many years serving the Lord, I can tell you: I count on and depend on the Lord’s visitation to me. Without it, I am finished. I have no ministry, no burden—I have nothing. So I learned to expect His visitation. I long to experience it. Therefore, I have conditioned myself for His visitation.

## Conditioning Ourselves to Receive the Lord

You may want Him to meet with you, or visit you, but you may not be in a condition to receive Him. If your condition is not right, then it does not work that way. So, brothers and sisters, you need to love and pursue the Lord, etc. I would suggest that all the time—every day, every moment—you would condition yourself for His visitation. Sometimes those visitations are unexpected, at an hour or time you did not expect. If you are not ready for this visitation, that visitation may not happen. It may not work.

God is always wanting to reveal Himself to someone—anyone who seeks Him, anyone who wants to know Him. God is ready at any time. Is man ready, though? Are you ready? That is the question.

How come certain believers always have the Lord’s visitation? It is not because they are smarter, or superior. It is because, yes, they have the Lord’s mercy for sure, but they have also readied themselves. They have conditioned themselves in case the Lord wants to talk to them.

## God can be Forced to Stay Away

Similarly, while the Lord desires to fellowship with man and have communion with man to unveil or reveal Himself and His heart—His desires, the intimate things, the inward parts—to man, there are times God is forced to stay away from man. Even in the case of Abram: because Abram did something that was not pleasing to God—he did not condition himself—God stayed away from him for thirteen years. One mistake—thirteen years. At least according to the biblical account, God did not speak to him or show up. God just left him.

So while the Lord desires to unveil Himself and appear to us, it is equally true that God can be forced not to do that, because you are in a wrong condition. You are not pleasing to Him. Your situation—your lack of cooperation to ready yourself—forces Him, in a sense, to stay away from you.

I will tell you, by my testimony, that there is probably nothing more painful—nothing more of a suffering—than to not have the Lord’s appearing, or to have the Lord’s appearing very far in between, rarely. If you say, “I meet the Lord once a year,” I would say, “Brother, I am really concerned for you.” How could the Lord meet you only once a year—on New Year’s Day only? That is not good. That is not something to boast about. You should be able to get to a point where you can say, “The Lord meets me every day.” Now, maybe not in a spectacular or extraordinary way, but He does. And He is ready to—if you are in the right state or condition. I say this to stir you up: in this training you should meet the Lord, or the Lord should meet with you, regularly and frequently.

Now, you cannot control that; but on the other hand, you can—by readying yourself.

## Paul saw the answer that Job was trying to find

So back to Job: because it was such a primitive time, even though the Lord showed up and talked to Job, He did not—or could not—reveal so much to Job. That is not because Job wasn’t seeking God; rather, it was because Job was in a very primitive stage of God’s present revelation.

The fullness of God’s revelation—of His heart, His purpose, His economy—awaited (1) the coming of Christ, and (2) the ministry of the apostle Paul. What God wanted to reveal to man was eventually shown through Paul. He completed the word of God—completed what began in Genesis.

Now you may say, “How about the New Jerusalem in John?” I would call that a further mending ministry of John—his Revelation, his Epistles, and his Gospel. But mainly it was with Paul.

This is why nobody could solve Job’s perplexing problem: Why did he suffer? Why did this good, perfect, complete, upright man—a man that God even boasted of to Satan—have to suffer such great loss? All his earthly possessions. All his earthly family—his whole family, except maybe his wife. Stripped of all his “wonderful” ethical achievements, until he was just in the dust, with boils all over him, this close to cursing God. Why? Nobody could understand or answer that question—not even in the Old Testament—until Paul.

## Some Live as though We are Still in Job's days

I am not going to get back into all of that; I just want to remind you. Today, we are two thousand years after Paul. But do you know what? To most people—including Christians—they live as if they are still in the days of Job. They can read the book of Job and still be perplexed, bewildered, disillusioned as Job was—today—two thousand years post-Paul. Can you believe that? It is true.

In fact, they have hardly any interest in Job. But this little message I gave should convince everyone: Job is a most important book concerning God’s divine economy—if you have Paul’s glasses on. Then you can say that. But Christians do not look at it that way.

# General Burden

## On Today's Christianity

There is a huge church in Newport Beach where I am—by all accounts, a successful mega-church. You go on their campus and you will find everything: a building for worship, and lots of buildings to meet any kind of need you have. It’s a pretty place. So I checked into the sermons by the senior pastor, and my heart just broke into pieces—into pieces.

There is such a church—so attractive—trying to render help to people, and I am sure they do render some kind of help. But the content being dispensed—the things being preached—if you have even a glimmer of a view of this, of Paul, of this ministry that we are under, you would mourn. You would weep for our fellow believers who do not know anything better, because they trust their pastor—who “knows everything” and went to all the theological seminaries, and so on.

Yet, in order to tickle ears and get people to come and listen, you have to speak something “relevant,” something applicable. Therefore, these sermons—how to date, how to have a successful marriage, and so forth. Not that these things are not important—please understand—but that can become the theme of their Sunday meeting, couched in biblical terms and spiritual language.

No. We need good marriages and good families. I am for that, and I speak for that. But that is not what God wants. We have to ask the question: What does the Lord want? What is His desire? What is His pleasure? What is His purpose in creating man, in making this universe, in coming to the earth Himself in space and time, in accomplishing His short earthly ministry that ended with a terrible criminal death on the cross, in His ascension to the heavens to be the Lord of all—not just to be worshipped and adored, but to administrate and govern this universe to further carry out and consummate that eternal purpose—the purpose of the ages? They have zero idea, zero interest.

## Our Pattern Moses

I mourn. I used to listen to Witness Lee talk about this; he mourned all the time. He was not a “happy” man in that sense—he was mourning for the state of affairs, especially among the believers.

Christianity today is more superficial than even fifty or sixty years ago in this country. One might conclude, “Maybe God has given us up… maybe God has given up.” Sometimes—because He did that in the Old Testament. He was even going to do a total redo. It was so bad. With Israel, He was going to wipe them out and raise up Moses and his line to replace Israel—except for Moses pleading and appealing to God’s faithful promise: “If You do this, all the nations will deride You, ridicule You—what kind of God is this, who brought a people out of Egypt only for them to die and be abandoned?” Moses “changed God’s mind,” apparently—actually, he did not. God just needed a man who would know His heart, who would, so to speak, hold God’s feet to the fire.

## Seeing Through Paul

Dear brothers and sisters—especially the new ones coming in this term in this FTTA—I like to tell you: you don’t know how blessed you are. You really do not know, but you ended up here. Just like fifty years ago, I wandered into this place. I was looking for something, but not this. And I stumbled into this place, and these sixty years, every day, I pinch myself: Why me? What got me here? Why am I here? Why are they not here? I still don’t have an answer. I can only attribute it to God’s sovereignty and mercy. You don’t know how blessed you are.

So what Job could not realize or understand, Paul supplied the answer. You have to read all of Paul—and, may I say, with the help of this ministry that we are under, it has been interpreted for us. I cannot say, “I don’t know why” anymore. I know why Job suffered. Even I do. It was simply God’s way to translate a person from the old creation into the new: to strip and destroy what is old in the fallen natural man, and to recreate, remodel, and rebuild a new man in Christ. Only this new man, constituted of Christ, is fit for God’s expression and God’s building—thereby fulfilling God’s economy.

So when I pass through things—maybe not as bad as Job, but you know what I mean—things in life, I know a lot more how to face those situations. Poor me if I don’t. I have been told and taught. Of course I still need the Lord’s grace and mercy, but I cannot say I do not know; that would be a flat lie for me.

## Paul's Revelation of the Mystery

Now, Paul—very important to know Paul and appreciate Paul—again and again used this term: the revelation of the mystery. Define that. In Romans, Ephesians, and Colossians you can find this phrase: “the revelation of the mystery.” This little phrase is the key—_the key_—to God accomplishing His heart’s desire with a group of people, even His chosen elect, including us—you and I.

Now I want to give you four conditions—especially the new ones—that you would not forget: to ready yourself all the time to receive the revelation of the mystery in this training.

## God's Wisdom is a Mystery

So I turn to 1 Corinthians, where Paul spoke similarly in chapter 2, verse 7: “We speak God’s wisdom in a mystery.” It’s like a riddle—like a jigsaw puzzle you don’t know how to put together—something the human mind cannot comprehend or figure out. It’s a divine mystery.

The mystery of God: God is a mystery. God being Triune is a further mystery—God the Father, the Son, and the Spirit. This creation—this visible universe—is still a mystery. People are still figuring out things about this universe that are mind-blowing: the laws, the physics, down to the atom, up to black holes and new galaxies being discovered. People don’t know what it is all about. And wait until they ask the question “Why.” Philosophers try, but still no answer—they make up something, but that is not the real answer.

God’s mystery, brothers, is Christ. Easy to say, huh? My goodness—those four words require the whole Bible to explain. You need so much light to see it.

And not just God’s mystery—you are a mystery. Aren’t you? Can you figure yourself out yet? I don’t think so. I’m getting older; there are things to take care of—my eyes, my ears. I need to know more about my body now. Things I took for granted throughout my life, now I need to understand a little so I can take care of them. I couldn’t believe how much of a mystery I am. Even my brain—I don’t know what’s happening there: wires and connections, chemicals and proteins. I have no idea. I just know: don’t eat so much fast food. That I can understand. But the nutritionists and neuroscientists—this is a mystery.

You are a mystery. The whole human race is a mystery. Why are we here? And Christ, who is the mystery of God, is Himself a mystery.

We know now, through Paul, that Christ’s mystery is the church—not this church and that church, not this denomination and that—the church.

Even to the point that you can be part of that ministry that Paul belonged to—what we call the New Testament ministry—that you can, to a greater or lesser degree (that’s not the point), serve in that ministry, so that more may know, more may be enlightened, more may see, and more may become what they see.

For this, brothers and sisters, I hope that in these two years—even if you don’t remember everything spoken at this podium, even if you forget some outlines—you would at least have received a vision. A vision.

A vision is not just revelation. Revelation is something covered being unveiled: now you can see it. Vision is what you see—simply. It is what you see. Revelation gives you access to the contents, and the contents of what you see in your spirit should become a controlling vision. When I first came into the church life, I could not speak this way—I was young and raw. I loved the Lord. But I would testify to you now: I have seen this vision, and this vision governs me. And, by the Lord’s mercy, I aim to be obedient to this vision until I see the Lord.

I hope these two years would at least have that effect—the beginning effect. A vision is more than knowledge. A vision is more than information. A vision is a scene you saw that you cannot deny, that sticks with you. You cannot unsee it. Some people try to unsee it with great effort, but they cannot, so what they do is deceive themselves and say they never saw it. They say it was wrong. No. I have seen this happen. Because they could not unsee what they had seen, they had to say something to justify that the vision was wrong.

## The Four Conditions

Now, what are the conditions? I will say:

#### 1) Love Him

You have to love Him. You have to love the Lord—desperately and enormously. How about every day you are here for the next two years—first-termers—every day you’re in love with Christ; every day you love the Lord. In this training, forget about good days and bad days, this and that. Try not to be thrown off or distracted by it. Every day, concentrate on loving the Lord Jesus.

I’ll give you my testimony: that is what happened. I loved the Lord so much—I wanted everything that He has for me.

#### 2) Seek Him

You have to want this—in other words, you have to seek. It’s a great blessing, brothers and sisters, to have a seeking spirit and a learning heart.

You are here (somewhat in Arabia). Paul went to Arabia for a period of time so that he could see the revelation before he came out some years later to carry out his ministry. Take this as your Arabia. Not a particularly attractive place—it’s in the wilderness. Every day you eat the same food. I don’t mean the food here; I mean forty years of manna. There are no Egyptian cucumbers, onions, garlic, or fish—just manna every day.

You are in Arabia, but oh, what a blessed place—when God appears to you in the wilderness, day after day after day. You need to seek Him. Every day love Him, and every moment seek Him. Two years will fly by—fourth-termers, am I correct? For me it’s more than two—it’s forty years. It’s gone.

Just set your heart to seek Him—His heart and His purpose.

#### 3) Obey the Revelation (The Obedience of Faith)

I would say—to quote Romans 16, where Paul says, “but now has been manifested, and through the prophetic writings… for the obedience of faith”—the third thing is: **OBEY. OBEY.**

Not just be satisfied to receive the revelation, to hear these things, and even to understand them, but have the readiness to obey what you hear. Obedience is more precious than sacrifice to God. He wants obedience more than anything else—especially obedience to what He has just spoken to you, or unveiled to you. He wants your obedience—the obedience of faith.

I can testify of this also: I had this within me. Over the thousands of messages throughout the years—I lost count—I may have had a bad day or week, but I would go to listen to a message and exercise myself with the readiness not just outwardly to say “amen” (that is easy), but inwardly to obey what I heard.

To this day, I am still obeying some of the things I heard many years ago, in this or that situation.

#### 4) Exercise Your Spirit to See and Hear in the Spirit

In order to see, and in order to hear (you can listen and not hear; you can gaze and not see), what is the difference? Paul’s prayer is the difference—in Ephesians 1: that the God would grant to you the spirit of wisdom and revelation.

Here it says only the spirit of man can discern the spiritual things. We are in a spiritual realm. These mysteries occur in the spiritual realm. So you have to hear in the spirit, see in the spirit, comprehend in the spirit, and apprehend in the spirit—not with your mind merely. The mind comes into play in terms of understanding and interpreting, but it must begin in the spirit.

“I was in spirit”—four times in Revelation. **I saw in spirit.** So exercise—these two years—dear ones—your spirit. Whether you want to or not, it’s your spirit. It’s your faculty. It belongs to you. You have a say in how you use your spirit. _The spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets._ So you and I, in this training, must exercise the spirit every day.

In your classes—whether you are half asleep or not—all kinds of things will happen, but still do your very best to learn to be in your spirit: by calling on Him deeply from within, by stronger and more aggressive prayer, by fanning that flame into fire within you. You do that. God cannot do all of these for you. You must cooperate.

And I take that one thing away from that message—that enlightened me, nourished me, convicted me, or exposed me.

What this implies is that even if Watchman Nee would change one day, Witness Lee would not change—because he was not following the man; he was following the vision that was brought to him by that man.

Since we have the up-to-date and ultimate vision, we should closely follow after it.

The leadership in the New Testament ministry is the leadership of the controlling God-given revelation of God’s economy—not a person, not even a group of people. The leadership is the revelation and this vision.

In the New Testament age, the Lord has one unique ministry—that is the ministry of the New Testament. That is the ministry we are under. Though in the past ages that ministry was not complete, it was not perfect—until now.

There is only one unique ministry. None of us here needs to raise up another ministry. There are so many ministries today that have torn up the church and divided the Body of Christ. We must adhere closely to this one unique ministry.

Brothers and sisters, may I pray for you for the rest of your life: to produce and build up the one unique Body as His one unique testimony.

## Characteristics

Here are the characteristics of this unique ministry that we are under. We are under the same ministry—like we are under Paul—not a different ministry.

#### 1) Healthy teaching + spiritual warfare against strange teachings

It ministers the healthy teaching of God’s economy and wars the good warfare against the different and strange teachings of the dissenters—with the “strange fire” of man’s natural enthusiasm, natural affection, natural strength, and natural ability.

I hope that by being here you will begin to see what this ministry is—to have a vision—and you will contrast it with what is offered in general Christendom today. You will see a difference… between gold and I don’t know what.

#### 2) The local churches as lampstands + building up the one Body

It introduces the local churches as the golden lampstands to be the testimony of Jesus, with the same essence, appearance, and expression; and it builds up the one Body of Christ by the one Spirit—perfecting all of us into the oneness of the Triune God.

I know this is dense—lots of words—but take your time. Two years. Slowly it will work itself into you—not just information, but light and revelation—eventually forging a vision in you.

#### 3) Preparing the overcomers as Christ’s bride and queen

It prepares the overcomers to be Christ’s bride—His queen. In Himself (as Psalm 45 indicates—the “royal abode”), the mutual abode of the King and the queen; and in the local churches (as in the Psalms—the “places of ivory”) to consummate in the New Jerusalem as the King’s palace.

This ministry betroths us to Christ, stirring up our love for Him in the simplicity and purity toward Christ, to make us His queen. This ministry is powerful—so powerful that it works out God’s economy in these rich aspects—unbelievable aspects.

#### 4) Strengthening us to follow Christ on the way of the cross

It strengthens us to follow Christ in the fellowship of His sufferings on the pathway to glory—the way of the cross—according to the manifestation and multiplication of life.

#### 5) Dispensing Christ into us for revelation, enjoyment, growth, and reigning

It dispenses Christ as grace and truth and life and the Spirit into us for our revelation of Christ, our enjoyment of Christ, and our growth in life—that we may be saved in life, to reign in life.

The true ministry of the New Testament accomplishes that. You won’t mainly get “help to date,” sorry—you will get help on how to enjoy Jesus and grow in life, eventually to reign in life.

#### 6) Sanctifying and shepherding through the word and the pneumatic Christ

It sanctifies us through the word of the truth and by the washing of the water in the word; it also shepherds us with a cherishing and nourishing presence of the pneumatic Christ.

If you remain under this ministry, you will receive this benefit.

#### 7) Bringing everyone into function + practicing the God-ordained way

It brings all of us into function to practice the God-ordained way, which is the scriptural way, and it leads us to follow the Lamb wherever He may go, for the preaching of the gospel of the kingdom to the whole inhabited earth.

#### 8) Bringing us into a new revival: living out the New Jerusalem

It brings us into a new revival—of living out the New Jerusalem and working out the New Jerusalem—to gain the reality of the Body of Christ as the highest peak in God’s economy.

“I mean… isn’t this the gospel, brothers and sisters? Isn’t this good news?”

#### 9) Keeping the local churches healthy in life and established in truth

The healthy teaching of God’s economy, according to the unique New Testament ministry, keeps the church—the local church—healthy in life and established in truth, to bring forth godliness: a life that lives Christ and expresses God in Christ.
