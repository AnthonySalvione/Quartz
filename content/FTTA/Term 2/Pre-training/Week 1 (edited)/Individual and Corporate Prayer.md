# Fellowship on Individual and Corporate Prayer (1)  
### The Significance of Prayer, the Principle of Prayer, and the Prayer Ministry of the Church  
  
**Scripture Reading:**  
Matt. 6:9–10; 18:18–20; John 15:7; Eph. 6:18; 1 John 5:14; Jude 20  
  
---  
  
# Introduction
  
I was thinking today as I looked at your schedule (it has probably changed some in the last thirty years), but it’s very interesting: when you wake up, pretty quickly after that there is some prayer. At the end of your day, I think it might end with some prayer. And throughout the day, I entered into the houses and I saw different groups praying. Every place in this training seems to be full of prayer—study time, mealtime, going to campus, coming back home, Tuesday night prayer meeting, ministry meeting, singing—**all prayer.** Prayer for the brothers speaking the word. It seems that prayer is everywhere. The training is permeated and saturated with prayer.  
  
So I hope prayer would not be a duty to you. I hope that from the points and verses in this outline you would receive some supply and see the energizing that comes through prayer. If you don’t see this, it could be that you have never touched the significance of praying—if prayer is only a religious duty or a struggle (a modern term would be “a grind”). If every time you go to pray it feels like gears are grinding with no oil, I hope this helps you.  
  
That is why I'd like to begin with Roman numeral I.  
  
---  
  
# I. The Real Significance of Prayer  
  
**Outline point:** The real significance of prayer is to contact God in our spirit and to absorb God Himself—Jude 20; John 14:13; 15:7.  
  
I remember reading a line in one of the Life Lessons when I was a teenager, and I thought, *“Really? That is prayer? I thought prayer was asking the Lord for things, and for Him to do things.”* If this is your concept, you would not be able to participate in any of the corporate prayer times of the church meetings.  
  
What it means to pray is to **contact God in our spirit.** I hope every time—both in individual and corporate prayer—it would be a **contact with God**, not merely saying words that come from your mind, environment, or feelings. It must be deeper. There has to be contact with God in our spirit, which is the deepest part of our being.  
  
I think the ministry uses this term—particularly Ed—“You have to pray yourself **into God**.” Before we can pray for God—before we can pray genuine, true prayers, prayers that matter, the highest prayers—we have to contact God in our spirit. And in my experience, many times it is hard to do that right away.  
  
I’ll give a simple example: I drove to the training center, I was late, I was speeding, and I cut off a sister. Then I went to pray, and the Lord was saying, “Brother, your driving was… not good.” So I couldn’t pray. I had to clear my conscience. That’s why we have prayers for confession—so the blockage can be removed.  
  
Sometimes we go strong and try to force our way through the Word or an outline, but there is no sense of flow within us. That may be because we haven’t taken the precaution of clearing and cleaning our conscience through prayers of confession.  
  
To know the Lord’s sense and the Lord’s anointing in the deeper part of our spirit—this is a huge part of learning how to pray. How do we pray? We don’t have a book or a manual. We are following the living God in our spirit. We are learning to sense Him and to utter the feeling that He imprints and implants in our inner being.  
  
So prayer is very tender and delicate in some ways. It requires a clear conscience and a clear sky before the Lord.  
  
But not only do we need to contact the Lord in our spirit—we need to **absorb God** in our spirit. You will see words in the outline like “mingle” and “inhale.” This is to absorb God. This is the significance of prayer: to contact God and to absorb God.  
  
In the morning, your first thought should be: *“Am I contacting God? Am I absorbing God?”* Because this is how we know we are praying the Lord’s prayers. Genuine prayer is **He Himself within us praying** for His will to be done. So it is crucial that we contact Him and absorb Him.  
  
---  
  
## A. Prayer as Contact with God  
  
**Outline point:** Prayer is the contact of the human spirit with the divine Spirit, during which we inhale God—Jude 20; John 4:24.  
  
“Inhale.” If you want to inhale God, it may be that you need to exhale other things. Many times we focus on the positive—enjoying and dispensing—but we can’t forget this: in order to be filled, we have to be emptied.  
  
If shouting louder and stronger doesn’t work, then check: *Is there something already filling me?* We need to exhale and breathe out many things in order to breathe Him in.  
  
I hope that in your personal time with the Lord, at the beginning, you would open to Him in this way—coming forward with boldness through His blood—and spend some time before Him, opening to Him, to absorb Him, and then inhale Him.  
  
If we do not have Roman numeral I, we can never really progress to the further aspects of prayer.  
  
---  
  
## B. Genuine Prayer: Mingling with God  
  
**Outline points:**  
- Genuine prayers are prayers in which we are mingled with God the Spirit in our spirit—Jude 20; Eph. 6:18; Rom. 8:16; 1 Cor. 6:17.  
- Prayer must be a joint prayer in which God is mingled with our spirit.  
- True prayers—prayers that involve God and man—are the issue of the Spirit of God being mingled with man’s spirit and of man’s spirit being mingled with the Spirit of God—Jude 20; Rom. 8:4, 26.  
- In this prayer, God and man are mingled together, and God is the Initiator and Motivator; God prays in man, and man prays in God—James 5:17.  
  
So many good verses here. In Jude 20: “But you, beloved, building…” It doesn’t say praying according to our human concept—it says **praying in the Holy Spirit.** We have to be one with Him. We have to get into Him in order to have genuine prayers.  
  
“Prayer must be a joint prayer.” Is your prayer such? If it is, it will be like surfing—because He is the wave. It will be like a sailboat: the Spirit is blowing, and you are being carried. I hope you all could experience this kind of prayer.  
  
True prayers are prayers that involve **both God and man.** If you are just praying yourself, you do not have a true prayer. True prayer is the issue of the Spirit of God being mingled with man’s spirit.  
  
This is the way: touching the spirit, absorbing Him, breathing Him in. Even to begin your prayer time, it is good just to inhale the Lord and tell Him you love Him.  
  
Sometimes we come to pray and we are too complicated. Or we come late to a prayer meeting and think, “Let me figure out what the burden is.” It is easy to get into your mind when praying. You have to say no to your mind and **jump into your spirit.**  
  
Regarding corporate prayer: the battle is that the enemy does not want you to open your mouth, because if you do, it indicates that the kingdom is coming. If we pray in one accord (as in Acts), the enemy is finished. He will do anything to keep you from entering into the prayer. When we pray, we enter into a battle. This is true.  
  
---  
  
## C. Praying in the Holy Spirit  
  
**Outline point:** If we would have genuine prayers—prayers initiated by God and touching God—we must pray in the Holy Spirit. Praying in the Holy Spirit means that we and the Holy Spirit pray together in the fellowship of the two spirits—Jude 20; 2 Cor. 13:14; Phil. 2:1.  
  
Real prayers are God-initiated prayers. Genuine prayer is initiated by God, and He not only initiates it—He motivates it, strengthening and empowering you to continue to pray.  
  
There is a verse in James about Elijah: he “prayed earnestly”—and the footnote says literally he **“prayed in prayer.”** Elijah “prayed in prayer.” He didn’t pray in his own prayer; he prayed in a prayer given to him by the Lord. He did not pray according to his circumstances, mood, or feelings to accomplish his own motive. He prayed according to the Lord’s will.  
  
“Lord, I do not want to pray in my feeling.” We may pray for someone with strong feelings, and our prayer may be impacted by our mood. The Lord is not interested in such prayers.  
  
I’m reminded of Peter: “Lord, no! Don’t go to the cross.” That is the natural man. The natural man does not want itself—or others—to be brought to the cross. We might pray, “Lord, heal them! Lord, give them a job!”—but we may be praying according to our own view of the situation, in the realm of knowledge, not according to God’s feeling.  
  
So we need to seek the Lord and ask, “What is Your will?”  
  
---  
  
## D. Genuine Prayers  
  
**Outline point:** Prayers in which we contact God, inhale God, absorb God, and are filled with God are genuine prayers. Only prayers of this kind should be offered to God—Rev. 5:8; 8:3–4.  
  
A quick reference: in Revelation it mentions golden bowls of incense, which are the prayers of the saints (Rev. 5:8), and then the angel offers the prayers of the saints with incense on the golden altar (Rev. 8:3–4).  
  
I am touched by this: **the bowls are the prayers.** My question is: *is your bowl empty, or is it filled with incense—Christ and all His merits?* This means we need Christ when we pray. Our prayer needs to be mingled with Christ.  
  
When we spend time with the Lord and inhale Him, our bowl is filled with incense. Then our prayer becomes acceptable, because it is filled with Christ as the incense.  
  
---  
  
# II. The Principle of Prayer  
  
**Outline point:** We need to pray according to the principle of prayer. Only those prayers that follow this principle are worthwhile—Matt. 6:9–10; John 15:7; 1 John 5:14.  
  
## A. God Works through Prayer  
  
**Outline points:**  
- The principle of prayer is that God’s people must pray before God will rise up to work—Matt. 18:18–20; John 15:16.  
- God will not act independently. He will accomplish His will only when His people agree with Him and are one with Him in prayer—Col. 1:9; 4:12.  
- God’s intention is to accomplish His will through our prayer—John 15:7; 1 John 5:14.  

  
First we contact God in our spirit and absorb Him. Then, when we are mingled with God in fellowship, we can pray so that **God can rise up to work.**  
  
God works through man’s prayer. In Matthew 6 we see: “Your kingdom come; Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth.” His will is done in heaven, but that will needs to be brought to the earth. This is the great privilege and responsibility of the church.  
  
It’s amazing: who are we? We are nobodies, yet we can bring the will of heaven to the earth.  
  
God will not act independently. He will not accomplish His will alone. He accomplishes His will when His people agree with Him and request it through prayer. It is His will, but it is our prayer.  
  
That is why we must spend time with Him: so we know His will—not what we *think* He wants, but what He wants **today**, right now. We want to be up-to-date with the living Christ by contacting the fresh Christ in prayer.  
  
---  
  
## B. Prayer as Cooperation with God  
  
**Outline points:**  
- Prayer is the believers’ act of working together with God to accomplish His will—Col. 4:2, 12; Matt. 6:9–10.  
- The purpose of prayer is that we be one with God’s will so that God can work—Rev. 4:11; 5:8; 8:3–4.  
- Prayer is the union of the believers’ will with God’s will and the utterance of God’s will—John 15:7; Matt. 6:9–10.  
- The true meaning of prayer is to seek the accomplishment of God’s will—Matt. 26:39.  
- a. We need to echo back to God His heart’s desire and empty our hearts to seek after Him.  
- b. The only legitimate prayer is the prayer that asks for the accomplishment of God’s will—Matt. 6:9–10; 7:21; 12:50; John 6:38.  
  
Have you ever thought of prayer as work? We might think we pray and then we go work—but prayer **is** the work. Prayer may be the most challenging work, the most difficult work. This is our real work.  
  
Many remember Watchman Nee’s word: *Why do we think so much and pray so little, when we could accomplish much more by praying much and not thinking?*  
  
The true meaning of prayer: in Matthew 26 the Lord prayed, “Not as I will, but as You will.” We are here today because He took the Father’s will. Real prayer is to seek the will of the One to whom we are praying.  
  
Prayer is to be an **echo**. To have a proper echo, there should be no other sound within us—no competing feeling or thought. We simply echo God’s desire and will.  
  
---  
  
## C. Opening the Way for God  
  
**Outline points:**  
- If we do not cooperate with God in prayer, He will not work—Matt. 6:9–10; 18:18–20.  
- Our most important work is to open the way for God—Acts 6:4; 10:9.  
- God works only where His people sympathize with Him—Acts 1:14; 4:24–31; 12:5.  
  
We can ask: how much prayer was there? Did we pray sufficiently? Did we bind the strong man to plunder his house? The Lord needs us to pray in order for Him to move, work, and operate. The key to His move is our prayer.  
  
In Acts, the pattern is clear: prayer governs the move. If everyone only serves tables and no one prays, Acts would be much shorter. In Acts 10, Peter went to the housetop to pray, and another man was praying. The Lord had a way to bring Gentiles into the Body.  
  
---  
  
## D. God’s Principle of Working  
  
**Outline points:**  
- We must see God’s principle of work—John 15:7.  
- God cannot do what He wants unless man wants it—John 4:34; 5:30.  
- Even when God decides on a matter, He does not act immediately—John 15:7.  
- God’s works require a cooperating will on earth—1 John 5:14.  
- God must have man’s will in harmony with His will—Matt. 6:9–10; 26:39.  
  
God cannot do what He wants unless man wants it. The Lord wants to spread into our heart so that our mind, emotion, and will—our whole inner being—can be worked on, so we can offer up proper prayers.  
  
A key promise: **1 John 5:14**—if we ask anything according to His will, He hears us. We can come with boldness, but we need to spend time with Him to know His will.  
  
---  
  
## E. One Accord in Prayer  
  
**Outline point:** The highest point of working with God is to ask in one accord for His will—1 Tim. 2:8; Acts 1:14; 6:4.  
  
This is the highest point of working together with God: to pray in one accord, as in Acts 1:14.  
  
Paul’s burden in 1 Timothy was that men would pray in every place, lifting up holy hands without wrath. And “first of all” there should be prayers.  
  
Example of something that rises: “Lord, increase the Full-time Training in Anaheim. Lord, I don’t agree with only 200. What about 300?” Something rises in you, and you realize it is according to God’s will—so you pray.  
  
---  
  
# III. The Prayer Ministry of the Church  
  
**Outline point:** The prayer meetings are for carrying out the prayer ministry of the church—Acts 1:14; 4:23–31; 13:1–4; 16:25.  
  
## A. God and the Church in Prayer  
  
**Outline point:** The prayer ministry is God telling the church what He wants to do, and the church praying it on earth—Matt. 18:18–20.  
  
In Acts 13:2, as they were ministering to the Lord and fasting, the Spirit spoke: “Set apart Barnabas and Saul.” They didn’t rush—they prayed and fasted, laid hands, identified them with the Body, and then sent them. This is the pattern: God tells the church what He wants, and the church prays it on earth.  
  
---  
  
## B. God Needs the Church  
  
**Outline point:** God cannot do His will alone; He needs the cooperation of the church—Acts 12:5.  
  
He was in prison, but there was a prayer meeting that night. God’s move on earth is tied to the church’s praying.  
  
---  
  
## C. Bringing Heaven to Earth  
  
**Outline points:**  
- The ministry of the church is to bring the will in heaven to earth—Matt. 6:9–10.  
- Prayer as ministry means standing on God’s side—Col. 1:9; 4:12.  
- The church is the outlet of God’s will through prayer—Matt. 6:9–10.  
  
“Earth controls heaven… the church is the outlet.” God’s power is unlimited in heaven, but on earth His move is carried out according to the church’s prayer. In Matthew, we bind and loose on earth what has already been bound and loosed in heaven; we carry out on earth what is already decided in heaven.  
  
---  
  
## D. The Church Prays First  
  
**Outline points:**  
- When God wants to act, the church must pray first—Matt. 18:18–20.  
- God’s power on earth is manifested according to the church’s prayer—Matt. 6:9–10.  
- The church must have big, serious, strong prayers—John 14:23; 15:16; 16:23–24; Acts 4:24–31.  
  
The Lord needs big, serious, strong prayers—prayers that begin with absorbing and mingling with Him, and that end with prayer concerning the building of the church and His will.

# Practical Fellowship on Corporate Prayer (Added)  
  
## 1) Jump in  
When you come to pray with others: **jump right in.** Don’t wait too long. If you wait, it’s easy to get distracted or critical. Jump in—even if you only pray a few lines. Once you get into the flow, the Lord will pray through you.  
  
## 2) Pray short prayers (pass the ball)  
In corporate prayer, it’s good not to pray too long. I like to illustrate with basketball: **keep passing the ball.** Don’t “dribble” the prayer from Genesis to the New Jerusalem so nobody can follow. Ask: **Is your prayer followable?**  
  
You don’t always have to have the “highest” prayer. Learn to pray in the Body.  
  
## 3) Pray specifically  
Rather than “Lord, save them,” pray: “Lord, save David—tomorrow.”  
Rather than “Lord, do something on campus,” pray: “Lord, I want this person to be baptized. I don’t want them to finish college without being baptized.”  
  
The Lord will be happy to hear such specific prayers.
