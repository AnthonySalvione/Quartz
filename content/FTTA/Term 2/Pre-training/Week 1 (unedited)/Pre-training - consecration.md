## CONSECRATION

Let's begin by reading Revelation 11:15 together out loud, two times.
## I. The definition of consecration:
A. Our consent to God’s working in us and on us.  
B. Our consent to God’s using us.  
C. Our consent to God’s directing our ways.

## II. Aspects of consecration:
A. The basis of consecration—God’s purchase—1 Cor. 6:19-20; Rom. 14:8.  
B. The motive of consecration—God’s love—2 Cor. 5:14-15; Rom. 12:1.  
C. The meaning of consecration—to be a sacrifice—Rom. 12:1; Num. 28:2-3.  
D. The purpose of consecration—to be used by God and to work for God—Eph. 2:10; Isa. 64:8.  
E. The result of consecration—to abandon our future—Lev. 1:9.

## III. The crisis of consecration and the process of consecration—Matt. 7:13-14

## IV. Consecration for changing the age—Rev. 11:15:
A. God wants to end this age and to bring in the age of the kingdom, and for this He needs a dispensational instrument—12:1-11.  
B. Everyone who is used by God to turn the age is one who consecrates himself voluntarily to the Lord and who is willing to go against the tide of this age—Rom. 12:2; 2 Tim. 4:10, 8.  
C. The characteristics of those who are used by God to turn the age—Dan. 2:17-18; 6:10-11, 19-22, 26; 9:1-4, 20, 27; 10:2-3, 12-13; 12:13:  
   1. Separation from an age that follows Satan—Rom. 12:2.  
   2. Being joined to God’s desire through His word—Dan. 9:1-4.  
   3. Cooperating with God through prayers—6:10-11; Matt. 6:9-10.  
   4. Not caring for one’s life—Acts 20:24; Rom. 16:3-4; Phil. 2:30; Rev. 12:11.
