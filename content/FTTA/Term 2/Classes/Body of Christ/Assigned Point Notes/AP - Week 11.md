Absolutely. Your section, **Message 215, point 7, subpoints a and b**, is really the **turning point** from seeing the church as Christ’s counterpart in doctrine and typology to seeing **how Christ practically produces, prepares, and perfects this counterpart**.

The three messages move like this:

**Message 213** shows the big vision: the church is Christ’s counterpart, His bride, His wife, and His satisfaction in love. The church is not merely a group of saved people; she is something that comes out of Christ, matches Christ, and becomes one with Christ. 

**Message 214** develops the type of Adam and Eve. Eve came out of Adam, returned to Adam, became one with Adam, and possessed Adam’s life and nature. This typifies the church coming out of Christ’s resurrection life to become His counterpart. 

**Message 215** then shows how this counterpart is prepared: Christ loved the church, gave Himself up for her, sanctifies her, cleanses her, nourishes her, and cherishes her until she is glorious, holy, and without blemish. 

## Your assigned point: 7. Loved by Christ

### a. Christ having given Himself up for her

The key verse is:

> “Christ also loved the church and gave Himself up for her” — Ephesians 5:25

The main burden here is that **Christ is not only a Savior who loved individual believers; He is a church-loving Christ**. Galatians 2:20 says, “He loved me and gave Himself up for me,” but Ephesians 5:25 shows something deeper and more corporate: **Christ loved the church and gave Himself up for the church**.

So your point should emphasize this:

Christ’s death was not only for our individual salvation. His death was mainly for the producing of the church as His counterpart. On the cross, He gave Himself up so that the church could come into existence.

This connects directly with John 19:34:

> “One of the soldiers pierced His side with a spear, and immediately there came out blood and water.”

The **blood** is for redemption. The **water** is for the impartation of life. This means Christ’s death accomplished two things: it solved the problem of sin, and it released His divine life for the producing of the church.

A strong way to say it:

> Christ loved the church not merely with a feeling, but by giving Himself up for her. His love was expressed through His death. From His pierced side came blood for redemption and water for life, so that the church could be produced as His counterpart.

### b. That He might sanctify her

The next phrase in Ephesians 5 says:

> “That He might sanctify her” — Ephesians 5:26

The main burden here is that **Christ did not only die to produce the church; He is now working to sanctify the church**.

Sanctification here is not merely outward improvement. It is not just becoming more proper, moral, or religious. According to the message, sanctification has two sides:

First, Christ **separates** the church unto Himself.

Second, Christ **saturates** the church with Himself.

That second part is very important. The message says that sanctification is not mainly outward correction, but **the dispensing of Christ’s element into us**. God’s goal is not merely to make bad people good. His goal is to work Christ into us so that the church would match Christ in life, nature, expression, and glory.

A strong way to say it:

> Christ gave Himself up for the church with a purpose: that He might sanctify her. This means He separates the church unto Himself and saturates her with Himself. God’s intention is not merely to improve us outwardly, but to add Christ into us inwardly, until the church becomes His real counterpart for His satisfaction.

## Key points you can use in your presentation

1. **Christ’s love is corporate.**
   He loved “me” in Galatians 2:20, but He also loved “the church” in Ephesians 5:25.

2. **Christ’s love was expressed by His giving Himself up.**
   His love was not sentimental; it was sacrificial, purposeful, and producing.

3. **His death was for redemption and life-impartation.**
   The blood redeems us from sin, and the water signifies the flowing life that produces the church.

4. **The church came into existence through Christ’s love and death.**
   Just as Eve came out of Adam’s side, the church comes out of Christ’s pierced side.

5. **Christ now sanctifies the church.**
   He does not only save the church; He is actively preparing the church.

6. **Sanctification means separation and saturation.**
   The church is separated from common things and saturated with Christ Himself.

7. **God’s goal is not outward improvement but inward constitution.**
   God wants to dispense Christ into us so that the church becomes Christ’s counterpart.

8. **This sanctification prepares the bride.**
   Christ loved the church, gave Himself for her, and now sanctifies her so that He can eventually present her to Himself glorious.
