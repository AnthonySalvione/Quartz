# Introduction to the Class

## Speaker Introduction and Course Description
For the first classes in the God-ordained way this term, welcome.

Brother Albert Lim, Kurt Kennard, and Paul Deng are all coordinating with me to teach this class in an alternating fashion. We get to enjoy pursuing the God-ordained way this term with you.

I was considering that “the God-ordained way” is almost an incomplete phrase. I mean, the God-ordained way implies that God will have His way, but it means to do... what? We may say it is for the building up of the Body of Christ for the consummation of God’s economy. We will see that the God-ordained way is so intrinsic, deep, and all-embracing that it implies the way for everything. When we get into it, see it, practice it, and involve ourselves in it, we not only learn how to do certain steps, but we begin to realize that this is how to do anything and everything.

As expected, we are beginning with the introductory speaking. The first words in the outline are “An Overview of the God-Ordained Way.” Notice that this is the first of the sessions on our syllabus, which you just received.

I will make brief note of it because it is very self-explanatory: the date of the class, every Wednesday morning, the numeric sequence, the reading assignment—please read it before coming to class. There are two texts: _Lessons on the God-Ordained Way_ and _Vital Groups_. These are two crucial, crucial publications.

As I mentioned, the God-ordained way is very broad and expansive in its significance. Brother Lee spent the major part of the last three years of his life on this burden. We have over 200 publications concerning the God-ordained way. It is good, however, to begin with an overview.

Sometimes when we give an overview, it can be triggering or stimulating, or you may relax a little because you think, “Oh, it is just an overview.” But it is much more than that.

For those of you who have heard this for three terms now, and for those who are here newly, you are probably not strangers to the terminology, the structure of the components, and the conversations on the God-ordained way. It is important to have that. But the God-ordained way often falls short in most contextual situations in the Lord’s recovery in terms of proper realization. What you may have understood has probably been largely practical, descriptive, almost like an index of things you should pay attention to and prioritize. The God-ordained way has received much attention from the leading brothers in most localities and from the saints in general, but I think we all have the realization that we have only scratched the surface.

Indeed, the God-ordained way is not merely that. It is that, but as is true in all of our realizations and experiences, what is apparently seen externally is simply an invitation or entry point to enter more deeply and find treasures and experiences.

## Two Governing Points for the Class

So in this overview, we will have two points as we go through the outline.

One is this: please remember that your coming to the FTTA was not like coming to a graduate school course, where you master a certain body of knowledge, become competent in a field, and receive a credential. Not at all. Then what do you do in the classes? You do have curriculum and content, so what is the difference?

The difference is that I hope you would realize that **every class** becomes an occasion for you to have a breathtaking new appreciation and view of the Lord’s wonderful person and work as it relates to you and your specific future with Him.

So what lies ahead, or should lie ahead, for you in the training is a series of life-changing encounters in which your view of yourself, of who you are, of what the universe is for, of how you fit, of what you aspire to in your human life, and of what criteria you would have for your endeavors—**all of this will change** upon encountering the truths in this class.

In the God-ordained way similarly, I hope that in every class you would receive not just illumination concerning procedural things that we should and must do, but that you would realize, as the first section of our outline says, that the God-ordained way is special and precious because it is the way that the divine economy can be carried out. This makes it very particular and very significant. We will talk about this more in a moment under Roman numeral I.

Secondly, the structure of the outline itself provides a challenge, or an exercise, for you. You have a transition from the first Roman numeral, which points out the relationship between the God-ordained way and God’s economy. Then we talk in overview about what the God-ordained way addresses, what it involves, its constituents and components. Then we talk about the living, the kind of living, and the setting of that living that makes it effective. All these things could, and probably do, sound very familiar to you.

But they present a challenge, because the first part of the outline shows you the magnitude, grandeur, and surpassing greatness of God’s economy, and how the God-ordained way is important because it allows us to work that out. Then when we move to the next sections, do we revert to our previous understanding? “Okay, how do I preach the gospel? How do I have the proper gospel dialogue? How do I best assess this person I am just speaking to for the first time? What words do I use? How do I best bring them to pray?”

Important questions, right? But these questions can, and typically are, considered apart from Roman numeral I. As we go through Roman II and III, the question is: will you revert back to your old understanding?

Do not think that this class is simply teaching you how to answer all these procedural questions. It is not that these things are unimportant, but the only way they ultimately become effective is by your having the vital realization of how they relate to Roman numeral I.

Say your field is public health. That would be like becoming an expert at gospel preaching. That is good, but that is not the God-ordained way as it relates to God’s economy.

There were some wonderful illustrations in the reading, even mysterious ones, such as: if you knock on someone’s door, is that hand of yours part of the being of the divine Christ? What is that?

Did you have that realization? There should be an abiding realization—Hymn 740, a lovely hymn—that not a word is without active touch with Him.

So I would like us to heed those two things.

Number one: realize that this class, and indeed every class in the FTTA, is for you to have a life-changing encounter with the Lord Jesus. If you do not have that, it is not the training’s fault. If that is your expectation and aspiration, you will have it. If you do not have it, then aspire and pray for it, and you will have it.

So in this treatment of the relationship between the God-ordained way and God’s New Testament economy, I am hoping that God’s New Testament economy and the God-ordained way would not become merely spiritual terms that you can define and leave there, but rather life-changing and life-reordering realizations.

Secondly, that we would learn the intrinsic revelation embodied in the significance of the Roman numerals in the outline.

## I. The God-ordained way is the way to practice God’s New Testament economy 
([[1 Timothy-01#v4|1 Tim. 1:4b]]; [[Colossians-01#v25|Col. 1:25]]; [[John-15#v16|John 15:16]]):  

Now we have key verses here, and the choice of these verses is very significant. The first two emphasize God’s economy, and the third emphasizes the relationship between the two.

So how should we take the God-ordained way? It is the way to practice God’s New Testament economy. I will just say that **the way** is an exclusive term. It implies that if you do not have this way, you do not have God’s economy. It is **the way**.

We can be enthralled by God’s eternal economy and given to it and dedicated to it, but if we are not interested in getting into the God-ordained way, then we do not have the way to carry it out. The God-ordained way is the way to carry out God’s economy.

On the other hand, we need to be careful that the use of the expression “the way” does not trigger in us a preconceived, structured idea of what it is. Because what it is, is ever-changing according to you, where you are, where you are with Him, and what He is doing with you. Although the God-ordained way is the way to carry out God’s eternal economy, be careful that you do not assess others or yourself by using Roman numeral II as a checklist. Does that make sense?

Can any of you give me a definition of God’s eternal economy? We have the hymn, we had it in the reading and in the outline. Can anyone give me a definition?

One person said: **God’s economy is God’s dispensing.**

Another said: **God’s economy is God’s plan, from His good pleasure and the desire of His heart, to gain a corporate bride as His expression. This is why He came, died, lived a human life, resurrected, and ascended, in order to release Himself as the life-giving Spirit to dispense Himself into every part of us so that we could match Him in every way, be one with Him, and build up the Body of Christ.**

Amen. Wonderful. That is an expository definition of God’s economy. Very good.

Now, to link these two definitions together a little bit: can you point out something in the first definition that is compelling by implication, even if not explicitly stated?

What is strongly implied in the phrase “God’s dispensing”? To those of us who know a little bit about what is involved in God’s dispensing, many people may ask, “What is God’s dispensing?” But we know: God’s dispensing is His impartation of Himself—His life, nature, and person—into us, to involve Himself with us and become one with us. That is His dispensing.

So He would like to dispense Himself into us, and that dispensing is God’s economy. But what does this imply? It implies **romance**. More specifically, it implies **responsiveness and involvement**.

We know that God is omnipotent. He can do anything any way He wants to. But because of how He has positioned Himself in what He is determined to do—because of how He has positioned Himself in relationship to us—His economy is to put us together with Him. God’s dispensing is to put God and man together.

And when they are together, God’s dispensing can occur. Does it happen all the time? We have this lovely phrase, “ordinary days under the divine dispensing.” We love to have ordinary days under the divine dispensing. It can be said that God’s dispensing should be going on all the time. But be careful: according to His principle and intention, it should go on all the time. But it does not go on all the time. It should—but it does not.

What does that imply? It implies the limitation of the unlimited God within the relationship in which He has placed us with Himself. That means that **we are the crucial factor**. God has lowered Himself and waits for us to choose to be involved with Him. If we are involved with Him, then He can dispense Himself into us.

How often does it happen that we do not respond to His dispensing of Himself into us? Most of the time? No. Some of the time? Maybe. Occasionally? Probably.

Since God’s economy is a relationship, and a relationship should be bidirectional—where each one gives the other precedence—the fact that God’s dispensing does not go on all the time indicates that we are not always very positive, exemplary, or wonderful partners in this co-relationship.

So God’s economy is for God to dispense Himself. But this implies a realization on our part. If we realize that He wants it, and we want it, then we would position ourselves in a way that He can carry this out. What way is that? That way is that **we care for Him**.

Do you care for Him? Care, in human terms, is a relative matter. Who do you care for? Do you care for your dad? Yes. More than anything? Maybe, maybe not. My point is that whatever you care for is relative. He dispenses Himself into us when we give Him the first place.

His economy is to put us together, and the fact that He would like this dispensing to go on all the time implies that He wants us to be responsive to Him all the time. Being responsive to Him all the time requires that we would turn our heart—but more than that, that we would **love Him**. Why? Because we have an increasing and enlarged appreciation of Him. Through inquiries and words heard in the church life, we come to appreciate Him more and more so that He becomes **unforgettable** to us.

Unforgettable does not merely mean that we are not forgetful people. It means that when we come to the Lord’s table, we **remember Him**. The Lord’s table is a type of our whole Christian life. Remembering includes and involves **our consciousness**. Through our appreciation of Him, we have an abiding consciousness of Him.

1 Timothy 1:4 relates to God’s economy, in which Paul encouraged Timothy to teach others according to God’s economy. The teaching here is important in many ways and in many forms and situations and contexts, but here the point is not merely that. Here the usage is mainly for us to contemplate what God’s economy is.

And when we combine our understanding with Colossians 1:25, it causes us to see what God’s economy is.

Someone commented: Paul is charging and exhorting us that we would see God’s economy. It is a lifelong seeing. The more we see, the more we participate in the stewardship. Every part of our seeing is given to us for others. This is God’s economy.

Yes. God’s economy is His intention from His side, from His point of view. He would like to bring us into a living touch with Him, putting us together. He wants to be involved in an ongoing, consensual, enjoyable, and wonderful interaction and touch with Him. That is His intention.

Now, the same Greek word, _oikonomia_, is used in both of these verses. In one place it is translated **economy**, and in another it is translated **stewardship**. The same word is now presented from another perspective.

Stewardship means a responsibility entrusted to a certain person to carry out a charge, right?

This means that God’s economy is not just unidirectional—not just Him working something out into you, imparting something into you, to make you a specimen of divine and human excellence. It means that **He seeks and requests your response in order to carry out a relationship**. That is God’s economy.

Then stewardship means that **you take, receive, and become aware of that**. And does it matter? Does it affect you? Are you interested?

Consider your past. When you hear about God’s economy, do you have a burning, churning sense within that says, “I need to make sure that I am paying attention to being involved with this”? If you consider it purely doctrinally, then maybe not. But the word _stewardship_ implies that this arrangement of God is a **relationship truth**.

Whether God’s economy is carried out or not depends not mainly on Him, but on how we handle this. Eventually, our stewardship of this economy is our purposeful, investigative endeavor to deepen our **captivation**, to deepen our enthrallment. He does not impose this on us, but if we are seeking Him, we will find and gain this.

Eventually, our consciousness, remembrance, and appreciation of Him are to become all-pervading. When this consciousness becomes all-pervading, that becomes our marker, assurance, and guarantee that the dispensing is going on.

How do you know if the dispensing is going on? He wants to dispense Himself, so is it going on? Well—do you have any consciousness and appreciation of Him? If you do, then for sure. If you do not, then it is questionable. I cannot say it is not going on, but it is questionable.

This realization—that everything carried out in God’s economy is by dispensing, and that He desperately wants to carry out this economy, yet centuries pass and nothing happens—why? Because He will not insist, He will not push, and He will not obligate. He is waiting for some who would like to respond.

So what is the God-ordained way? It is the **place and way** in which this relationship can thrive, can emerge, and can permeate everything involved in God’s economy for the building up of the church.

Have you ever been in a church life that was not so enjoyable, and yet you were thankful to be in the church life? If that was the case, then you were not really in the stewardship. If you were, then regardless of the outward situation, you would have been irreversibly enthralled.
### A. For us to practice the New Testament economy, we need to see the divine economy; this economy is clearly revealed in the holy Word 
([[1 Timothy-01#v4|1 Tim. 1:4]]; [[Ephesians-01#v10|Eph. 1:10]]; [[Ephesians-03#v9|3:9]]; [[Colossians-01#v25|Col. 1:25]]; [[1 Corinthians-09#v17|1 Cor. 9:17]]).  

So, two main points here: we need to see the divine economy, and we need to see that the divine economy **is the center of the Word**. You need to be impressed by this. It is the key revelation in the Scriptures. This economy opens the Bible. It is contained in every outline of every book and every chapter.

Where you have God’s economy, you have His dispensing. When you have His dispensing, the Triune God is there, dispensing is there, and man is there. This is repeated throughout the Scriptures from thousands of angles. But if you do not see it, or if it is not revealed to you, then you cannot pick it up in a living way, even if rightly it should be our reference point.

God’s economy is revealed in the Word, and the Word is opened in the ministry to show that God’s economy is the revelation of the Bible.

That economy is not merely that God is dispensing Himself into you to make you like Him in life and nature—that is true—but that alone would still be too one-sided. It only happens when **you order your inner being**, when you orient and position yourself toward Him. Then it happens.

Here is the key thing: for us to practice God’s New Testament economy, the God-ordained way is the **practice** of God’s New Testament economy.

Do you see the divine economy? Yes, you do. But probably you cannot say that you see it in its entirety. You do see it, but you see it dimly. If we see the divine economy, we would realize that there is a need to live in an experiential touch with Him. This is the divine economy. If we see this, then our seeing is measured by what we experience. If we do not experience it, it is questionable whether we have really seen it.

We need to see that God’s divine economy is for you to live in an intimate touch with Him—initiated by Him, yes, but now initiated with your cooperation as well.

Has anyone seen this? Do you see it? Well, the seeing is relative, because if you see it, you do it and have it. So, do you live in experiential contact with Him? Do you live in this?

For each one of us, the answer is different, but never absolute. That is why the Lord brought you to the training—class after class, giving you encounters that are life-changing—so that you would live in relationship mode with Him and in experiential touch.

If you see this, then every day becomes an incredible new set of opportunities to apply this fact.
### B. Our going out to visit people by knocking on their doors is for this economy and should be linked with the divine economy 
([[John-15#v5|John 15:5]], [[John-15#v16|16]]; [[Luke-10#v3|Luke 10:3-6]]).  

In our reading, it talks about knocking on doors, which has come to represent to us a practice of the God-ordained way. But are you linked to the divine economy or not? That means that not only are you going to seek lost sheep because you realize that they have been chosen and are sons of peace—that is all part of our realization—but that does not automatically connect you in time and space to the divine economy.

The divine economy means that we do this with a present, ever-more urgent sense within: “I need to have this pervading consciousness of the One who has put me and Him together for us to be involved in our living.”

Is my living in this experiential touch or not?

John 15:16 and John 15:5 show us the relationship. “I chose you and set you that you should go forth and bear fruit, and that your fruit should remain.” This is the coalescence of the result of God’s economy being fulfilled, when we simply respond to Him in stewardship to what He has initiated and desired in His economy. We are set with Him, and that brings forth fruit and remaining fruit.

We will talk about this more at other times. It does not mean that if we are joined to the Lord there is no fruit; rather, this fruit will happen factually if you are living this way. The result is permanent if you live this way.

John 15:5 says that apart from Him we can do nothing. The indication is that if we knock on a door and our hand is not linked to Christ, then even if someone gets saved and touched, it is nothing. The vindication, attestation, and reward in whatever you do depends on whether you have positioned yourself in the stewardship, responding to God’s economy and contacting the Lord so that He can impart Himself into you. Then, in that way, you go and knock on that door, and **that is something**.

Now, I do not mean to say that if you have not done this, or are not sure how to do this, that you should not go knock on doors. The Lord works with us in our present experience, revelation after revelation, helping orient us so that eventually this becomes our condition and way of living.

In this section, our brother talked about **leaves versus branches**. This is very significant and important. In the Word and in our practice, something is either central—of the Word, of God, of Christ, of the Spirit—or, if it is not directly related to those things, it is secondary, something that adorns or completes. But even those secondary things rely on their connection to the trunk in order to be useful and contain life.

So is the God-ordained way on the trunk, or is it only a branch?

That is why the name of this class is _Lessons on the God-Ordained Way_. Because in the typical contextual frame of the saints and serving ones, the God-ordained way is often treated like a procedural manual on how to bear fruit and practice the church life for the building up of the Body of Christ. **It is not that.** Saints, be clear that it is not merely that. **This class is not on the branch. This class is on the trunk.**

Our brother said in the reading that whether something is on the trunk or on a branch is not intrinsically dependent on the thing itself, but rather on **how you see it**. Is the God-ordained way just steps to you? If that is all it is, then it is a branch to you. But clearly, according to this section, no. We do everything in the God-ordained way within the contextual seeing that we are to thrive in this marvelous divine and human experience in God’s economy, in which we do nothing apart from our loving, intimate touch with Him. That touch conditions and trains our being to have a consciousness of Him, and that consciousness serves as a marker that we are allowing Him to dispense Himself into us in our relationship.
### C. God’s eternal economy involves and includes millions of sons of peace; these sons of peace need us to go to visit them in their homes 
([[Ephesians-03#v6|Eph. 3:6-11]]).  

So—baptism. Is it a branch or trunk?

Baptism is often described and practiced merely as a practice. Most believers would say it is a branch. They do not see that this is the person of Christ in His work and in His economy, but rather a ritual that we carry out according to Scripture to testify to what has happened to us, both for other believers and for the world, that we are members of a new society.

Is that what baptism means to you? Oh—Romans 6:3-5. Oh—Galatians 3:27. No. As was true in the Lord Jesus’ own case, our baptism is the fulfilling of our own righteousness and a declaration that we expect to live in the heavens. When we live in that principle, we fulfill all righteousness.

So there needs to be a reordering of what we are doing in our gospel time. You are on the trunk unless you define yourself otherwise. Although it is often said in various places and in the training that technically speaking, as held in the concept of most believers, the God-ordained way is thought of as a branch of the trunk, it is only a branch if you hold it that way. The overview of this class is to train you **not** to hold it that way.
  
## II. The success of the new way depends upon bringing the church to the homes and bringing all the saints into the truth 
([[Acts-05#v42|Acts 5:42]]; [[Acts-02#v42|2:42]]):  

Again, the emphasis here is that we do not want merely to do things legally by description, but rather according to God’s economy, which is experiential. If you do not consider God’s economy together with romance, you are severely limited. It cannot be fully understood unless you catch the Lord’s romantic intention in this arrangement.

So, we are taking the organic way. What does that mean? To be in the church instead of in the world? Yes, but is that all? No. The homes are an outward way to gather a large number of believers and bring the saints into the truth. But the real point is that our living should be a **moment-by-moment touch** with the Lord.

So, are we clear? Is this clear—that this is where life consists? You can formulate your own life trajectory and plan and do something else, as most people do. But God and the divine economy are this: that your intention, and the reordering of your life, would be such that you sustain experiential touch with Him as your priority, just as He does with you. When I speak these words, I can hardly believe I am in the Lord’s recovery and how blessed I am to be here.

That is discipling the nations.

We have many lessons on this, but this includes and is not limited to the cherishing that mothers give to babies. For a spiritual babe, the cherishing needed is not just comfort or affection, but help to know what it is to be related to God’s economy. Is that too early? No.

## IV. The living of the new way is 
([[Lamentations-03#v22|Lam. 3:22-23]]; [[2 Corinthians-04#v16|2 Cor. 4:16]]):  

What is the first thing that should be brought up in a home meeting when someone is brought to the Lord? Yes—you let them know that this phrase, God’s economy, sums up the entire Bible and explains how they are involved. The issue should be the realization that we are to live a joined life with the Lord in fellowship with Him.

I would like to emphasize the living. I appreciated the brothers this morning declaring Lamentations 3:22-23.

What we are talking about here is not meant to be a reserved or occasional experience. Yes, we can link to God’s economy when we are irritated, when we are struggling in our work, or when we are at the door and do not know what to say. That is true and wonderful. But our frail concept is often that this is merely a helpful resource we can draw from. No. This requires us to call on the Lord and to be able to declare and confirm that we ourselves are living this way.

This requires, first of all, that we be **revived every morning**. To be revived every morning implies that we wake up dead. If you have to be revived, it implies that you were not revived previously. If you are not revived, that means dead.

Why would the Lord ordain that we would have this kind of cyclical deadness, regardless of who we are or what we do? Why do we wake up every morning disoriented? Because He wants an ongoing, ever-new, and fresh touch in romantic expression. He wants to express something romantically to you every morning. He wants you to be renewed—not merely by awakening or by Christ shining, though that is true—but such that every renewal becomes a romantic experience.

Every time a romance is renewed, it becomes stronger than it was before. If it passes through difficulty and then is renewed, the relationship becomes stronger than it was before the trouble even began.

So every morning we wake up dead, and He is calling us back. Whether we respond depends on us. If we do respond, we are revived, and that begins the loving process of being in God’s economy.

I was never a very good track athlete, but there was a saying that you cannot get to the finish line in record time unless you get out of the blocks well. If you do not get a good start, you may not get to the finish line properly. Every morning is a chance to have an ever more explosive start. We are to be renewed day by day.

Again, the word _renewed_ implies the need to be renewed every day. The same principle applies whenever we are down at any point in the day. If we are renewed then, we are given an experience that exceeds where we were before we became deadened.

This is a living, and we should not relinquish it. If we do not relinquish it, then there is no need to worry about oldness. “The path of the righteous is like the rising sun.”

Can a believer have not a complicated progression, but a positive, ongoing, upward, ascending experience without exception? Yes. The Lord does not limit that. If we do not have it, it is because we ourselves did not respond in the stewardship of His economy.

All of this is to happen in the God-ordained way, and in our being linked to Him in the steps of the God-ordained way.

We need to be concerned about persons, and our intimate concern comes through our intimate touch with the Lord. He is concerned about them, and so are we if we are connected to Him.

The home is the best place to touch someone in heart-to-heart, eye-to-eye fellowship. It is not merely a place to talk about the weather forecast, politics, or crises. It has to do with being an agent, a participant, in the emergence of the end of the age—those who match His intention in His stewardship with their cooperation in God’s economy.

# Overflow

## Trainee 1

We need to realize that we have a wonderful product to present to people. We need to have this product ourselves so that we would have confidence in what we are presenting.

## Trainee 2

God’s economy is a relationship. Even the Greek word has two sides—one on God’s side and one on man’s. This dispensing is something He wants to carry out constantly. There is no problem on His side. If it is not happening, it is because we are not living in the moment-by-moment touch with Him in our experience. If we realize we are not having this touch, we need to turn back to Him to receive this dispensing.

## Jachin

God’s economy is a contextual matter. Just like a water fountain, the water is ready to be dispensed, but we need to take the first action to begin the flow. In the same way, we need to activate our living touch, moment by moment, with the Lord in our experience.

## Nicolina

I went door-knocking for the first time during my gospel trip. I was nervous. But actually, after this gospel trip, I want to do it again, and I want to do it with the abiding realization that I am knocking as one with the processed and consummated Triune God.

God is a God of knocking, because He is knocking on our hearts nonstop. We need to go forth because there are millions of sons of peace that the Lord wants to gain.
