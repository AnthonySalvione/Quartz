This is largely taken from the publication of the 'Ministry of the Word'... but now extracted into a booklet form. The foreword should be read - this gives you the proper attitude to come to these lessons.

The student work is for sure more expansive then just what we would regard as the YP work. Why do I say this? That is what genuine shepherds do: lead, guide, shepherd the sheep. So, how to lead the young people is really how to shepherd the young people. This understanding of lead also can be applied to our understanding, and our thinking when we hear the term "the leading ones." When you hear about leading ones in the churches: elders, overseers... often times we might have this notion of 'rank'... right? The leading ones are the ones who shepherd the sheep. So, if we have that view, we would never go to an elder or co-worker to seek PERMISSION as if we are honoring the chain of command... Rather, we go to these ones seeking fellowship to 'guide us', lead us, help us in knowing our way. Where to lie down. Where to go to get the nourishment. Anyways, I hope that that might give you a different impression the next time you hear the term 'leading ones.'

Last thing I want to mention: the matter of *principles*. Please note that these 6 points are PRINCIPLES. WHy is that important? Sometimes we read a point and turn them into law or make them an ordinance: something we have to do. But actually, principles are things that we apply not blindly, but by the use of exercising of judgment, discernment... even with some wisdom. According to that person or subject or situation or circumstance. So, you have to make a determination: when we are shepherding, leading the YP, as to whether or not this principle applies. Even if it does apply, what sort of variation or degree do we apply that principle? In short, these principles do NOT replace our need to learn to shepherd the young people or the college students or new ones... According to God. The point is: you can't come away from these points and say "now I know how to lead the YP". All this does is increase your vocabulary when you come to the Chief Shepherd, then in your fellowship with Him you could use these ways to apply to our care for the YP. He is the one who is uniquely able to shepherd the sheep, no matter what their present state or condition is.

With this in mind, let's come to this lesson. We will cover three of these principles this week, and three next week. Please keep this in mid: that these are principles that we are learning.
