## II. Deviations that deny the three eternal divine hypostases—modalism:
### A. Technically, modalism is one of two varieties of monarchianism, which is the teaching that there is no plurality in God. Monarchianism holds that He is one (Gk. mono-) existing source of Being (Gk. arche) but that the temporal manifestation of His one Being is manifold.
### B. Modalistic monarchianism, as it is properly called and still popular in the modern age, teaches that the one Being of God manifests in time in three stages, or modes: the Father in the Old Testament, the Son in the Gospels, and the Spirit in the church. When each is manifested, the others do not exist.

Do we teach this? No.

### C. Dynamic monarchianism, also known as adoptionism and much more popular in the early church, teaches that the one God adopted the man Jesus and made Him the Son of God by sheer divine power. The Father is viewed as the true God and Creator, the Son as a creature (with or without divine essence), and the Holy Spirit as some impersonal force.

Most consider adoption to have happened when Jesus was baptized.

### D. Modalism is deviant in that it fails to recognize the scriptural revelation that the Three of the Godhead exist eternally.

So here, when we talk about the one essence and the three hypostases, the point is that the essence is eternal and the hypostases are also eternal. In modalism or monarchianism, the view is that there is just one essence, and the hypostases are just temporal. One begins, and one ends, but we don't hold to this. Actually, one of the things that is silly is that we were accused of dynamic modalism. Is that even in here? No. Oh Lord Jesus...
