## I. The Capital Truth of the Christian Faith

### A. To Be a Christian Is to Believe in the Person and Work of Christ

To be a Christian is to believe in the person and work of Christ.

To believe in the person of Christ is to believe He is the Son of God. To believe in the work is to believe He is the One appointed by God for His economy to carry out salvation, in order for the producing of the Body of Christ as the expression of God.

### B. Faith in Christ Implies Plurality in the Godhead

Our faith in Christ recognizes that Christ is God just as the Father is God. This implies plurality in the Godhead.

### C. Believing in Christ Requires Belief in and Appreciation of the Divine Trinity

Therefore, to believe in Christ in this way requires our full belief in and appreciation of the Divine Trinity.

## III. The Scriptures Have the Triune God as Their Intrinsic Content

I need to say this now or I will forget. We typically use three terms when we talk about our relationship with the Triune God.

1. **Union.** Okay? We say we are united, or we have a union in life, or an organic union. All the same thing.
2. Then we talk about **mingling.** Yes? When we talk about mingling, we talk about a mingling in… spirit? No. A mingling in **nature** without forming a third thing. Alright. By the end of this term I hope you got this. Oh Lord.
3. The third word we use is **incorporation.** Okay? When we talk about incorporation we are talking about an incorporation of persons—something we can’t even describe. It’s an indwelling by way of co-inherence.

When you touch this matter of incorporation, you are beginning to touch the mode of existence of God Himself. You can say, “We have a union in life with the Lord Jesus.” You can say that. But it is difficult to say that God has a union of life in Himself, but that doesn’t explain much to you.

However, in Christ there is the mingling of nature—divine and human—without forming a third thing… but you can’t really say that for the whole Trinity, because of incarnation and things like that.

**But when you come to incorporation, you are touching a reality that only exists in the Triune God.** The Father is in the Son, the Son in the Father… Father, Son, and Spirit are all one.

This is why John 1:1 can say something like, “The Word was with God and the Word was God.” This is why the Lord can say in John 14, “Philip, have you been so long a time with Me and you have not seen the Father?”

Now, we speak of incorporation in **two ways**:

1. The mutual indwelling of persons. That is the talk about existence.
2. The other is the working together as one.

So… think about this verse—I hope you know what I am talking about: “But if I, by the Spirit of God, cast out the demons… then the kingdom of God is among you”—something like that. Think about the verse carefully.

This is the Son. The Son is about to cast out demons. So… do you think that the Son (being the Son of God) needs the Spirit to cast out demons? Yes? No?

The fact is this—the Son is almighty. All authority has been given to Him… He can do whatever He wants. **However**, the verse says, “by the Spirit of God.” In His working to cast out demons, He incorporates the operation of the Spirit.

So… He is casting out the demons, but He is doing it by the Spirit. His doing it by the Spirit—but the kingdom that comes is the kingdom of the Father.

So even in their actions—you know we were talking about the cross—even in the actions—one manifest action, but always **the incorporation of the others in the Trinity.** So, when one is doing something, the other two are always doing something.

When Paul says, “Grace to you and from our Lord Jesus Christ”—the Spirit is in there somewhere.
