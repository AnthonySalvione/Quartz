## The Lord's Purpose Being to Impart Himself into Man as Life

The reality of the table is related to the Lord's purpose, which is to impart Himself into us as life. In His Word the Lord likens Himself to a seed of life imparted into us in order to grow in us and produce something of Himself as fruit. Jesus is the seed of life. He has sown Himself into us, and our heart, our inner being, is the soil in which He grows (Matt. 13:3-23; Mark 4:3-20).

## The Lord Accomplishing His Purpose by Being Crucified and Buried as a Seed Sown into the Ground

The Lord accomplished His purpose not merely by teaching but by going to the cross in order to be crucified and buried as a seed sown into the ground. In order for a seed to grow, it must first be sown into the ground to die. The more the seed dies, the more the life within it is given the opportunity to grow. In reference to Himself as the seed of life, the Lord said, “Unless the grain of wheat falls into the ground and dies, it abides alone; but if it dies, it bears much fruit” (John 12:24).

## Two Aspects of the Cross

The cross of Christ has two aspects. One aspect is that Jesus Christ died on the cross under God's judgment in order to accomplish redemption (Gal. 3:13; Titus 2:14; 1 Pet. 2:24; 3:18; Heb. 9:12). The other aspect is that Jesus Christ as the seed of life went to the cross in order to be sown, buried into the earth, so that He as life could come up in resurrection to be the life-giving Spirit (1 Cor. 15:45).

By becoming such a Spirit through His resurrection, He is able to enter into whoever calls on Him. On the one hand, by crucifixion Christ accomplished redemption, and on the other hand, through death He was released to become a life-giving Spirit so that all who are willing to call on Him may receive Him. Today when a person calls, “O Lord Jesus,” the Lord as the life-giving Spirit enters into him (Rom. 10:12-13).

On the road to Damascus, Saul of Tarsus, a great opposer and persecutor of Jesus, called on Him ignorantly. He said, “Who are You, Lord?” (Acts 9:5). As soon as he said, “Lord,” he was captured by the Lord. When Saul of Tarsus, who was also Paul, called on the name of the Lord, the Lord Jesus entered into him. From that moment, even if Paul wanted Jesus to leave, it was too late. Once Jesus enters into us, He never leaves. Even if we repent of receiving Him, He will never repent of entering into us. In this sense, Saul of Tarsus was caught, captured by the One who entered into him.

## The Significance of the Lord's Table — Receiving and Living by Christ as Life in Order to Grow unto Maturity for His Return

When we come to the Lord's table to remember Him, we eat the bread and drink the cup. To eat and drink is to receive an element into us that was previously outside of us. To eat the bread and drink the cup at the Lord's table signifies that we receive the Lord Jesus into us. This is the deep significance of the Lord's table.

Hence, when we attend the Lord's table, we should not merely exercise our mind to recall who the Lord was, what He did, what He was to us in the past; rather, we should exercise our spirit to take Him in. In 1 Corinthians 11, in his review of the definition of the Lord's table, the apostle Paul says that the believers should eat the bread and drink the cup unto the remembrance of the Lord (vv. 23-25). This indicates that we remember the Lord by eating and drinking Him.

The Lord's table is a declaration and a testimony to the entire universe that our daily life is a life of eating and drinking Jesus. The Christian life is a life not of being taught but of daily taking Jesus in. Day by day, morning and evening, we take Jesus in. Then, whenever we come together for the Lord's table, we make a declaration to the devil, the demons, the angels, and the whole universe that our daily life is the issue not of improving ourselves or doing good but of taking Christ in. We take Christ in by eating and drinking Him, and we declare to the whole universe that we live by eating and drinking Him (John 6:57).

## Growth and Maturity

A mother helps her child grow not by teaching him but by feeding him. By our daily eating and drinking Jesus, we experience the growth of Jesus within us (Col. 2:19). With growth comes maturity. However, maturity takes time. Although a mother may feed her child in a daily way and observe the child's growth yearly, the child does not reach maturity until after eighteen years of age. First there is birth, then feeding that issues in growth, and eventually maturity. This is a shadow of the spiritual things.

First, we were regenerated, born of God in our spirit (John 1:12-13; 3:6; 1 Pet. 1:23). From that day we began to feed on the Lord Jesus in order to grow in the divine life. Through this continued growth in the Lord, sooner or later, we will reach maturity. Although many have been regenerated, and some are growing in life, few can say that they are mature in life. Today we are in the process of growing unto maturity.

When the Lord Jesus established the table, He told His disciples that He would not drink of the product of the vine until the day that He drinks it new with all His overcoming believers in the Father's kingdom (Matt. 26:29). The Lord Jesus will return when a sufficient number of His believers are mature.

Today many believers are like the Corinthians in that they are still infants in Christ (1 Cor. 3:1). Because of such a situation, there is a need for much “diaper work” in the church life. Nevertheless, the leading ones in the churches are glad to do the diaper work, since by doing such work, they can assist in the growth of their spiritual children. As a result of this growth, one day all the saints will be matured.

## Declaring the Lord's Death Until He Comes

First Corinthians 11:26 says, “As often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you declare the Lord's death until He comes.” According to this verse, whenever we partake of the Lord's table, we make a show, a declaration, of the Lord's death. In other words, when we eat the bread and drink the cup, we exhibit the death of Christ until He comes.

The Lord has not returned, because we are not mature. We are like wheat that is still green and not ready for harvest. Thus, all that the Lord is able to do at this time is to feed us, water us, and add some “fertilizer.” He will do this until we are like golden yellow wheat and are ready for harvest. That will be the time for the Lord Jesus to return and reap His harvest (Rev. 14:1-5, 14-16).

## Hastening the Lord's Return by Growing unto Maturity Early

Our knowing the doctrines concerning the Lord's return does not hasten His coming. The Lord's coming requires our growth and maturity. As long as we remain spiritually immature, we will delay the Lord's coming. Crops that do not ripen on time delay the farmer's harvest, but those that ripen early hasten the harvest. Likewise, the quicker we mature in the divine life, the more we hasten the Lord's harvest.

We all need to grow by eating Jesus; the more we eat Him and grow, the more we hasten His return. Merely to declare, “Lord Jesus, come quickly,” is not sufficient. When we make such declarations, the Lord Jesus may respond, “Eat, grow, and mature quickly. As long as you are not mature, I cannot return.”

Just as a farmer cannot harvest his crops until they are ripe, the Lord Jesus will not return until a significant number of His believers are mature. Hence, we need to keep feeding on Jesus.

## The Genuine Church Life Being a Life for Hastening the Lord's Return

In order to hasten the Lord's return, what we need is not religion but food. Christianity is a religion, but Jesus is genuine food (John 6:35, 41, 51, 55-57). What we need is Christ as our real food. Whenever we come to the Lord's table, we come not to a desk for studying but to a table for dining. The church is not merely a school; it is also a dining hall.

We have been in the “classroom” of traditional Christianity for too long; we have learned many doctrines, but we have not been filled with food. In the genuine church life we have a dining room in addition to the healthy teaching. We not only eat and are satisfied but also have food to offer to others. We may attend a meeting to receive one meal and leave with enough for three more meals.

This is the church life, a life for the hastening of the Lord's return. In the churches we are eating and growing in order to mature early and bring the Lord back. We are not waiting for His return in a vain way. Rather, we desire to eat and grow more quickly so that the Lord Jesus may return quickly. Every Lord's table should serve as a reminder that we need to eat, grow, and mature so that the Lord Jesus may return. Our responsibility today is to grow unto maturity so that the Lord may return quickly.
