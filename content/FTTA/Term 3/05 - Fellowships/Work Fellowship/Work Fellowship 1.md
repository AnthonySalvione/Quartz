# An Overview of the Young People's Work in the Lord's Recovery

**Scripture Reading:** [Col. 1:27b-29](Colossians-01#v27); [Matt. 13:3](Matthew-13#v3), [38](Matthew-13#v38); [John 10:10b](John-10#v10); [1 Tim. 2:4](<1 Timothy-02#v4>); [3:15](<1 Timothy-03#v15>); [Rom. 1:9](Romans-01#v9); [John 21:15-17](John-21#v15); [Eph. 6:4](Ephesians-06#v4)

I hope you don't have any kind of thought that we are trying to impart to you something that maybe we do in Irvine, or what we do in Anaheim, or even the United States young people's work. You have to throw that out. We must have the vision of the young people's work as it relates to the context and sphere of the Lord's recovery. **The goal and the vision are the same wherever we go, although it may look a little different.** Wherever we serve, the goal and vision should be the same.

There are many ways to go to the airport, but the goal is the airport. So, one locality may have a certain way, and you will learn some things—even practical things—of how to serve with young people and children. I hope you don't take that way and go back home and say, "This is the way," because there are many ways. It depends on the saints, the makeup, the young people, what age, how many, etc. But **the goal and vision are the same**. We all should have the same goal and vision when we consider the young people's and the children's work.

## I. The goal of the work with the children and young people in the Lord's recovery:

### A. To present every man fullgrown in Christ—to produce normal, living, functioning members of the Body of Christ—[Col. 1:27b-29](Colossians-01#v27).

So, there is a goal. I think it's very easy for us to look at that and say, "Okay, I see this." But is this the goal with the children? I mean, they aren't saved yet! We will see today that we should consider our labor as just **one work**. Whether you serve in any area—community, college, or young people—the goal is the same. How we get there is different.

Maybe on campus, lots of students come, and for the most part they want to be there and they are hungry. But with the young people, a good percentage of them may not want to be there. We hope they do, but some of you were young people in the church life, and sometimes you wanted to run away, but Mom said you had to go to the meeting. So, it is a different way to labor, but **the goal is the same**.

Our goal is to present every man fullgrown in Christ, meaning to produce **normal, living, functioning members of the Body of Christ**. Don't think now that you will have to teach every young person how to release their spirit and teach the truth. No, they are all at different stages in life, so you need to go to the Lord specifically for each one. We have to be very sensitive to the Spirit, because we may have something we like to do, but the Spirit may say, "No, he is too young. You need to build more basic steps." So, our goal is the same wherever you are serving: **to present every man fullgrown in Christ**.

In the past, as I mentioned, the children's work was mainly to take care of the children. But just to give you a little overview today of how we labor, one of the main things we must consider with the children is to **cultivate their humanity**. We nurture them in our work because we have the view that we are nurturing **the next generation**. So, no longer is it just babysitting. No longer is it just taking care of the children so the saints can enjoy the meeting. **We have a goal with these children.** We want to position them to receive the Lord. We don't want them to be here and there and not open. No, our care for them is so that, first of all, they would have a proper humanity.

I have some examples of poor and proper humanity with children. There were some children, maybe ten years old, and I could tell they had proper humanity. We would take them out with our kids because they were a similar age. We would go to Red Robin and say, "What would you like to have? You could have anything you want." They looked and said, "I'll just have a hamburger," while my kid wanted a shake, dessert, combo meal, etc. These children just wanted water. I thought, "Wow, where did they learn that? That is proper humanity." They realized they were guests, so they wouldn't have uncontrolled lust. Of course, we got them something better than they asked for, but it was so refreshing to hear a young child be polite and proper humanly.

On the other side, there was a young brother whom I love dearly. Nothing against this brother; I spent time with him. But initially, when he would come to my house, he found out I had a beverage refrigerator. He walks in the house, takes off his shoes, and without a pause walks over to the refrigerator and pulls out a drink. I just stood and looked at him, and he is watching me, and he says, "What!" I said, "Why would you do that? This isn't your home. Of course I would give you the drink, no problem, but you need to ask." This is just a small example of **poor humanity**. At least he is open to me, so I can help him and have a way to show him that his humanity is not proper. The Lord has a very difficult time using those without a proper humanity.

So, with the children we **cultivate their humanity**, and gradually what we do is convey to them a **deep impression that there is a God**. As they progress in age, we teach them that there is a God and that God created everything—all about creation and how creation shows that God exists. Then, as they get older, we begin to teach them about the fall of man, sin, and the Lord Jesus, who is our Savior. You know, this is now going on to fifth grade. Then eventually, we help them to receive the Lord and be saved **in a definite way**. This is how we take care of the children, with the view that one day they will be saved. Then we can labor on them to produce them as fullgrown men in Christ.

A small example to show you is Daniel. I've always been impressed with Daniel. This description of who he is impressed me immensely today. I read it again a couple of times. This was when the king was looking for young men, so he told the chief of the eunuchs to bring some of the sons of Israel. He specifically says children, not young men, *"who showed insight in all wisdom, understanding in knowledge, and apprehension in thought, and in whom was the ability to stand in the king's palace."*

Think about that statement. **What kind of children were they?** They were probably the kind of children who went to our children's meetings, because this is what we desire to produce—children who can stand in the king's palace, and who are thoughtful, not lustful and greedy and doing things without thinking. "Insight in all wisdom" means they see and understand things, and according to that understanding they are not reckless. They take the time to understand things and don't just assume they understand. "Apprehension in thought" means they are very considerate and not quick to act. Even in their considerations, they go through a process. This happens through a proper upbringing and education.

We know that Daniel was educated in the things of God. He prayed; he worshipped God. So, not only did he have a proper humanity, shown by the fact that he could stand in the king's palace, but also in the way he conducted himself and the things he did, you know he was taught in the things of God. **This is what we try to do in our children's meetings without giving them any premature knowledge.** It is all related to their humanity, to knowing God exists, even learning the stories in the Word, and eventually we teach sin and salvation and help them to receive the Lord.

What does this eventually produce? This brings us to the next point.

### B. To produce "seeds of the gospel"—[Matt. 13:3](Matthew-13#v3), [38](Matthew-13#v38); [Rom. 1:9](Romans-01#v9), [14-16](Romans-01#v14); [1 Tim. 2:4](<1 Timothy-02#v4>).

This kind of care with the children produces **seeds of the gospel**. So, this is what our labor is for. This is the goal of our labor: to present every man fullgrown in Christ, and specifically with our children—but also with everyone we meet—we want them to be seeds of the gospel. When they go to school or work, they become seeds of the gospel, meaning they can speak the gospel and convey the seed of life into others.

Let me read you something concerning seeds of the gospel. This is from *Crucial Words of Leading in the Lord's Recovery, Book 5*:

> "When children are saved, they become our young brothers and sisters. After they graduate from primary school, they become seeds of the gospel in junior high school."

This is after they get saved. So, after they get saved:

> "When we work in the schools, they become our inside helpers and bring their classmates to us."

This is the meaning of **seeds of the gospel**. At least here in California, we can't go on the junior high campuses; it is not allowed. So, we have no way to reach our young people's classmates. If they are raised up to be seeds of the gospel, the implication here is that something is produced. If we don't help our young people to learn how to speak and how to be bold and proud that they love God—to be bold in their faith—then they will not open their mouths and will not be seeds of the gospel. So, they are there to be our **inside helpers**. I love that word.

In this way, it is easier to work in the junior high schools. It is difficult to bring the gospel to a school where there are no students or teachers who are saints. The children are waiting to sprout by responding to our call and bringing their classmates to salvation. Then they again become seeds of the gospel in high school. We could bring three times as many people to salvation in high school. They are seeds of the gospel in college. In this way, **the number of people saved is continually multiplied**. This is truly a great thing.

This is really our goal in taking care of the children and young people. Eventually, when the children get saved, if you don't have the proper goal, then our labor with the young people becomes something like **entertainment-focused care for them**, and there is not much result. So, we need to be those who realize that in our labor, our young people are seeds of the gospel. We need to help them, nurture them, and perfect them. We will get into this a bit more in the weeks to come—how to do this.

Let's move on to the next point now.

## II. The preciousness of the children and young people and the battle over the children and young people:

I asked in the beginning that many of you probably are not serving with young people, but I would like to ask you now: Why did you choose young people? On the other hand, why did you not choose young people? I know some of you probably said either one, but some of you maybe chose young people because you like them, and they like you. You get along with them, and you have a heart for them. Some of you didn't choose young people because it was like, "I was a young person; I have nothing to do with that now." You don't want to deal with that. Or you may not like them, or may not have any thought concerning the young people. Either one doesn't matter.

I hope today you would have a change of mind, that all of you would have a proper heart. This is because although some of you who have signed up for young people have a "natural way" with young people—you just like them, you like to be with them, so of course you would sign up for young people—I have to tell you, if that is the only reason you signed up for young people, it is just as easy for you to jump ship if you don't enjoy it. Maybe there is no outward progress or they don't respond to you, so you become discouraged. This is because you had this kind of natural heart for them, a natural interest.

Now, it is not a bad thing. Don't get discouraged. It is human. We are all human beings. It is very human to have a natural liking for certain things. My wife said, "I married you because you are so good with young people and children." I like young people; that is why I have four children! But that is not, in a sense, good enough to do the young people's work that carries out God's economy, that is in the Lord's recovery. **We have to see the preciousness of the young people.**

There is a phrase—I will read it to you. This portion in the outline came out from there:

> "If we truly want to do the young people's work and do it in a weighty manner, we must let God open our eyes to see the preciousness of the young people and their importance in God's hands. If we see this, we will appreciate this work, and spontaneously we will have a heart within for them."

The key is **to do the young people's work in a weighty manner**. We must let God open our eyes to see the preciousness of the young people and their importance in God's hands. If we see this, we will appreciate this work, and spontaneously we will have a heart within us for it. You don't even have to try; it is spontaneous. It's not whether you like them or not. It has nothing to do with our natural feelings.

There are three things here: **there is a goal, a condition, and then there is a revelation**. The goal is the weighty matter. What is this? We can go right back to the first point. What qualifies or makes our labor weighty? It's because we have the goal to present every man fullgrown in Christ. We realize this is our goal, and we realize this is a goal which requires longevity.

Those of us who have served with the young people for a long time know that you may not see any result with them until they graduate college. This happened to me many times. I bumped into a married brother, and I was like, "Who are you?!?" I was shocked he was still here! You don't even expect it because you didn't see anything at all. So, this is a labor that requires **longevity**, because you have a view and vision.

The goal is for us to labor in a weighty manner, meaning we have the goal of bringing every man to be fullgrown in Christ and making every young person a seed of the gospel. So, this goal would cause us to pray **desperately**. This goal—I'll go back to the verses in the beginning, Col. 1:27-29—mentions first, "Christ in you," in verse 27. So, the first thing is **Christ in you**. With the children, we want to conclude our labor with the children with **Christ in you**. Every single one should have Christ in them.

But then, we have to continue that we would present every man fullgrown in Christ: **"for which also I labor, struggling."** Don't forget that part. If you don't have the proper heart, you will not want to struggle, and you will give up, because the labor with the young people requires a lot of struggle, and that struggle is mainly in prayer: "Lord, what about David? He hasn't come to the meeting in two weeks. LORD, TOUCH HIM!" We labor and struggle. Why? Because we want to present him fullgrown in Christ. We must have a goal in our labor. But in order to have this goal, we need to go on to the next point, which is the condition.

The **condition** is that we must let God open our eyes. If you don't see the importance of the young people's work, I am not going to convince you in one meeting. But I say, "Go to the Lord. Lord, I want to see the young people how You see them. I want to see them in the context of Your economy. Open my eyes." So, we have a change in heart and vision because our goal changes.

Finally, there is **revelation**: we see their preciousness and their importance in God's hands. We must have these three things.

We see children—innately they are precious—but when we see that they are not only precious, but precious because they are **important in God's hands**, that creates what I would say is a **strategic value** with the young people. You have to realize that the young people and young ones have a strategic value to God.

Consider the entire history of the Bible. It is very difficult to find, when God is trying to do something new and change the age, that He goes to an old person. **It is always the young ones.** They have strategic value to God. We have cases like Moses, Joshua, Samuel—a young man, a prophet, a priest, and a man of prayer. He turned the age from the priesthood to the kingship. Then we have David. I mentioned Daniel, and we have his three friends; they turned the age from the age of captivity. Then, in the New Testament, we have John the Baptist. He was declaring the coming of Christ to change the age from the Old Testament to the New Testament. Paul changed the age in many ways.

Even considering our history, the Lord raised up some young servants who were faithful to Him to bring in all the matters of His recovery. Today we are benefiting from all the labor of these faithful servants. So, we must realize that **God's work—His work to bring in something new, His work to change the age—is mainly with the young ones**. That includes all of you!

How we bring them up, perfect them, and care for them will determine their usefulness to the Lord. So, we must consider our labor with them, and we should labor in a way that we could present them fullgrown in Christ, in a way that they would be seeds of the gospel.

This last term in Irvine, among two high schools there, I was just in awe. I was amazed and full of joy the whole semester because our young people were bringing their friends. It wasn't just one or two, but ten or fifteen. One would bring two or three; another brought two. It was just wonderful to see. They were really **seeds of the gospel**.

In contrast, among those, there were also a couple who brought friends, but the reason their friends didn't come more than once was because of the poor humanity of these young brothers. You know, one brother brought a friend, but he wanted to go fishing on the Lord's Day, or he would come and just be loose. That kind of humanity might bring someone the first time, but it won't keep someone. **You represent Christ!** So, what do they get from you?

You might have a heart for them, but if you don't have the proper humanity, it makes it **much more difficult** for your friends to see Christ and be attracted by Christ. They may come because you are there, but that wears off pretty quickly. I just wanted to highlight this matter again of the proper humanity with the children and young people, and helping them to open their mouths to be seeds of the gospel.

Christian — I enjoyed this message a lot. Taking care of the young people and the campus work with the college students is very related. Even though the college students are older in age, they may still be young in spiritual capacity. We don't come to them with an agenda. The way we shepherd is that we just hold their hands and walk with them.

I enjoyed that because that is my own experience. I couldn't feed the young people unless first I took something in myself, and shepherding comes with this too, because when I shepherd this young one, **I am shepherded**. I enjoyed [Eph. 4:11](Ephesians-04#v11). These shepherds and teachers are together—not only giving them knowledge, but ministering life that comes into us. What the brother said is very true: if you feel you don't have the capacity, **put yourself in that situation and the Lord will be the One who ministers**.

We need to have this view, or our work will be natural. Sometimes when I am with the young people, I just want them to have a good time. But I realized that if I just have this low view, none of them will go on. How are they going to become a fullgrown member in the Body of Christ? The young people and children are our future. If the Lord doesn't come back in our generation, the Lord may come back in their generation. So, we must have the view that these people may be the ones who bring the Lord back. We need to be fullgrown in Christ.

Liliyana — We have one goal: **to present each man fullgrown in Christ**. Apart from this one goal, I appreciated that we don't do this alone. I've learned over three terms of being a young people's trainee that I am not alone, and this is not a work I do alone. We must rely on all the saints burdened for them, their parents, and the Lord, who will complete the work **in them**.

Matt Sharmin — We need to see the preciousness of the young people. If we don't see this, we need to go to the Lord. It is our responsibility to go to Him to receive a burden and heart for the children and young people. If we do this, we will appreciate the work and spontaneously have a heart for it.

Acacia — Last term we really enjoyed [1 Tim. 4:15](<1 Timothy-04#v15>). One of the sweetest things about serving with young people is that you are just **growing in the Lord together**, and there is an atmosphere of mutual shepherding. So, it is not just them telling you what to do; they are helping **you** to grow. You realize then how much of a function they have, and as they grow in life, their function is so spontaneous and sweet. It is so awesome to watch them grow alongside you.

I appreciated that we need to see the preciousness of the young people in the Lord's eyes. We need to shepherd them according to the vision to present them fullgrown in Christ. We need the same heart as the Lord. **The Lord needs the young people for the sake of His move on the earth.**
