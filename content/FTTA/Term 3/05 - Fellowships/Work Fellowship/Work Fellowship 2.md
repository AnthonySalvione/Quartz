# Contacting New Students on the College Campuses

For many of you who are on campus, we have a unique opportunity or window **only at the beginning of the semester**, so we have to say something concerning new students on campus. This is a crash course on contacting new students.

If you are in your second year, you've read a book called *Preaching the Gospel on the College Campuses*. In that book, there are six essential things that we need to preach the gospel—six non-negotiables. You can't miss them. You must have these six items to preach the gospel on the college campuses, **plus one additional item**.

So, all right, you need to remember this. If you are a 3rd- or 4th-termer, you should know this—the six items plus the additional item. If you don't know, take your best guess.

## The Six Essential Items Plus One Additional Item

1. **The Spirit**
2. **The Body / Oneness**
3. **Prayer**
4. **The Word**
5. **A Heart for Man / Universal Love**
6. **A Proper Daily Living**
7. **Additional help: the homes**

### 1. The Spirit and the Body

Chapter 1 is **the Spirit and the Body**. So, we need the Spirit. We can't do this by ourselves, so we need the Body—the team, gospel partner, and local saints.

### 2. Prayer and the Word

Chapter 2 is **prayer and the Word**. I will leave out the additional item until the end. You can be the best speaker and know all the right things to say, but if you do not pray, your preaching will not be effective. You could pray all day long, but if you don't know the Word, likewise you will not be effective in preaching the gospel.

### 3. Universal Love and a Proper Daily Living

Chapter 3 is **universal love and a proper daily living**. Again, if you use words but your living doesn't match that, you won't go anywhere. Likewise, if you use the right words but have no love, nothing will happen.

### The Additional Item: The Homes

The additional help is **the homes**. That is a real basic, general overview of preaching the gospel: **three chapters—six plus one**.

# Practical Skills for Contacting People

Now, just some practical tips here in terms of your contact with people.

## 1. Learn How to Ask Good Questions

One skill to master in your contact with people is **learning how to ask good questions**. It is biblical. This is the way God Himself preached the gospel in the Bible in Genesis. It was not by giving lectures; it was by asking questions. After Adam sinned, He didn't give him a lecture on what he did, but He said, **"Where are you?"** He just asks questions.

Now, those questions were not random; they were strategic. God knew the answers. He wasn't asking the question because He didn't know. He asked, *"Where are you?"* because He wanted to touch his conscience and help him to realize where he was.

So, you could have a few questions ready. You could think about this ahead of time. You don't have to shoot off the cuff. You can actually prepare to do things like this. You can talk with your teammates about what are some great questions to ask students—specifically freshmen—that engage them and bring out a little bit of where they are.

There **are** bad questions. There are questions that are firstly too hard to answer, and there are questions that would make them just say, *"Are you kidding me? That is too easy."* Then there are questions that are really good for engaging in conversation—not too hard nor too easy—with these freshmen.

For instance, whether you are at a table or doing cold gospel, you need to learn how to **small talk and keep the conversation going**, even with human questions like their schooling. You could always ask about their background. Usually that is not hard for them to answer. But if you ask, *"Hey, so what is the meaning of your life?"* that is a good question, but almost nobody—if you are trying to generate conversation—is going to have an easy answer to that. Now, if your goal is to stump them and just have some silence so that you can then give them a lecture, that is probably a good question to ask. But if you want them to talk to you, you should probably ask an easier question or set of questions:

- *"Did you ever feel that the Lord was real to you?"*
- *"Have you prayed before?"*
- *"Have you grown up religious?"*
- *"Have you read the Bible before?"*

This is going to give you some information about where someone is with the Lord, but in much easier little bites. It also helps them to talk to you instead of just me talking to them. We are trying to **engage in a conversation here**. A lecture is not that fun for a college freshman. For me to just dispense and answer them for 15 minutes—most freshmen probably are not going to remember that too joyfully. But if you are going back and forth and engaging them with these questions, that is a little something.

## 2. Be Ready to Answer Their Questions

Be ready for these questions:

- *"What do you guys do?"*
- *"What does the club do?"*
- *"What makes you different from other Christian clubs?"*
- *"Are you associated with a church?"*

If you are tabling, **be ready to answer that**. There are bad answers and great answers for this. This is a chance for you to minister something encouraging at their level, and also a chance to turn them off.

*"Are you associated with a church?"* You are going to get that question. In LA, I say, *"Yeah, we are."* Keep in mind, **this is not a pastor**. Now, if a pastor asks you this question, you better answer it differently than a freshman. That doesn't mean we are trying to hide something; it just means what they are really asking for is different.

If a pastor asks, he wants to know your theology and organizational connections. He is trying to put you in a box theologically. When a freshman asks that question, mostly what are they looking for? Probably not to put you in a category. They are looking for **a community**, maybe. Again, their definition of the word *church* is a lot of times either an organization or a building off campus where you could go to worship God.

So, mostly, if they ask that question—if I am answering a freshman in LA about this question—I am going to say:

> "Yes, we are. Our club is supported by an off-campus church called the Church in LA. It's Christian, and we meet in such-and-such a place really close to campus."

**That is what they are looking for.** They want to know that there is a place where people go to worship God and that it is Christian. So, be ready to answer that. You should fellowship with those on your campus about what is going on and how you answer that.

## 3. Get Their Contact Information

In terms of initial contact, as a full-timer with **28 years of practice preaching**, in all these years preaching the gospel and talking to people, I've given away many flyers, QR codes, websites, my own phone number, my email, my Instagram. When I give them **my** information, how many of the—let's call it maybe even a thousand—people that I've contacted have then come back and reached out to me? I can probably count on one hand the number of people who have done that.

The lesson here is that **if you want to see them again, if you leave your information with them, most likely you are not going to see them again**. In many cases, that is actually a smooth way to end a bad conversation. You get what I am saying? On their part, it's a smooth way for them to end the conversation.

It's not zero. But if you want to see them again, **you need to get their number**. Even when you ask for their number, you can practice doing it. There are awkward and easy ways to ask for someone's number. A less awkward way is, *"Hey, I would like to text you later about some of our events."* That is pretty okay, right?

Or I could be like, *"Um, you know, I would really like to have your number to contact you..."* Now, that is awkward. I am not joking.

So, think about these things, and then practice as we reach out to these ones. There are skillful ways to do this and less skillful ways to do this.

# Inoculating New Students

We have eight minutes now on inoculation. Satan is active. We have to remember that our fighting is not against flesh and blood but against Satan and his rulers and the authorities. Especially on some campuses it is more prevalent than others, but all of us have exposure to the internet. All the students we contact could easily go online and find a lot of negative stuff out there about us, whether on Reddit, TikTok, etc. There are things they may be able to find about us. There is always that risk.

Again, we are specifically now talking about **freshmen**. The inoculation might look a little different for someone who has been meeting with us for a few months. But with a brand-new freshman, I will take a certain strategy to inoculate that would be different from someone else.

## The Biblical Principle of Inoculation

First of all, the idea of inoculation is in **John 16:1-4**. The Lord Jesus Himself inoculated the disciples and told them that they would even be killed by people who thought they were offering service to God. He spoke those things so that they would **not be stumbled**. This is the principle of inoculation.

When you really boil it down, from a freshman's perspective, there is really mainly **one concern** that they are going to have. Are they going to be concerned about whether we are "modalists"? I don't think so. Most of them probably haven't heard that word or know what it means. Are they going to be concerned about the teaching of the ground of the church? Probably not. Again, they may think that a church is just a physical building they go to on Sundays.

## Addressing the Main Concern Directly

What are they concerned about? Just **"cult."** That is what it boils down to. There are easy ways to "inoculate" them against thinking that we are a cult. We are not a cult. There are cults on campus. Where I am, at both campuses—USC and UCLA—there are cults, and that is not us.

So, there are different ways to go about this. If you are at one of these campuses like I am, where there are actually very active cults, we could warn them about other groups being cults. I am not joking. You could say:

> "Hey, as you are considering what club to join, just consider that there are some groups on campus that are cults, meaning they put a lot of pressure on you to join their activities. I want you to know that that is not who we are. We don't put any pressure on you. We are just praying for and supporting you, and if you decide to go another direction, that is no problem. If you have any questions, we have nothing to hide."

That is an easy way to mention the **"c-word."**

You could kind of dance around it and say:

> "Hey, sometimes people say rumors about different clubs and things. If you ever hear anything, feel free to reach out and ask questions."

That is kind of a weak inoculation. But if you are really in a serious situation—again, on the campuses where I am, and maybe where you are too; some places won't be as desperate—at UCLA we are coming out in the first contact to say something.

In any case, to me, to really say that you inoculated somebody—a freshman, let's say—there are all types of inoculation and all different levels, but to say you truly inoculated a freshman, I would say you **have to mention the c-word**. Just getting that out there gives them the ground to bring it up to you if they do hear something.

Another way to say it is:

> "Hey, at the beginning of the year, there are a lot of Christian groups around. There is competition, and sometimes because of competition people throw around false claims. We do not try to hide who we are. If you have any questions, again, feel free to reach out and don't hesitate to ask. We have nothing to hide."

## Inoculation and Maintaining Their Trust

This is all in the area of inoculation. There is **no guarantee**. Even if we do inoculate, there is no 100 percent guarantee that everyone we inoculate will not somehow leave, get turned off, or stop meeting. But for sure, **not inoculating greatly increases the chances** that down the road they will find something and think that we hid something from them by not letting them know up front. If that happens, then you lose their trust, and it is a great danger to lose their trust.

There are all kinds of things here. There are **higher-risk factors**. If someone is saying, *"You are the sixth club I am going to..."* that is pretty high risk that they are going to hear something negative about our club. Or, *"Man, I enjoy your fellowship, and I've been telling my pastor about what you are saying"*—**red light, high risk**. You must inoculate when they say these kinds of things. *"My uncle's a pastor"*—the list goes on and on of risk factors and how quickly you inoculate someone.

A recent history on your campus, etc.—even race. There are certain races that have more vehement opposition on social media and online to the Lord's recovery. Just know this, and again, we are just doing our best.
