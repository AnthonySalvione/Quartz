## I. The definition of consecration:

### A. Our consent to God's working in us and on us.

To give your consent—it's a matter of your will. It's to agree with what God wants. This doesn't mean, according to His 'permissive will', but according to His absolute will. What does God *want*? We would consent, and say, "Lord, what do you want? Whatever it is, I agree with You. That You should have this, I give this to You. This is not mine, this is Yours." 

So, consecration, very much is a matter of our will. Even, you tell the Lord, "Yes Lord, I am willing!" Or, if you are exposed, when the Lord comes to claim something that is already His... and you are not willing, you are not EXCUSED. Don't think, "I'm not willing so the Lord will leave me alone!" That is a dangerous condition... to be passive when the Lord comes to touch something. To think it's okay if we are not willing to just do nothing. No, we should have the response: "Lord, I am willing! I agree with you!" If we can't pray that, then pray "Lord, make me willing. I am not willing but make me willing! Do what you need to do to make me willing to give this to you and surrender it to you"

### B. Our consent to God's using us.

We'll have a point in the outline... we must *wait* for God to first work in us.
