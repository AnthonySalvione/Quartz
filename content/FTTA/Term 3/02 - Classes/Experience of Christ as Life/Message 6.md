# THE EXPERIENCE AND GROWTH IN LIFE

## MESSAGE SIX

## THE GROWTH IN LIFE (3)

**Scripture Reading:** Rom. 8:1-6

## I. Romans 8:1-6 speaks of our being freed from the law of sin and of death:

### A. Romans 7 and 8 speak of our involvement with several persons:

#### A woman—7:2-3.

#### Two husbands—the fallen old man and Christ—vv. 2-4.

#### “I”—vv. 7-24.

#### Sin—vv. 8-9, 11, 13-24, 17, 20.

#### Christ Jesus—8:1-2.

#### The Spirit of life—v. 2.

### B. In Romans 7 and 8 there are three lives and four laws:

#### The first law is the law of God, the ten commandments, which portrays God and defines Him and places many demands and requirements on fallen man—7:2, 23, 25.

#### The second law is the law of good in the mind, which is man's natural life with its good nature created by God and its desire to keep the law of God—v. 23.

#### The third law is the law of sin (and of death), which is the satanic life in man's fallen body—vv. 23, 25; 8:2.

#### The fourth law is the law of the Spirit of life, which is Christ as the Spirit of life, who dwells in our spirit—v. 2.

### C. Romans 8:1 says, “There is now then no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus”:

#### We are now in Christ Jesus, not in the old husband, the “I,” or sin; however, if in our experience we remain in the old husband or the “I,” we are under sin—7:14.

#### According to Romans 7, sin is a person (vv. 8, 9, 11, 17, 20); it is difficult to say whether this person is Satan or our self, because these two are one—cf. Matt. 16:23-24.

#### On the one hand, sin is Satan himself; on the other hand, because Satan injected himself into the human race, the entire human race also became sin; that is, the entire human race became one with Satan—Matt. 3:7; John 8:44; 1 John 3:10; cf. 2 Cor. 5:21.

#### In the eyes of God, Satan, sin, and the human race are one; Jesus Christ is the only person in the entire universe who is not one with Satan—John 14:30; 2 Cor. 5:21; Heb. 4:15; 7:26; 1 Pet. 2:22.

#### If we live by the “I,” we cannot avoid sin, because the “I” is sin; we can never get away from sin, and we can never overcome sin, because we ourselves are sin.

#### Regardless of how good one might seem to be in the eyes of others, in the eyes of God he is sin—cf. Exo. 23:23 and note, paragraph 2.

#### The experience of sin in Romans 7 leads us into condemnation—7:24; 8:1:

##### The condemnation in Romans 8:1 is not the condemnation concerning the things we did in the past; rather, it is the condemnation of our current situation.

##### The condemnation mentioned here is subjective, in our conscience, and is the result of our being inwardly defeated by the evil law of the indwelling sin, as described in 7:17-18, 20-24.

##### No one else condemns himself as much as a seeking Christian.

##### The condemned person unveiled in Romans 7 eventually says, “Wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me from the body of this death?”—v. 24.

#### In Romans 7 Paul could not escape from sin; however, in chapter 8 he declares, “There is now then no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus”—v. 1:

##### Paul was no longer in himself; therefore, he was not in sin; that is, he was not in Satan.

##### He was in another person, Christ Jesus, who in our experience of life is the Spirit of life—v. 2.

##### In Christ we are freed from condemnation because Christ Jesus today is the Spirit of life—1 Cor. 15:45b; 2 Cor. 3:17.

### D. Romans 8:2 says, “For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has freed me from the law of sin and of death”:

#### Strictly speaking, it is not the person of Christ in Romans 8:2 who frees us; it is the law of this person.

#### With this person of life, there is a law, the law of the Spirit of life, that frees us from the law of sin and death:

##### Every life has a law and even is a law.

##### Our sinful living does not come from our habit; it comes from our life as a law.

#### The law of the Spirit of life (Rom. 8:2), the law of good in the mind (7:23), and the law of sin and of death (7:23; 8:2) are natural laws, not outward regulating laws:

##### A natural law is not a regulation; it is a natural power.

##### Without another power to restrain us, we sin spontaneously, just as an object falls downward when dropped because of the law of gravity.

##### When one lies, he does it spontaneously—cf. Matt. 26:72; John 8:44.

#### Sin and death are companions, for the law of sin is also the law of death—Rom. 5:12.

#### However, within us is a stronger person, Jesus Christ as the Spirit of life:

##### This stronger person is a stronger law, and this stronger law defeats us as the weaker law.

##### The law of the Spirit of life frees us from the law of sin and of death because the stronger law frees us from the weaker law.

### E. In order to experience the freeing by the law of the Spirit of life, we need to set our mind on the mingled spirit—8:6:

#### Today the Spirit, who is the Spirit of life, Christ Jesus Himself, is one with us in our spirit (1 Cor. 6:17; 2 Tim. 4:22); all day long we need to set our mind on nothing but our spirit.

#### Our mind set on our spirit is life, and this life is the processed Triune God—the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, the consummation of the Triune God—Rom. 8:10.

#### Moreover, a person whose mind is set on the spirit walks according to the spirit—v. 4:

##### The Spirit of life is the Spirit of Christ, and Christ corresponds with the law of God—v. 9b.

##### This Spirit within us spontaneously fulfills all the righteous requirements of the law through us when we walk according to Him—cf. Gal. 5:16.

#### We must learn to see these truths and learn to practice setting our mind on our spirit all the time; this is the experience of life which produces the growth of life.

## II. Some crucial points in the Questions and Answers are as follows:

### A. Strictly, the experience in Romans 7 is that of an unbeliever, but when a believer returns to the standing of an unbeliever under the law, trying to do good to please God, he experiences Romans 7; to try to do good is sin.

### B. Paul did not charge us to try to set our mind on the spirit; he simply said, “The mind set on the spirit is life and peace”—Rom. 8:6b:

#### After hearing a message on setting the mind on the spirit, some may resolve to do their best to do this, but we need to stop our doing.

#### Faith is not doing or trying; faith is simply believing what God has said and saying Amen; this faith keeps us in rest—cf. Gal. 2:20.

### C. We should not consider how to apply the law of the Spirit of life or how to stop the law of sin:

#### If we have seen the law of the Spirit of life, we should simply say, “Hallelujah, Amen!”

#### The law of the Spirit of life will work by itself; it does not need us to apply it.

### D. We should not try to overcome our sin; we should simply believe:

#### Hallelujah! We are in Christ. Christ is our great I Am, and He is the Spirit of life—1 Cor. 1:30a; John 8:58; Rom. 8:2, 10.

#### We have a Savior (Matt. 1:21); if we do something for ourselves, we are attempting to be our own savior.

#### Faith stops any kind of activity and always praises God; faith even “blinds” us, making us like Abraham, who did not know where the Lord was leading him or what route he was taking but knew only that the Lord was leading him—Heb. 11:8.

#### To be too clear is to not believe.

#### Faith is always real and true; the environment is a lie; listen to faith, not to the lie:

##### Faith always tells the environment that it is a lie, not a giant; to deny the environment is faith—Num. 14:6-9.

##### We should say, “I am not defeated. My being defeated is a lie. Satan, this lie must return to you.”

##### To proclaim in this way is to exercise faith; faith is against the environment.
