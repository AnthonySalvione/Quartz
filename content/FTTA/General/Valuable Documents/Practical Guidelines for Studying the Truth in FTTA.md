- Decide on which day and at what time you will study for each training class and make a written schedule. A sample schedule has been provided (see exhibit 2). You will be asked to turn in a copy of your written schedule at a later date.

- *Allocate no less than one hour per week to study for each class. It is recommended that the one hour of study for each class be used as follows:*
	- **Gather** the syllabus, outline(s), reading assignment, Recovery Version with footnotes, writing utensils, etc. “Studying the Bible is like working at a trade; we have to have the proper tools” (*How to Study the Bible*, p. 97).
	- **Prepare your being.** Use your spirit to call on the name of the Lord, confess your sins, and pray for a spirit of wisdom and revelation ([[Ephesians-01#v17|Eph. 1:17]]) as you study. Have an open heart and a single eye with continuous obedience. Do not be careless nor subjective nor curious (*How to Study the Bible*, ch. 1). (2 minutes).
	- **Read the key verses** for the lesson and consider pray-reading one or two verses (5 minutes). This helps you become familiar with the Biblical basis of the lesson.
	- **Review your notes** from class and any related outline(s) to get a feel for the main points and burden of the reading. Scan the content of the material and assess the amount of material you will need to cover. Also consider your familiarity with the lesson and write down a few questions to guide your reading (5 minutes).
	- **Actively read** the material using techniques such as asking questions as you read, highlighting, underlining, summarizing sections, and considering the Biblical basis as you read. At the same time, pace your reading so as to cover the material in the allotted amount of time (30 minutes).
	- **Review the reading** so as to answer the study questions or you can use the following questions for your review (10 minutes):
		- What are the main points of this reading and what are their Biblical bases (*Elders’ Training, Book 3: The Way to Carry Out the Vision*, ch. 1)?
		- How do the main points relate to one another?
		- What was the burden of this lesson?
		- What portion of this message enlightened or touched me the most?
	- **Produce** a summary paragraph (3-4 sentences) or outline a prophecy on one point that touched you and practice speaking your prophecy (8 minutes). Trainees will need to produce Lesson Summaries for two classes each week.

- Strictly adhere to your schedule. You may go back and complete unfinished reading assignments or study extra for a class once you have completed one hour of study for each class.

## Study Tips

- Take notes in class in order to keep your being engaged and your renewed mind active (*Three Aspects of the Church: Book 1, The Meaning of the Church*, pp. 14-15). Notes do not need to be word-for-word, but should be substantial and enough to remind you of the main points and burden of the message.
- Maintain a strong spirit of prayer as you study.
- Repetition and review allow the truth to penetrate our being. “The truth gets into us through our mentality” (*Elders’ Training, Book 3: The Way to Carry Out the Vision*, p. 93).
- Speak what you have seen and heard to participate in God’s economy and so the Lord can give you more. Refrain from any unsanctified speaking (*Song of Songs*, p. 72). Idle words leak life (*The Collected Works of Watchman Nee, (Set 2) Vol. 37: General Messages (1)*, ch. 9).
