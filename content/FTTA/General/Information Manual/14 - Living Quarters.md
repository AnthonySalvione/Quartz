## Section One — General

1. __General rules:
	1. Do not move or shift any furniture. No personal furniture is permitted.
	
	2. Ironing should be done on ironing boards only.
	
	3. Do not use candles at any time. Do not alter electrical outlets, except with extension cords provided by the Training
	
	4. Do not feed or keep animals in or in the vicinity of the residences.
	
	5. Do not use pull-up bars on the door frames.

2. __Bedroom rules:
	1. Keep all personal belongings neat. There should be no loose items.
	
	2. Keep the bedroom quiet. No exercise or loud talking is permitted in the bedroom. Do not disturb ones who may be resting by using a bathroom inside a bedroom; use a bathroom in a hallway.
	
	3. Do not receive visitors in the bedroom. No brothers should enter a sisters' house and no sisters should enter a brothers house without express permission from a Training assistant brother or maintenance brother.
	
	4. Do not cook, eat, or store food in the bedroom.

3. __Personal storage:
	1. Personal storage should be used only in the space that has been allocated to you and for those items you will use during your time in the Training.
	
	2. The desks, closets, chests, luggage racks in the garages (in Grace Gardens), kitchen cabinets, and bathroom cabinets may be used to store your personal belongings.
	
	3. Other than luggage, bicycles, and large boxes, no personal items may be stored in the garages.
	

4. __Be responsible to maintain the residences that have been arranged for you:
	1. Save water and electricity. Turn off utilities when not in use. Do not tamper with the thermostats.
	
	2. Any cost that is the result of willful or negligent damage to or loss of any Training property, or willful abuse of utilities by trainees will be borne by them. Fellowship with a Training assistant brother to avoid any such cost.

## Section Two — House Cleaning and Inspections

_"The character that you build up during the training has to become your habit and eventually your daily life. No matter how busy I am in my daily life, I make my bed when I get up every day, unless my wife voluntarily does it for me. After I change, I do not place my clothes in random places. I always place them neatly in specific places. Sometimes when I get up in the middle of the night, I do not even need to turn on the light to find my clothes. I can reach out my hand and know exactly where they are. Character depends wholly on our continual exercise to develop the habits of our daily living. If you cannot even set your clothes straight, how can you cut straight the word of the truth? Your ability to cut straight the word of the truth depends on whether or not you have built up your character. These two matters are very much related to one another.  

_Our bedrooms should be clean and neat. We should have a habit of cleanliness... You cannot say that it does not matter if your bedroom is a mess, that it is all right since the main purpose of a bedroom is to have a place to sleep. You must remember that when your room is in order, then your preaching will be orderly, your work will be orderly, and your leading of the church will also be orderly. If your room looks improper, then when you manage the church affairs, you will make a mess of them as well" (Vessels Useful to the Lord, ch. 5).

_Regardless of where I move to, the first thing I do is clean the house thoroughly and then settle  
down. If you conduct yourselves in a loose way, seeking only for a place to lie down and sleep, this  
will spoil your character. Not only so, when you work or serve, you will be unprepared and  
bewildered. This is not acceptable" (Vessels Useful to the Lord, ch. 3).

In order to be useful to the Lord, we need to allow the Lord to touch our entire being and living. The trainees subject themselves to the Training with the ultimate intention of surrendering all that they are to the Lord in order that they could become useful to their Master.

As part of the overall burden of the Training, residences should be cleaned daily and will be inspected on a regular basis. Even though this aspect is not the main focus, it is an integral part of the Training. The goal of the house inspections is not merely to have all trainees conform to a uniform cleaning standard. More so, it is to help the trainees learn how to coordinate with others, to drop their own standards and concepts, and to have a proper daily living before the Lord

May the Lord bless all the trainees by supplying them with Himself as grace to meet every outward demand in order that they might receive the full benefit of the Training.

1. __Schedule and Principles:
	1. Exercise to coordinate closely with your housemates to complete the house cleaning each day at the time designated by the Training. Work together, help one another, and check if others need help before leaving each cleaning session:
		1. A portion (20—25 minutes) of your rest day is designated for deep cleaning. The following tasks should be assigned to specific trainees in each house:
			1. Bathtubs and showers—using Soft Scrub, scrub and remove all soap scum, water residue, mold, and mildew. Wash shower curtains.
			
			2. Ovens, stoves, and microwaves—scrub thoroughly, using oven cleaner in the ovens, Airx 75 on the stoves, and soap and water in the microwaves, if necessary.
			
			3. Refrigerators—throw away expired or spoiled food items. Remove all other items and wipe down shelves and drawers with soap and water. Wipe down the door seal and the outside with soap and water.
			
			4. Floors—mop. Wood floors should only be mopped with water.
			
			5. Exterior windows—using 20/20 Vision, clean all exterior first floor windows.
			
			6. Dusting—baseboards, door frames, window sills, and blinds.
	    
	    2. Monday evening from 9:45 p.m. to 10:00 p.m. is designated for preparing the house for the upcoming week.
	    
	    3. Tuesday through Saturday morning from 6:30 a.m. to 7:00 a.m. is designated for grooming and personal area cleaning; 7:00 a.m. to 7:10 a.m. is designated for corporate area cleaning
	    
	2. Exercise to improve your cleaning skills each week. Learn to follow instructions closely. Coordinate with your housemates regarding house uniformity.
	
	3. Cleaning chemicals should be used sparingly and properly:
	    1. 20/20 Vision—ready-to-use glass cleaner. Directions: Spray onto a clean, dry rag and wipe desired surface.
	    
	    2. Bowl-o-Cherry—ready-to-use toilet bowl cleaner. Directions: Squirt onto desired surface, agitate with a brush, and flush.
	    
	    3. General Spotter—ready-to-use carpet and upholstery spot and stain remover. Directions: Spray directly onto the stain as soon as the stain is detected. Allow the cleaner to work on the stain for five minutes. Gently work stain with a clean, dry rag in "pat" motions from the outside inward. Do not rub.
	    
	    4. Mildew Stain Remover—ready-to-use mildew remover. Directions: Open the windows. Spray directly onto the mildew stain. Walk out and let dry. Mildew stain should be gone when the area dries.
	    
	    5.  Soft Scrub—ready-to-use oxygen bleach cleanser. Directions: Wet surface. Apply cleanser freely, Agitate with a wet scrub sponge. Rinse.
	    
	    6. Ajax—ready-to-use heavy-duty oxygen bleach cleanser. Directions: Wet surface. Sprinkle cleanser freely. Agitate with a wet scrub sponge. Rinse. Take care when using so as not to harm surfaces.
	    
	    7.  Airx 75—ready-to-use all-purpose cleaner and disinfectant. Directions: Spray onto a clean, dry rag and wipe desired surface.

2. __Duties of the RIC:__
	1. Monitor the overall house cleaning by housemates and complete a final inspection before leaving for breakfast.
	
	2. Ensure all utilities are turned off, all windows are closed, and all doors are locked.
	
	3. All trainees in the same house will take turns serving as room-in-charge. Satisfactory house inspection results issue from a corporate effort of all members and with a feeling of mutuality and Body-consciousness.
	
	4. Take the house departure roll and report it to one of the HCs upon arriving at the Training Center for breakfast.

3. __Corporate Areas:__
	Corporate areas are areas used by all members of the house. The patio, porch, living room, dining room, hallways, and laundry room fall into this category. The kitchen and bathrooms will be covered separately.
	
	All lights, ceiling fans, water taps, air conditioners, and heaters should be turned off or left in  
	the program mode when no one is home. Any of these items left on will receive an automatic 
	"U" grade.
	
	1. Patio/Porch—the front and back patio and porch should be swept weekly. They should be free from large debris. All spider webs, bird nests, and wasp nests should be removed from any corner or overhang. All exterior doors should be locked
	
	2. Uniformity—items in the house should be uniform in appearance. This is also the "catch-  
	    all" grade and may be affected when something unusual is discovered in a house that could not be marked under one of the other categories. For example, crooked picture frames, uneven sofa cushions, curtains and/or blinds not uniform, unusual items left out, and improperly placed furniture fall into this category.
	
	3. Windows—cleaned once a week. Particular consideration will be given for second floor  
	    windows, which are difficult to clean. In addition, consideration will be given for  
	    environmental factors (e.g., recent rain, strong winds). All windows should be closed and 
	    locked. Within each room, the blinds should be uniform.
	
	4. Floors—swept or vacuumed. Floors should be free from dirt, hair, debris, or loose items.
	
	5. Dusting—items such as picture frames, coffee tables, end tables, desks, door frames, blinds, ceiling fans, lamps, laptops, bookcases, and baseboards should be dusted twice per week. Leniency will be given for ceiling fans that cannot be reached using a chair and for the tops of lights, as the bulbs could easily break when dusted.
	
	6. Mirror—clean and without streaks and spots. Points will be taken off only if spots are removable and are not permanent blemishes on the glass.*
	
	7. Laundry Room—the floor and surfaces should be free from any dirt, dust, hair, and debris. There should be no piles of clothes left out, but clothes in the machines is acceptable. The washer and dryer doors should be closed. The dryer lint trap should be emptied after each load. The laundry room door should be left open. All laundry rooms must have a trash bin with a liner, and it must be less than half full. Trash bins more than half full will receive an automatic "U" grade.*
    
		_* Items 6-7 should be marked only if the score warrants an "A" or below._

4. __Kitchen:
	1. Sink—clean and free from stains, spots, hair, food, and dishes. The faucet should be dry and wiped clean. The faucet head and handle must be aligned to the center. Due to the need for hand washing after cleaning, consideration will be given for a wet sink or what looks like recent water spots. A hand soap dispenser must be present and available for use.
	
	2. Counters—the counters and table must be dry, wiped clean, and free from grease, crumbs, stains, spots, hair, dishes, and loose items. This is a "catch-all" grade for the kitchen. For instance, if the stovetop or table is dirty or a cabinet door is left open, it can fall into this category. Refrigerator magnets should be functional.
	
	3. Floors—swept or vacuumed. Floors should be free from dirt, hair, debris, or loose items.
	
	4. Trash—must have a liner and be less than half full. Trash bins more than half full will receive an automatic "U" grade.

5. __Bathrooms:
	1. Sink—clean and free from stains, spots, hair, and toothpaste. The counter and faucet should be dry and wiped clean. The faucet head and handle must be aligned to the center. This is a "catch-all" grade for the bathroom. Due to the need for hand washing after cleaning, consideration will be given for a wet sink or what looks like recent water spots. A hand soap dispenser must be present and available for use. Toothbrushes can be left out to dry as long as they are clean and in a holder. Otherwise, personal bathroom items must be neatly stowed away.
	
	2. Mirror—clean and without streaks and spots. Points will be taken off only if spots are removable and are not permanent blemishes on the glass.
	
	3. Floors—swept or vacuumed. Floors should be free from dirt, hair, debris, or loose items.
	
	4. Trash—must have a liner and be less than half full. Trash bins more than half full will receive an automatic "U" grade.
	
	5. Toilet—the toilet seat and lid must be in the down position. The seat, lid, rim, and tank cover must be clean and free from dust, debris, or fluids. There should be no toilet paper or any other obvious item left in the bowl. The toilet paper roll should be hanging neatly.
	
	6. Shower—the shower, bathtub, and faucets should be clean and free from dirt, hair, grime, and streaks. Shampoo bottles must be clean and neatly arranged. The soap bar should be neat, orderly, and free from any hair. Consideration will be given for a shower that is wet or has recent water spots. A small gap (one or two handbreadths) between the wall and curtain or sliding door should be left on both sides for airflow. There should be no mold. Consider drying the shower each morning after use for bathrooms with mold or mildew problems. A bathmat should be outside each shower.

6. __Individual Bed Areas:
	1. Uniformity—this is the "catch-all" grade. Factors that affect this score include the placement of bedding on the beds, whether the blinds and/or curtains are uniform, whether the lampshades are straight, and anything else that does not have a designated topic.
	
	2. Beds—sheets must cover the bed neatly, being tightly wrapped around the mattress without any wrinkles. Beds should be free from hair, lint, or debris. The pillow should be centered across the width of the mattress with any pillowcase excess being tucked underneath the pillow. Blankets or other items used for cover should be neatly folded and centered across the width of the mattress at the end of the bed. The largest U-fold of the blankets should be toward the foot of the bed and the neater folded side of the blanket should be toward the main entrance of the bedroom. Blankets must be of a conservative style. Sheets and pillowcases must be solid white. There should be no stuffed animals. All unoccupied top bunks should have a full set of FTTA hospitality linens, including an FTTA-issued pillow. These should be folded neatly in the order displayed in the picture on the next page, placed in a bag at the foot of the bunk, and centered across the width of the mattress. Fitted sheets should be folded to the same size as the flat sheets. A pillow should also be placed at the head of every unoccupied top bunk for hospitality, centered across the width of the mattress.
	
	3. Order of the hospitality set within the bag (from top to bottom):
		1. Face towel
		2. Pillowcase
		3. Towel
		4. Flat Sheet
		5. Fitted Sheet
		6. Blanket
	
	4. Trash—must have a liner and be less than half full. Trash bins more than half full will receive an automatic "U" grade.
	
	5. Towels—towels should be white or solid in color without characters, patterns, or designs. Every occupied bunk must have a corresponding towel in place. Towel edges must be aligned and the tag tucked in. Within each bathroom, towels and washcloths (if used) should be hung uniformly, either folded or unfolded. If folded, the fold of the towel should be facing the eye of the inspector where he or she would enter the bathroom.
	
	6. Shoes—stored in the closet, if possible. All pairs of shoes should face the same direction, arranged neatly side-by-side with the laces tucked into the shoes. The placement of shoes in the closet should be uniform to the other shoes in the closet.*
	
	7. Closets/Bins—clothes should be hung in the closet according to the closet configuration guide below. A top button must be buttoned on all shirts. Clothes must be facing left; the waists of hanging folded pants should be on the left. Each personal space should be arranged from left to right when viewing the clothes rack or rod. Exceptions will be made for clothes unable to conform to the guide (e.g., long dresses). Empty hangers should be stored at either end of the closet and should not be left between hangers with clothing. The overall feeling when viewing the closet is that it is neat, including the arrangement of all articles on the shelves and the floor. Closet doors should be closed; they will receive a "U" if open. Bins/laundry baskets should be neatly arranged and covered from view. No food should be kept in the bedroom or closet.*
	
	8. Books—should be stored upright on shelves, but larger or excess books may be stored on desks using bookends, aligned from tallest to shortest, left to right.*
	
	9. Desks—there should be a lamp with the lampshade straight and the crease toward the back in line with the power cord. There can be a picture frame of family, a ministry quote, or a verse, but it must be conducive to the exercise of the spirit and approved by a Training assistant brother. No picture collages. Chairs should be pushed in. Office supplies should be stored in the drawers. Laptops should be stowed away or placed neatly on the desk. Laptops may be left plugged in for charging, but chargers not in use should be neatly stowed away.*
	
	10. Windows—cleaned once a week. Particular consideration will be given for second floor windows, which are difficult to clean. In addition, consideration will be given for environmental factors (e.g., recent rain, strong winds). All windows should be closed and locked. Within each room, the blinds should be uniform.*
	
	11. Dusting—items such as picture frames, coffee tables, end tables, desks, door frames, blinds, ceiling fans, lamps, laptops, bookcases, and baseboards should be dusted twice per week. Leniency will be given for ceiling fans that cannot be reached using a chair and for the tops of lights as the bulbs could easily break when dusted.*
	
	12. Floors—swept or vacuumed. Floors should be free from dirt, hair, debris, or loose items. Any loose item left on the floor automatically warrants a "U".*
	
	13. Mirror—clean and without streaks and spots. Points will be taken off only if spots are removable and are not permanent blemishes on the glass.*

4. __Additional Guidelines:
	1. You should have a proper attitude regarding the inspection report (e.g., no murmuring and complaining). If there is a major concern or mistake, only the house coordinators should fellowship with a Training assistant brother. Any discrepancies or fellowship regarding score changes must be resolved by 10:15 a.m. on the Wednesday following receiving the inspection report.
	
	2. Every grade that is not an "E" should have a corresponding comment to help each house improve. House coordinators should review each inspection report with the house and make all necessary adjustments based on the comments.
	
	3. When there is a sick trainee, a note should be posted on the bedroom door. "Sick Trainee" doors should be shut and all other bedroom doors open. There should only be one "Sick Trainee" bathroom per house, unless the sick trainees are on different floors or one sick trainee is in a bedroom with an attached bathroom. If a "Sick Trainee" bedroom or bathroom door is open and no trainee is present, the room will be graded as usual. Sick trainees should go to and remain in their bedroom if an inspector arrives. Consideration will be given when grading a corporate area recently used by a sick trainee.
	
	4. All bunks, desks, and towel bars should be clearly numbered.
	
	5. Houses can be inspected from 8:25 a.m. to 11:30 a.m., Tuesday through Saturday, including Wednesdays. Houses can be inspected during conferences, although a pillow or blanket on the bed is not required as you may need them for the conference.
	
	6. Short-term trainees should have their bunk and towel labeled "Short-term Trainee" to avoid being inspected.
	
	7. The schedule of the daily room-in-charge must be posted on the refrigerator or bulletin board along with the previous week's inspection report. Inspectors may also check that important handouts are appropriately posted in the houses.

## Section Three — Laundry Procedures

1. Every training residence has a laundry room or area.

2. Do not do laundry in the bathroom. Do not assemble clotheslines or hang wet clothes.

3. Clearly mark all laundry items with initials to avoid loss or confusion.

4. Do personal laundry in machines designated for this purpose. Strictly adhere to any schedule posted by the house coordinators.

5. Combine clothes with another trainee if needed to obtain an optimal load.

6. The Training only provides laundry detergent. Other items (e.g., bleach, spot remover, starch) are your responsibility. Be extremely careful when using bleach in the houses.

## Section Four — Exercise Rooms

1. Keep the facilities clean and tidy. Return any equipment used to its original and proper location.

2. Enter the facilities via the assigned entrances only. Be respectful of the resident trainees and do not go through the houses.

3. Do not bring non-trainees to the facilities.

4. Be considerate of the noise level and maintain a proper testimony to the resident trainees and the neighbors by keeping noise to an acceptable volume. Do not drop equipment. There should be no raucous behavior.

5. Equipment is Training property only to be used at the facilities and may not be taken for personal use.

6. Bring your own water bottle to the facilities.

7. If you are the last one to leave the facilities, lock and/or shut the assigned entrance securely, even if the resident trainees are at home.

8. Exercise rooms may not be used during morning exercise or after house prayer/curfew.

## Section Five — Duties of House Coordinators

House coordinators (HCs) have been assigned by the Training as liaisons between the Training and the living quarters. It is expected that all the members of the house would render due respect and proper regard to the house coordinators. House coordinators serve to:

1. Uphold the rules for living quarters and personal tidiness. Maintain cleanliness, order, and safety in the house.

2. Schedule the room-in-charge and establish the house cleaning (including rest day tasks) and laundry schedule. Arrange a schedule for putting out and returning the trash (black), recyclables (green), and yard waste (brown) containers.

3. Convey to every trainee in the house the instructions from the Training. Check the house mail slots.

4. Maintain close fellowship with fellow house coordinators and the Training

5. Submit relevant maintenance, linens, and framing requests on the attendance server; dishware requests to the Training office. Ensure that all supplies provided by the Training are adequately on hand. The burden for any other items must be borne by the house.

6. Attend all meetings called by the Training.

7. Maintain oversight of sick ones, ensuring that they are in contact with health care professionals and the Training assistants.

8. Assist in settling disputes and friction among trainees.

9. Contact a Training assistant brother for any questions related to schedules or roll calls. Do not change any roll calls without express permission from a Training assistant brother.

10. Investigate and report to a Training assistant brother any trainee who stays out without express permission from a Training assistant brother for the night.

11. Ensure promptness of trainees in waking up, in attendance, in house prayer, and in retiring.

12. Coordinate the care of the short-term trainees, including the assignment of gospel teams, if unassigned.

13. Report house attendance on the attendance server by 7:55 a.m. Tuesday through Saturday, and by 8:15 a.m. on Lord's Day.

14. Keep mobile phones on at the residences in case of an emergency.
