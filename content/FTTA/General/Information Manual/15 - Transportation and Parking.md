
1. __Motor Vehicles:
	1. You should ensure that your driving is calm, legal, and safe at all times, and take care of the feeling of any passengers in your vehicle in these matters.
	
	2. Passengers should not distract but rather support those driving to help ensure safety.
	
	3. Consider before the Lord helping with the expenses borne by the drivers.
	
	4. Keep vehicles locked at all times. Anti-theft devices are recommended.
	
	5. Leave no visible or valuable items in vehicles.
	
	6. The speed limit for vehicles at the LSM campus and Founders Square is 15 mph. The speed limit for vehicles at Grace Gardens is 5 mph.
	
	7. Automobiles and buses are permitted for travel to the LSM campus from any residence. Bicycles and foot travel are permitted for travel to the LSM campus only from Founders Square and other satellite residences close to the LSM campus. Other means of transportation, including skateboards, rollerblades, and scooters are not permitted from any residence.
	
	8. California drivers must carry liability insurance of $15,000 for injury/death to one person, $30,000 for injury/death to more than one person, and $5,000 for damage to property.
	
	9. California law prohibits drivers from using a handheld wireless telephone while operating a motor vehicle (VC §23123) and from texting while operating a motor vehicle (VC §23123.5). Drivers may use a "hands-free" device.

	13. Car accidents—driving in Southern California can be hazardous. Although drivers are legally responsible to carry minimum liability insurance, many drivers are uninsured. We strongly recommend that you add uninsured motorist coverage to your existing policy. The additional cost incurred is minimal, and this added coverage could save you time, money, and unnecessary suffering if you are ever involved in an accident with an uninsured driver. If you are involved in an automobile accident:
	
	14. Obtain the following information at the scene of the incident:
	    1. Date of the accident.
	    
	    2. Time of the accident.
	    
	    3. Location of the accident. Be as specific as possible. Include cross streets.
	    
	    4. Name of other driver(s).
	    
	    5. Address of other driver(s).
	    
	    6. Telephone number of other driver(s).
	    
	    7. Driver's license number of other driver(s).
	    
	    8. Insurance company name of other driver(s).
	    
	    9. Insurance policy number of other driver(s).
	    
	    10. Description of other vehicle(s).
	    
	    11. Contact information of witnesses.
	    
	    12. Notes detailing facts of the accident.
	    
	    13. Pictures of damage to both vehicles.
	
	15. Call the local police department or 911 to request that an officer be sent to take a written report. Some police departments will not send an officer to take a report in a non-injury accident. If a written report will not be filed, it would be helpful to have eyewitnesses who can confirm your report. For accidents in the city of Anaheim, call the Anaheim Police Department at (714) 765-1900.
	
	16. If your car is not drivable, it is recommended that you do not allow the police department to dispatch a tow truck if you or someone with you has AAA or similar service. Police-dispatched towing companies charge exorbitant towing fees and impound fees that can easily be avoided
	
	17. Notify your insurance company of the accident immediately. Have your notes handy when you call. The insurance company will need to know the details of the accident. The phone number to report a loss should be on your insurance card or the Proof of Financial Responsibility Form you are required to keep in your vehicle.
	
	18. File a Traffic Accident Report (SR-1) with the Department of Motor Vehicles within ten days of the accident if there was an injury, death, or property damage in excess of $750. The SR-1 form can be downloaded online at [www.dmv.ca.gov/forms/sr/srl.pdf](http://www.dmv.ca.gov/forms/sr/srl.pdf). You can fill in the form online, print it, and send it to the address on the form via U.S. mail. Drivers are required by law to submit the SR-1 report to the DMV regardless of fault.
	
	19. Notify a Training assistant brother. You may use your mobile phone for any calls that you need to make at the scene of the accident or immediately following your departure from the scene. Any traffic accident qualifies as an emergency during which you have the liberty to use your mobile phone as needed.
	
	20. For the sake of avoiding potential medical and/or insurance issues, visit an urgent care center as soon as practically possible to be evaluated.

2. __Bicycles:
	1. Keep bicycles locked at all times. U-locks are required over cable locks.
	
	2. Bicycle helmets can reduce head injuries by 85 percent. Helmets are mandatory for all trainee cyclists.
	
	3. Using lights and reflectors at night is the law (VC 821201).
	
	4. Avoid busy streets. Plan your route through residential streets. We suggest riding in pairs whenever possible.
	
	5. Anaheim is not cyclist friendly. Be on the alert and ride defensively. Be aware of your environment (e.g., cars exiting parking lots, car doors that may be opening). Even if you obey all traffic laws, there is always a risk of being hit by a motorist who is not obeying the laws, who is not familiar with bicycle signals, who will not wait for you, or who simply does not see you.
	
	6. Cyclists are considered the same as motorists while riding. As such, cyclists must ride in the same direction of traffic at all times and obey all traffic laws. Cyclists are required to use bicycle lanes where clearly marked. Where lanes are not clearly marked, cyclists are permitted on the sidewalk, but should still ride in the same direction of traffic.
