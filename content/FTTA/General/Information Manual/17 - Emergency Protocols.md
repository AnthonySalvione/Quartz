
### Medical — if a life-threatening emergency transpires on the grounds surrounding the Training Center or Ministry Conference Center:

1. Call 911 and give the operator the following information:
    1. The nature of the medical emergency.
    
    2. The address of 1212 N. Hubbell Way, Anaheim, CA 92801 for the Ministry Conference Center, and 1200 N. Electric Way for the Training Center.
    
    3. Any other information requested by the operator.
    
2. Call Argos Security and give the officer on duty the following information:
    1. Notify the officer that 911 has been contacted and an emergency vehicle has been dispatched.
    
    2. Give them the location of the medical emergency (e.g., “in the parking lot on the south side of 1200 N. Electric Way”). If the emergency happens inside the Training Center or Ministry Conference Center, tell them which entrance to use and the room in which it is occurring. Have someone meet them at that entrance to let them inside the building.
    
    3. Argos Security will meet the emergency response team at the gate and guide them to the location of the incident.
    
3. Report the incident through to a Training assistant brother to notify them of the medical emergency once the emergency has been dealt with.
    
4. Stay with the person until help arrives unless you need to meet Argos Security at an entrance.

### 2. Major Earthquake:

1. Drop immediately — drop to the ground where you are.
    
2. Cover — take cover by getting under a sturdy desk or table. At a minimum, cover your head.
    
3. Hold — hold on to something sturdy and stay where you are until the shaking stops.
    
4. Exit the structure; do not collect your belongings. Get a head count of all the people in your residence. Do not allow anyone to leave, with the exception of those designated by the Training with specific functions.
    
5. Turn off the natural gas if you smell a leak, hear a leak, or see a leak.
    
6. Check the structural integrity of the house. If the house is structurally sound, get the necessary emergency supplies (e.g., food, water, blankets, flashlights).
    
7. Contact a Training assistant brother for further instructions. If you are unable to reach a Training assistant brother, with all the members of the house, head to your assigned assembly location unless otherwise directed by emergency personnel.
    
8. At your assigned assembly location, get a second head count of all the people in your residence and wait for further instructions by emergency personnel or from the Training.

### 3. Fire:

1. Exit the structure; do not collect your belongings. Get a head count of all the people in your residence. Do not allow anyone to re-enter the building.
    
2. Call 911 and inform the dispatcher of the fire and if any medical assistance is needed.
    
3. Report the incident to a Training assistant brother.
    
4. Keep your distance from the house.
    
5. When Anaheim Fire or Police arrive, follow their instructions.

### 4. Flood:

1. Secure your house. Move essential items to an upper floor or higher part of the house.
    
2. Turn off utilities at the main switches or valves if instructed to do so. Disconnect electrical appliances, but do not touch electrical equipment if you are wet or standing in water.
    
3. Report the incident to a Training assistant brother.
    
4. Do not walk through moving water. Six inches of moving water can make you fall. If you have to walk in water, walk where the water is not moving. Use a stick to check the firmness of the ground in front of you.
    
5. Do not drive into flooded areas. If floodwaters rise around your car, abandon the car and move to higher ground if you can do so safely. You and the vehicle can be quickly swept away. Six inches of water will reach the bottom of most passenger cars, causing loss of control and possible stalling. A foot of water will float many vehicles. Two feet of rushing water can carry away most vehicles, including sport utility vehicles and pick-up trucks. Stay together as a house and wait for further instructions by emergency personnel or from the Training.

### 5. Active Assailant Situation (GHT):

1. Quickly determine the most reasonable way to protect your own life, regardless of whether you are directly involved and/or can hear gunshots in the immediate vicinity:
    1. Get out — take the quickest accessible escape path out of the premises, including through windows. Do not wait for validation; you may or may not receive oral (e.g., public address system announcement) or written (e.g., text message) communication from the Training. Leave your belongings behind. Run in a zigzag pattern, if necessary. Close doors behind you if they will become an impediment to the active assailant but not to the other evacuees. Communicate while running; help others to evacuate and prevent them from entering an area where the active assailant may be, if possible. Do not attempt to move wounded people when doing so might further endanger yourself, the wounded, or others in a hideout area. Find a safe area outside, making sure you are not being followed. When it is safe to do so, call 911, and then report the incident or mark yourself safe in the Active Defender app.
        
    2. Hide out — proceed to the nearest room out of the active assailant’s view that can be locked and barricaded. Your hiding place should not trap you or restrict your options for movement. Close, lock, and thoroughly barricade the doors. Turn off the interior lights. Close the blinds, being sure not to leave any gaps. Turn off any source of noise. Keep yourself out of sight and away from any potential lines of sight. Spread out and seek protective cover (not just concealment) behind heavy furniture. If in a group, try to hide in different places. If it is safe to do so, call 911. Even if you are unable to talk, law enforcement will be able to track your location. Remain calm and quiet until law enforcement arrives. Adopt the survival mindset; re-evaluate an evacuation and/or ready yourself to take action. Do not open the door or respond to fire alarms or voice commands until you can verify with certainty that they are being issued by law enforcement. When it is safe to do so, then report the incident or mark yourself safe in the Active Defender app.
        
    3. Take out — as a last resort and only when the active assailant is at close range and your life is in imminent danger. Charge, swarm, and overpower in a group, if possible. Adopt the survival mindset; disrupt and incapacitate the active assailant by active physical aggression, throwing items, improvising weapons, yelling, and committing to your actions. Disarm, take down, and hold the active assailant until law enforcement arrives. Do not keep/hold the weapon as you may be perceived as a threat by law enforcement. When it is safe to do so, have someone not directly restraining the assailant call 911, and then report the incident or mark yourself safe in the Active Defender app.
    
2. How to respond when law enforcement arrives on the scene:
    1. Remain calm and follow the instructions of the officers precisely.
        
    2. Put down any items in your hands. Immediately raise your hands with your fingers spread. Keep your hands visible at all times.
        
    3. Avoid making sudden movements toward the officers, including attempting to hold on to them for safety. Avoid pointing, screaming, and yelling. Do not approach the officers for help or impede their movement while you are being evacuated.
        
    4. Evacuate in the direction from which officers are entering the premises.
    
3. Information you should provide to law enforcement or the 911 operator:
    1. Your name and current location.
        
    2. Number, last known location(s), physical description(s), weapons, direction of travel, and identity of active assailants).
        
    3. Location and description of any suspicious or known improvised explosive devices.
        
    4. Number and location of potential victims.
    
4. Do not leave the safe area until directly instructed to do so by law enforcement or Training staff. Do not re-enter the scene, such as to retrieve belongings, even when the situation is under control.
