# The Vision and the Ministry of the Age (3) — Continuing in the Vision of the Age and in the Content of the Vision of the Age (21-09)

**Scripture Reading:** [[Proverbs-29#v18|Prov. 29:18a]]; [[Acts-26#v19|Acts 26:19]] (4-13); [[1 Timothy-01#v3|1 Tim. 1:3]]; [[1 Timothy-06#v3|6:3]]; [[1 Timothy-03#v15|3:15-16]]; [[Acts-20#v29|Acts 20:29]]; [[2 Timothy-01#v15|2 Tim. 1:15]], [[2 Timothy-01#v13|13]], [[2 Timothy-01#v14|14]]

## I. We must have a vision that matches the age and continue in it:

### A. The vision we have received is the ultimate consummation of the divine revelations in the entire Bible from Genesis to Revelation—[[Revelation-22#v18|Rev. 22:18-19]].

### B. This vision that the Lord has given to His recovery is an all-inclusive one that includes the economy of God, the mingling of God and man, the dispensing of the Divine Trinity, the believers' salvation in Christ, and the New Jerusalem—[[Acts-20#v27|Acts 20:27]]; [[Colossians-01#v25|Col. 1:25-26]]; [[Ephesians-01#v10|Eph. 1:10]], [[Ephesians-01#v5|5]], [[Ephesians-01#v7|7]], [[Ephesians-01#v11|11]], [[Ephesians-01#v13|13-14]]; [[Ephesians-03#v17|3:17a]]; [[Romans-05#v10|Rom. 5:10]]; [[Revelation-21#v2|Rev. 21:2]].

## II. According to the Bible, a servant of the Lord must be governed by a vision; from the day the Lord met Paul and called him while on the way to Damascus, he became a man with a vision; throughout the Bible we see that the ones used by the Lord were governed by a vision that they had seen; a vision is a scene that God unfolds to man:

### A. We must be clear that in every age there is the vision of that age; we all have to serve God according to this vision.

### B. Before Adam fell, when he was first created, God showed him a clear yet relatively simple vision by placing him in front of two trees in the garden of Eden: the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil; after he fell, God showed him that the seed of the woman would bruise the head of the serpent and would inflict upon him a death blow; in Adam is also seen God's redemption.

### C. In Abel is seen God's way of redemption.

### D. In Enosh is seen man's realization that he is a frail and empty being needing reality, which is just God Himself, and to gain this reality man needed to call upon Him to enjoy His riches.

### E. In Enoch is seen a redeemed one walking with God on the pathway of redemption.

### F. In Noah is seen one who walked with God and worked with God to build the ark to meet the need of that generation.

### G. In Abraham is seen God's calling, God's promise, justification by faith, living by faith, and the living in fellowship with God.

### H. In Isaac is seen the inheriting of grace, all that Abraham possessed, and the rest and enjoyment of the good land.

### I. In Jacob is seen God's selection, the transformation in life, and the maturity in life.

### J. In Joseph is seen the reigning aspects of the maturity in life and God using him to manage the food supply in Egypt and bless the entire earth with this supply.

### K. In Moses is seen the tabernacle and the ordinances regarding the offerings and other matters.

### L. In Joshua is seen his leading the Israelites into Canaan, their inheriting the good land, and his dividing it unto them for their inheritance.

### M. In Samuel is seen one who lived according to the vision he saw; through him the age was changed from the confused age of the judges to the age of the kingdom; Saul was anointed king by Samuel, yet Saul did not live in the vision; another person who inherited the vision from Samuel was David; David was a man living under the vision in his age.

### N. Beginning from the reign of Solomon, he and his descendants gradually departed from all the visions; nearly none of the kings during the age of the kings in Israel served according to a vision.

### O. Under such circumstances God raised up the prophets; these ones actually received visions; they lived and served under a vision; the prophets were called "seers."

### P. The prophets raised up by God turned the kings back from the things contrary to the visions to a service that was once again under the visions.

### Q. The last two books of the Old Testament, Zechariah and Malachi, contain rich utterances concerning the vision of Christ.

### R. In the record of the New Testament John the Baptist suddenly appeared with the vision of the age given to him by God; he served totally apart from the traditional rituals and ordinances and only according to the present vision given to him; it was a vision with a particular ministry that ushered in a new beginning; it was a pioneering and initiating ministry that brought all to Christ and His ministry.

### S. When the Lord Jesus began to minister, only He had the ministry of that age; while the Lord Jesus was fulfilling His ministry on the earth, a group of Galilean fishermen and a few others followed Him as His disciples; those who were following Him were the only ones who had inherited the visions of the previous ages and who were at the same time catching up with the vision that matched that age; the followers of Jesus were a blessed people.

### T. In the book of Acts, after the Lord's ascension, Peter and later Paul continued to serve in the ministry; although Paul had many co-workers, no one except him saw any other vision; they all served under one vision, which was the vision that Paul saw.

### U. Thirty years after Paul left, John wrote the book of Revelation; this constituted the ultimate consummation of the divine revelation; after this there is nothing more left to be said or seen.

### V. The Bible shows us clearly that in every age God only gives the vision of the age to one man.

### W. "The knowledge and discovery of divine revelation develop and advance with the ages. Today we are not in the age of Martin Luther. We are not in the age of Zinzendorf or the age of John Wesley. At the time of the Reformation in the 1520s, when Luther was raised up, anyone who wanted to serve under a vision had to join himself to Luther. In the seventeenth century, anyone who wanted to serve under a vision had to join himself to Madame Guyon. In the eighteenth century, anyone who wanted to serve under a vision had to join himself to Zinzendorf. Even John Wesley received help from Zinzendorf. In the nineteenth century, J. N. Darby took the lead among the Brethren, and the vision was with him. In the twentieth century, the vision came to us."

### X. "I can say with full confidence that the Lord's recovery is serving under the vision. There is no doubt about this" (*The Vision of the Age*, pp. 7-53).

## III. The knowledge of the content of the present vision of the age is unveiled in the holy Word:

### A. "The summarized content of the whole Scripture is God at the beginning and the New Jerusalem at the end" (*The Triune God's Revelation and His Move*, p. 2):

#### 1. The Bible begins with God: "In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth"—[[Genesis-01#v1|Gen. 1:1]]:

##### a. At the beginning of the Bible, God is mentioned.

##### b. In Hebrew *God* in [[Genesis-01#v1|Genesis 1:1]] is *Elohim*, a plural noun, but the verb *created* is singular; this reveals that God is triune.

#### 2. The concluding verse in the Bible is based upon the revelation of the New Jerusalem: "The grace of the Lord Jesus be with all the saints. Amen."—(*The Triune God's Revelation and His Move*, p. 2); [[Revelation-22#v21|Rev. 22:21]] and notes 1 and 3:

##### a. Grace is the enjoyment of the processed and consummated Triune God.

##### b. The saints are the chosen, called, redeemed, regenerated, renewed, sanctified, transformed, conformed, and ultimately glorified people of God, who participate in His economy for the fulfillment of His eternal economy.

##### c. The New Jerusalem is the fulfillment of God's eternal economy, consummated through the enjoyment of grace by the saints.

#### 3. "The entire Bible presents a picture that in the universe there is One who is. This One is God. He is triune, and He has been speaking throughout the generations. He has moved and is still moving in and according to His speaking. This move will consummate in the New Jerusalem. In its essence the Bible speaks about the Triune God and the New Jerusalem. This is simple but all-inclusive. The Bible begins with the Triune God, and it consummates with the New Jerusalem" (*The Triune God's Revelation and His Move*, p. 11).

### B. Particularly, the Scriptures reveal that God operates in His eternal economy as:

#### 1. The single, but triune, God from eternity past in the Old Testament:

##### a. To create man in His image and after His likeness for His expression—Gen. 1—2.

##### b. To call out a nation from which He could come forth as the God-man to accomplish His economy.

##### c. To indicate by way of the Old Testament types, shadows, and figures how His economy will be accomplished through Christ.

#### 2. The incarnated God in the four Gospels (*The Triune God's Revelation and His Move*, pp. 66-67):

##### a. To bring God into man, mingling God with man to make God one with man in the divine life and divine nature (but not in the Godhead) and in the human life and human nature—[[John-01#v14|John 1:14]]; [[1 Timothy-03#v16|1 Tim. 3:16]].

##### b. To express God in man through His divine attributes expressed in His human virtues.

##### c. To terminate the old creation with all its involvement.

##### d. To release the divine life into His redeemed chosen people so that He may be their life and everything.

##### e. To produce a group of redeemed persons for the church.

#### 3. The compound and inclusive God in the Acts and the Epistles (*The Triune God's Revelation and His Move*, pp. 67-68):

##### a. To designate (uplift) His humanity into the divine sonship, producing Him to be the firstborn Son of God—[[Romans-01#v4|Rom. 1:4]]; [[Acts-13#v33|Acts 13:33]].

##### b. To make Him the life-giving Spirit, the pneumatic Christ—[[1 Corinthians-15#v45|1 Cor. 15:45b]].

##### c. To regenerate all the people chosen by God, making them the children (sons) of God, members of Christ for the constitution of the organic Body of Christ—[[1 Peter-01#v3|1 Pet. 1:3]]; [[John-01#v12|John 1:12]]; [[Romans-08#v16|Rom. 8:16]]; [[Romans-12#v4|12:4-5]].

##### d. To produce the church as His expression.

#### 4. The intensified God in Revelation 1—20 (*Incarnation, Inclusion, and Intensification*, pp. 18-20):

##### a. To become the sevenfold intensified Spirit—[[Revelation-01#v4|Rev. 1:4]]; [[Revelation-04#v5|4:5]]; [[Revelation-05#v6|5:6]].

##### b. To deal with the degradation of the church.

##### c. To produce the overcomers so that the Body of Christ may be produced.

##### d. To consummate the New Jerusalem.

#### 5. The corporate God in eternity future in Revelation 21—22 in the New Jerusalem (*How to Be a Co-Worker and an Elder and How to Fulfill Their Obligations*, pp. 49-52; *Incarnation, Inclusion, and Intensification*, pp. 35-36):

##### a. As a mystical and organic constitution of Himself as the processed and consummated Triune God with His chosen, redeemed, regenerated, sanctified, renewed, transformed, built-up, conformed, and glorified people.

##### b. As His bride, His wife, and His counterpart.

##### c. As the tabernacle as His eternal dwelling place and the temple as an eternal dwelling place for His glorified saints—[[Revelation-21#v3|Rev. 21:3]], [[Revelation-21#v22|22b]].

##### d. As the eternal expansion and enlargement of the processed and consummated Triune God in the regenerated, transformed, and glorified humanity.

### C. Thus, the central revelation of God and the Lord's recovery is God becoming the flesh, the flesh becoming the life-giving Spirit, and the life-giving Spirit becoming the sevenfold intensified Spirit to build up the church that becomes the Body of Christ and consummates the New Jerusalem (*The Divine and Mystical Realm*, pp. 17-18).

### D. Simply put, in order for us to serve God today, our vision must extend all the way from the first vision of Adam in Genesis to the ultimate vision of the manifestation of the church, the New Jerusalem. This and this alone is the complete vision. It is not until today that this vision has been fully opened to us (*The Vision of the Age*, p. 48).

### E. From the time of the apostles until today, for two thousand years, all these servants of the Lord who serve according to the revelation of the Bible serve according to the vision; this is the standard and the basis of our service.

### F. Since we have the up-to-date and ultimate vision, we should closely follow after it. We are absolutely not following a man; rather, we are following a vision. It is grossly wrong to say that we are following a certain person. We are following a vision that belongs to the present age. It is God's consummate vision (*The Vision of the Age*, p. 49).
