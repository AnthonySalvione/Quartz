# Consecration (2): Consecration and the Channel of God (7-03)

## I. The definition of consecration:

### A. Our consent to God's working in us and on us.

### B. Our consent to God's using us.

### C. Our consent to God's directing our ways.

## II. The basis of consecration—God's purchase—[[1 Corinthians-06#v19|1 Cor. 6:19-20]]:

### A. True consecration rests on the realization of God's right to us, based on His purchase.

### B. The stability and security of our consecration.

## III. The motive of consecration—[[2 Corinthians-05#v14|2 Cor. 5:14-15]]; [[Romans-12#v1|Rom. 12:1]]:

### A. The motive of consecration refers to one's heart in consecration.

### B. Consecrating ourselves willingly to God because our feelings have been touched by the love of God.

### C. Constrained by the love of God to have a consecration full of sweetness and intensity.

## IV. The meaning of consecration—to be a sacrifice—[[Romans-12#v1|Rom. 12:1]]; [[Numbers-28#v2|Num. 28:2-3]]:

### A. Set apart for God and laid on the altar.

### B. A change in position and in use.

### C. Offering ourselves to God as a living sacrifice for God's satisfaction.

### D. Not compelled by God but by an act of voluntary will.

### E. Being restricted by God's will and imprisoned in His hand.

## V. The purpose of consecration—to be used by God and to work for God—[[Ephesians-02#v10|Eph. 2:10]]; [[Isaiah-64#v8|Isa. 64:8]]:

### A. Letting God work in us and then working for God.

### B. The object of consecration: letting God work in us in order that we might reach the stage of working for God.

## VI. The result of consecration—to abandon our future—[[Leviticus-01#v9|Lev. 1:9]]:

### A. Abandoning our hopes and belonging wholly to God.

### B. Living purely and simply in the hand of God.

### C. Being what God wants us to be and doing what God wants us to do.

## VII. God's central work is to work Himself into His chosen and redeemed people—[[Ephesians-03#v16|Eph. 3:16-17a]]:

### A. Spiritual progress consists in allowing God to gain ground within us.

### B. God desires to put Himself into us so that His life can flow through us and out of us.

### C. The crucial question is whether we will allow God to work Himself into us.

## VIII. God is seeking man to be His channel, His way, on earth:

### A. Who is willing to be the Lord's channel today?

### B. Who is willing to be His way to match Him and to be of the same mind with Him?

### C. Who is willing to move with Him and work with Him?

## IX. A believer's genuine consecration is to allow God to come into him and work in him:

### A. Consecration is to let God pass through us.

### B. The great God desires to pass through man as His channel and issue out of man as His way.

## X. A prayer of consecration to be the channel of God:

### A. O Lord, here I am; You can do whatever You want.

### B. Lord, no matter what You want me to do and no matter what You want to do in me, I am willing.

### C. Lord, pass through me and gain my entire being.

### D. I have no conditions, no choices, no views of my own, and no future of my own; all I have is in You.

### E. Lord, You are my choice, my view, and my future.

### F. I desire only what You desire; I choose only what You choose; I would do only what You would do.

### G. Lord, my being here is Your being here.

### H. Lord, You are in me, and I am in You; You and I are one.

## Excerpts from the Ministry:

### [[2 Samuel-11#v4|2 Sam. 11:4]]:

After all the enemies of Israel were subdued and David was exalted as the king of Israel, David committed a great sin while he was in a peaceful situation. This indicates that whenever we are at ease in a peaceful situation, it is easy for us to be seduced to indulge our flesh (cf. [[1 Peter-04#v1|1 Pet. 4:1]] and note 4). David's sin was the issue of his indulging of the lust of the eyes and the lust of the flesh ([[1 Peter-04#v2|vv. 2b-3]]). David, abusing the power of his kingship ([[1 Peter-04#v5|vv. 4-5]]), committed willful adultery by robbery. After committing such an act, David tried to cover his evil deed by means of a pretense ([[1 Peter-04#v6|vv. 6-13]]). Then he murdered Uriah, his faithful servant, by conspiring with Joab, so that he could take Uriah's wife ([[1 Peter-04#v14|vv. 14-25]]; [[1 Corinthians-12#v9|12:9]]). David's behavior here was utterly different from that of Joseph, who ran and escaped from adultery ([[Genesis-39#v1|Gen. 39:1-12]]), and Boaz, who kept himself clean from the indulgence of the flesh of sin ([[Ruth-01#v3|Ruth 3]]). By his one sin David broke the last five of the Ten Commandments ([[Exodus-20#v13|Exo. 20:13-17]]). David's sin was a great insult and offense to God, and it nearly annulled all his attainments from the past. David, a man according to God's heart ([[1 Samuel-13#v14|1 Sam. 13:14]]), was perfect in every way, but he failed in the matter of the indulgence of the lust of the flesh ([[1 Kings-15#v5|1 Kings 15:5]]). In this matter David was loose, and he sacrificed his high attainment in the pursuit of God. Even though he was a person of high attainment in his spiritual pursuit, he was still able to commit such a great sin. This should be a warning to all of us.

The account of God's punishing judgment on David is written as a warning to us today ([[1 Corinthians-10#v11|1 Cor. 10:11]]). We should read this account seriously in the presence of God. This account warns us that the indulgence of the flesh is a serious thing. David was tempted simply by a glance and then he failed to restrict himself. In contacting the opposite sex, we in the Lord's recovery need to be sanctified and separated unto God. The evil concerning sex is very contagious. We must exercise our spirit to overcome our flesh and our old man. This must not be a mere teaching; it must be a practice in our daily life. All the saints, especially the young ones, should search their hearts and make a strong resolution of heart ([[Judges-05#v15|Judg. 5:15-16]]) never to go the way of the indulgence of the flesh.

Our God is living, and Christ is real. With Christ as the life-giving Spirit, everything is real, but apart from Him everything is vanity of vanities. The Spirit is moving within us in a very gentle way. We need to say, "Lord Jesus, I love You, I need You, and I receive You." If we say this, He will be our Savior and our dynamic salvation. As the pneumatic Christ, He will save us, preserve us, and protect us from the contamination of this age, so that we may keep the glory that we have gained.
