# From CWWL, *1989*, vol. 3, "The Experience and Growth in Life," pp. 129-134:

## A. After giving trainings in Taiwan on thirty points of character (see the book *Character*), Brother Lee discovered that character is the expression of something inward, that is, the matter of disposition:
### 1. Outwardly it is character, but inwardly it is disposition; to deal with the character but neglect the disposition is futile.
### 2. Disposition refers to something within our being, and character refers to something in our outward being:
#### a. Within we have our disposition, and without we have our character.
#### b. Disposition is what we are within, and character is what we express without.
#### c. The inward disposition and the outward character are the extract, the essence, of our being.
#### d. If our character and disposition were taken away, there would be nothing left to our being.

### 3. The terms *character* and *disposition* cannot be found in the New Testament, but the facts are implied in verses such as [[Matthew-16#v26|Matthew 16:26]] and [[Luke-09#v25|Luke 9:25]]:
#### a. The soul-life in [[Matthew-16#v26|Matthew 16:26]] is the self in [[Luke-09#v25|Luke 9:25]].
#### b. The soul is the life of the self, and the matters of disposition and character are very much related to both the self and the soul.

### 4. The way a turtle and a rabbit move is related to a particular thing within the nature of each animal; this inward thing is what we may call its disposition, or its makeup:
#### a. Our disposition is what we are in our makeup by birth; each of us has a particular and unique disposition.
#### b. Although our disposition is something made by God, it still needs to be dealt with by God.


## B. Character refers to our outward expression:

### 1. The Chinese equivalent to the English word *character* means an outward form that expresses the inner nature.
### 2. Thus, character is the outward form that expresses our inner being.
### 3. The disposition is always explicitly expressed in our character; character is at least a part of the expression of the disposition.
### 4. If we were born slow, quick, humble, or proud, these things will be a part of the outward expression, the character, of our person.
### 5. Character is composed of about thirty percent nature and of about seventy percent habits:
#### a. A child placed in a Chinese home will be cast into a Chinese mold, and the same child placed in a family of another nationality will resemble a person of that nationality when he grows up.
#### b. Our outward character is composed of our nature by birth and our habits by living.
#### c. To deal with our disposition is to deal with what we are inwardly, but to deal with our character is to deal with what we express outwardly, including what we are inwardly.
#### d. The intrinsic element of our outward character is our inner nature by birth.
#### e. A certain kind of training, such as military service, can change a person's outward character, but it cannot change his inward nature, or disposition.


## C. Our dealings with sins, sin, the world, and the conscience are superficial dealings, but our dealing with the disposition is the deepest dealing:

### 1. Sometimes we are simply in our disposition and not in anything sinful; however, we must realize that our fallen disposition is close to the edge of the deep well of sin and mistakes; thus, it is very easy for us to fall into this well.
### 2. Dealing with our disposition and character will safeguard us from making mistakes and committing sins; our mistakes and wrongdoings are closely related to our disposition and character.
### 3. To deal only with our habits is not sufficient because our habits are only the outward expression of our character; in the Gospels, from the time the Lord first called Peter, He took every opportunity to deal with Peter's character and disposition.
### 4. Since our disposition and character cause problems in our Christian life, we have to deal with, and take care of, or correct them, and even put them to death.
### 5. In the New Testament disposition and character are implied in such terms as *self*, *soul-life*, and *the old man*, and also the *I* in [[Galatians-02#v20|Galatians 2:20]].
### 6. Actually, to deal with our disposition means to deal with our self, our old man, our soul-life, and the "I."
### 7. The life of a Christian is not an exchanged life but a grafted life:
#### a. To live a grafted life means that two lives have been grafted, even mingled, together.
#### b. The two lives have been grafted, but both lives continue to exist; on one hand, we are finished in the death of Christ, and on the other hand, we still live in the resurrection of Christ—[[Galatians-02#v20|Gal. 2:20]].
#### c. Thus, even in eternity we will continue to live with the divine element added to, grafted to, and mingled with our being.
#### d. This does not change our God-related nature or characteristic; our nature and characteristic will remain forever, with something new and living added.
#### e. In eternity we will see and recognize all the brothers and sisters, but they will be much newer and much more living.


## D. Transformation deals mainly with our disposition, and renewal deals mainly with our character:

### 1. Both transformation and renewal simply mean to deal with our disposition and character.
### 2. A transformed person will not remain in his old disposition, and a renewed person will not remain in his old character or expression.
### 3. In dealing with our disposition and character, the divine dispensing always works to transform us, not only to correct or change us.
### 4. To transform indicates that something divine, the divine element, something of the Lord that we do not have by our habits or birth, is dispensed into us.
### 5. This element works in us to transform us; without the divine nature dispensed into us, we could have only a change but not transformation.
### 6. In order for us to experience this transformation, there is the need of the breaking of our disposition and character because our disposition and character are the greatest obstacles to God's dispensing of Himself into us and to His transforming and renewing work on us.
