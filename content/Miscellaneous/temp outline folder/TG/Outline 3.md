## I. God is one, and the basis of the oneness is the divine essence (Gk. ousia;
## Lat. essentia or substantia):
### A. God is one by virtue of the one divine essence in the Godhead
### B. The divine essence is not divided or divisible, nor is it distributed among each
### of the Three.
### C. Rather, the divine essence is held in its entirety by each of the Three, so that it
### can be said that each of the Three is "the complete God." Just as the Father,
### the Son, and the Spirit are not "parts" of God, so is it that They do not each
### possess a portion or part of the divine essence-John 16:13-15; Col. 2:9.
### D. The Bible does not speak directly of the "essence" of God, but it does refer to
### God's divine essence with its nature in some predicative ways (see note 3 in
### 1 John 1:5), that is, in ways that assert the attributes or qualities of the one
### divine essence with its nature:
#### 1. "God is Spirit," referring to the one nature of God's person-John 4:24 and
#### notes 1 and 2 there.
#### 2. "God is love,'
#### " referring to the one nature of God's essence-1 John 4:8, 16.
#### "God is light," referring to the one nature of God's expression—1 John 1:5.
### E. Because the Three of the Divine Trinity coinhere, all the divine essence with
### its divine nature is found in each of the Three of the Divine Trinity. When we
### experience any of the Three, we experience all the divine nature of all the
### divine essence in the sense of its completeness, though certainly not in the
### sense of its totality. We can be partakers of the divine nature, which refers to
### the basis of the oneness in the Divine Trinity-2 Pet. 1:4.

## II. God is also three, and the basis of the threeness is the divine hypostases
## (Gk. hypostaseis; Lat. personae):
### A. God is three by virtue of the three divine hypostases in the Godhead. The
### Father, the Son, and the Spirit "are not three separate Gods but three hypos-
### tases of the one same undivided and indivisible God" (note 1 on 2 Cor: 13:14,
### quoting Philip Schaff).
### B. Person has been and can be used casually to refer to each of the Three of the
### Divine Trinity;

#### 1. It is less appropriate than the more technical hypostasis because the
#### former implies separate personality with exclusive attributes for each of
#### the Three, which is not according to the divine revelation, while the latter
#### is neutral in this respect.
#### 2.
#### "We may use the word persons to speak of the Three of the Triune God
#### However, we must be careful not to press this word too far. W. H. Griffith
#### Thomas recognized the danger of this. In The Principles of Theology he said
#### this concerning the Trinity: "The term person is also sometimes objected to.
#### Like all human language, it is liable to be accused of inadequacy and even
#### positive error. It certainly must not be pressed too far or it will lead to
#### tritheism.? Those who press the term persons too far will end up not merely
#### with the three Persons of the Godhead, but with three Gods. Eventually,
#### what they have is tritheism instead of the Trinity" (LS/ Philippians, pp. 44-
#### 45).
### C. Hypostases, the plural of hypostasis, has been used in historical theology and
### among us in the Lord's recovery to refer to the distinct Three of the Divine
### Trinity-the Father, the Son, and the Spirit:
#### 1. Hypostasis is a transliterated Greek word which originally meant 'underly-
#### ing support or substance'. "Moreover, it can be translated confirmation,
#### reality, essence (which denotes the real nature of things, as opposed to the
#### appearance), foundation, or supporting ground" (note 2 on Heb. 11:1, where
#### the word is translated substantiation.)
#### 2. The Greek word is composed of hypo, meaning 'under', and stasis, meaning
#### 'standing. It is cognate with the Latin substantia, composed of sub,
#### meaning 'under', and stans, meaning 'standing.
#### 3. Historically, in the 3d and 4" centuries AD, there was some confusion
#### between the Latin-speaking and Greek-speaking churches because the
#### Greek word was used to define the threeness of the Trinity, but the Latin
#### word was used to define the oneness of the Trinity (see I. above). Eventu-
#### ally, it was agreed that, though the terms were linguistically cognate, they
#### would not be used to refer to the same thing in their respective languages.
#### 4. When we in the Lord's recovery casually use substance in the sense of the
#### threeness, we are doing so with reference to its Greek cognate hypostasis,
#### not with respect to its common English (and Latin) meaning. For example,
#### in Hymn 608, line two of stanza one reads: "In person three, in substance
#### all are one." Here person is used in the sense of the Greek hypostasis, and
#### substance is used with its common English and Latin meaning. A proposed
#### change to this line reads: "In substance three, in essence all are one." This
#### casual reference to three substances reflects the relationship between the
#### cognate words substance and hypostasis. We may say that in "substance"
#### (as the cognate of hypostasis) God is three insofar as there are three eter-
#### nally distinct and never separate hypostases in the Godhead, but there is
#### one substance (in the English and Latin meaning of the term) in the
#### Godhead insofar as there is one essence in Him.

### D. The classical formula used to capture both aspects of truth regarding the
### Divine Trinity is "one essence in three hypostases"; though not very palatable,
### it is extremely accurate and precisely according to the divine revelation.

## II. The bases of the distinctions in the Divine Trinity have been expressed in
## various ways, both historically and among us in the Lord's recovery:
### A. The Lord's own declaration of the distinctions among the Three of His Divine
### Trinity-the Father, the Son, and the Spirit-should serve as the foundation of
### all expressions concerning the distinctions-Matt. 28:19.
### B. Paul's triune blessing to the Corinthian believers, applying the distinctions
### among the Three of the Divine Trinity, should serve as the guide for all
### expressions concerning the distinctions—2 Cor. 13:14:
#### 1. "This verse is strong proof that the trinity of the Godhead is not for the
#### doctrinal understanding of systematic theology but for the dispensing of
#### God Himself in His trinity into His chosen and redeemed people. In the
#### Bible the Trinity is never revealed merely as a doctrine. It is always
#### revealed or mentioned in regard to the relationship of God with His crea-
#### tures, especially with man, who was created by Him, and more particularly
#### with His chosen and redeemed people" (note 1 in 2 Cor. 13:14).
#### 2. The various bases of the distinctions in the Divine Trinity found in the
#### Scriptures respect the revelation of the divine dispensing
### C. From the terms Father, Son, and Spirit we should perceive that the Three:
#### 1. Are organic, as the terms themselves are organic labels.
#### 2. Are eternal and interdependent, as the terms themselves are relational
#### labels, and each must be understood to refer to eternal existence if the
#### relations are to be ascribed to God
#### 3. Exist in a relationship of dispensing among Themselves, as the terms
#### themselves imply the dispensing of the divine essence in and among the
#### Three-John 16:15, 13.
### D. Historically, the basis of the distinctions in the Divine Trinity has been
### understood to be:
#### 1. The Father as the Begetter-John 1:14; Acts 13:33; Heb. 1:5; 5:5.
#### 2. The Son as the Begotten—John 1:14, 18; 3:16, 18; 1 John 4:9.
#### 3. The Spirit as some ill-defined third Equal. His distinctiveness was not
#### clearly understood nor formally defined:
##### a. Augustine (d. 430) and Aquinas (d. 1274) understood the Spirit as the
##### love between the Father and the Son.
##### b. Though this is not wrong, it does not fully capture the distinctiveness of
##### the Spirit in relation to the Father's begetting and the Son's being
##### begotten. Rather, perhaps in this historical formulation the Spirit can
##### be understood to be the essence between Father and Son in the Divine
##### Trinity-John 4:24.
### E. Among us and based upon the ministry materials, the bases of the distinctions
### in the Divine Trinity have been expressed variously (Note: The following list is
### exemplary, not exhaustive):

#### 1. In its most basic expression, the basis of the distinctions is the Father as
#### the source, the Son as the expression, and the Spirit as the transmission:
##### a. This is seen clearly in 2 Corinthians 13:14, where the Three are
##### expressly mentioned (see note 1 there).
##### b. It is also seen in 1 John 1:3, where the Father and the Son are
##### expressly mentioned and the Spirit is implied in the word fellowship
##### (see note 4 there).
##### c. This basis of the distinctions in the Divine Trinity applies to all other
##### bases below.
#### 2. In the full economy of God's eternal purpose, the basis of the distinctions in
#### the Divine Trinity is:
##### a. The Father as the Purposer, who selected and predestinated-Eph. 1:3-6.
##### b. The Son as the Accomplisher, who redeemed-Eph. 1:7-12.
##### c. The Spirit as the Applier, who sealed and serves as pledge-Eph. 1:13-
##### 14.
#### 3. In the enjoyment of the believers for the mutual satisfaction of God and
#### man, the basis of the distinctions in the Divine Trinity is the Father as the
#### fountain of living water, the Son as the spring gushing up, and the Spirit as
#### the river or flow into the New Jerusalem as the destination of the flowing
#### Triune God-John 4:14.
#### 4. In the Body of Christ as the organism of the Triune God, the bases of the
#### distinctions in the Divine Trinity are:
##### a. The Father as the Originator, the Son as the Creator or Accomplisher
##### (Eph. 2:14-16), and the Spirit as the Executor (Eph. 4:3) of the oneness
##### of the Body.
##### b. The Father as the source (Eph. 4:6), the Son as the element, and the
##### Spirit as the essence for the intrinsic constitution of the Body-Eph.
##### 4:4-6:
###### (1) The source refers to the Father's impartation of His divine life and
###### nature into the believers for their constitution as the Body of
###### Christ.
###### (2) The element refers to the sphere of existence and identity of the
###### Body. Christ as the divine element of the Body is He in whom the
###### Body exists and He who bears the identity of the Body. The Body is
###### of Christ, not of the Father or of the Spirit per se, and Christ is the
###### Head of the Body. The Body expresses Him as the expression of the
###### Triune God.
###### (3) The essence refers to the intrinsic divine substance of the Body.
###### The Spirit is the essence with the divine nature and within the
###### divine element (Christ) that makes the Body a divine organism, the
###### organism of the Triune God.
###### (4) We may use the illustration of an orange to distinguish between
###### element and essence:
###### (a) The element of the orange is the orange itself. Only the full
###### fruit can be identified and called an orange.

###### (b) Within the orange as the element, there is an essence that can
###### be extracted and makes the orange different from a grapefruit
###### or lemon. The essence is not the fruit itself but is intrinsic to
###### the fruit and necessary for the existence of the fruit. Without
###### the essence, the pulp of a fruit could easily be confused as
###### being either orange, (small grapefruit, or (large) lemon.
#### 5. In the New Jerusalem as the eternal mutual dwelling place of God and
#### man, the consummation of the eternal economy of God, the bases of the
#### distinctions in the Divine Trinity are:
##### a. The Father as the divine base and way (the gold base with its street—
##### Rev. 21:18b, 21b), the Son as the divine entry and universal availability
##### (the gates with its availability-Rev. 21:12b-13, 21a), and the Spirit as
##### the divine transformation, appearance, and building (the wall with its
##### foundations-Rev. 21:12a, 14, 15b, 17-20) for the structure of the eter-
##### nal mutual dwelling place.
##### b. God as the center and administration (the throne and the street), the
##### Son as the provision (the tree of life), and the Spirit as the flowing forth
##### (the river of water of life) for the supply of the eternal mutual dwelling
##### place-Rev. 22:1-2.
##### c. God as the light, the Son (the Lamb) as the lamp, and the Spirit im-
##### plied as the essence of the light (the oil in the lamp) for the illumina-
##### tion of the eternal mutual dwelling place-Rev. 21:23-24.
##### d. The Father as the reigning center (signified by the throne-Rev. 22:1),
##### the Son as the abiding place (signified by the temple-Rev. 21:22), and
##### the Spirit as the enlightening and shining light (signified by the oil
##### implied in the lamp-Rev. 21:24) for the furnishings of the eternal
##### mutual dwelling place.
