# Judges-17 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Judges-17|Judges 17]]

## Footnotes for verse 3

### Footnote 3.1 — idol

**Verse:** [[Judges-17#v3|Judges 17:3]]

Micah’s mother offered something to God, but her offering to God was mixed with the leaven of idolatry (cf. [[Matthew-13#v33|Matt. 13:33]] and notes).

## Footnotes for verse 5

### Footnote 5.1 — house

**Verse:** [[Judges-17#v5|Judges 17:5]]

The house of Micah as a house of gods, with its idols (as replacements of Christ), its ephod (representing the authority of God), and its hired priest (representing the clergy-laity system—[[Judges-17#v7|vv. 7-13]]; see [note 6¹](<Revelation-02.fn#Footnote 6.1 — Nicolaitans>) in [[Revelation-02|Rev. 2]]), portrays the chaotic situation related to the worship of God among Christians today.

### Footnote 5.2 — consecrated

**Verse:** [[Judges-17#v5|Judges 17:5]]

Lit., filled the hands of. So also in [[Judges-17#v12|v. 12]].

## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — no

**Verse:** [[Judges-17#v6|Judges 17:6]]

In their degradation Israel became chaotic in three ways: in government, in worship, and in morality. Although God’s tabernacle was at Shiloh ([[Judges-18#v31|18:31]]) and the high priest had the Urim and Thummim (see [note 30¹](<Exodus-28.fn#Footnote 30.1 — judgment>) in [[Exodus-28|Exo. 28]]), there was no government, no administration, in Israel because Israel had annulled God and His status as their King (cf. [[1 Samuel-08|1 Sam. 8]]). Therefore, the children of Israel did what was right in their own eyes, and as a result they became rotten and corrupted. See [note 1¹](<Judges-01.fn#Footnote 1.1 — And>) in [[Judges-01|Judges 1]].
