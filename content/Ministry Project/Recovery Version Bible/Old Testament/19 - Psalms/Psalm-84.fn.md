# Psalm-84 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Psalm-84|Psalm 84]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — lovely

**Verse:** [[Psalm-84#v1|Psalm 84:1]]

This psalm, concerning the psalmist’s love for the house of God with Christ, follows the psalms on the stripping of God’s seekers and the desolation of God’s house, beginning with [[Psalm-73|Psa. 73]]. In the recovery and restoration ([[Psalm-80|Psa. 80]]) the loveliness and sweetness of God’s house is intensified. 

The intrinsic content of [[Psalm-84|Psa. 84]] is the secret revelation concerning the enjoyment of Christ as the incarnated Triune God, the God-man. The center of this secret revelation is the house of God ([[Psalm-84#v4|vv. 4]], [[Psalm-84#v10|10]]), typified by the tabernacle ([[Exodus-40#v2|Exo. 40:2-8]]) and the temple ([[1 Kings-06#v1|1 Kings 6:1-3]]; [[1 Kings-08#v3|8:3-11]]). Christ as the embodiment of the Triune God ([[Colossians-02#v9|Col. 2:9]]) is the fulfillment of the types of the tabernacle and the temple. This fulfillment commenced in His incarnation as the individual Christ ([[John-01#v14|John 1:14]]; [[John-02#v21|2:21]]) and will continue until it consummates in the New Jerusalem as the corporate Christ, the great God-man ([[Revelation-21#v2|Rev. 21:2-3]], [[Revelation-21#v22|22]]). The New Testament, from Matthew through Revelation, covers the entire span of the incarnation of the Triune God and is a record of the divine incarnation. The enjoyment of Christ as the incarnated Triune God in God’s house is portrayed by the arrangement of the tabernacle and its furnishings (see [note 3¹](<Psalm-84.fn#Footnote 3.1 — two>) in this chapter and notes [4¹](<Hebrews-09.fn#Footnote 4.1 — Having>) and [4³](<Hebrews-09.fn#Footnote 4.3 — pot>) in [[Hebrews-09|Heb. 9]]).

## Footnotes for verse 3

### Footnote 3.1 — two

**Verse:** [[Psalm-84#v3|Psalm 84:3]]

The bronze altar for the sacrifices and the golden altar of incense. The two altars signify the leading consummations of the work of the incarnated Triune God, who is Christ as the embodiment of God for His increase. The mentioning of these two altars together in [[Exodus-40#v5|Exo. 40:5-6]] indicates that they are closely related in our spiritual experience. At the bronze altar, a type of the cross of Christ, our problems before God are solved through the crucified Christ as the sacrifices. This qualifies us to enter into the tabernacle, a type of Christ as the incarnated and enterable Triune God, and to contact God at the incense altar. At the golden altar of incense in front of the Holy of Holies (see [note 4¹](<Hebrews-09.fn#Footnote 4.1 — Having>) in [[Hebrews-09|Heb. 9]]), the resurrected Christ in His ascension is the incense for us to be accepted by God in peace. Through our prayer at the incense altar we enter into the Holy of Holies—our spirit ([[Hebrews-10#v19|Heb. 10:19]])—where we experience Christ as the Ark of the Testimony with its contents. Through such an experience of Christ we are incorporated into the tabernacle, the incarnated Triune God, to become a part of the corporate Christ ([[1 Corinthians-12#v12|1 Cor. 12:12]]) as God’s testimony for His manifestation. See notes [33¹](<Exodus-16.fn#Footnote 33.1 — pot>) in [[Exodus-16|Exo. 16]] and [33¹](<Jeremiah-31.fn#Footnote 33.1 — law>), par. 2, in [[Jeremiah-31|Jer. 31]].

### Footnote 3.2 — sparrow

**Verse:** [[Psalm-84#v3|Psalm 84:3]]

Signifying the believers, who are small and frail.

### Footnote 3.3 — home

**Verse:** [[Psalm-84#v3|Psalm 84:3]]

A home is a place of rest, and a nest is a place of refuge. Through the two altars God’s redeemed can find a nest as their refuge and a home with God in rest. The cross of Christ, typified by the bronze altar, is our “nest,” our refuge, where we are saved from our troubles and where we “lay” our young, i.e., produce new believers through the preaching of the gospel. When we experience the resurrected Christ in His ascension, typified by the golden altar of incense, we are accepted by God in such a Christ and find a home, a place of rest, in the house of God. This house is the processed and consummated Triune God united, mingled, and incorporated with all His redeemed, regenerated, and transformed elect ([[John-14#v1|John 14:1-23]]) to be the Body of Christ in the present age and the New Jerusalem as the mutual dwelling place of God and His redeemed in eternity ([[Revelation-21#v3|Rev. 21:3]], [[Revelation-21#v22|22]]).

## Footnotes for verse 4

### Footnote 4.1 — house

**Verse:** [[Psalm-84#v4|Psalm 84:4]]

In type, the house is the church as a totality ([[1 Timothy-03#v15|1 Tim. 3:15]]), and the tabernacles ([[Psalm-84#v1|v. 1]]) are the local churches ([[Revelation-01#v11|Rev. 1:11]]).

## Footnotes for verse 5

### Footnote 5.1 — highways

**Verse:** [[Psalm-84#v5|Psalm 84:5]]

The highways to Zion signify our intention to enter into the church as the house of God and to seek the incarnated Triune God in His consummations, typified by the furniture in the tabernacle (see [note 3¹](<Psalm-84.fn#Footnote 3.1 — two>) in this chapter and note [4³](<Hebrews-09.fn#Footnote 4.3 — pot>) in [[Hebrews-09|Heb. 9]]). On the one hand, we have entered into God; on the other hand, we are still on the highways to enter into God. That the highways are in our heart means that we need to take the way of the church internally, not merely externally.

## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — Baca

**Verse:** [[Psalm-84#v6|Psalm 84:6]]

Meaning weeping. On the one hand, those on the highways to Zion are strengthened in God ([[Psalm-84#v5|v. 5]]); on the other hand, they are opposed by Satan, who causes them to suffer persecution. The trouble and persecution caused by Satan can make the highway a valley of weeping. This special term indicates that the psalmist had been disciplined by God and had been stripped by Him (see notes [26¹](<Psalm-73.fn#Footnote 26.1 — portion>) in [[Psalm-73|Psa. 73]] and [1¹](<Job-03.fn#Footnote 1.1 — cursed>) in [[Job-03|Job 3]]).

### Footnote 6.2 — spring

**Verse:** [[Psalm-84#v6|Psalm 84:6]]

When we pass through the valley of Baca, God makes this valley a spring (cf. [[Colossians-01#v24|Col. 1:24]]; [[Hebrews-10#v34|Heb. 10:34]]). This spring is the Spirit ([[John-04#v14|John 4:14]]; [[John-07#v38|7:38-39]]). The more we weep on the highways to Zion, the more we receive the Spirit. While we are weeping, we are being filled with the Spirit, and the Spirit becomes our spring.

### Footnote 6.3 — early

**Verse:** [[Psalm-84#v6|Psalm 84:6]]

Those who come into the church life by passing through the valley of weeping find that this weeping eventually becomes a great blessing to them. This blessing is the Spirit ([[Galatians-03#v14|Gal. 3:14]]). The tears they shed are their own, but these tears issue in a spring, which becomes the early rain, the Spirit as the blessing.
