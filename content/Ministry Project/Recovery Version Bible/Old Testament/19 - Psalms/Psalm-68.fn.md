# Psalm-68 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Psalm-68|Psalm 68]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — Let

**Verse:** [[Psalm-68#v1|Psalm 68:1]]

A quotation of Moses’ prayer in [[Numbers-10#v35|Num. 10:35]], uttered when the Ark of the Covenant set out from Mount Sinai. [[Psalm-68|Psalm 68]] is the highest peak of the divine revelation concerning Christ in all the Psalms. It is written in poetry, with types and signs, portraying the steps in God’s move on the earth in Christ. The Ark (typifying Christ—[[Exodus-25#v10|Exo. 25:10-22]] and notes) was the center of the tabernacle (also typifying Christ—[[John-01#v14|John 1:14]]). God’s move in the tabernacle with the Ark among Israel, His elect, from Sinai to Zion typifies the Triune God’s move in Christ as His all-inclusive embodiment from Christ’s incarnation to His ascension. In the type, God’s move was from Sinai ([[Psalm-68#v8|vv. 8b]], [[Psalm-68#v17|17b]]), beginning with the decreeing of the law and the erecting of the tabernacle, which signifies the incarnated Christ, through the wilderness ([[Psalm-68#v4|vv. 4b]], [[Psalm-68#v7|7b]]), which signifies the earth, to Mount Zion ([[Psalm-68#v16|v. 16]]), God’s dwelling place, which signifies God’s dwelling place in the heavens ([[Revelation-14#v1|Rev. 14:1]]). In the reality, God’s move in Christ and through Christ for the accomplishing of His New Testament economy began with Christ’s incarnation, which was the setting up of the real and living tabernacle in whom God dwelt and through whom He could move on earth; continued through Christ’s human living on the earth with His crucifixion and resurrection; and concluded with Christ’s ascension to Zion in the heavens. This unique move of God is portrayed in [[Psalm-68#v1|vv. 1-18]] of this psalm. See [note 8¹](<Ephesians-04.fn#Footnote 8.1 — height>) in [[Ephesians-04|Eph. 4]].

### Footnote 13.2 — dove

**Verse:** [[Psalm-68#v13|Psalm 68:13]]

The dove signifies the Spirit ([[Matthew-03#v16|Matt. 3:16]]), and the wings signify the moving power of the Spirit; silver signifies Christ in His all-inclusive redemption for His believers’ justification as the entrance into His full salvation; pinions (the feathers at the end of a bird’s wings that supply the strength to fly and soar) signify the soaring power of the Spirit; and gold signifies God in His divine nature. Here the gold is greenish-yellow, green signifying the divine life and yellow, the divine glory. Hence, greenish-yellow gold signifies God in His nature glittering in His life and glory. The contents of the above items, as the spoil ([[Psalm-68#v12|v. 12]]) gained by Christ in His victory for the enjoyment of God’s elect, are actually the Triune God—the Spirit, Christ, and God the Father—with all the items of His complete, full, and all-inclusive salvation.

## Footnotes for verse 27

### Footnote 27.1 — Benjamin

**Verse:** [[Psalm-68#v27|Psalm 68:27]]

In the praise rendered to God by His elect in [[Psalm-68#v24|vv. 24-28]], a scenery in typology is portrayed concerning God’s New Testament economy in the accomplishing of God’s redemption by Christ for God’s salvation and in the spreading of the glad tidings of Christ’s accomplishment with the goodly words of the gospel. Benjamin, mentioned here, had two names, the first of which was Ben-oni. This name, given to him by his mother Rachel as she was dying in childbirth, means son of my affliction ([[Genesis-35#v18|Gen. 35:18a]]). As the son of affliction, Benjamin typifies Christ, who, as the man of sorrows ([[Isaiah-53#v3|Isa. 53:3]]) in His incarnation and human life on earth, accomplished God’s eternal redemption for His full salvation ([[Hebrews-09#v12|Heb. 9:12]]). Whereas Rachel named her son Ben-oni, Jacob immediately changed the child’s name to Benjamin, which means son of the right hand ([[Genesis-35#v18|Gen. 35:18b]]). To be at the right hand is to be in a position of glory and honor. As the son of the right hand, Benjamin typifies Christ, who, as the Son of the right hand in His resurrection, victory, and ascension, ministers in the heavens to carry out the application of God’s redemption for His salvation ([[Hebrews-08#v1|Heb. 8:1-2]]; [[Hebrews-07#v25|7:25]]). Christ was incarnated to be Ben-oni, the man of sorrows, but in resurrection He became Benjamin, the Son of the right hand in glory and honor ([[Psalm-80#v17|80:17]]; [[Mark-16#v19|Mark 16:19]]; [[Acts-02#v33|Acts 2:33]]; [[Acts-05#v31|5:31]]).

### Footnote 27.2 — Judah

**Verse:** [[Psalm-68#v27|Psalm 68:27]]

Judah is the lion with the power and the scepter, and he is the peace (Shiloh) to God’s people ([[Revelation-05#v5|Rev. 5:5a]]; [[Genesis-49#v8|Gen. 49:8-10]]). Whereas Benjamin typifies Christ mainly in His humanity, Judah typifies Christ mainly in His divinity. In His divinity Christ is not a man of sorrows but a lion with power and authority (signified by the scepter). In particular, Judah typifies Christ as the victory for God’s people and the peace to God’s people. In the application of redemption, Christ is our peace ([[Ephesians-02#v14|Eph. 2:14-15]]). 

Judah, the kingly tribe, was accompanied always by Benjamin, a warrior tribe ([[Genesis-49#v27|Gen. 49:27]]), for God’s kingdom on the earth. In typology Judah and Benjamin, who were joined geographically, form a group for the accomplishment and application of Christ’s redemption for God’s salvation. With Benjamin the emphasis is on the accomplishment of redemption; with Judah the emphasis is on the application (in Christ’s ascension) of redemption.

### Footnote 27.3 — Zebulun

**Verse:** [[Psalm-68#v27|Psalm 68:27]]

Dwelling at the shore of the sea (Galilee) and being a shore for ships ([[Genesis-49#v13|Gen. 49:13]]), Zebulun typifies Christ as the “shore” of the evangelists for the transportation and spreading in the preaching of God’s gospel. After Christ accomplished all the things that are to be proclaimed as the gospel, on the day of Pentecost at least 120 gospel “ships,” all of whom were Galileans ([[Acts-02#v7|Acts 2:7]]; [[Acts-13#v31|13:31]]), set out from the “shore” to spread the gospel. See [note 13¹](<Genesis-49.fn#Footnote 13.1 — ships>) in [[Genesis-49|Gen. 49]].

### Footnote 27.4 — Naphtali

**Verse:** [[Psalm-68#v27|Psalm 68:27]]

According to [[Genesis-49#v21|Gen. 49:21]] (see note there), Naphtali is a hind let loose, and he gives beautiful words. Naphtali typifies Christ as the One who is released from death in resurrection, signified by the hind let loose ([[Psalm-22|Psa. 22]], title; [[Song-02#v8|S.S. 2:8-9]]), and gives beautiful words for the preaching of His gospel ([[Matthew-28#v18|Matt. 28:18-20]]). 

In typology Zebulun and Naphtali form a group for the spreading and the propagating of the glad tidings of Christ’s redemption, accomplished for God’s salvation. The people of both Zebulun and Naphtali were men of Galilee ([[Matthew-04#v12|Matt. 4:12-17]]; [[Acts-01#v11|Acts 1:11]]), from whom the gospel of Christ has been spread, preached, and propagated.
