### Footnote 1.1 — law

**Verse:** [[Psalm-119#v1|Psalm 119:1]]

The word law is used twenty-five times in this psalm ([[Psalm-119#v1|vv. 1]], [[Psalm-119#v18|18]], etc.). A number of different synonyms for law are also used, including testimony (once, in [[Psalm-119#v88|v. 88]]), testimonies (twenty-two times, in [[Psalm-119#v2|vv. 2]], [[Psalm-119#v14|14]], etc.), word (thirty-six times, in [[Psalm-119#v9|vv. 9]], [[Psalm-119#v11|11]], etc.), words (six times, in [[Psalm-119#v57|vv. 57]], [[Psalm-119#v103|103]], etc.; cf. [[Exodus-34#v28|Exo. 34:28]], lit.), commandment (once, in [[Psalm-119#v96|v. 96]]), commandments (twenty-one times, in [[Psalm-119#v6|vv. 6]], [[Psalm-119#v10|10]], etc.), statutes (twenty-two times, in [[Psalm-119#v5|vv. 5]], [[Psalm-119#v8|8]], etc.), ordinances (seventeen times, in [[Psalm-119#v7|vv. 7]], [[Psalm-119#v13|13]], etc.), judgments (three times, in [[Psalm-119#v75|vv. 75]], [[Psalm-119#v120|120]], [[Psalm-119#v137|137]]), and precepts (twenty-one times, in [[Psalm-119#v4|vv. 4]], [[Psalm-119#v15|15]], etc.). All these terms from law to precepts consummate in the way (four times, in [[Psalm-119#v14|vv. 14]], [[Psalm-119#v27|27]], [[Psalm-119#v32|32]], [[Psalm-119#v33|33]]) or ways (three times, in [[Psalm-119#v3|vv. 3]], [[Psalm-119#v15|15]], [[Psalm-119#v37|37]]), signifying Christ as the way of God for His people ([[John-14#v6|John 14:6]]). [[Psalm-119|Psalm 119]] is a psalm of 176 verses describing Christ, who is the reality of the law, the commandments, the ordinances, the statutes, the precepts, and the judgments.

### Footnote 2.2 — seek

**Verse:** [[Psalm-119#v2|Psalm 119:2]]

There are two kinds of people in relation to the law: (1) the letter-keepers, illustrated by the Judaizers and Saul of Tarsus ([[Philippians-03#v6|Phil. 3:6b]]), and (2) the God-seekers, illustrated by the psalmists, especially by the writer of this psalm, and by the apostle Paul ([[2 Corinthians-03#v6|2 Cor. 3:6]]). As a lover of God, the psalmist sought God with all his heart, loved God’s name and remembered it ([[Psalm-119#v132|vv. 132]], [[Psalm-119#v55|55]]), sought God’s favor by entreating His countenance ([[Psalm-119#v58|v. 58]]), asked God to cause His face to shine upon him ([[Psalm-119#v135|v. 135]]), walked in God’s presence ([[Psalm-119#v168|v. 168]]), considered God’s law to be His living and loving word breathed out of His mouth ([[Psalm-119#v13|vv. 13]], [[Psalm-119#v72|72]], [[Psalm-119#v88|88]]; cf. [[2 Timothy-03#v16|2 Tim. 3:16]]), tasted God’s word and found it sweeter than honey to his mouth ([[Psalm-119#v103|v. 103]]), esteemed God’s word as more precious than fine gold ([[Psalm-119#v127|v. 127]]), and considered God’s word a lamp to his feet and a light to his path ([[Psalm-119#v105|v. 105]]).

## Footnotes for verse 9

### Footnote 9.1 — word

**Verse:** [[Psalm-119#v9|Psalm 119:9]]

Christ is the reality of the law in the sense of its being not only the testimony of God but also the word of God. In total, He is the Word of God ([[John-01#v1|John 1:1]]; [[Revelation-19#v13|Rev. 19:13b]]). The words of this psalm are the written words of God, but Christ is the living Word of God breathed out by God ([[2 Timothy-03#v16|2 Tim. 3:16a]]). The written words are the letters, but the living Word is the Spirit ([[John-06#v63|John 6:63]]; [[Ephesians-06#v17|Eph. 6:17]]), who is the reality of the letters. The law is the person of Christ, and the person of Christ is the Spirit ([[1 Corinthians-15#v45|1 Cor. 15:45]]; [[2 Corinthians-03#v17|2 Cor. 3:17]]). The Spirit is the reality of whatever God is ([[John-16#v13|John 16:13]]; [[1 John-05#v6|1 John 5:6]]). Hence, as the Spirit, Christ is the reality of the law.

## Footnotes for verse 15

### Footnote 15.1 — muse

**Verse:** [[Psalm-119#v15|Psalm 119:15]]

Rich in meaning, the Hebrew word for muse (often translated meditate in the KJV) implies to worship, to converse with oneself, and to speak aloud. To muse on the word is to taste and enjoy it through careful considering. Prayer, speaking to oneself, and praising the Lord may also be included in musing on the word. To muse on the word of God is to enjoy His word as His breath ([[2 Timothy-03#v16|2 Tim. 3:16]]) and thus to be infused with God, to breathe God in, and to receive spiritual nourishment.

## Footnotes for verse 50

### Footnote 50.1 — enlivened

**Verse:** [[Psalm-119#v50|Psalm 119:50]]

There are two aspects of the law—the aspect of the letter and the aspect of the Spirit. “The letter kills, but the Spirit gives life” ([[2 Corinthians-03#v6|2 Cor. 3:6]]). If our attitude in coming to the law is to care only for the commandments in letters, we will have the law in the aspect of the killing letter. However, if we take every part of the law—all the commandments, ordinances, statutes, precepts, and judgments—as the word breathed out by the God whom we love, we will have the law in the aspect of the life-giving Spirit. 

The function of the law also has two aspects. On the negative side, the law exposes man’s sin ([[Romans-03#v20|Rom. 3:20b]]; [[Romans-07#v7|7:7b]]) and subdues sinners before God ([[Romans-03#v19|Rom. 3:19]]). It also guards God’s chosen people in its custody that they might be conducted to Christ ([[Galatians-03#v23|Gal. 3:23-24]]). On the positive side, as God’s living testimony, the law functions to minister the living God to His seekers ([[Psalm-119#v2|vv. 2]], [[Psalm-119#v88|88]]), and as God’s living word, the law functions to dispense God Himself as life and light into those who love the law ([[Psalm-119#v25|vv. 25]], [[Psalm-119#v116|116]], [[Psalm-119#v130|130]]). See [note 8¹](<Exodus-19.fn#Footnote 8.1 — All>), par. 3, in [[Exodus-19|Exo. 19]].
