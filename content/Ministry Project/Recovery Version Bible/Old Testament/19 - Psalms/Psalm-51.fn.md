# Psalm-51 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Psalm-51|Psalm 51]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — Be

**Verse:** [[Psalm-51#v1|Psalm 51:1]]

Or, Have mercy on me. See [note 1¹](<Psalm-49.fn#Footnote 1.1 — Hear>) in [[Psalm-49|Psa. 49]].

### Footnote 1.2 — blot

**Verse:** [[Psalm-51#v1|Psalm 51:1]]

In [[Psalm-51#v3|vv. 3-5]] and [[Psalm-51#v17|17]] David repented and confessed his sins to God. The verbs used by David—blot out ([[Psalm-51#v1|vv. 1]], [[Psalm-51#v9|9]]), wash ([[Psalm-51#v2|vv. 2]], [[Psalm-51#v7|7]]), cleanse ([[Psalm-51#v2|v. 2]]), and purge ([[Psalm-51#v7|v. 7]])—indicate that his repentance and confession were thorough and that his asking for forgiveness was genuine.

## Footnotes for verse 5

### Footnote 5.1 — brought

**Verse:** [[Psalm-51#v5|Psalm 51:5]]

To confess, as David did here, that we were born in sin indicates that we have no trust in ourselves (cf. [[Romans-07#v18|Rom. 7:18]]; [[Philippians-03#v3|Phil. 3:3]]). Realizing that we are sinful and God is holy, we trust only in Him. Also, we realize that we need Christ to be our Mediator and our sacrifice (see [note 7¹](<Psalm-51.fn#Footnote 7.1 — hyssop>)).

## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — inward

**Verse:** [[Psalm-51#v6|Psalm 51:6]]

The inward parts are the parts of the human soul—the mind, the emotion, and the will. The hidden part is the human spirit, which is hidden within the soul. See [note 4¹](<1 Peter-03.fn#Footnote 4.1 — hidden>) in [[1 Peter-03|1 Pet. 3]].

## Footnotes for verse 7

### Footnote 7.1 — hyssop

**Verse:** [[Psalm-51#v7|Psalm 51:7]]

Hyssop typifies Christ in His humble and humiliated human nature ([[1 Kings-04#v33|1 Kings 4:33a]]; [[Exodus-12#v22|Exo. 12:22a]]; [[Philippians-02#v5|Phil. 2:5-8]]). Here it implies Christ as the Mediator ([[1 Timothy-02#v5|1 Tim. 2:5]]; [[1 John-02#v1|1 John 2:1]]) and the sacrifice ([[Hebrews-10#v5|Heb. 10:5]], [[Hebrews-10#v10|10]]).

## Footnotes for verse 11

### Footnote 11.1 — presence

**Verse:** [[Psalm-51#v11|Psalm 51:11]]

God’s presence is actually the Spirit of His holiness. If we lose God’s presence, we lose everything.

### Footnote 11.2 — Spirit

**Verse:** [[Psalm-51#v11|Psalm 51:11]]

The title the Spirit of holiness used here and in [[Isaiah-63#v10|Isa. 63:10-11]] is not the same as the Holy Spirit used in the New Testament. The Spirit of holiness in the Old Testament is for God to make His people holy by separating them unto Himself. The Holy Spirit at the initiation of the New Testament age was for God’s incarnation, in which God in His divine nature was imparted into humanity and mingled with the human nature (without forming a third nature) to produce a God-man who was intrinsically holy ([[Matthew-01#v18|Matt. 1:18]]; [[Luke-01#v35|Luke 1:35]]). See notes [15²](<Luke-01.fn#Footnote 15.2 — Holy>), [35²](<Luke-01.fn#Footnote 35.2 — overshadow>), and [35³](<Luke-01.fn#Footnote 35.3 — holy>) in [[Luke-01|Luke 1]].

## Footnotes for verse 18

### Footnote 18.1 — good

**Verse:** [[Psalm-51#v18|Psalm 51:18]]

[[Psalm-51#v18|Verses 18-19]] signify the participation in the enjoyment of God in the local church as God’s house, God’s dwelling, and in the universal church as God’s city, God’s kingdom, through the all-inclusive Christ as the offerings. If we are those who repent, confess our sins, and ask God for His purging ([[1 John-01#v9|1 John 1:9]]), we will have the enjoyment of God in Christ in His house, the local church, and in His city, the universal church. This enjoyment, as the “good” mentioned here, includes God’s building up the church, His filling the church with His glory, and His granting the church His rich presence with Himself as joy, peace, life, light, security, and every spiritual blessing (cf. [[Ephesians-01#v3|Eph. 1:3]]).
