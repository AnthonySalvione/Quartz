# 2 Samuel-11 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[2 Samuel-11|2 Samuel 11]]

## Footnotes for verse 4

### Footnote 4.1 — lay

**Verse:** [[2 Samuel-11#v4|2 Samuel 11:4]]

After all the enemies of Israel were subdued and David was exalted as the king of Israel, David committed a great sin while he was in a peaceful situation. This indicates that whenever we are at ease in a peaceful situation, it is easy for us to be seduced to indulge our flesh (cf. [[1 Peter-04#v1|1 Pet. 4:1]] and [note 4](<1 Peter-04.fn#Footnote 1.4 — suffered>)). David’s sin was the issue of his indulging of the lust of the eyes and the lust of the flesh ([[2 Samuel-11#v2|vv. 2b-3]]). David, abusing the power of his kingship ([[2 Samuel-11#v4|vv. 4-5]]), committed willful adultery by robbery. After committing such an act, David tried to cover his evil deed by means of a pretense ([[2 Samuel-11#v6|vv. 6-13]]). Then he murdered Uriah, his faithful servant, by conspiring with Joab, so that he could take Uriah’s wife ([[2 Samuel-11#v14|vv. 14-25]]; [[2 Samuel-12#v9|12:9]]). David’s behavior here was utterly different from that of Joseph, who ran and escaped from adultery ([[Genesis-39#v1|Gen. 39:1-12]]), and Boaz, who kept himself clean from the indulgence of the flesh of sin ([[Ruth-03|Ruth 3]]). By his one sin David broke the last five of the Ten Commandments ([[Exodus-20#v13|Exo. 20:13-17]]). David’s sin was a great insult and offense to God, and it nearly annulled all his attainments from the past. 

David, a man according to God’s heart ([[1 Samuel-13#v14|1 Sam. 13:14]]), was perfect in every way, but he failed in the matter of the indulgence of the lust of the flesh ([[1 Kings-15#v5|1 Kings 15:5]]). In this matter David was loose, and he sacrificed his high attainment in the pursuit of God. Even though he was a person of high attainment in his spiritual pursuit, he was still able to commit such a great sin. This should be a warning to all of us.
