## Footnotes for verse 9

### Footnote 9.1 — law

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v9|Leviticus 6:9]]

The laws of the offerings are the ordinances and regulations regarding the offerings, i.e., regarding the enjoyment of Christ as the offerings. Since the reality of the offerings is Christ, the laws of the offerings correspond to the law of the life of Christ, which is the law of the Spirit of life ([[Romans-08#v2|Rom. 8:2]]). These laws indicate that even in the enjoyment of Christ we should not be lawless but should be regulated by the law of life (cf. [[1 Corinthians-09#v26|1 Cor. 9:26-27]]; [[1 Corinthians-11#v17|11:17]], [[1 Corinthians-11#v27|27-29]]; [[Galatians-06#v15|Gal. 6:15-16]]; [[Philippians-03#v13|Phil. 3:13-16]]).

### Footnote 9.2 — hearth

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v9|Leviticus 6:9]]

The burnt offering being on the hearth signifies that anything offered as a burnt offering must be put on the place of offering to be burned. Those who offer themselves to God as a burnt offering must be on the place of burning and must be willing to become a heap of ashes.

### Footnote 9.3 — all

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v9|Leviticus 6:9]]

All night until the morning signifies that a burnt offering should remain in the place of burning through the dark night of this age until the morning, until the Lord Jesus comes again ([[2 Peter-01#v19|2 Pet. 1:19]]; [[Malachi-04#v2|Mal. 4:2]]).

### Footnote 9.4 — kept

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v9|Leviticus 6:9]]

The continual burning of the fire on the altar ([[Leviticus-06#v9|vv. 9b]], [[Leviticus-06#v12|12a]], [[Leviticus-06#v13|13]]) signifies that God as the holy fire in the universe ([[Hebrews-12#v29|Heb. 12:29]]) is always ready to receive (burn) what is offered to Him as food, and that God’s desire to accept what is offered to Him never ceases.

## Footnotes for verse 10

### Footnote 10.1 — ashes

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v10|Leviticus 6:10]]

The ashes, the result of the burnt offering, are a sign of God’s acceptance of the offering. The priest’s putting on linen garments ([[Leviticus-06#v10|v. 10]]) signifies that fineness, purity, and cleanness are needed in handling the ashes. His putting on other garments to carry the ashes outside the camp ([[Leviticus-06#v11|v. 11]]) signifies that the handling of the ashes of the burnt offering was done in a stately manner. 

Ashes indicate the result of Christ’s death, which brings us to an end, i.e., to ashes ([[Galatians-02#v20|Gal. 2:20a]]). The putting of the ashes beside the altar toward the east ([[Leviticus-01#v16|1:16]]), the side of the sunrise, is an allusion to resurrection. In relation to the burnt offering, the ashes are not the end, for Christ’s death brings in resurrection ([[Romans-06#v3|Rom. 6:3-5]]; [[2 Corinthians-04#v10|2 Cor. 4:10-12]]; [[Philippians-03#v10|Phil. 3:10-11]]). God has a high regard for these ashes, for eventually the ashes will become the New Jerusalem. Our being reduced to ashes brings us into the transformation of the Triune God ([[Romans-12#v2|Rom. 12:2]]; [[2 Corinthians-03#v18|2 Cor. 3:18]]). In resurrection we as ashes are transformed to become precious materials—gold, pearl, and precious stones—for the building of the New Jerusalem ([[Revelation-21#v18|Rev. 21:18-21]]).

## Footnotes for verse 12

### Footnote 12.1 — morning

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v12|Leviticus 6:12]]

The priest’s burning wood on the altar every morning signifies the need of the serving one’s cooperation with God’s desire (see [note 9⁴](<Leviticus-06.fn#Footnote 9.4 — kept>)) by adding more fuel to the holy fire to strengthen the burning for the receiving of the burnt offering as God’s food (cf. [[Romans-12#v11|Rom. 12:11]]; [[2 Timothy-01#v6|2 Tim. 1:6]]). The morning signifies a new start for the burning.

### Footnote 12.2 — burnt

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v12|Leviticus 6:12]]

See [note 5¹](<Leviticus-03.fn#Footnote 5.1 — burnt>) in [[Leviticus-03|Leviticus 3]]. The burning of the burnt offering laid a foundation for the sweetness of the peace offering. This indicates that our offering ourselves to God as a continual burnt offering (cf. [[Romans-12#v1|Rom. 12:1]]) should be laid as a foundation for our sweet fellowship with God, signified by the burning of the fat of the peace offering. The burning of both the burnt offering and the peace offering signifies that both our absoluteness for God and our enjoyment of the Triune God should be a matter of burning.

## Footnotes for verse 16

### Footnote 16.1 — Aaron

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v16|Leviticus 6:16]]

The meal offering is not common food. It is food only for the priests, i.e., only for those believers in the church life who are actual and practical priests, serving God in the priesthood of the gospel ([[Romans-01#v9|Rom. 1:9]]; [[Romans-15#v16|15:16]]; [[1 Peter-02#v9|1 Pet. 2:9]]).

### Footnote 16.2 — without

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v16|Leviticus 6:16]]

Eating the priests’ portion of the meal offering without leaven in a holy place signifies that we enjoy Christ as the life supply for our service without sin (leaven) in a separated, sanctified realm. Since the Tent of Meeting typifies the church (see [note 1³](<Leviticus-01.fn#Footnote 1.3 — Tent>) in [[Leviticus-01|Leviticus 1]]), eating the meal offering in the court of the Tent of Meeting signifies that Christ should be enjoyed as our life supply in the sphere of the church life.

## Footnotes for verse 17

### Footnote 17.1 — leaven

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v17|Leviticus 6:17]]

Not baking the meal offering with leaven signifies that our laboring on Christ to partake of Him as our life supply must be without sin.

### Footnote 17.2 — sin

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v17|Leviticus 6:17]]

The law of the meal offering refers us here to the sin offering and the trespass offering, signifying that if we would enjoy Christ as our life supply, we need to deal with the sin in our fallen nature and with the sins (trespasses) in our conduct. See [note 3⁴](<Leviticus-04.fn#Footnote 3.4 — sin>), par. 1, in [[Leviticus-04|Leviticus 4]].

## Footnotes for verse 18

### Footnote 18.1 — male

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v18|Leviticus 6:18]]

Those who partake of Christ as the life supply should be strong in the divine life (males) and also should be God’s serving ones, God’s priests (sons of Aaron).

## Footnotes for verse 20

### Footnote 20.1 — day

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v20|Leviticus 6:20]]

The offering of a meal offering by Aaron and his sons on the day when Aaron was anointed signifies that the enjoyment of Christ as the life supply is related to the priestly service. Cf. [note 35¹](<Leviticus-07.fn#Footnote 35.1 — anointing>) in [[Leviticus-07|Leviticus 7]].

### Footnote 20.2 — tenth

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v20|Leviticus 6:20]]

The tenth of an ephah of fine flour for a continual meal offering, half in the morning and half in the evening, signifies that the top portion, the tenth part, of the enjoyment of Christ should be for God, and that this kind of enjoyment of Christ should continue in our priestly service.

## Footnotes for verse 25

### Footnote 25.1 — burnt

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v25|Leviticus 6:25]]

The slaughtering of the sin offering before Jehovah in the place where the burnt offering was slaughtered signifies (1) that Christ as our sin offering was slain before God and (2) that Christ is the sin offering for us based on His being the burnt offering. Christ must be the burnt offering for God’s satisfaction that He might be qualified to be our sin offering, and we must enjoy Christ as our burnt offering, as the One who is absolutely for God, before we can realize how sinful we are, i.e., how much we are for ourselves and not for God.

### Footnote 25.2 — most

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v25|Leviticus 6:25]]

The sin offering being most holy signifies that Christ as our sin offering was most holy in that He dealt with sin in our nature intrinsically and entirely.

## Footnotes for verse 26

### Footnote 26.1 — priest

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v26|Leviticus 6:26]]

That the priest who offered the sin offering ate it in a holy place, in the court of the Tent of Meeting, signifies that the one who serves sinners with Christ as their sin offering enjoys Christ as the sin offering in a separated, sanctified realm, in the sphere of the church (cf. [note 16²](<Leviticus-06.fn#Footnote 16.2 — without>)).

## Footnotes for verse 27

### Footnote 27.1 — holy

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v27|Leviticus 6:27]]

This signifies that whoever touches Christ as the sin offering is separated and sanctified (holy) and will forsake sin and have his flesh dealt with, for Christ as the sin offering has dealt with sin and our sinful flesh on the cross ([[Romans-08#v3|Rom. 8:3]]).

### Footnote 27.2 — wash

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v27|Leviticus 6:27]]

This signifies that the daily walk (signified by the garment—[[Isaiah-64#v6|Isa. 64:6a]]) of the one who has received redemption through the blood of Christ as the sin offering should be dealt with in a separated, sanctified realm (cf. [[Ephesians-04#v22|Eph. 4:22-24]]). We should have regard for the blood of Christ and should never consider it common (cf. [[Hebrews-10#v29|Heb. 10:29]]).

## Footnotes for verse 28

### Footnote 28.1 — broken

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v28|Leviticus 6:28]]

This signifies that the natural life of the one who, as an earthen vessel (cf. [[2 Corinthians-04#v7|2 Cor. 4:7]]), has a relationship with Christ as the sin offering should be broken.

### Footnote 28.2 — scoured

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v28|Leviticus 6:28]]

This signifies that the one who has been enlightened and judged by the Spirit (likened to a bronze mirror—cf. [note 18²](<Exodus-30.fn#Footnote 18.2 — bronze>) in [[Exodus-30|Exo. 30]]) to be regenerated needs not to be broken but to be dealt with by being scoured and rinsed ([[1 Corinthians-06#v11|1 Cor. 6:11]]; [[Titus-03#v5|Titus 3:5]]).

## Footnotes for verse 29

### Footnote 29.1 — male

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v29|Leviticus 6:29]]

This signifies that all the stronger ones (males) can enjoy Christ as the most holy sin offering in ministering Christ as the sin offering to sinners.

## Footnotes for verse 30

### Footnote 30.1 — expiation

**Verse:** [[Leviticus-06#v30|Leviticus 6:30]]

The expiation mentioned here was made in the Holy of Holies ([[Leviticus-16#v27|16:27]]), where God was. This verse signifies that Christ as the sin offering that dealt with our sin and with our sinful nature on the cross to accomplish God’s redemption for us is wholly for God’s enjoyment, and we have no share in it. However, in our ministering Christ as the sin offering to sinners, we can share in Him ([[Leviticus-06#v26|v. 26]]). Concerning Christ as the sin offering, both God and the serving priests have a portion, the best portion being for God’s enjoyment.
