# Jeremiah-11 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Jeremiah-11|Jeremiah 11]]

## Footnotes for verse 3

### Footnote 3.1 — covenant

**Verse:** [[Jeremiah-11#v3|Jeremiah 11:3]]

The covenant of Jehovah with Israel, called the first covenant and also the old covenant ([[Hebrews-08#v7|Heb. 8:7]], [[Hebrews-08#v13|13]]), was the covenant of the law of the Ten Commandments ([[Exodus-20#v1|Exo. 20:1]]—[[Exodus-24#v8|24:8]]). The law, which itself is holy, righteous, good, and spiritual ([[Romans-07#v12|Rom. 7:12]], [[Romans-07#v16|16]], [[Romans-07#v14|14a]]), became weak through man’s flesh ([[Romans-08#v3|Rom. 8:3a]]). The law of the Ten Commandments was given to test man and expose man as to his genuine nature and condition ([[Romans-03#v20|Rom. 3:20b]]; [[Romans-05#v20|5:20a]]; [[Romans-07#v7|7:7b]]). Through their failure under the test of the law, Israel was to learn that they were unable to keep the law and that they had no righteousness by the law ([[Romans-08#v3|Rom. 8:3a]]; [[Romans-09#v31|9:31]]; [[Romans-10#v3|10:3]]). They were also to come to know that they needed Christ to be Jehovah as their righteousness ([[Jeremiah-23#v6|23:6b]]; [[Jeremiah-33#v16|33:16b]]; [[1 Corinthians-01#v30|1 Cor. 1:30]]), and they needed to have the divine life with its law in the new covenant of God ([[Jeremiah-31#v31|31:31-34]]; [[Hebrews-08#v8|Heb. 8:8-12]]; [[Hebrews-10#v16|10:16-17]]).

## Footnotes for verse 10

### Footnote 10.1 — broken

**Verse:** [[Jeremiah-11#v10|Jeremiah 11:10]]

Israel broke the covenant of the law by their idolatrous worship ([[Jeremiah-11#v10|vv. 10b]], [[Jeremiah-11#v13|13]], [[Jeremiah-11#v15|15a]], [[Jeremiah-11#v17|17b]]; [[Jeremiah-13#v10|13:10b]], [[Jeremiah-13#v27|27a]]) and also by their evil conduct ([[Jeremiah-11#v8|vv. 8]], [[Jeremiah-11#v10|10a]], [[Jeremiah-11#v19|19]]; [[Jeremiah-12#v4|12:4a]]). By these two things they completely annulled the law, making it of no effect to them. See [note 1¹](<Jeremiah-05.fn#Footnote 1.1 — Go>) in [[Jeremiah-05|Jeremiah 5]].

## Footnotes for verse 11

### Footnote 11.1 — evil

**Verse:** [[Jeremiah-11#v11|Jeremiah 11:11]]

Although God’s intention in giving Israel His law was to test and expose them, He still needed to punish them for the sake of His righteousness and holiness.

## Footnotes for verse 20

### Footnote 20.1 — vengeance

**Verse:** [[Jeremiah-11#v20|Jeremiah 11:20]]

Jeremiah’s prayer for vengeance was contrary to the New Testament teaching, which tells us to love our enemies and pray for those who persecute us ([[Matthew-05#v44|Matt. 5:44]]). Cf. [note 7¹](<Psalm-03.fn#Footnote 7.1 — strike>) in [[Psalm-03|Psa. 3]].
