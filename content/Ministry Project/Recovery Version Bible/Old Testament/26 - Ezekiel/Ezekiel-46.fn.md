## Footnotes for verse 13

### Footnote 13.1 — every

**Verse:** [[Ezekiel-46#v13|Ezekiel 46:13]]

The law of Moses required that the continual daily burnt offering be offered both in the morning and in the evening ([[Numbers-28#v3|Num. 28:3-4]]), but here there is no offering in the evening. This may indicate that in the service of the priests in the millennium there is no evening; they are continually in the freshness of the morning.

## Footnotes for verse 19

### Footnote 19.1 — place

**Verse:** [[Ezekiel-46#v19|Ezekiel 46:19]]

The places in the holy chambers were for the priests to boil their offerings and to bake the meal offerings ([[Ezekiel-46#v19|vv. 19-20]]). These were for the priests, whereas the courts in [[Ezekiel-46#v21|vv. 21-24]] were for the people.

## Footnotes for verse 21

### Footnote 21.1 — court

**Verse:** [[Ezekiel-46#v21|Ezekiel 46:21]]

The four enclosed courts at the four corners of the outer court are the boiling houses for the sacrifices of the people ([[Ezekiel-46#v21|vv. 21-24]]). This indicates that, by God’s grace, the preparation of Christ for others’ enjoyment is spreading to every corner, making Christ available everywhere. The measurement of the corner chambers is forty by thirty ([[Ezekiel-46#v22|v. 22]]). The number forty signifies trials and testing ([[Matthew-04#v1|Matt. 4:1-2]]; [[Hebrews-03#v9|Heb. 3:9]]) and indicates here that apart from the Lord’s suffering, testing, and trials, He could not have been “cooked,” prepared for our enjoyment.
