# Numbers-25 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Numbers-25|Numbers 25]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — fornication

**Verse:** [[Numbers-25#v1|Numbers 25:1]]

Unable to defeat Israel militarily, politically, or through the use of religion, Balak, under the evil counsel of Balaam ([[Numbers-31#v16|31:16]]; [[Revelation-02#v14|Rev. 2:14]]), induced Israel to fall into fornication and idolatry ([[Numbers-25#v1|vv. 1-3a]]). Fornication destroys the person of the God-created man, and idolatry insults the divine person of God. According to the record of the Bible, fornication and idolatry go together ([[Acts-15#v29|Acts 15:29]]; [[1 Corinthians-10#v7|1 Cor. 10:7-8]]; [[Ephesians-05#v5|Eph. 5:5]]). 

God’s view of Israel, revealed in Balaam’s parables ([[Numbers-23|Numbers 23—24]]), is altogether positive; but according to their actual situation and condition in their adamic nature, the children of Israel were fallen and sinful, a people of fornication and idolatry. The situation is the same with the believers in Christ. On the one hand, in Christ we are a heavenly people ([[Ephesians-02#v6|Eph. 2:6]]; [[Philippians-03#v20|Phil. 3:20a]]; [[Colossians-03#v1|Col. 3:1-3]]); on the other hand, in ourselves we are a fallen and sinful people, worthy to be judged by God ([[Romans-07#v18|Rom. 7:18]]; [[Ephesians-02#v1|Eph. 2:1-3]]).

## Footnotes for verse 9

### Footnote 9.1 — plague

**Verse:** [[Numbers-25#v9|Numbers 25:9]]

The plague was a purification of God’s people, a sifting that dealt with the mixture among them (cf. [[Numbers-11#v4|11:4]]). Since the history of Israel is a complete type of the church ([[1 Corinthians-09#v24|1 Cor. 9:24]]—[[1 Corinthians-10#v11|10:11]]), the mixture among the children of Israel is a type of the mixture in the church (cf. [[Acts-04#v32|Acts 4:32]]—[[Acts-05#v11|5:11]]; [[1 Timothy-01#v20|1 Tim. 1:20]]; [[2 Timothy-02#v17|2 Tim. 2:17-21]]; [[2 TImothy-04#v10|4:10]], [[2 TImothy-04#v14|14-15]]). God uses the failure and turmoil among His people to purify them from mixture. God also uses the frustrations suffered by His chosen and redeemed people as a means to humble them and test them in their journey with Him ([[Deuteronomy-08#v2|Deut. 8:2]]). The punishment in this chapter was the last purification of the children of Israel before they entered into the good land.
