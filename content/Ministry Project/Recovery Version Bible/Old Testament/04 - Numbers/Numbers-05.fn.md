# Numbers-05 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Numbers-05|Numbers 5]]

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — leper

**Verse:** [[Numbers-05#v2|Numbers 5:2]]

The three kinds of defilement dealt with in the camp of Israel typify all the uncleanness that needs to be dealt with in the church. Leprosy signifies the evil issues of the natural man, especially in rebellion (see note 21 in [[Leviticus-13|Lev. 13]]). Spiritually, discharges are the excessive, abnormal, and unrestrained issues of the natural man, signifying one’s being without control, unbridled, in relation to one’s self, temper, preferences, likes, and dislikes (see notes 21 and 41 in [[Leviticus-15|Lev. 15]]). Uncleanness through contact with the dead signifies the defilement of spiritual deadness that issues from contacting those who are spiritually dead (see notes on [[Leviticus-11#v31|Lev. 11:31]]). God is righteous, holy, and living. Thus, these three kinds of defilement cannot be allowed to remain in God’s dwelling place, army, and priesthood.

## Footnotes for verse 7

### Footnote 7.1 — confess

**Verse:** [[Numbers-05#v7|Numbers 5:7]]

To deal with the guilt of our sinning against God is to deal with the defilement of unrighteousness, of being guilty before man and condemned before God. After making a thorough confession of our sins to God ([[1 John-01#v9|1 John 1:9]]), we should go to the one we have wronged to make restitution in full for our trespass, even adding something to what we owe ([[Luke-19#v8|Luke 19:8b]]).

## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — kinsman

**Verse:** [[Numbers-05#v8|Numbers 5:8]]

If the wronged one is deceased, then the restitution should go to his relative. If the deceased one has no relatives, the restitution becomes holy, like a holy portion given to God for the priest. In this case we may give the restitution to the church or to one who serves God as a priest and lives by faith.

### Footnote 8.2 — ram

**Verse:** [[Numbers-05#v8|Numbers 5:8]]

The ram of the expiation here typifies Christ as the propitiation for our sins ([[1 John-02#v2|1 John 2:2]]; [[1 John-04#v10|4:10]]; see note 11 in [[Leviticus-16|Lev. 16]]). In dealing with the defilement of unrighteousness, first we confess our sin to God and then go to make restitution (see note 71). Afterward, we come back to God to offer Christ as our propitiatory sacrifice. This process shows how fine and detailed God is.

## Footnotes for verse 14

### Footnote 14.1 — jealousy

**Verse:** [[Numbers-05#v14|Numbers 5:14]]

The dealing with a wife over whom her husband was jealous typifies Christ’s jealousy over His believers and His church ([[2 Corinthians-11#v2|2 Cor. 11:2-3]]). Christ’s fighting army is composed of His overcomers, who are a fighting wife to match Christ ([[Revelation-19#v7|Rev. 19:7-9]], [[Revelation-19#v11|11-14]]). In order to be a part of the Lord’s fighting army, we must be chaste toward Him. Our seeking and pursuing anything other than Christ is spiritual adultery in the eyes of God (cf. [[James-04#v4|James 4:4]]). Those who commit spiritual adultery will be judged and cursed by God ([[1 Corinthians-16#v22|1 Cor. 16:22]]) and will not be able to fight for God and serve God.

## Footnotes for verse 15

### Footnote 15.1 — priest

**Verse:** [[Numbers-05#v15|Numbers 5:15]]

The priest here may signify Christ or someone who is very close to God.

### Footnote 15.2 — meal

**Verse:** [[Numbers-05#v15|Numbers 5:15]]

The meal offering signifies Christ in His humanity offered to God that God’s people may be acceptable (see notes in [[Leviticus-02|Lev. 2]]). In this case, the meal offering was of barley meal (the resurrected Christ—see note 92, par. 2, in [[John-06|John 6]]) with no oil (the Holy Spirit) or frankincense (resurrection) upon it. Such a meal offering of jealousy brought iniquity to remembrance.

## Footnotes for verse 18

### Footnote 18.1 — hair

**Verse:** [[Numbers-05#v18|Numbers 5:18]]

The letting of the woman’s hair go loose shows that she had not subjected herself to the headship (cf. [[1 Corinthians-11#v10|1 Cor. 11:10]], [[1 Corinthians-11#v15|15]]).

## Footnotes for verse 22

### Footnote 22.1 — Amen

**Verse:** [[Numbers-05#v22|Numbers 5:22]]

A Hebrew adjective meaning firm, steadfast.

## Footnotes for verse 25

### Footnote 25.1 — wave

**Verse:** [[Numbers-05#v25|Numbers 5:25]]

The waving of the meal offering signifies resurrection (see note 241 in [[Exodus-29|Exo. 29]]). The handful of the meal offering that was burned on the altar was offered to God as a memorial portion ([[Numbers-05#v26|v. 26]]).

## Footnotes for verse 27

### Footnote 27.1 — womb

**Verse:** [[Numbers-05#v27|Numbers 5:27]]

For the womb (abdomen) to swell meant that it became abnormal. For the thigh to waste away meant that the woman’s strength was decreasing. Such a dealing was surely miraculous.
