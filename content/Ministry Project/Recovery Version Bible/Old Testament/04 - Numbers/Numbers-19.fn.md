# Numbers-19 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Numbers-19|Numbers 19]]

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — red

**Verse:** [[Numbers-19#v2|Numbers 19:2]]

The heifer, the principal component of the water for impurity ([[Numbers-19#v9|v. 9]]), signifies the redeeming Christ. The color red signifies the likeness of the flesh of sin, which is for the bearing of man’s sin outwardly. The heifer being without defect signifies that although Christ was in the likeness of the flesh of sin, He did not have the sinful nature ([[Romans-08#v3|Rom. 8:3]] and [note 3](<Romans-08.fn#Footnote 3.3 — likeness>)). That the heifer was without blemish indicates that Christ was perfect (see [note 6¹](<Exodus-12.fn#Footnote 6.1 — fourteenth>), par. 1, in [[Exodus-12|Exo. 12]]). The heifer having never been under a yoke signifies that Christ was never used by anyone, especially by or for God’s enemy, Satan (cf. [[Exodus-12#v5|Exo. 12:5]] and [note 1](<Exodus-12.fn#Footnote 5.1 — year-old>)).

## Footnotes for verse 3

### Footnote 3.1 — outside

**Verse:** [[Numbers-19#v3|Numbers 19:3]]

Christ was crucified outside the camp ([[Hebrews-13#v12|Heb. 13:12-13]]), on Calvary, a small mount outside the city of Jerusalem ([[Matthew-27#v33|Matt. 27:33]] and [note](<Matthew-27.fn#Footnote 33.1 — Golgotha>)).

## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — cedar

**Verse:** [[Numbers-19#v6|Numbers 19:6]]

Cedar wood signifies Christ in His dignified humanity, hyssop signifies Christ in His humbled humanity, and scarlet signifies redemption in its highest significance (see [note 4²](<Leviticus-14.fn#Footnote 4.2 — cedar>) in [[Leviticus-14|Lev. 14]]). The high and dignified Christ and the lowly and humbled Christ in His redemption were elements for the composition of the water for impurity ([[Numbers-19#v9|v. 9]]).

## Footnotes for verse 9

### Footnote 9.1 — ashes

**Verse:** [[Numbers-19#v9|Numbers 19:9]]

Ashes signify Christ reduced to nothing ([[Mark-09#v12|Mark 9:12]]). These ashes were kept for the water for impurity; it was a purification of sin, or a sin offering.

### Footnote 9.2 — impurity

**Verse:** [[Numbers-19#v9|Numbers 19:9]]

The impurity in this chapter does not refer to sin but to death ([[Numbers-19#v11|vv. 11]], [[Numbers-19#v13|13-16]]). Death issues from sin, and sin is the root of death ([[Romans-05#v12|Rom. 5:12]]). From the sin of rebellion ([[Numbers-11|Numbers 11—14]] and [[Numbers-16|16]]), death became prevailing among the children of Israel ([[Numbers-16#v49|16:49]]). Thus, there was the need for the water for impurity. Only the working of Christ’s redemption, through His dignified and humbled humanity, with His death and the Spirit of His resurrection ([[Numbers-19#v17|v. 17]] and [note](<Numbers-19.fn#Footnote 17.1 — running>)), could heal and cleanse the situation from the uncleanness of death.

## Footnotes for verse 12

### Footnote 12.1 — third

**Verse:** [[Numbers-19#v12|Numbers 19:12]]

In typology, the third day signifies resurrection, and the seventh day signifies completion.

## Footnotes for verse 17

### Footnote 17.1 — running

**Verse:** [[Numbers-19#v17|Numbers 19:17]]

Lit., living. The living water here signifies the Holy Spirit in the resurrection of Christ ([[John-07#v37|John 7:37-39]] and [[John-07.fn#Footnote 38.2 — rivers|notes 38²]] and [[John-07.fn#Footnote 39.1 — Spirit|39¹]]). In the water for impurity, there is the efficacy of Christ’s redemption with the washing power of the Spirit of His resurrection.
