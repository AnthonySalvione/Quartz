# Exodus-25 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Exodus-25|Exodus 25]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — Then

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v1|Exodus 25:1]]

The law of God comprises the moral law ([[Exodus-20|Exo. 20—23]]) and the ceremonial law ([[Exodus-25|Exo. 25]]—[[Leviticus-27|Lev. 27]]). The moral law, as God’s testimony based on His divine attributes, is composed of the Ten Commandments ([[Exodus-20#v2|20:2-17]]), the statutes ([[Exodus-20#v22|20:22-26]]), and the ordinances ([[Exodus-21#v1|21:1]]—[[Exodus-23#v19|23:19]]) and typifies Christ as God’s testimony, God’s expression (see [note 1¹](<Exodus-20.fn#Footnote 1.1 — words>) in [[Exodus-20|Exodus 20]]). The ceremonial law was composed of the laws of the tabernacle, the offerings, the priesthood, and the feasts. The tabernacle typifies Christ as the embodiment of God ([[Colossians-02#v9|Col. 2:9]]; [[John-01#v14|John 1:14]]) for God’s people to contact Him and to enter into Him for their enjoyment; the offerings typify Christ as all kinds of sacrifices ([[Leviticus-01|Lev. 1—7]]; [[Hebrews-10#v5|Heb. 10:5-12]]) to meet the need of God toward His people and the need of His people before Him; the priesthood typifies Christ as the High Priest ([[Hebrews-08#v1|Heb. 8:1]]), who takes care of God’s chosen people before God; and the feasts typify Christ as the bountiful enjoyment in every aspect assigned by God to His chosen people ([[Colossians-02#v16|Col. 2:16-17]]; [[Philippians-01#v19|Phil. 1:19]]). 

God knew that no man could keep the Ten Commandments, the moral section of His law, to be justified by Him ([[Romans-03#v20|Rom. 3:20]]). Hence, by His grace and according to His economy He also gave His people the ceremonial section of His law that through the priesthood and the offerings the condemned sinners, the breakers of the moral law of God, could be saved from the condemnation under the moral law and could contact God and enter into Him to enjoy Him as their everything. In this way sinners could be justified by God to be righteous men (cf. [[Matthew-01#v19|Matt. 1:19a]]; [[Luke-01#v6|Luke 1:6]], [[Luke-01#v75|75]]; [[Luke-02#v25|2:25]]; [[Luke-23#v50|23:50]]) and could have fellowship with God. 

The entire law of God was decreed by God to His people with the intention of exposing and convicting His people by the moral section of His law, that they would be conducted to the ceremonial section of His law, signifying that God’s people should be conducted to the all-inclusive Christ as the embodiment of the Triune God for their redemption, salvation, and bountiful enjoyment in every aspect ([[Galatians-03#v23|Gal. 3:23-24]]) through all the ages unto eternity.

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — heave

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v2|Exodus 25:2]]

To heave is to lift up. The heave offering typifies the uplifted Christ, the Christ who was lifted up in His ascension ([[Acts-01#v9|Acts 1:9]]); it was often accompanied by the wave offering, a type of the resurrected Christ ([[Exodus-29#v26|29:26-28]]; [[Leviticus-07#v30|Lev. 7:30-32]]; [[Numbers-18#v11|Num. 18:11]]). The fact that the materials for the building of the tabernacle were offered to God by His people as a heave offering signifies that the church is built up not with any natural materials but with the very Christ who has been gained, possessed, enjoyed, and experienced by God’s people in resurrection and in the heavenlies ([[Philippians-03#v7|Phil. 3:7-14]]; [[Ephesians-03#v8|Eph. 3:8]]; [[Ephesians-02#v5|2:5-6]]). Cf. [[1 Corinthians-03#v12|1 Cor. 3:12]] and notes. 

All the materials for the building of the tabernacle signify the virtues of Christ’s person and work. Twelve kinds of materials were used, in three categories: minerals, signifying Christ’s building life ([[1 Corinthians-03#v9|1 Cor. 3:9-12]]); plants, signifying Christ’s generating life ([[John-12#v24|John 12:24]]); and animals, signifying Christ’s redeeming life ([[John-01#v29|John 1:29]]). The redeeming life is for the generating life, and the generating life is for the building life. The fact that minerals were mentioned as the first category indicates that whatever Christ is and whatever He has done and is doing are all for God’s building ([[Matthew-16#v18|Matt. 16:18]]). In the New Jerusalem, the ultimate consummation of God’s building, there will be only minerals: gold, pearl, and precious stones ([[Revelation-21#v18|Rev. 21:18-21]]). However, in order to arrive at God’s goal, the redeeming life and the generating life are needed.

## Footnotes for verse 3

### Footnote 3.1 — gold

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v3|Exodus 25:3]]

The minerals in [[Exodus-25#v3|vv. 3-9]] signify Christ as the building material ordained and prepared by God. Gold signifies Christ’s divine nature, which is pure and everlasting. Silver ([[Exodus-26#v19|26:19]]; [[Exodus-27#v10|27:10]]) signifies Christ’s redemption, which also involves termination and replacement (see [note 24¹](<Exodus-20.fn#Footnote 24.1 — altar>) in [[Exodus-20|Exodus 20]]). Bronze ([[Exodus-27#v2|27:2-4]], [[Exodus-27#v6|6]]; [[Exodus-30#v18|30:18]]) signifies Christ’s being tested by God’s judgment ([[Numbers-16#v39|Num. 16:39]]; [[Numbers-21#v8|21:8-9]]). Onyx stone ([[Exodus-25#v7|v. 7]]; [[Exodus-28#v9|28:9-10]]), having red in it, signifies Christ’s blood shed for redemption. The other precious stones ([[Exodus-25#v7|v. 7]]; [[Exodus-28#v17|28:17-20]]) signify the different aspects of the transforming work of the Holy Spirit ([[2 Corinthians-03#v18|2 Cor. 3:18]]) based on Christ’s redemption.

## Footnotes for verse 4

### Footnote 4.1 — blue

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v4|Exodus 25:4]]

The color blue signifies that which is heavenly in both nature and appearance (cf. [[1 Corinthians-15#v47|1 Cor. 15:47-48]]). Purple denotes royalty, that which is kingly in both position and behavior ([[Esther-08#v15|Esth. 8:15]]; [[John-19#v2|John 19:2]], [[John-19#v19|19-22]]), and scarlet, a dark red color, signifies the blood of Christ shed for redemption ([[Hebrews-09#v22|Heb. 9:22]]; cf. **Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v4|Exodus 25:4]]

Fine linen ([[Exodus-26#v1|26:1]]) signifies the righteous conduct of Christ as a pure and perfect human being (cf. [[Revelation-19#v8|Rev. 19:8]]).

### Footnote 4.3 — goats’

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v4|Exodus 25:4]]

Goats signify sinners ([[Matthew-25#v33|Matt. 25:33]], [[Matthew-25#v41|41]]), and goats’ hair signifies the sins of sinners. Goats’ hair as a covering on the tabernacle ([[Exodus-26#v7|26:7]]) signifies Christ being made sin for us in His redemptive work ([[2 Corinthians-05#v21|2 Cor. 5:21]]).

## Footnotes for verse 9

### Footnote 9.1 — pattern

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v9|Exodus 25:9]]

The pattern of the tabernacle and all its furnishings is a full and complete type of both the individual Christ as the Head and the corporate Christ as the Body, the church, including many details of the experience of Christ for the church life. See [note 4³](<Hebrews-09.fn#Footnote 4.3 — pot>) in [[Hebrews-09|Heb. 9]].

### Footnote 9.2 — tabernacle

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v9|Exodus 25:9]]

The physical tabernacle (and later the temple) as God’s dwelling place in the Old Testament was actually a symbol of a corporate people, the children of Israel as the house of God (see [note 6¹](<Hebrews-03.fn#Footnote 6.1 — house>) in [[Hebrews-03|Heb. 3]]). At the beginning of the New Testament age the incarnated Christ as God’s embodiment was both the tabernacle and the temple of God ([[John-01#v14|John 1:14]]; [[John-02#v19|2:19-21]]). Through His death and resurrection the individual Christ was enlarged to be the corporate Christ, the church composed of the New Testament believers as the temple, the house of God, and the Body of Christ ([[1 Corinthians-03#v16|1 Cor. 3:16-17]]; [[1 Timothy-03#v15|1 Tim. 3:15]]; [[Hebrews-03#v6|Heb. 3:6]]; [[1 Corinthians-12#v12|1 Cor. 12:12]]). Ultimately, the tabernacle and the temple will consummate in the New Jerusalem—the Triune God mingled with His redeemed people of both the Old Testament and the New Testament—as God’s eternal dwelling place ([[Revelation-21#v3|Rev. 21:3]], [[Revelation-21#v22|22]]). See [note 15³](<Revelation-07.fn#Footnote 15.3 — will>) in [[Revelation-07|Rev. 7]] and notes [3¹](<Revelation-21.fn#Footnote 3.1 — tabernacle>), [12⁴](<Revelation-21.fn#Footnote 12.4 — Israel>), [14²](<Revelation-21.fn#Footnote 14.2 — apostles>), and [22¹](<Revelation-21.fn#Footnote 22.1 — no>) in [[Revelation-21|Rev. 21]].

## Footnotes for verse 10

### Footnote 10.1 — ark

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v10|Exodus 25:10]]

Because the law, God’s testimony (see [note 1¹](<Exodus-20.fn#Footnote 1.1 — words>) in [[Exodus-20|Exodus 20]]), was placed in the Ark ([[Exodus-25#v16|vv. 16]], [[Exodus-25#v21|21]]; [[Deuteronomy-10#v1|Deut. 10:1-5]]), the Ark was called the Ark of the Testimony ([[Exodus-25#v22|v. 22]]; [[Exodus-26#v33|26:33-34]]); and because the Ark was in the tabernacle, the tabernacle was called the Tabernacle of the Testimony ([[Exodus-38#v21|38:21]]; [[Numbers-01#v50|Num. 1:50]], [[Numbers-01#v53|53]]). As the embodiment of God’s testimony, the Ark typifies Christ as the embodiment of God ([[Colossians-02#v9|Col. 2:9]]). The Ark as a type of Christ indicates that God’s redeemed people can contact God and enjoy God in Christ and through Christ (see [note 22¹](<Exodus-25.fn#Footnote 22.1 — meet>); [[John-14#v6|John 14:6]]; [[Hebrews-10#v19|Heb. 10:19-20]]). 

As the center and content of the tabernacle, the Ark also signifies Christ as the center and content of the church as God’s tabernacle, God’s house ([[Ephesians-02#v21|Eph. 2:21-22]]; [[1 Timothy-03#v15|1 Tim. 3:15]]). The fact that the Ark is the item first mentioned in the vision of the tabernacle and its furniture indicates that it occupies the place of preeminence (cf. [[Colossians-01#v18|Col. 1:18]]). It also indicates that the church, the Body of Christ, typified by the tabernacle, comes out of Christ, typified by the Ark. See [note 15²](<Exodus-26.fn#Footnote 15.2 — acacia>) in [[Exodus-26|Exodus 26]] and note [22¹](<Genesis-02.fn#Footnote 22.1 — built>) in [[Genesis-02|Gen. 2]]. Cf. notes [8¹](<Exodus-36.fn#Footnote 8.1 — made>) in [[Exodus-36|Exodus 36]] and [3¹](<Exodus-40.fn#Footnote 3.1 — Ark>) in [[Exodus-40|Exodus 40]].

### Footnote 10.2 — acacia

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v10|Exodus 25:10]]

Acacia wood ([[Exodus-25#v5|vv. 5]], [[Exodus-25#v10|10]], [[Exodus-25#v23|23]]; [[Exodus-26#v15|26:15]]; [[Exodus-27#v1|27:1]]) signifies Christ’s human nature, strong in character and high in standard. Christ’s humanity is the basic element, the basic substance, for Him to be God’s testimony on earth. See notes [1¹](<Mark-01.fn#Footnote 1.1 — beginning>), par. 2, in [[Mark-01|Mark 1]]; [3¹](<Luke-01.fn#Footnote 3.1 — me>), par. 2, in [[Luke-01|Luke 1]]; and [9³](<Colossians-02.fn#Footnote 9.3 — bodily>) in [[Colossians-02|Col. 2]].

### Footnote 10.3 — two

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v10|Exodus 25:10]]

The measurements of the Ark are halves of the numbers three and five, the numbers of God’s building (see [note 15²](<Genesis-06.fn#Footnote 15.2 — three>) in [[Genesis-06|Gen. 6]]). This signifies that the Ark is a testimony, another half being needed to make a complete unit, a full testimony ([[Deuteronomy-17#v6|Deut. 17:6]]; [[Deuteronomy-19#v15|19:15]]; [[Matthew-18#v16|Matt. 18:16]]; [[Matthew-19#v5|19:5-6a]]). This implies that Christ, typified by the Ark, needs the church as His counterpart, His bride, to be a testimony in full in humanity ([[Ephesians-05#v22|Eph. 5:22-32]]; [[Ephesians-03#v21|3:21]] and notes).

## Footnotes for verse 11

### Footnote 11.1 — gold

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v11|Exodus 25:11]]

The Ark was made of acacia wood ([[Exodus-25#v10|v. 10]]) overlaid with gold, signifying that Christ is one person with two natures, the human nature and the divine nature. He is both God and man—a God-man. That the acacia wood was overlaid with gold both inside and outside signifies the divine nature mingled with the human nature—God and man becoming one—without a third nature being produced by the mingling. It also signifies that the divine nature penetrates the human nature and rests on the human nature so that it may be expressed through the human nature.

### Footnote 11.2 — rim

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v11|Exodus 25:11]]

Or, crown; i.e., a border in the form of a wreath as a crown. So also in [[Exodus-25#v24|vv. 24]], [[Exodus-25#v25|25]]; [[Exodus-30#v3|30:3]], [[Exodus-30#v4|4]]; and [[Exodus-37#v2|37:2]], [[Exodus-37#v11|11-12]], [[Exodus-37#v26|26-27]]. The rim of gold signifies the glory of the divine nature. Christ as God’s embodiment expresses God by showing forth His glory ([[Hebrews-01#v3|Heb. 1:3a]]; [[2 Corinthians-04#v6|2 Cor. 4:6]]). Through Christ’s human life the divine nature was expressed as a wreath, or crown, of glory. 

The glory of the divine nature as a rim also signifies the divine keeping power and holding strength. The Christ whom we live and magnify ([[Philippians-01#v20|Phil. 1:20-21a]]) becomes the glory expressed through us, and this glory is a rim that holds us and keeps us.

## Footnotes for verse 12

### Footnote 12.1 — rings

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v12|Exodus 25:12]]

The four rings and two poles ([[Exodus-25#v13|v. 13]]) were for the move of the Ark ([[Numbers-10#v33|Num. 10:33]]). The number four signifies the four corners of the earth, to which Christ as the embodiment of God’s testimony should be borne to reach all men ([[Revelation-07#v1|Rev. 7:1]]; [[Matthew-28#v19|Matt. 28:19]]; [[Acts-01#v8|Acts 1:8]]). A ring, which has no beginning or ending, signifies the eternal Spirit ([[Hebrews-09#v14|Heb. 9:14]]; [[Luke-15#v22|Luke 15:22]]). The four rings of gold signify that the Spirit of Christ is the linking factor and power for bearing Christ as God’s testimony. The casting of the gold into rings signifies the experience of the cross through which the eternal life-giving Spirit becomes the linking power in us.

### Footnote 12.2 — rings

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v12|Exodus 25:12]]

The two rings with the two poles ([[Exodus-25#v14|v. 14]]) on the two sides of the Ark signify that the move of Christ as God’s embodied testimony is through a good coordination as a testimony in every respect ([[Luke-10#v1|Luke 10:1]] and [note 2](<Luke-10.fn#Footnote 1.2 — two>)).

## Footnotes for verse 13

### Footnote 13.1 — poles

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v13|Exodus 25:13]]

The poles made of acacia wood signify that Christ’s human nature is the strength for His move as God’s testimony. Their being overlaid with gold signifies that Christ’s divine nature is the expression of His move. The move of Christ is always by His two natures, human and divine, mingled as one. See [note 11¹](<Exodus-25.fn#Footnote 11.1 — gold>).

## Footnotes for verse 14

### Footnote 14.1 — put

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v14|Exodus 25:14]]

The poles being put into the rings for carrying the Ark signifies that the move of Christ is by men bearing God’s testimony in their bodies (cf. [[2 Corinthians-04#v10|2 Cor. 4:10-12]]) in the uniting power of the Spirit ([[Ephesians-04#v3|Eph. 4:3]]). Those who bore the Ark carried it on their shoulders, indicating that they were one with the Ark. In reality, to bear the Ark is to live Christ as His testimony, His witnesses, wherever we go ([[Acts-01#v8|Acts 1:8]] and [note 3](<Acts-01.fn#Footnote 8.3 — witnesses>); [[Acts-23#v11|23:11]] and [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 11.4 — testify|note 4]]). That the two poles were to be in the rings and were not to be taken from the Ark ([[Exodus-25#v15|v. 15]]) signifies our readiness for the move of Christ as God’s testimony.

### Footnote 17.1 — expiation

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v17|Exodus 25:17]]

According to [[Revelation-08#v3|Rev. 8:3]], it is also the throne of God’s authority, the throne of the divine administration. The pure gold of which the cover was made signifies Christ’s pure divine nature. See [note 1¹](<Leviticus-16.fn#Footnote 1.1 — Then>) in [[Leviticus-16|Lev. 16]].

## Footnotes for verse 23

### Footnote 23.1 — table

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v23|Exodus 25:23]]

The table of the bread of the Presence signifies Christ as the nourishing feast for the believers as God’s priests ([[1 Peter-02#v5|1 Pet. 2:5]], [[1 Peter-02#v9|9]]; [[Revelation-01#v6|Rev. 1:6]]; [[Revelation-05#v10|5:10]]). This table was set up in the Holy Place within the tabernacle ([[Exodus-40#v22|40:22]]; [[Hebrews-09#v2|Heb. 9:2]]), signifying within, or among, God’s people as His builded habitation (see [note 15¹](<Exodus-26.fn#Footnote 15.1 — boards>) in [[Exodus-26|Exodus 26]]). In the Bible a table signifies not an individual feasting but a corporate feasting ([[1 Corinthians-10#v16|1 Cor. 10:16]], [[1 Corinthians-10#v21|21]]; cf. [[Psalm-23#v5|Psa. 23:5]]). Christ as the food of God’s priests is for a corporate feasting within God’s dwelling place. 

In the sequence of God’s revelation, the table comes after the Ark ([[Exodus-25#v10|vv. 10-22]]), implying that the table is connected to the Ark. In spiritual experience, when we meet with God upon Christ as the propitiation cover, enjoying fellowship with God and hearing words from His mouth, the Ark becomes the table of the bread of the Presence, where we enjoy a nourishing feast. This means that Christ, the embodiment of God’s testimony, issues in our enjoyment of Him. Furthermore, in our experience, our enjoyment of Christ always brings us back to Him as God’s testimony.

### Footnote 23.2 — acacia

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v23|Exodus 25:23]]

Acacia wood here signifies that Christ’s humanity is the basic element for Him to be our feast ([[John-06#v51|John 6:51]], [[John-06#v53|53-55]]). The gold that overlaid the wood signifies Christ’s divinity as the expression of God. As we enjoy Christ as the supply by which we serve God, the outcome will be gold—Christ’s divinity as the expression of God.

### Footnote 23.3 — two

## Footnotes for verse 30

### Footnote 30.1 — bread

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v30|Exodus 25:30]]

The bread on the table is called the bread of the Presence because the table was set before God, i.e., in God’s presence, not far from the Ark. Whereas manna was gathered by all the people in the wilderness outside the court of the tabernacle, the bread of God’s presence was enjoyed only by the priests in the presence of God in the Holy Place within the tabernacle ([[Exodus-40#v22|40:22-23]]; [[Hebrews-09#v2|Heb. 9:2]]; [[Leviticus-24#v9|Lev. 24:9]]). Manna typifies Christ as the life supply of God’s people for their living (see [note 19¹](<Exodus-16.fn#Footnote 19.1 — leave>) in [[Exodus-16|Exodus 16]]); the bread on the table typifies Christ as the life supply of God’s priests, enabling them not only to live but also to serve God. This bread indicates that God’s people should no longer live by themselves but by Christ as their life and life supply ([[John-06#v57|John 6:57]]). 

The bread of the Presence was the most holy of Jehovah’s offerings by fire ([[Leviticus-24#v7|Lev. 24:7]], [[Leviticus-24#v9|9]]). It signifies the surplus of the believers’ enjoyment of Christ that is offered to God to be His food for His satisfaction. God caused certain loaves of this offering to be spared, brought into the Holy Place, and arranged and displayed on the table to be food for the serving priests.

### Footnote 30.2 — Presence

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v30|Exodus 25:30]]

Or, Face. The bread of the Presence, the face-bread, means that God’s presence, God’s face, is the life supply to the serving priests (cf. [[2 Corinthians-02#v10|2 Cor. 2:10]]; [[2 Corinthians-04#v6|4:6-7]]; [[2 Corinthians-03#v18|3:18]]). In our experience the reality of God’s presence is the Spirit in our spirit ([[2 TImothy-04#v22|2 Tim. 4:22]]; cf. [[2 Corinthians-03#v17|2 Cor. 3:17]]), who is also the reality of Christ as the bread of life ([[John-06#v33|John 6:33]], [[John-06#v51|51a]], [[John-06#v63|63]]).

## Footnotes for verse 31

### Footnote 31.1 — lampstand

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v31|Exodus 25:31]]

As described in [[Exodus-25#v31|vv. 31-36]], the lampstand consisted of a base, a central stalk, and three branches on each of its two sides. Underneath each pair of branches there was a calyx, which held two branches at the stalk. On each branch there were three cups shaped like almond blossoms, each cup being composed of a calyx (the leafy green bottom of the blossom) and a blossoming bud. The whole flower, including the calyx and the blossom, was a cup shaped like an almond blossom. On the lampstand there were twenty-five calyxes—one at the base of each pair of branches, three on each of the six branches, and four on the shaft—and twenty-two blossoms (the three calyxes at the base of each pair of branches did not have blossoms). The divine thought here is that the lampstand is actually a living and growing tree with calyxes and blossoms. 

The lampstand signifies the Triune God embodied and expressed. Pure gold as the substance of the lampstand ([[Exodus-25#v31|v. 31]]) signifies God the Father in His divine nature; the form of the lampstand signifies God the Son as the embodiment of God the Father ([[John-14#v9|John 14:9-11a]]; [[2 Corinthians-04#v4|2 Cor. 4:4b]]; [[Colossians-01#v15|Col. 1:15]]; [[Colossians-02#v9|2:9]]); and the seven lamps ([[Exodus-25#v37|v. 37]]) signify God the Spirit being the seven Spirits of God for the sevenfold intensified expression of the Father in the Son ([[Revelation-04#v5|Rev. 4:5]]; [[Revelation-05#v6|5:6]]). The lampstand in this chapter signifies Christ as the embodiment and expression of the Triune God shining with the seven lamps, the seven Spirits of God ([[Colossians-02#v9|Col. 2:9]]; [[Matthew-04#v16|Matt. 4:16]]; [[John-01#v4|John 1:4-9]]); the lampstand in [[1 Kings-07#v49|1 Kings 7:49]] signifies the enlarged Christ; the lampstand in [[Zechariah-04|Zech. 4]] signifies the nation of Israel as God’s shining testimony with the sevenfold intensified life-giving Spirit as the reality of Christ ([[Zechariah-04#v2|Zech. 4:2]], [[Zechariah-04#v6|6]], [[Zechariah-04#v10|10]]; [[Revelation-05#v6|Rev. 5:6]]); and the lampstands in [[Revelation-01|Rev. 1]] signify the local churches as the reproduction of Christ and the reprint of the Spirit ([[Revelation-01#v11|Rev. 1:11-12]], [[Revelation-01#v20|20]]). The consummation and aggregate of all the lampstands in the Scriptures is the New Jerusalem, the ultimate, unique, and eternal golden lampstand, with Christ as the lamp and God as the light shining within Him and through the city for the expression of the Triune God in eternity ([[Revelation-21#v11|Rev. 21:11]], [[Revelation-21#v18|18]], [[Revelation-21#v23|23]]; [[Revelation-22#v5|22:5]]). See [note 12³](<Revelation-01.fn#Footnote 12.3 — lampstands>) in [[Revelation-01|Rev. 1]]. 

That the lampstand is revealed after the table of the bread of the Presence ([[Exodus-25#v23|vv. 23-30]]) indicates that Christ as the supply of life, signified by the table, becomes the light of life to us ([[John-01#v4|John 1:4]]; [[John-08#v12|8:12]]), signified by the lampstand. The placing of the lampstand in the Holy Place opposite the table and near the Ark ([[Exodus-26#v34|26:34-35]]) indicates that in the church the light of the truth ([[1 John-01#v5|1 John 1:5-6]]) and the supply of life must match and balance each other for the carrying on of Christ as God’s testimony. In the making and displaying of the furniture of the tabernacle, the lampstand was followed by the incense altar ([[Exodus-37#v23|37:23-25]]; [[Exodus-40#v24|40:24-27]]), indicating that the shining of Christ in resurrection as the divine light leads us to enjoy Christ as the fragrant incense of resurrection in the prayer of fellowship with God. The light from the lampstand directs us to enjoy Christ as our life supply and also guides us into the Holy of Holies to enjoy Christ in the deepest way as the testimony of God with the throne of grace (see notes [10¹](<Exodus-25.fn#Footnote 10.1 — ark>) and [17¹](<Exodus-25.fn#Footnote 17.1 — expiation>)).

### Footnote 31.2 — pure

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v31|Exodus 25:31]]

Pure gold, signifying the pure divine nature, indicates that as the embodiment of God the Father for His expression, Christ is purely divine. Although as a man Christ has humanity, His shining as the light of life ([[John-08#v12|John 8:12]]) is related not to His humanity but to His divinity ([[John-01#v1|John 1:1]], [[John-01#v4|4-5]]). The same is true of the church as the lampstand ([[Revelation-01#v20|Rev. 1:20]])—the actuality and the shining of the church depend not on humanity but on how much of the divine element has been infused and wrought into the church ([[2 Peter-01#v4|2 Pet. 1:4]]).

### Footnote 31.3 — base

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v31|Exodus 25:31]]

The base for stability and the shaft for strength signify that the Lord Jesus was always stable and strong ([[Matthew-08#v24|Matt. 8:24-26]]; [[Luke-04#v29|Luke 4:29-30]]).

### Footnote 31.4 — beaten

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v31|Exodus 25:31]]

The beating of the gold to shine forth the light signifies that Christ’s shining of the divine light as the expression of the divine glory ([[Revelation-21#v23|Rev. 21:23]]) is through sufferings (cf. [note 18¹](<Exodus-25.fn#Footnote 18.1 — cherubim>)), through which Christ was constituted as the divine light-holder to shine in God’s dwelling place so that God’s serving ones could serve there. The beating of the gold to form a stand also signifies the believers’ participation in Christ’s sufferings and their being blended together through the cross and by the Spirit for the producing and building up of the church, the Body of Christ ([[Romans-08#v17|Rom. 8:17]]; [[1 Peter-02#v21|1 Pet. 2:21]]; [[Philippians-03#v10|Phil. 3:10]]; [[Colossians-01#v24|Col. 1:24]]; [[1 Corinthians-12#v24|1 Cor. 12:24]]).

### Footnote 31.5 — cups

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v31|Exodus 25:31]]

A cup, equal to a complete flower, consists of a calyx (the outer leafy green layer of a flower) and a blossom bud, which is actually the flower itself ([[Exodus-25#v33|vv. 33-34]]). The cups shaped like almond blossoms signify the resurrection life blossoming ([[Numbers-17#v8|Num. 17:8]]). To shine the divine light is to blossom. This indicates that Christ’s being the light of life and our shining forth the light of life as the church are in resurrection (cf. [note 32¹](<Exodus-25.fn#Footnote 32.1 — six>)). The calyxes containing the blossom buds signify the resurrection life as a container to sustain and support our shining forth of the divine light. The blossom buds signify the expression of the resurrection life.

## Footnotes for verse 35

### Footnote 35.1 — calyx

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v35|Exodus 25:35]]

According to the description given in this chapter, in the divine thought the golden lampstand is actually a living and growing tree with calyxes and blossoms (see [note 31¹](<Exodus-25.fn#Footnote 31.1 — lampstand>), par. 1). Thus, the lampstand portrays the Triune God embodied in Christ as a living tree, growing, branching, budding, and blossoming to shine the light for His full expression. The calyx under each pair of branches signifies life branching out by growing to produce the shining. The repetition in this verse related to the calyxes and the branches also indicates growth. As the central shaft, or stalk, of the lampstand grows upward, it produces three pairs of branches, and as the branches grow out, calyxes, buds, and blossoms appear on the branches ([[Exodus-25#v33|v. 33]]). The light of the lampstand is the blossoming of the resurrection life ([[John-01#v4|John 1:4]]; [[Galatians-05#v22|Gal. 5:22]]; [[Ephesians-05#v9|Eph. 5:9]]), and the shining of the light is the issue of the growing, branching, budding, and blossoming. That there are twenty-five calyxes and twenty-two blossoms indicates that with the lampstand the responsibility for growth is greater than the matter of budding, blossoming, and shining.

### Footnote 35.2 — six

**Verse:** [[Exodus-25#v35|Exodus 25:35]]

Six is the number of man, since man was created on the sixth day ([[Genesis-01#v26|Gen. 1:26]]). Here the number six is composed of three plus three, three signifying the Triune God in resurrection. Hence, the six branches signify the believers in Christ as created men who are in the Triune God in resurrection. The picture of the lampstand indicates that Christ as the resurrection life is growing, branching, budding, and blossoming both in Himself as the central stalk and in His believers as His branches ([[John-15#v5|John 15:5a]]; [[Colossians-02#v19|Col. 2:19]]) to shine the divine light for the expression of the Triune God.

### Footnote 37.2 — light

[[Revelation-21#v23|Rev. 21:23]]; [[Revelation-22#v5|22:5]]). Eventually, through our enjoyment of Christ in the Holy of Holies the shining light of the lampstand is replaced by and becomes the shekinah glory of God for our closest fellowship with God ([[2 Corinthians-03#v18|2 Cor. 3:18]]; [[2 Corinthians-04#v6|4:6]]).
