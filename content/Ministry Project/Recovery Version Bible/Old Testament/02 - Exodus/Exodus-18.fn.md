# Exodus-18 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Exodus-18|Exodus 18]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — Now

**Verse:** [[Exodus-18#v1|Exodus 18:1]]

[[Exodus-18|Exodus 18]] presents a type, a portrait, of the kingdom of God, in which the tabernacle, God’s dwelling place, was built. The fact that this portrait is presented after the war against Amalek signifies that when the flesh as God’s enemy is dealt with, the kingdom with the kingship immediately comes in (cf. [[Galatians-05#v17|Gal. 5:17-25]]), and the church as the Body of Christ is built up (cf. [[1 Kings-01|1 Kings 1—8]]). In order to realize the kingdom with the building of the church, we must utterly repudiate the flesh in both its good and evil aspects (cf. [[Philippians-03#v3|Phil. 3:3-10]]). Saul lost the kingship because he did not utterly destroy Amalek but spared the best of what was to be destroyed ([[1 Samuel-15|1 Sam. 15]] and notes). 

According to historical sequence, the events described in [[Exodus-18|Exodus 18]] took place after the building of the tabernacle and not long before the children of Israel began their journey with the tabernacle toward the good land ([[Deuteronomy-01#v6|Deut. 1:6-18]]). Under divine inspiration Moses inserted these events after [[Exodus-17|Exodus 17]] to show that in the experience of God’s full salvation the kingdom comes after God’s people have been delivered from Satan (Pharaoh) and the world (Egypt), and after the flesh (Amalek) has been defeated and subdued. After the defeat of Amalek, the kingdom is needed as the sphere, the environment, for the building up of God’s dwelling place on earth.

### Footnote 1.2 — Jethro

**Verse:** [[Exodus-18#v1|Exodus 18:1]]

Jethro, a priest of Midian ([[Exodus-18#v1|vv. 1]], [[Exodus-18#v5|5]], [[Exodus-18#v10|10-12]]), represents the Gentiles who turn to God and become seekers of God in the kingdom ([[Isaiah-02#v2|Isa. 2:2-3]]; [[Zechariah-08#v20|Zech. 8:20-23]]).

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — Zipporah

**Verse:** [[Exodus-18#v2|Exodus 18:2]]

Zipporah, the Gentile wife of Moses secured by him during his period of rejection by Israel ([[Exodus-02#v13|2:13-22]]), typifies the Gentile church gained by Christ as His wife during the time of His rejection by the children of Israel ([[Romans-11#v11|Rom. 11:11-25]]; cf. [note 45²](<Genesis-41.fn#Footnote 45.2 — Asenath>) in [[Genesis-41|Gen. 41]]). When the kingdom comes, the overcomers in the church participate in the kingdom as the ruling authority in the kingdom ([[Revelation-02#v26|Rev. 2:26-27]]; [[Revelation-20#v4|20:4]], [[Revelation-20#v6|6]]).

## Footnotes for verse 21

### Footnote 21.1 — leaders

**Verse:** [[Exodus-18#v21|Exodus 18:21]]

The leaders here and in [[Deuteronomy-01#v15|Deut. 1:15]] were appointed to maintain a good order among God’s people in their daily relationships with one another in His kingdom. They are different from the seventy elders in [[Numbers-11|Num. 11]], who were appointed to take care of the relationship between God’s people and God.
