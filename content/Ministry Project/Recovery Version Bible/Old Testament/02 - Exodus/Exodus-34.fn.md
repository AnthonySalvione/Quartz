**Verse:** [[Exodus-34#v4|Exodus 34:4]]

## Footnotes for verse 10

### Footnote 10.1 — wonders

**Verse:** [[Exodus-34#v10|Exodus 34:10]]

God’s wonderful doings were for the goal of bringing His people into the good land for the building up of the temple as God’s testimony and His dwelling place on earth. Likewise, God performs wonders for us with the intention of bringing us into Christ, the reality of the good land (see note 71 in [[Deuteronomy-08|Deut. 8]]), for the building up of the church as God’s testimony and God’s temple ([[Revelation-01#v2|Rev. 1:2]]; [[1 Corinthians-03#v16|1 Cor. 3:16]]).

## Footnotes for verse 14

### Footnote 14.1 — god

**Verse:** [[Exodus-34#v14|Exodus 34:14]]

[[Exodus-34#v12|Verses 12-17]] are a warning concerning the snare of idolatry, a repetition of the first three of the Ten Commandments ([[Exodus-20#v2|20:2-7]]). An idol is any person, matter, or thing that replaces God and keeps us from the full enjoyment of Christ as our good land. God declared that He would do everything to bring the people into the good land ([[Exodus-34#v10|vv. 10-11]]), but He also pointed out the danger of idolatry, which would nullify His wondrous doings on behalf of the children of Israel. Likewise, God has promised to do everything necessary to bring us into the all-inclusive Christ for our enjoyment (cf. [[Colossians-01#v12|Col. 1:12-13]]), but we must take heed to God’s warning concerning idolatry (cf. [[1 John-05#v21|1 John 5:21]]).

## Footnotes for verse 18

### Footnote 18.1 — keep

**Verse:** [[Exodus-34#v18|Exodus 34:18]]

It was not God’s intention that His people would endeavor in their own strength to keep the Ten Commandments. Rather, His intention was to command His people to enjoy Him—to feast with Him and rest with Him. This is contrary to our natural desire to always want to do something for God (cf. [[Luke-15#v18|Luke 15:18-32]] and note 192). The keeping of God’s commandments must be secondary; it must be the issue of feasting with the Lord and resting with Him (see note 171, par. 1, in [[Genesis-02|Gen. 2]]). 

For the significance of the three annual feasts mentioned in [[Exodus-34#v18|vv. 18]] and [[Exodus-34#v22|22-23]], see notes on [[Exodus-23#v14|23:14-16]] and on [[Leviticus-23|Lev. 23]].

## Footnotes for verse 21

### Footnote 21.1 — seventh

**Verse:** [[Exodus-34#v21|Exodus 34:21]]

In addition to the annual feasts ([[Exodus-34#v18|vv. 18]], [[Exodus-34#v22|22-23]]), every week the children of Israel were to keep the Sabbath, ceasing from their labor and resting with God in remembrance of Him as their Creator and Redeemer. The inserting of the word concerning the Sabbath between the word concerning the feasts indicates that while we are enjoying Christ, simultaneously we rest with Him.

## Footnotes for verse 23

### Footnote 23.1 — Three

**Verse:** [[Exodus-34#v23|Exodus 34:23]]

For the children of Israel the feasts were held yearly and the Sabbath ([[Exodus-34#v21|v. 21]]) was kept weekly. However, this should be a portrait of our daily experience of Christ. Every day we should enjoy Christ as the three feasts mentioned in this chapter, and many times during the day we should cease from our work and rest with Christ in remembrance of Him.

## Footnotes for verse 24

### Footnote 24.1 — For

**Verse:** [[Exodus-34#v24|Exodus 34:24]]

This verse gives the reason that God’s people need to feast with Him and rest with Him. The best way to fight against the enemy and to keep the enemy away from us is to enjoy the Lord.

### Footnote 24.2 — drive

**Verse:** [[Exodus-34#v24|Exodus 34:24]]

The driving out of the nations signifies the demolishing of the things that preoccupy and usurp us (see note 231 in [[Exodus-23|Exodus 23]]); the enlarging of the borders of the good land signifies the enlarging of our capacity to enjoy Christ ([[1 Chronicles-04#v10|1 Chron. 4:10]] and note); and the keeping away of the coveting enemies signifies the guarding of the enjoyment of Christ.

## Footnotes for verse 27

### Footnote 27.1 — speaking

**Verse:** [[Exodus-34#v27|Exodus 34:27]]

Lit., mouth. God’s speaking in this chapter is a repetition of what He spoke in [[Exodus-20|Exodus 20—23]]. The focus of this repeated speaking is not on the keeping of the Ten Commandments and the ordinances of the law but on the enjoyment of God through the enjoyment of the riches of the good land and the repeated feasting and resting with Him. This matches the focus of the New Testament teaching, which is concerned primarily with the enjoyment of Christ ([[1 Corinthians-01#v9|1 Cor. 1:9]]; [[1 Corinthians-05#v8|5:8]]). It was not God’s intention merely to have a people to keep His commandments and ordinances. God’s intention was to dispense Himself into His chosen people so that they would be fully infused with Him in order to express Him (see note 291).

## Footnotes for verse 29

### Footnote 29.1 — His

**Verse:** [[Exodus-34#v29|Exodus 34:29]]

Or, his (Moses’) speaking with Him (Jehovah); cf. [[Numbers-07#v89|Num. 7:89]]. Through God’s speaking to Moses during his lengthy stay with God, Moses was thoroughly infused with God and saturated with Him. As a result, Moses’ face shone. According to this chapter, God did not first give Moses the tablets of the law; rather, He first spent time to infuse Moses with Himself by speaking to Moses concerning the enjoyment of Himself (cf. [[2 Corinthians-03#v3|2 Cor. 3:3]]). Before God gave the law to Moses, God gave Himself to him. This clearly portrays God’s intention.
