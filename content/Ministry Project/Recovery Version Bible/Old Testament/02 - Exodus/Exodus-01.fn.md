# Exodus-01 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Exodus-01|Exodus 1]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — Now

**Verse:** [[Exodus-01#v1|Exodus 1:1]]

Historically, Exodus continues Genesis; however, Exodus portrays the corporate aspect of the spiritual experience of God’s people, whereas Genesis portrays primarily the individual aspect. These two aspects represent the complete spiritual experience of God’s people.

### Footnote 1.2 — Egypt

**Verse:** [[Exodus-01#v1|Exodus 1:1]]

Egypt typifies the world full of fleshly enjoyment, which brings God’s people into slavery and bondage under Satan, the ruler of the world ([[John-12#v31|John 12:31]]; [[Ephesians-02#v2|Eph. 2:2]]), typified by Pharaoh, the king of Egypt.

## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — died

**Verse:** [[Exodus-01#v6|Exodus 1:6]]

The book of Genesis concludes with a dead man in a coffin in Egypt ([[Genesis-50#v26|Gen. 50:26]]). This indicates that God’s chosen people were in a situation of death in Egypt. Although they were in death, they were very living and active (cf. [[Exodus-01#v7|v. 7]]). While they were making a living in Egypt and were enjoying the pleasures of Egypt, they were usurped and enslaved by Pharaoh to serve him with harshness ([[Exodus-01#v10|vv. 10-14]]; [[Exodus-02#v23|2:23]]; [[Exodus-05#v6|5:6-18]]). Thus they were detained from fulfilling the purpose for which God had created and chosen them. The situation of the children of Israel in Egypt under Pharaoh’s tyranny is a full picture of the life of fallen mankind in the world under the usurping and enslaving hand of Satan and his evil power of darkness. See [[Ephesians-02#v1|Eph. 2:1-3]] and notes.

## Footnotes for verse 11

### Footnote 11.1 — Pithom

**Verse:** [[Exodus-01#v11|Exodus 1:11]]

In Hebrew the two names mean, respectively, mouth of integrity and thunder of the standard. These names indicate that the cities were built for pride and boastful display.

### Footnote 16.2 — son

**Verse:** [[Exodus-01#v16|Exodus 1:16]]

According to the Bible the male life represents the life that is for God’s purpose (cf. [[Genesis-01#v26|Gen. 1:26]]; [[1 Corinthians-11#v7|1 Cor. 11:7a]]; [[Ephesians-02#v15|Eph. 2:15]]; [[Revelation-12#v5|Rev. 12:5]]) and the female life, especially among the fallen people, represents the life that is for man’s pleasure (cf. [[Genesis-02#v18|Gen. 2:18]]; [[1 Corinthians-11#v9|1 Cor. 11:9]]). Pharaoh’s intention to kill the sons and spare the daughters typifies Satan’s strategy to destroy the life that is for God’s purpose and preserve the life that is for man’s pleasure.

## Footnotes for verse 17

### Footnote 17.1 — feared

**Verse:** [[Exodus-01#v17|Exodus 1:17]]

At the beginning of the Old Testament, Satan visited a female, Eve, and used her to deaden the male life ([[Genesis-03#v1|Gen. 3:1-6]]), but at the beginning of the New Testament, God visited the virgin Mary and used her to bring in His salvation ([[Luke-01#v26|Luke 1:26-38]]; [[Luke-02#v30|2:30]]). In the same principle, Pharaoh attempted to use the midwives to kill the male life, but God used them to keep alive the life that is for His purpose.
