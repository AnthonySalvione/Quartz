# Isaiah-01 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Isaiah-01|Isaiah 1]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — Isaiah

**Verse:** [[Isaiah-01#v1|Isaiah 1:1]]

Meaning the salvation of Jah. The book of Isaiah, in its content concerning God’s eternal economy in Christ, is the leading book among all the books of the prophets. This book is the vision that Isaiah saw ([[Isaiah-01#v1|v. 1]]), the word that Isaiah saw ([[Isaiah-02#v1|2:1]]), and the burden that Isaiah saw ([[Isaiah-13#v1|13:1]]; [[Isaiah-15#v1|15:1]]). The vision, word, and burden in Isaiah are concerned with God’s eternal economy in Christ, which is thoroughly covered in this book. 

The book of Isaiah unveils that God’s dealing in love with His beloved Israel and His righteous judgment upon the nations bring in Christ, the Savior ([[Isaiah-43#v3|43:3]]; [[Isaiah-49#v26|49:26]]), who is God ([[Isaiah-09#v6|9:6]]) incarnated to be a man ([[Isaiah-07#v14|7:14]]), possessing both the divine nature and the human nature ([[Isaiah-04#v2|4:2]]), living on this earth ([[Isaiah-53#v2|53:2-3]]; [[Isaiah-42#v1|42:1-4]]), crucified ([[Isaiah-53#v7|53:7-10a]], [[Isaiah-53#v12|12]]), resurrected ([[Isaiah-53#v10|53:10b-11]]), ascended ([[Isaiah-52#v13|52:13]]), and coming ([[Isaiah-40#v10|40:10]]; [[Isaiah-64#v1|64:1]]) to meet the need of God’s chosen people and the nations ([[Isaiah-09#v1|9:1-7]]; [[Isaiah-49#v6|49:6]]) in God’s all-inclusive salvation ([[Isaiah-12#v2|12:2-3]]), that the restoration of all things, of the created yet fallen universe ([[Isaiah-02#v2|2:2-5]]; [[Isaiah-11#v6|11:6-9]]; [[Isaiah-35#v1|35:1-10]]; [[Isaiah-30#v26|30:26]]), may be brought in, which will consummate in the new heaven and new earth for eternity ([[Isaiah-65#v17|65:17]]). Hence, the content of Isaiah covers God’s entire economy of the New Testament, from the incarnation ([[Matthew-01#v18|Matt. 1:18-25]]) to the new heaven and new earth ([[Revelation-21|Rev. 21—22]]), with the Old Testament background of God’s dealing with Israel and His judgment upon the nations. According to Isaiah’s prophecy, the Christ who was processed for the divine purposes is the centrality and universality of the great wheel of the move of the Divine Trinity ([[Ezekiel-01#v15|Ezek. 1:15]] and [note 1](<Ezekiel-01.fn#Footnote 15.1 — wheel>)) for the accomplishing of His economy in the divine dispensing of Himself into His elect.

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — children

**Verse:** [[Isaiah-01#v2|Isaiah 1:2]]

In the book of Isaiah, God’s love toward Israel is exercised in a threefold way: as a Father ([[Isaiah-01#v2|1:2-3]]; [[Isaiah-63#v16|63:16]]; [[Isaiah-64#v8|64:8]]), as a nursing Mother ([[Isaiah-66#v13|66:13]]), and as a Husband ([[Isaiah-54#v5|54:5]]). Since God deals with His beloved Israel in a loving way, His dealing with them, in general, is a matter not of judgment but of chastisement. God’s dealing with the Gentiles, the nations, however, is a matter of judgment based on God’s righteousness, on His justice. 

God deals with people according to what He is. God is holy and righteous; He is the Holy One and the righteous One ([[Isaiah-01#v4|v. 4]]; [[Isaiah-24#v16|24:16a]]). As the Holy One, He chastises His people that they may be holy ([[Hebrews-12#v10|Heb. 12:10]]), and as the righteous One, He judges the nations because they are not just and righteous. See [note 13¹](<Isaiah-26.fn#Footnote 13.1 — lords>) in [[Isaiah-26|Isaiah 26]].

## Footnotes for verse 16

### Footnote 16.1 — Wash

**Verse:** [[Isaiah-01#v16|Isaiah 1:16]]

After Jehovah’s chastisement on His beloved children, Isaiah presents Jehovah’s loving exhortation ([[Isaiah-01#v16|vv. 16-17]]) and promise to His chastised people. Jehovah’s loving promise is a promise of forgiveness ([[Isaiah-01#v18|v. 18]]) and of restoration ([[Isaiah-01#v26|vv. 26-27]]).

## Footnotes for verse 18

### Footnote 18.1 — snow

**Verse:** [[Isaiah-01#v18|Isaiah 1:18]]

In God’s full salvation He not only forgives our sins, exempting us from the penalty of our sins and removing the record of our sins from before Him; He also washes away the traces of sins in us, making us as white as snow and white like wool. Both snow and wool are naturally white. Hence, as a result of God’s washing, we become not only white but also naturally white, as if we had never been defiled. The washing that makes us as white as snow is a positional washing from without through the blood of Jesus Christ ([[1 John-01#v7|1 John 1:7]]; [[Hebrews-01#v3|Heb. 1:3b]]; [[Revelation-01#v5|Rev. 1:5]]), whereas the washing that makes us white like wool is a washing of our nature metabolically from within by God’s Spirit and by His life ([[1 Corinthians-06#v11|1 Cor. 6:11]] and [note](<1 Corinthians-06.fn#Footnote 11.1 — washed>); [[Titus-03#v5|Titus 3:5]] and [note 4](<Titus-03.fn#Footnote 5.4 — regeneration>)). Cf. [note 14²](<Revelation-01.fn#Footnote 14.2 — white>) in [[Revelation-01|Rev. 1]].
