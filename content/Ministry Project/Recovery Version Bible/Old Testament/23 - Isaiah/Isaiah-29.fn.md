# Isaiah-29 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Isaiah-29|Isaiah 29]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — Ariel

**Verse:** [[Isaiah-29#v1|Isaiah 29:1]]

Meaning a lion of El. A symbolic name for Jerusalem, and representing the southern kingdom of Judah.

## Footnotes for verse 13

### Footnote 13.1 — draws

**Verse:** [[Isaiah-29#v13|Isaiah 29:13]]

The northern kingdom of Israel was full of drunkards ([[Isaiah-28|Isaiah 28]]), and the southern kingdom of Judah was full of hypocritical worshippers. These two conditions characterize the condition of fallen mankind on the earth. First, people are drunk by loving things other than the Lord, and second, they are not true but false. The kind of hypocritical worship described in this verse continued until the time of Christ ([[Matthew-15#v1|Matt. 15:1-14]]; [[John-04#v20|John 4:20-24]]). As revealed in [[Isaiah-29#v1|vv. 1-12]], [[Isaiah-29#v14|14-16]], Jehovah judged the hypocrisy of the worshippers in Jerusalem.

## Footnotes for verse 15

### Footnote 15.1 — hide

**Verse:** [[Isaiah-29#v15|Isaiah 29:15]]

With the hypocritical worship spoken of in this chapter, there was vanity but no reality ([[Isaiah-29#v13|v. 13]]) and blindness but no wisdom ([[Isaiah-29#v9|vv. 9-12]], [[Isaiah-29#v15|15-16]]). Through His incarnation Christ brought to us the very reality of the universe—the Triune God, the Divine Trinity, embodied in a person, Jesus Christ ([[John-01#v14|John 1:14]], [[John-01#v17|17]]). When we realize the embodied reality, the Divine Trinity in Christ, our eyes, our ears, and our understanding are opened, and we have wisdom. Christ is the reality and wisdom to God’s redeemed people ([[John-14#v6|John 14:6a]]; [[1 Corinthians-01#v24|1 Cor. 1:24]], [[1 Corinthians-01#v30|30]]) that makes them true worshippers of God ([[John-04#v23|John 4:23-24]]).
