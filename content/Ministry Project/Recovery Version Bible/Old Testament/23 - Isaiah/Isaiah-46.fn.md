# Isaiah-46 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Isaiah-46|Isaiah 46]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — Bel

**Verse:** [[Isaiah-46#v1|Isaiah 46:1]]

One of the gods of the Babylonians, supposed by some to be the Babylonian name of Baal.

### Footnote 1.2 — Nebo

**Verse:** [[Isaiah-46#v1|Isaiah 46:1]]

A Babylonian idol.

### Footnote 1.3 — burden

**Verse:** [[Isaiah-46#v1|Isaiah 46:1]]

When Israel was captured by the Babylonians, God’s people still would not give up their idols and had to carry them from the good land to Babylon. Anything that replaces God or occupies the position of God is an idol that becomes a burden to the worshipper.

## Footnotes for verse 11

### Footnote 11.1 — bird

**Verse:** [[Isaiah-46#v11|Isaiah 46:11]]

Referring to Cyrus in [[Isaiah-44#v28|44:28]] and [[Isaiah-45#v1|45:1]]. In [[Isaiah-46|Isaiah 46—48]] Christ as the Servant of Jehovah is typified by Cyrus to release Jehovah’s captives of the beloved Israel from Babylon. See [note 2¹](<Isaiah-41.fn#Footnote 2.1 — him>) in [[Isaiah-41|Isaiah 41]]. Cyrus was a “bird of prey” called by God to subdue the nations.
