## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — Abel

**Verse:** [[Genesis-04#v2|Genesis 4:2]]

Meaning breath or vanity. As a result of man’s first fall, everything in human life became vanity ([[Ecclesiastes-01#v2|Eccl. 1:2-3]]).

### Footnote 2.2 — tender

**Verse:** [[Genesis-04#v2|Genesis 4:2]]

Or, a shepherd, a feeder. Before the flood man was permitted to eat only fruits and vegetables ([[Genesis-01#v29|1:29]]; [[Genesis-03#v18|3:18]]), not meat (cf. [[Genesis-09#v3|9:3]]). Thus, Abel’s tending of sheep was not to produce food for his living but to provide offerings for God’s satisfaction. Abel not only believed the gospel (see [note 4¹](<Genesis-04.fn#Footnote 4.1 — offering>)) but also practiced the gospel and lived for it.

### Footnote 2.3 — tiller

**Verse:** [[Genesis-04#v2|Genesis 4:2]]

Or, laborer. In his occupation Abel took care only of God’s satisfaction, whereas Cain cared only for his own living.

## Footnotes for verse 3

### Footnote 3.1 — offering

**Verse:** [[Genesis-04#v3|Genesis 4:3]]

Cain did not follow the way of God’s salvation through the anticipated redemption by the bleeding sacrifice ([[Genesis-03#v21|3:21]]; [[Hebrews-09#v22|Heb. 9:22]]) but continued man’s fall by presumptuously offering the fruit of his own labor to God. Cain’s way of worshipping God was to invent a religion according to his human concept and opinion ([[Jude-01#v11|Jude 11]] and [note 1](<Jude-01.fn#Footnote 11.1 — way>)), which were motivated by Satan, the subtle one ([[Genesis-04#v7|v. 7]] and [note](<Genesis-04.fn#Footnote 7.1 — sin>); [[1 John-03#v12|1 John 3:12]]). Throughout the centuries and generations there have been countless followers of Cain, people in every place and time who have invented their own religion.

## Footnotes for verse 4

### Footnote 4.1 — offering

**Verse:** [[Genesis-04#v4|Genesis 4:4]]

According to [[Hebrews-11#v4|Heb. 11:4]], Abel’s offering, a sacrifice, was offered to God by faith. Faith comes by hearing the word of the gospel ([[Romans-10#v17|Rom. 10:17]], [[Romans-10#v14|14]]). This indicates that Abel’s parents, Adam and Eve, must have proclaimed to their children the glad tidings that God had announced to them ([[Genesis-03#v15|3:15]], [[Genesis-03#v21|21]]). Like his father and mother, Abel believed the gospel and presented his offering to God according to God’s revelation in the word proclaimed by his parents. Thus, the first family on earth was a family of the gospel, a family of believers. 

Abel was the first priest of God, representing all the believers in Christ ([[1 Peter-02#v5|1 Pet. 2:5]], [[1 Peter-02#v9|9]]). In type, Abel offered Christ to God. According to [[Numbers-18#v17|Num. 18:17]], the firstling of a cow or sheep or goat, a type of Christ, had to be offered to God. This offering included the sprinkling of the blood upon the altar for redemption and the burning of the fat as a satisfying fragrance to God. Hence, Abel’s offering, corresponding exactly with what was later revealed in the Mosaic law, proves that his way of worshipping God was according to God’s divine revelation, not according to his own concept.

## Footnotes for verse 5

### Footnote 5.1 — no

**Verse:** [[Genesis-04#v5|Genesis 4:5]]

Cain rejected God’s way of taking Christ as God’s righteousness to cover him ([[Genesis-03#v21|3:21]]; [[Philippians-03#v9|Phil. 3:9]]; [[1 Corinthians-01#v30|1 Cor. 1:30]]). Like the religious Jews, he sought to establish his own righteousness, ignoring God’s righteousness and not submitting to it ([[Romans-10#v3|Rom. 10:3]]). Thus, his offering was an insult to God, and God rejected it.

## Footnotes for verse 7

### Footnote 7.1 — sin

**Verse:** [[Genesis-04#v7|Genesis 4:7]]

Sin and Satan are one ([[Romans-07#v8|Rom. 7:8]] and [note](<Romans-07.fn#Footnote 8.1 — sin>)). If we reject God’s way of salvation, Satan as sin is crouching at the door, waiting for the opportunity to seize and devour us (cf. [[Luke-22#v31|Luke 22:31]]; [[1 Peter-05#v8|1 Pet. 5:8]]).

## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — Let

**Verse:** [[Genesis-04#v8|Genesis 4:8]]

This clause is present in many ancient versions but lacking in the Hebrew text.

### Footnote 8.2 — slew

**Verse:** [[Genesis-04#v8|Genesis 4:8]]

Cain murdered his brother because of his anger provoked by his religious jealousy ([[Genesis-04#v4|vv. 4-5]]). Throughout the generations those who worship God according to the flesh have opposed, persecuted, and even murdered those who worship according to the Spirit ([[Matthew-23#v35|Matt. 23:35]]; [[John-16#v2|John 16:2]]; [[Revelation-17#v6|Rev. 17:6]]; cf. [[Galatians-04#v29|Gal. 4:29]]). In his offering to God and his murdering his brother, Cain was on the line of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. In contrast, Abel was on the line of the tree of life. See [note 9³](<Genesis-02.fn#Footnote 9.3 — tree>), par. 2, in [[Genesis-02|Genesis 2]].

## Footnotes for verse 9

### Footnote 9.1 — I

**Verse:** [[Genesis-04#v9|Genesis 4:9]]

This lie showed Cain’s arrogance toward God. Cain’s murdering of Abel and his lying to God were alluded to by the Lord Jesus in [[John-08#v44|John 8:44]], indicating that in the acts of murdering and lying, Cain was one with the Devil, Satan, who was his source ([[1 John-03#v12|1 John 3:12]]). Because Cain had rejected God’s way and God’s warning ([[Genesis-04#v7|v. 7]]), he was gained by Satan, the murderer and the liar, and became a murderer and a liar with Satan.

### Footnote 17.1 — Enoch

### Footnote 17.2 — city

**Verse:** [[Genesis-04#v17|Genesis 4:17]]

After leaving God’s presence ([[Genesis-04#v16|v. 16]]), Cain constructed a city for his protection and self-existence. Within this city he produced a culture without God. In the garden God was everything to man—his protection, maintenance, supply, and amusement. When man lost God, he lost everything. Man’s loss of God forced man to invent human culture, the main elements of which were cities for existence, cattle-raising for making a living, music for amusement, and weapons for defense ([[Genesis-04#v20|vv. 20-22]]). The godless culture invented in [[Genesis-04|Gen. 4]] will continue to develop until it climaxes in the great Babylon ([[Revelation-17|Rev. 17—18]]).

### Footnote 26.2 — call

**Verse:** [[Genesis-04#v26|Genesis 4:26]]

The Hebrew word means to call out to, to cry unto, i.e., to cry out audibly. Because men realized that their life was vanity (see [note 2¹](<Genesis-04.fn#Footnote 2.1 — Abel>)) and that they were frail and mortal (see [note 26¹](<Genesis-04.fn#Footnote 26.1 — Enosh>)), they spontaneously began to call upon the name of Jehovah, the eternal One (see [note 4⁴](<Genesis-02.fn#Footnote 4.4 — Jehovah>) in [[Genesis-02|Genesis 2]]). Although they were vain and weak, by calling on the name of the Lord, they were made rich and strong, for they entered into the riches and strength of the One on whom they called ([[Romans-10#v12|Rom. 10:12-13]] and notes). See [note 21¹](<Acts-02.fn#Footnote 21.1 — calls>) in [[Acts-02|Acts 2]].
