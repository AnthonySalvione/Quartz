# Matthew-20 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Matthew-20|Matthew 20]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — For

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v1|Matthew 20:1]]

For indicates that the parable in [[Matthew-20#v1|vv. 1-16]] is an explanation of [[Matthew-19#v30|19:30]].

### Footnote 1.2 — man

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v1|Matthew 20:1]]

Denoting Christ, who is the householder.

### Footnote 1.3 — morning

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v1|Matthew 20:1]]

Morning here is 6:00 a.m., denoting the earliest part of the church age, when Christ came to call His disciples into the kingdom.

### Footnote 1.4 — workmen

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v1|Matthew 20:1]]

The workmen denote the disciples.

### Footnote 1.5 — vineyard

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v1|Matthew 20:1]]

The vineyard denotes the kingdom.

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — agreed

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v2|Matthew 20:2]]

Referring to the agreement the Lord made in [[Matthew-19#v27|19:27-29]].

### Footnote 2.2 — denarius

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v2|Matthew 20:2]]

See [note 7¹](<John-06.fn#Footnote 7.1 — denarii>) in [[John-06|John 6]]. The denarius here denotes the reward the Lord offered Peter in His agreement with him in [[Matthew-19#v27|19:27-29]].

### Footnote 3.3 — marketplace

The marketplace denotes the world.

## Footnotes for verse 7

Outside the kingdom of God, no human beings are employed by God.

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v7|Matthew 20:7]]

Even near the end of the church age the Lord still calls people.

## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — evening

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v8|Matthew 20:8]]

6:00 p.m., denoting the end of the church age.

### Footnote 8.2 — last

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v8|Matthew 20:8]]

This is against the natural and commercial concept. It indicates that the wages paid to the last workmen are not according to their work but according to the gracious wish of the Lord of the vineyard.

## Footnotes for verse 10

### Footnote 10.1 — first

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v10|Matthew 20:10]]

These first workmen included Peter, who made a deal with the Lord in [[Matthew-19#v27|19:27-29]].

## Footnotes for verse 12

### Footnote 12.1 — Saying

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v12|Matthew 20:12]]

They did not know [[Romans-09#v14|Rom 9:14-15]], [[Romans-09#v20|20]]. There is no unrighteousness with the Lord. He will have mercy on whom He will have mercy. Who were they to reply against the Lord?

### Footnote 12.2 — equal

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v12|Matthew 20:12]]

Peter’s natural concept, representing that of all believers, was commercial; he did not know the Lord’s gracious wish.

## Footnotes for verse 13

### Footnote 13.1 — one

By one of them, the Lord was referring undoubtedly to Peter.

### Footnote 13.2 — Friend

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v13|Matthew 20:13]]

Lit., comrade, companion, partner.

### Footnote 13.3 — agree

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v13|Matthew 20:13]]

Denoting the agreement the Lord made with Peter in [[Matthew-19#v27|19:27-29]].

## Footnotes for verse 14

### Footnote 14.1 — Take

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v14|Matthew 20:14]]

This was a strong answer to Peter from the Lord, indicating that the Lord had given him what he thought he deserved. But the Lord has the right to give the same wages to the last workmen according to His own wish, in the principle not of work but of grace. This shattered Peter’s natural and commercial mind and corrected his concept.

## Footnotes for verse 15

### Footnote 15.1 — wish

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v15|Matthew 20:15]]

In dealing with the Lord in [[Matthew-19#v27|19:27]], Peter’s concept was altogether commercial, according to the principle of work, not grace. In His answer to Peter, the Lord strongly indicated that His reward to His followers is not a commercial matter but a matter according to His desire and grace. For the disciples to gain the kingdom of the heavens, they needed to leave all and follow the Lord; but what He will give them as a reward is more than they deserve. It is not according to the principle of commerce but according to the Lord’s good pleasure. This is an incentive to His followers.

### Footnote 15.2 — evil

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v15|Matthew 20:15]]

Cf. [note 22²](<Mark-07.fn#Footnote 22.2 — envy>) in [[Mark-07|Mark 7]].

## Footnotes for verse 16

### Footnote 16.1 — last

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v16|Matthew 20:16]]

The last are the latest workmen, and the first are the earliest ones. In working, the earliest come first, but in receiving the reward, the last become the first. It is in this way that the Lord makes the last first and the first last.

## Footnotes for verse 18

### Footnote 18.1 — death

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v18|Matthew 20:18]]

This was the third time the Lord revealed His death to the disciples. The first was in Caesarea Philippi, before His transfiguration ([[Matthew-16#v13|16:13]], [[Matthew-16#v21|21]]). The second was in Galilee, after His transfiguration ([[Matthew-17#v22|17:22]]). This third time was on the way to Jerusalem. This revelation was a prophecy, altogether strange to the natural concept of the disciples; yet it would be fulfilled literally, in every detail.

## Footnotes for verse 20

### Footnote 20.1 — sons

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v20|Matthew 20:20]]

John and James.

## Footnotes for verse 22

### Footnote 22.1 — drink

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v22|Matthew 20:22]]

If we ask to sit on the throne in the kingdom, we must be prepared to drink the cup of suffering. To suffer the cross is the way to enter into the kingdom ([[Acts-14#v22|Acts 14:22]]). The selfish entreaty of the mother of John and James afforded the Lord an opportunity to reveal the way to enter into the kingdom.

## Footnotes for verse 23

### Footnote 23.1 — not

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v23|Matthew 20:23]]

Standing in the position of man, the Lord was fully subject to the Father; He did not assume the right to do anything outside the Father.

## Footnotes for verse 26

### Footnote 26.1 — servant

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v26|Matthew 20:26]]

This is absolutely contrary to the natural, self-seeking mind. The indignation of the ten disciples ([[Matthew-20#v24|v. 24]]) also afforded the Lord an opportunity to reveal the way to live in the kingdom: to be willing to serve others as a servant, even as a slave ([[Matthew-20#v27|v. 27]]), rather than rule over others.

## Footnotes for verse 28

### Footnote 28.1 — Man

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v28|Matthew 20:28]]

In this book, the book on the kingdom, the Lord stood always in the position of man. Although the kingdom of the heavens is constituted with the divine life, it is carried out in humanity.

### Footnote 28.2 — life

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v28|Matthew 20:28]]

Lit., soul, soul-life.

## Footnotes for verse 30

### Footnote 30.1 — Son

**Verse:** [[Matthew-20#v30|Matthew 20:30]]

The royal title of Christ for the children of Israel. See [note 22²](<Matthew-15.fn#Footnote 22.2 — Lord>) in [[Matthew-15|Matthew 15]].
