# Matthew-21 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Matthew-21|Matthew 21]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — drew

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v1|Matthew 21:1]]

They began the journey from Galilee in [[Matthew-19#v1|19:1]]. They were on the way in [[Matthew-20#v17|20:17]] and passed through Jericho in [[Matthew-20#v29|20:29]]. Now they came to the Mount of Olives, near Jerusalem.

## Footnotes for verse 2

## Footnotes for verse 3

## Footnotes for verse 5

### Footnote 5.1 — daughter

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v5|Matthew 21:5]]

Denoting the people of Jerusalem (cf. [[Psalm-137#v8|Psa. 137:8]]; [[Psalm-45#v12|45:12]]).

### Footnote 5.2 — foal

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v5|Matthew 21:5]]

Signifying the humble and lowly state in which the King of the heavenly kingdom was willing to remain.

## Footnotes for verse 7

### Footnote 7.1 — garments

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v7|Matthew 21:7]]

Garments signify the human virtues in people’s conduct. The disciples honored the lowly King by putting their garments on the donkey and the colt for Him to ride on, and the crowd honored Him by spreading their garments in the road for Him to pass through ([[Matthew-21#v8|v. 8]]).

## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — branches

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v8|Matthew 21:8]]

The branches of the palm tree ([[John-12#v13|John 12:13]]), which signify the victorious life ([[Revelation-07#v9|Rev. 7:9]]) and the satisfaction of enjoying the rich produce of that life, as typified by the Feast of Tabernacles ([[Leviticus-23#v40|Lev. 23:40]]; [[Nehemiah-08#v15|Neh. 8:15]]). The crowd used both their garments and the palm tree branches to celebrate the lowly King’s coming.

## Footnotes for verse 9

### Footnote 9.1 — Hosanna

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v9|Matthew 21:9]]

A Hebrew expression meaning do save, we pray ([[Psalm-118#v25|Psa. 118:25]]).

### Footnote 9.2 — Son

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v9|Matthew 21:9]]

The royal title of the lowly King.

## Footnotes for verse 15

### Footnote 15.1 — indignant

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v15|Matthew 21:15]]

The stubborn chief priests and scribes were indignant, even after they saw the wonders the lowly King performed. Their indignation was due to their pride and jealousy, which blinded them from seeing any vision concerning the heavenly King.

## Footnotes for verse 17

### Footnote 17.1 — Bethany

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v17|Matthew 21:17]]

In His last visit to Jerusalem the Lord remained there only during the day for His ministry. Every evening He departed to lodge in Bethany, on the eastern slope of the Mount of Olives ([[Mark-11#v19|Mark 11:19]]; [[Luke-21#v37|Luke 21:37]]), where the house of Mary, Martha, and Lazarus and the house of Simon were ([[John-11#v1|John 11:1]]; [[Matthew-26#v6|Matt. 26:6]]). In Jerusalem He was rejected by the leaders of Judaism, but in Bethany He was welcomed by His lovers.

## Footnotes for verse 18

### Footnote 18.1 — hungry

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v18|Matthew 21:18]]

This signified that the Lord was hungry for fruit from the children of Israel, that God might be satisfied.

## Footnotes for verse 19

### Footnote 19.1 — fig

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v19|Matthew 21:19]]

The fig tree here is a symbol of the nation of Israel ([[Jeremiah-24#v2|Jer. 24:2]], [[Jeremiah-24#v5|5]], [[Jeremiah-24#v8|8]]). This tree was full of leaves but fruitless, signifying that at that time the nation of Israel was full of outward show but had nothing that could satisfy God.

### Footnote 19.2 — no

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v19|Matthew 21:19]]

This signifies the curse on the nation of Israel.

### Footnote 19.3 — dried

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v19|Matthew 21:19]]

From that time the nation of Israel was truly dried up.

## Footnotes for verse 24

### Footnote 24.1 — thing

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v24|Matthew 21:24]]

Lit., word.

## Footnotes for verse 27

### Footnote 27.1 — not

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v27|Matthew 21:27]]

This was a lie.

### Footnote 27.2 — Neither

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v27|Matthew 21:27]]

This indicated that the Lord knew that the Jewish leaders would not tell Him what they knew; hence, neither would He tell them what they asked. They lied to the Lord in saying, “We do not know.” But the Lord spoke the truth wisely to them, exposing their lie and avoiding their question.

## Footnotes for verse 31

### Footnote 31.1 — first

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v31|Matthew 21:31]]

In [[Luke-15#v1|Luke 15:1-2]], [[Luke-15#v11|11-32]], the Lord likened the leaders of Judaism to the firstborn son, and the tax collectors and sinners to the second son; but here the Lord likened them in the opposite order. This indicates that the Jews were the firstborn of God ([[Exodus-04#v22|Exo. 4:22]]) and had the birthright. However, because of their unbelief the birthright was shifted to the church, which has become God’s firstborn ([[Hebrews-12#v23|Heb. 12:23]]).

## Footnotes for verse 32

### Footnote 32.1 — righteousness

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v32|Matthew 21:32]]

The Gospel of Matthew, as the book on the kingdom, stresses the matter of righteousness, for the kingdom life is one of strict righteousness, which we must seek ([[Matthew-05#v20|5:20]], [[Matthew-05#v6|6]]; [[Matthew-06#v33|6:33]]). John the Baptist came in the way of such righteousness, and the Lord Jesus was willing to be baptized by John to fulfill such righteousness ([[Matthew-03#v15|3:15]]).

## Footnotes for verse 33

### Footnote 33.1 — householder

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v33|Matthew 21:33]]

The householder is God, the vineyard is the city of Jerusalem ([[Isaiah-05#v1|Isa. 5:1]]), and the vinedressers are the leaders of the Israelites ([[Matthew-21#v45|v. 45]]).

## Footnotes for verse 42

### Footnote 42.1 — stone

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v42|Matthew 21:42]]

The stone is Christ, who is for God’s building ([[Isaiah-28#v16|Isa. 28:16]]; [[Zechariah-03#v9|Zech. 3:9]]; [[1 Peter-02#v4|1 Pet. 2:4]]), and the builders are the Jewish leaders, who should have been working on God’s building.

### Footnote 42.2 — head

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v42|Matthew 21:42]]

Christ is not only the foundation stone ([[Isaiah-28#v16|Isa. 28:16]]) and the topstone ([[Zechariah-04#v7|Zech. 4:7]]) but also the cornerstone.

## Footnotes for verse 43

### Footnote 43.1 — kingdom

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v43|Matthew 21:43]]

The kingdom of God was already there among the Israelites, but the kingdom of the heavens had only drawn near ([[Matthew-03#v2|3:2]]; [[Matthew-04#v17|4:17]]). This proves that the kingdom of the heavens is different from the kingdom of God. (See [note 3⁴](<Matthew-05.fn#Footnote 3.4 — kingdom>) in [[Matthew-05|Matthew 5]].)

### Footnote 43.2 — nation

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v43|Matthew 21:43]]

This is the church.

## Footnotes for verse 44

### Footnote 44.1 — he

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v44|Matthew 21:44]]

This is a person who stumbles on Christ; referring in particular to the unbelieving Israelites ([[Isaiah-08#v15|Isa. 8:15]]; [[Romans-09#v32|Rom. 9:32]]).

### Footnote 44.2 — whomever

**Verse:** [[Matthew-21#v44|Matthew 21:44]]

These are the nations, the Gentiles, that Christ will smite and break into pieces at His coming back ([[Daniel-02#v34|Dan. 2:34-35]]). To the believers Christ is the foundation stone, the One in whom they trust ([[Isaiah-28#v16|Isa. 28:16]]); to the unbelieving Jews He is the stone of stumbling ([[Isaiah-08#v14|Isa. 8:14]]; [[Romans-09#v33|Rom. 9:33]]); and to the nations He will be the smiting stone.
