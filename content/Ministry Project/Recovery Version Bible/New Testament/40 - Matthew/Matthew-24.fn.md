# Matthew-24 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Matthew-24|Matthew 24]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — from

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v1|Matthew 24:1]]

The Lord came out from the temple, indicating that He had left the temple. This was to fulfill His word in [[Matthew-23#v38|23:38]], concerning His leaving the temple to the rejecting Jews as their house of desolation. This was equivalent to God’s glory leaving the temple in the ancient time ([[Ezekiel-10#v18|Ezek. 10:18]]).

### Footnote 1.2 — temple

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v1|Matthew 24:1]]

The entire precincts of the temple.

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — thrown

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v2|Matthew 24:2]]

This was fulfilled in A.D. 70 when Titus and the Roman army destroyed Jerusalem.

## Footnotes for verse 3

### Footnote 3.1 — Mount

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v3|Matthew 24:3]]

To receive the vision of the Lord’s prophecy concerning this age, we need to climb the high mountain to enter into His presence.

### Footnote 3.2 — When

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v3|Matthew 24:3]]

The disciples’ question concerned three matters: (1) the time when “these things” would take place, including not only the destruction of the temple ([[Matthew-24#v2|v. 2]]) but also the things mentioned in [[Matthew-23#v32|23:32-39]]; (2) the sign of Christ’s coming; and (3) the sign of the consummation of the age. The Lord’s word from [[Matthew-24#v4|24:4]]—[[Matthew-25#v46|25:46]] answered the disciples’ question concerning these three matters.

### Footnote 3.3 — coming

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v3|Matthew 24:3]]

Gk. parousia, which means presence. Christ’s coming will be His presence with His people. This presence (parousia) will begin with the rapture of the man-child ([[Revelation-12#v5|Rev. 12:5]]) and the rapture of the firstfruits ([[Revelation-14#v1|Rev. 14:1-4]]) and will end with His appearing on the earth with the saints. During the period of His parousia there will be the great tribulation ([[Matthew-24#v21|v. 21]]; [[Revelation-09#v1|Rev. 9:1-21]]; [[Revelation-11#v14|11:14]]; [[Revelation-16#v1|16:1-21]]), which will begin with supernatural calamities ([[Revelation-06#v12|Rev. 6:12-17]]; [[Revelation-08#v7|8:7-12]]); Christ’s descending to the air ([[Revelation-14#v14|Rev. 14:14]]); the rapture of the majority of the believers to the air ([[1 Thessalonians-04#v15|1 Thes. 4:15-17]]); the judgment seat of Christ ([[2 Corinthians-05#v10|2 Cor. 5:10]]); and the marriage of the Lamb ([[Revelation-19#v7|Rev. 19:7-9]]). (See “The Chart of the Seventy Weeks and the Coming of Christ, with the Rapture of the Saints” at the end of the printed edition.)

## Footnotes for verse 4

### Footnote 4.1 — answered

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v4|Matthew 24:4]]

The Lord’s answer has three sections: the first section ([[Matthew-24#v4|vv. 4-31]]) concerns the Jews, who are the chosen; the second ([[Matthew-24#v32|v. 32]]—[[Matthew-25#v30|25:30]]) concerns the church; and the third ([[Matthew-25#v31|25:31-46]]) concerns the Gentiles (the nations). The first section, concerning the Jews, should be interpreted literally, whereas the second section, concerning the church, should be interpreted spiritually, because it is spoken in parables for the reason given in [[Matthew-13#v11|13:11-13]]. For instance, the winter in [[Matthew-24#v20|v. 20]] is the actual winter, but the summer in [[Matthew-24#v32|v. 32]] is a symbol signifying the time of restoration. The third section, concerning the Gentiles, also should be interpreted literally.

### Footnote 4.2 — See

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v4|Matthew 24:4]]

Some aspects of the prophecy in [[Matthew-24#v4|vv. 4-14]] have been fulfilled, and some are in the process of being fulfilled. At the time of the great tribulation, which will be the consummation, the end, of this age, the prophecy will be fully fulfilled.

## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — wars

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v6|Matthew 24:6]]

Wars here denotes all the wars from the first century to the present. They are signified by the red horse of the second seal in [[Revelation-06#v3|Rev. 6:3-4]].

### Footnote 6.2 — end

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v6|Matthew 24:6]]

The end denotes the consummation of this age ([[Matthew-24#v3|v. 3]]; [[Daniel-12#v4|Dan. 12:4]], [[Daniel-12#v9|9]], [[Daniel-12#v6|6-7]]), which will be the three and a half years of the great tribulation. Although [[Matthew-24#v6|vv. 6-9]] and 14 are in the section concerning Israel, the tribulations and the preaching of the gospel mentioned in these verses take place generally, in the whole world, from the time of Christ’s ascension to the end of this age. [[Matthew-24#v15|Verse 15]], immediately following [[Matthew-24#v14|v. 14]], speaks concerning the beginning of the great tribulation ([[Matthew-24#v21|v. 21]]), which is the time when Antichrist will set up his image in the temple of God, which image will thereby become the abomination of desolation.

## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — birth

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v8|Matthew 24:8]]

Birth pangs are for the bringing forth of a child. In His New Testament economy, God’s desire and purpose are to bring forth many sons ([[Galatians-03#v26|Gal. 3:26]]; [[Hebrews-02#v10|Heb. 2:10]]) as His expression. Hence, birth pangs here refers to all the tribulations in the New Testament age, including wars, famines, earthquakes, afflictions, and persecutions, as mentioned in [[Matthew-24#v6|vv. 6-9]] and [[Matthew-24#v21|21]]. The birth pangs here are suffered only by the New Testament believers for the Lord’s sake, whereas the travailing in birth in [[Revelation-12#v2|Rev. 12:2]] refers to all the travailings suffered throughout the generations by God’s people in both the Old Testament and the New Testament. These tribulations, along with the preaching of the gospel ([[Matthew-24#v14|v. 14]]), are used by God to produce His many sons so that He can constitute the church in this age, accomplish the kingdom in the coming age, and produce the New Jerusalem in eternity as the ultimate consummation of the church and the kingdom that He may have an eternal expression in eternity future. [[Galatians-04#v19|Galatians 4:19]] and [[Revelation-12#v2|Rev 12:2]] and 5 confirm this viewpoint.

## Footnotes for verse 14

### Footnote 14.1 — gospel

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v14|Matthew 24:14]]

The gospel of the kingdom, which includes the gospel of grace ([[Acts-20#v24|Acts 20:24]]), brings people not only into God’s salvation but also into the kingdom of the heavens ([[Revelation-01#v9|Rev. 1:9]]). The gospel of grace emphasizes forgiveness of sin, God’s redemption, and eternal life, whereas the gospel of the kingdom emphasizes the heavenly ruling of God and the authority of the Lord. This gospel of the kingdom will be preached in the whole earth for a testimony to all the nations before the end of this age. Hence, that preaching, signified by the white horse of the first seal in [[Revelation-06#v1|Rev. 6:1-2]], will be a sign of the consummation of this age.

### Footnote 14.2 — testimony

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v14|Matthew 24:14]]

The gospel of the kingdom is a testimony to all the nations (Gentiles). This testimony must spread to the whole earth before the end of this age, the time of the great tribulation.

### Footnote 20.1 — winter

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v20|Matthew 24:20]]

### Footnote 20.2 — Sabbath

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v20|Matthew 24:20]]

On the Sabbath one was allowed to walk only a short distance ([[Acts-01#v12|Acts 1:12]]), a distance not adequate for escaping. The mentioning of the Sabbath here indicates that the Jews would still keep the Sabbath after the restoration of the nation of Israel. The disciples, the audience for the Lord’s word here, had a twofold status, one as representatives of the remnant of the Jews and the other as the New Testament believers, who constitute the church. In the section of the Lord’s word that concerns the Jews ([[Matthew-24#v4|vv. 4-31]]), the disciples represent the remnant of the Jews, whereas in the section that concerns the church ([[Matthew-24#v32|24:32]]—[[Matthew-25#v30|25:30]]), they represent the New Testament believers. In the four Gospels, in matters regarding outward circumstances, the Lord treated His disciples as Jews, but in matters concerning spirit and life, He considered them New Testament believers.

## Footnotes for verse 27

### Footnote 27.1 — lightning

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v27|Matthew 24:27]]

The second coming of Christ has two aspects: one is the secret aspect, related to His watchful believers; the other is the open aspect, related to the unbelieving Jews and Gentiles. The lightning here signifies the open aspect, which will take place after the great tribulation ([[Matthew-24#v29|vv. 29-30]]), whereas the thief’s coming in [[Matthew-24#v43|v. 43]] signifies the secret aspect, which will occur before the great tribulation. Lightning may be considered as being concealed in a cloud, waiting for an opportunity to flash forth. In a similar way, Christ will be clothed with a cloud ([[Revelation-10#v1|Rev. 10:1]]) in the air for a time and then will suddenly appear, like a flash of lightning striking the earth. Hence, the Lord’s coming like a flash of lightning will be a sign of the end of the Lord’s parousia (see [note 3³](<Matthew-24.fn#Footnote 3.3 — coming>)). This implies also that the Lord is like electricity.

## Footnotes for verse 28

### Footnote 28.1 — corpse

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v28|Matthew 24:28]]

Taken in context, [[Matthew-24#v15|vv. 15]] and 21 imply that at the end of this age Antichrist will be the cause of the great tribulation. It is he who will need to be judged and destroyed. All people in Adam are dead ([[1 Corinthians-15#v22|1 Cor. 15:22]]); thus, the evil Antichrist, who with his evil armies will war against the Lord at Armageddon ([[Revelation-19#v17|Rev. 19:17-21]]), is in the eyes of the Lord a stinking corpse, good for the vultures’ appetite. Furthermore, in the Scriptures both the Lord and those who trust in Him are likened to an eagle ([[Exodus-19#v4|Exo. 19:4]]; [[Deuteronomy-32#v11|Deut. 32:11]]; [[Isaiah-40#v31|Isa. 40:31]]), and the swift, destroying armies are likened to flying eagles ([[Deuteronomy-28#v49|Deut. 28:49]]; [[Hosea-08#v1|Hosea 8:1]]). Hence, the vultures here, being predatory birds of the eagle kind, refer undoubtedly to Christ and His overcomers, who will come as a swift, flying army to war against Antichrist and his armies and destroy them at Armageddon, thus executing God’s judgment upon them. This indicates not only that at His appearing, Christ with His overcoming saints will appear in the place where Antichrist is with his armies, but also that Christ with His overcomers will appear swiftly from the air like vultures. This corresponds with the flash of lightning in the foregoing verse.

## Footnotes for verse 37

### Footnote 37.1 — days

The Lord’s coming (parousia) will be like the days of Noah. This indicates that when the Lord’s parousia is about to come, it will be like the days of Noah; that is, the situation before the Lord’s coming will be like that in the days of Noah.

## Footnotes for verse 38

### Footnote 38.1 — For

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v38|Matthew 24:38]]

For indicates that this verse is the explanation of why and how the Lord’s parousia will be like the days of Noah. In the days of Noah the following conditions existed: (1) people were befuddled by eating, drinking, marrying, and giving in marriage, and (2) they did not know that judgment was coming, until the flood came and took them away. When the Lord’s parousia is about to come, people will likewise be befuddled by the necessities of this life and will not know that God’s judgment (signified by the flood) will come upon them by the Lord’s appearing. The believers, however, should be de-drugged and should soberly know that Christ is coming to execute God’s judgment upon this corrupted world.

### Footnote 38.2 — eating

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v38|Matthew 24:38]]

In the beginning, eating, drinking, and marriage were ordained by God for man’s existence. But because of man’s lust Satan utilizes these necessities of human life to occupy man and keep him from God’s interest. At the end of this age, near the coming of the Lord’s parousia, this kind of situation will be intensified.

### Footnote 48.1 — evil

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v48|Matthew 24:48]]

The evil slave is a saved believer, as seen in the fact that (1) he was appointed by the Lord ([[Matthew-24#v45|v. 45]]), (2) he called the Lord “my Master,” and (3) he believed that the Lord was coming.

## Footnotes for verse 49

### Footnote 49.1 — beat

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v49|Matthew 24:49]]

This is to mistreat fellow believers.

### Footnote 49.2 — eats

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v49|Matthew 24:49]]

This is to keep company with worldly people, who are drunk with worldly things.

## Footnotes for verse 51

### Footnote 51.1 — cut

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v51|Matthew 24:51]]

Referring to being cut off from the glorious Christ, from the glory of His kingdom, and from His glorious presence in His kingdom, unable to participate in Christ and the glory of His kingdom in the manifestation of the kingdom, which the faithful slaves will enjoy ([[Matthew-24#v45|v. 45]]; [[Matthew-25#v21|25:21]], [[Matthew-25#v23|23]]). This corresponds with cast out—into the outer darkness in the conclusion of the parable of the talents ([[Matthew-25#v14|25:14-30]]), which completes this section, [[Matthew-24#v45|vv. 45-51]].

### Footnote 51.2 — portion

**Verse:** [[Matthew-24#v51|Matthew 24:51]]

This is not to perish eternally but to be chastened dispensationally. See [note 28¹](<Hebrews-12.fn#Footnote 28.1 — kingdom>) in [[Hebrews-12|Heb. 12]].
