## Footnotes for verse 9

### Footnote 9.1 — Samaritans

**Verse:** [[John-04#v9|John 4:9]]

Samaria was the leading region of the northern kingdom of Israel and was the site of its capital ([[1 Kings-16#v24|1 Kings 16:24]], [[1 Kings-16#v29|29]]). Around 700 B.C., the Assyrians captured Samaria and brought people from Babylon and other heathen countries to the cities of Samaria ([[2 Kings-17#v6|2 Kings 17:6]], [[2 Kings-17#v24|24]]). From that time the Samaritans became a people of mixed heathen and Jewish blood. History tells us that they had the Pentateuch (the five books of Moses) and worshipped God according to that part of the Old Testament. But they were never recognized by the Jews as being part of the Jewish people.

## Footnotes for verse 18

### Footnote 18.1 — five

**Verse:** [[John-04#v18|John 4:18]]

The woman tried the first husband, drinking of that “water,” and she was not satisfied. Then she tried the second, third, fourth, and fifth husbands. Since none of these satisfied her, she was trying another. Her changing of husbands fully proved that however much she drank of that “water,” she was still thirsty. “Everyone who drinks of this water shall thirst again.” This word of the Lord’s is true!

## Footnotes for verse 24

### Footnote 24.1 — God

**Verse:** [[John-04#v24|John 4:24]]

God here is the complete Triune God—the Father, the Son, and the Spirit.

### Footnote 24.2 — Spirit

**Verse:** [[John-04#v24|John 4:24]]

Spirit here refers to the nature of the complete Triune God; it does not refer merely to the Lord Spirit. To worship God, who is Spirit, we must worship with our spirit, which is of the same nature as He is.

### Footnote 24.3 — worship

**Verse:** [[John-04#v24|John 4:24]]

This word was given to instruct the Samaritan woman regarding her need to exercise her spirit to contact God the Spirit. To contact God the Spirit with the spirit is to drink of the living water, and to drink of the living water is to render real worship to God.

### Footnote 24.4 — spirit

**Verse:** [[John-04#v24|John 4:24]]

This is our human spirit. According to typology, God should be worshipped (1) in the place chosen by God for His habitation ([[Deuteronomy-12#v5|Deut. 12:5]], [[Deuteronomy-12#v11|11]], [[Deuteronomy-12#v13|13-14]], [[Deuteronomy-12#v18|18]]), and (2) with the offerings ([[Leviticus-01|Lev. 1—6]]). The place chosen by God for His habitation typifies the human spirit, where God’s habitation is today ([[Ephesians-02#v22|Eph. 2:22]]). The offerings typify Christ; Christ is the fulfillment and reality of all the offerings with which the people worshipped God. Hence, when the Lord instructed the woman to worship God the Spirit in spirit and truthfulness, He meant that she should contact God the Spirit in her spirit instead of in a specific location, and through Christ instead of with the offerings. Since Christ, as the reality that issues in the human virtue of truthfulness, has come ([[John-04#v25|vv. 25-26]]), all the shadows and types are over.

### Footnote 24.5 — truthfulness

**Verse:** [[John-04#v24|John 4:24]]

According to the context of this chapter and the entire revelation of John’s Gospel, truthfulness here denotes the divine reality becoming man’s genuineness and sincerity (which are the opposite of the hypocrisy of the immoral Samaritan worshipper—[[John-04#v16|vv. 16-18]]) for the true worship of God. The divine reality is Christ (who is the reality—[[John-14#v6|14:6]]) as the reality of all the offerings of the Old Testament for the worship of God ([[John-01#v29|1:29]]; [[John-03#v14|3:14]]) and as the fountain of the living water, the life-giving Spirit ([[John-04#v7|vv. 7-15]]), partaken of and drunk by His believers to be the reality within them, which eventually becomes their genuineness and sincerity in which they worship God with the worship that He seeks. See [note 6⁶](<1 John-01.fn#Footnote 6.6 — truth>) in [[1 John-01|1 John 1]]; [[Romans-03#v7|Rom. 3:7]]; and [note 8²](<Romans-15.fn#Footnote 8.2 — truthfulness>) in [[Romans-15|Rom. 15]].

## Footnotes for verse 54

### Footnote 54.1 — second

**Verse:** [[John-04#v54|John 4:54]]

The first sign in Cana ([[John-02#v1|2:1-11]]) signifies the changing of death into life, setting forth the principle of life. The second sign here is a continuation, an application of the principle of the changing of death into life. The source of death is the tree of knowledge, and the source of life is the tree of life (cf. [[Genesis-02#v9|Gen. 2:9]], [[Genesis-02#v17|17]]).
