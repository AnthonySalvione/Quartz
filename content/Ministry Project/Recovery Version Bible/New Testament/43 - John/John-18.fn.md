## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — these

**Verse:** [[John-18#v8|John 18:8]]

While suffering betrayal at the hands of His false disciple and arrest by the soldiers, the Lord still took good care of His disciples. This reveals that He was at ease in passing through the process of death.

## Footnotes for verse 13

### Footnote 13.1 — led

**Verse:** [[John-18#v13|John 18:13]]

The Lord was the Lamb of God ([[John-01#v29|1:29]]), and He was killed on the day of the Passover ([[John-18#v28|v. 28]]). As the passover lamb was examined before it was killed ([[Exodus-12#v3|Exo. 12:3-6]]), so He was examined by the whole of mankind, represented by the high priest of the Jews and the governor of the Romans, and proved to be without blemish ([[John-18#v38|v. 38b]]; [[John-19#v4|19:4]], [[John-19#v6|6]]). See [note 37¹](<Mark-12.fn#Footnote 37.1 — son>) in [[Mark-12|Mark 12]].

## Footnotes for verse 32

### Footnote 32.1 — kind

**Verse:** [[John-18#v32|John 18:32]]

The Jews’ way to put criminals to death was to stone them ([[Leviticus-24#v16|Lev. 24:16]]). But the Lord Jesus predicted, according to the type in the Old Testament ([[Numbers-21#v8|Num. 21:8-9]]), that He would be lifted up ([[John-03#v14|3:14]]; [[John-08#v28|8:28]]; [[John-12#v32|12:32]]). It was of God’s sovereignty that not long before that time the Roman Empire had made it a law that criminals sentenced to death should be crucified. It was in this way that the Lord was executed. This proves that the Lord’s death was not accidental but was predetermined by God ([[Acts-02#v23|Acts 2:23]]).
