### Footnote 1.2 — Gaius

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v1|3 John 1:1]]

Not the Gaius of Macedonia in [[Acts-19#v29|Acts 19:29]], nor the Gaius of Derbe in [[Acts-20#v4|Acts 20:4]], nor the Gaius of Corinth in [[1 Corinthians-01#v14|1 Cor. 1:14]] and [[Romans-16#v23|Rom. 16:23]], but another named Gaius (the name was very common at that time). According to the contents of this Epistle, he must have been an outstanding brother in the church.

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — all

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v2|3 John 1:2]]

According to the context of the verse, this refers to external and material things.

### Footnote 2.2 — wish

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v2|3 John 1:2]]

Probably in the sense of prayer.

### Footnote 2.3 — prosper

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v2|3 John 1:2]]

The Greek word means to go on well, as in a journey ([[Romans-01#v10|Rom. 1:10]]). I.e., to succeed in reaching a desired end; thus, to prosper.

### Footnote 2.4 — health

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v2|3 John 1:2]]

Bodily health, as in [[Luke-05#v31|Luke 5:31]]; [[Luke-07#v10|7:10]]; and 15:27.

### Footnote 2.5 — soul

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v2|3 John 1:2]]

Man is of three parts: spirit, soul, and body ([[1 Thessalonians-05#v23|1 Thes. 5:23]] and [note 5](<1 Thessalonians-05.fn#Footnote 23.5 — spirit>)). The soul is the organ that mediates between the body and the spirit; it possesses self-consciousness, that man may have his personality. It is contained in the body and is the vessel to contain the spirit. As to the believer, God as the Spirit dwells in his regenerated spirit ([[Romans-08#v9|Rom. 8:9]], [[Romans-08#v16|16]]) and spreads from his spirit to saturate his soul that it may be transformed to express Him ([[Romans-12#v2|Rom. 12:2]]; [[2 Corinthians-03#v18|2 Cor. 3:18]]). This is the prosperity of the believer’s soul. When our soul is occupied and directed by the Spirit of God through our spirit so that it directs and uses our body for God’s purposes, it prospers. The apostle wished that the recipient, who was a beloved brother, outstanding in such a prosperity of his soul, might prosper in all things and in bodily health, just as his soul prospered in the divine life.

### Footnote 3.1 — rejoiced

See [note 4¹](<2 John-01.fn#Footnote 4.1 — rejoiced>) in [[2 John-01#v1|2 John 1]].

### Footnote 3.2 — your

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v3|3 John 1:3]]

Lit., your truth. This is the truth concerning Christ, especially His deity (see [[2 John-01.fn#Footnote 1.5 — truth|note 1⁵]] in [[2 John-01|2 John]]). By the revelation of this truth the recipient’s way of life was determined, and the recipient held to this truth as his fundamental belief. It is the objective truth that had become subjective in the recipient’s daily walk.

### Footnote 3.3 — walk

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v3|3 John 1:3]]

The recipient not only held to the truth but also walked and lived in the truth. The truth concerning the person of Christ should be not only our belief but also our living, a living that testifies to our belief.

### Footnote 3.4 — truth

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v3|3 John 1:3]]

See [note 4²](<2 John-01.fn#Footnote 4.2 — truth>) in [[2 John-01#v1|2 John 1]].

## Footnotes for verse 5

### Footnote 5.1 — whatever

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v5|3 John 1:5]]

Referring to the providing of hospitality (as taught by Paul in [[Romans-12#v13|Rom. 12:13]] and [[Hebrews-13#v2|Heb. 13:2]]), the receiving of the brothers ([[3 John-01#v7|vv. 7-8]]) who traveled for the gospel and the ministry of the word.

### Footnote 5.2 — strangers

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v5|3 John 1:5]]

Most of the recipients of the hospitality were strangers, unacquainted with Gaius.

## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — love

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v6|3 John 1:6]]

In the past.

### Footnote 6.2 — church

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v6|3 John 1:6]]

The church where the apostle was.

### Footnote 6.3 — will

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v6|3 John 1:6]]

In the future. On one hand, the apostle praised Gaius for what he had done in receiving the traveling brothers in the past; on the other hand, he encouraged him to send them forward in the future.

### Footnote 6.4 — worthy

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v6|3 John 1:6]]

The sending forward should be in a manner that matches God, who is generous, indicating that the sending forward must be with generosity.

## Footnotes for verse 7

### Footnote 7.1 — Name

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v7|3 John 1:7]]

The exalted and glorious name of the wonderful Christ ([[Philippians-02#v9|Phil. 2:9]] and [note 3](<Philippians-02.fn#Footnote 9.3 — name>); [[Acts-05#v41|Acts 5:41]]; [[James-02#v7|James 2:7]]).

### Footnote 7.2 — Gentiles

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v7|3 John 1:7]]

The pagans, who have nothing to do with God’s move on this earth to carry out His economy. If anyone who works for God’s New Testament economy receives help for God’s work, especially financial support, from the unbelievers, this is a shame and even an insult to God. In the apostle’s time, the brothers who worked for God took nothing from the pagans.

## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — support

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v8|3 John 1:8]]

The Greek word is made up of two words: under and to take; thus, to take up (from underneath), that is, to undertake, to sustain, to support. We, the believers, including the apostle, ought to support and undertake for the needs of the brothers who work for God in His divine truth and who take nothing from the Gentiles.

### Footnote 8.2 — truth

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v8|3 John 1:8]]

Denoting the revealed divine reality as the contents of the New Testament, according to the apostles’ teaching concerning the Divine Trinity, especially the person of the Lord Jesus, for God’s economy. All the apostles and faithful brothers worked for this.

## Footnotes for verse 9

### Footnote 9.1 — church

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v9|3 John 1:9]]

Of which Gaius was a member.

### Footnote 9.2 — Diotrephes

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v9|3 John 1:9]]

The Greek word is made up of of Zeus (Zeus was the chief of the gods in the Greek pantheon) and to nourish; hence, Zeus-nourished. This indicates that Diotrephes, a professing Christian, never dropped his pagan name, contrary to the practice of the early believers, who took a Christian name at their baptism. According to history, he advocated the Gnostic heresy, which blasphemed the person of Christ. It is no wonder that he mingled with the believers as one who loved to be first among them.

### Footnote 9.3 — to

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v9|3 John 1:9]]

This is against the words of the Lord in [[Matthew-20#v25|Matt. 20:25-27]] and [[Matthew-23#v8|23:8-11]], which keep all His believers on the same level, that of brothers. In [[2 John-01#v9|2 John 9]] the Cerinthian Gnostics took the lead to advance in doctrine beyond the teaching concerning Christ. Here in [[3 John-01#v9|3 John 9]] is one who was under the influence of Gnostic heretical doctrine and loved to be first in the church. The former was a matter of intellectual arrogance; the latter, of self-exaltation in one’s actions. These two evils are sharp weapons used by God’s enemy, Satan, to execute his evil plot against God’s economy. One damages the believers’ faith in the divine reality; the other frustrates the believers’ work in God’s move.

### Footnote 9.4 — receive

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v9|3 John 1:9]]

I.e., receive hospitably.

## Footnotes for verse 10

### Footnote 10.1 — babbling

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v10|3 John 1:10]]

The Greek word is derived from a word meaning to boil over, to overflow with words, to talk idly; hence, to babble, to speak folly or nonsense.

### Footnote 10.2 — evil

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v10|3 John 1:10]]

The Greek word means pernicious, as in [[1 John-05#v19|1 John 5:19]] (see [[1 John-05.fn#Footnote 19.4 — evil|note 4]] there).

## Footnotes for verse 11

### Footnote 11.1 — evil

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v11|3 John 1:11]]

The Greek word means worthless, wicked, depraved.

### Footnote 11.2 — does

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v11|3 John 1:11]]

The Greek word means to be a well-doer (as a favor or a duty), practicing good; hence, to do good.

### Footnote 11.3 — of

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v11|3 John 1:11]]

Lit., out of (see [note 19¹](<1 John-05.fn#Footnote 19.1 — of>) in [[1 John-05|1 John 5]]). God is the source of good. A well-doer, a doer of good, is one who is out of God and who belongs to God (cf. [[1 John-03#v8|1 John 3:8]]).

### Footnote 11.4 — does

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v11|3 John 1:11]]

The Greek word means to be an evildoer, practicing evil; hence, to do evil.

### Footnote 11.5 — not

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v11|3 John 1:11]]

An evildoer not only is not out of God but has not even seen God.

## Footnotes for verse 12

### Footnote 12.1 — Demetrius

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v12|3 John 1:12]]

Demetrius, who might have been one of the brothers who traveled in working for the Lord ([[3 John-01#v5|vv. 5-8]]), perhaps was the one who bore this Epistle to Gaius. Hence, it was necessary that he receive a favorable and strong commendation from the writer.

### Footnote 12.2 — all

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v12|3 John 1:12]]

This indicates that Demetrius must have been a brother working among the churches, and thus was well known.

### Footnote 12.3 — truth

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v12|3 John 1:12]]

The revealed truth of God as the reality of the essence of the Christian faith (see [note 6⁶](<1 John-01.fn#Footnote 6.6 — truth>) in [[1 John-01|1 John 1]]). This truth is the divine rule for the walk of all believers, and by it the believers’ walk is determined. Thus, it gives good testimony to him who walks in it, as it did to Demetrius.

### Footnote 12.4 — we

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v12|3 John 1:12]]

The apostle John and his associates.

### Footnote 12.5 — true

**Verse:** [[3 John-01#v12|3 John 1:12]]

The Greek word (an adjective akin to truth (reality)—cf. [note 20⁵](<1 John-05.fn#Footnote 20.5 — true>) in [[1 John-05|1 John 5]]) means genuine, real; hence, true.
