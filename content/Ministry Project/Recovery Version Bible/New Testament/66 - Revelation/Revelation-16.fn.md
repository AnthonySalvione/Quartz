# Revelation-16 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Revelation-16|Revelation 16]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — bowls

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v1|Revelation 16:1]]

The bowls here signify that though the seven last plagues, the plagues of the seven bowls, are the ultimate fury of God, His fury is limited; otherwise, the entire earth with its inhabitants would be destroyed. For the fulfillment of His eternal purpose, God, in His judgment upon the earth, still places a limit on His ultimate fury.

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — sore

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v2|Revelation 16:2]]

In His ultimate fury God will mark the rebellious ones with a sore on their skin because they bear the mark of the beast. This shows that what God does in His ultimate fury toward the rebellious ones is a reaction to what Antichrist does.

## Footnotes for verse 3

### Footnote 3.1 — every

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v3|Revelation 16:3]]

Or, every soul of life; referring to every animal that has a soul.

See [note 4⁴](<Revelation-01.fn#Footnote 4.4 — who>) in [[Revelation-01|Revelation 1]].

## Footnotes for verse 12

### Footnote 12.1 — sixth

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v12|Revelation 16:12]]

The plague of the second bowl is more severe than that of the second trumpet ([[Revelation-08#v8|8:8-9]]); the plague of the third bowl is more severe than that of the third trumpet ([[Revelation-08#v10|8:10-11]]); and the plague of the fourth bowl is more severe than that of the fourth trumpet ([[Revelation-08#v12|8:12]]). The plague of the fifth bowl, the judgment upon the throne of Antichrist and his kingdom, is related to the fifth trumpet, in which Antichrist, as the king of the demon-possessed locusts, torments men ([[Revelation-09#v7|9:7-11]]); and the plague of the sixth bowl is related to the sixth trumpet ([[Revelation-09#v14|9:14]]), because the plagues of both the sixth bowl and the sixth trumpet are related to the same river, the Euphrates.

## Footnotes for verse 13

### Footnote 13.1 — unclean

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v13|Revelation 16:13]]

The unclean spirits will stir up the rebellious kings ([[Revelation-16#v14|v. 14]]) to send forth their forces to persecute God’s people. Being spirits, the unclean spirits should be in the heavenlies, but having become frogs, they can move about only on the earth. This indicates that Satan and his power to act will be confined to the earth.

## Footnotes for verse 15

### Footnote 15.1 — come

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v15|Revelation 16:15]]

According to the context, this word must be spoken by the Lord at the end of the great tribulation, before the war at Armageddon. This proves that at that time there will still be believers (the remaining, living majority of the believers) left on the earth. To them, the appearing of the Lord in His coming back will still be like the coming of a thief, at an hour unknown to them.

## Footnotes for verse 16

### Footnote 16.1 — gathered

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v16|Revelation 16:16]]

At the end of the great tribulation three unclean spirits out of the mouths of Satan, Antichrist, and the false prophet will go forth to stir up the kings of the whole inhabited earth and to gather their forces ([[Revelation-16#v13|vv. 13-14]])—including the two hundred million horsemen mentioned in [[Revelation-09#v14|9:14-16]] (see [note 12¹](<Revelation-16.fn#Footnote 12.1 — sixth>))—to the war at Armageddon, which will be the last war among mankind before the millennium. In that war Satan’s intention will be to destroy Israel ([[Zechariah-14#v2|Zech. 14:2]]) and fight against Christ and His army. For this he will use all of rebellious mankind ([[Revelation-17#v12|17:12-14]]; [[Revelation-19#v11|19:11-19]]). Christ and His chosen overcomers will defeat and destroy them all ([[Revelation-19#v20|19:20-21]]; [[Micah-04#v11|Micah 4:11-13]]; [[Zephaniah-03#v8|Zeph. 3:8]]; [[Zechariah-14#v3|Zech. 14:3]], [[Zechariah-14#v12|12-15]]; [[Zechariah-12#v4|12:4]], [[Zechariah-12#v9|9]]) and will save the nation of Israel ([[Zechariah-12#v3|Zech. 12:3-8]]; [[Zechariah-14#v4|14:4-5]]; [[Joel-03#v14|Joel 3:14-17]]). This is the treading of the winepress, which is recorded in [[Revelation-14#v17|14:17-20]], [[Isaiah-63#v1|Isa. 63:1-6]], and [[Joel-03#v9|Joel 3:9-13]].

### Footnote 16.2 — Armageddon

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v16|Revelation 16:16]]

The word is a Hebrew compound of Har, meaning mountain, and Megiddon, i.e., Megiddo ([[Judges-05#v19|Judg. 5:19]]; [[2 Kings-23#v29|2 Kings 23:29]]; [[Zechariah-12#v11|Zech. 12:11]]), meaning slaughter; hence, the mountain of slaughter.

## Footnotes for verse 17

### Footnote 17.1 — temple

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v17|Revelation 16:17]]

The temple is for God’s testimony, whereas the throne is for God’s judgment. Out of the temple from the throne indicates that God’s judgment comes out of God’s testimony and is for God’s testimony. Once the last bowl has been poured out, marking the end of the great tribulation, every negative thing will have been eradicated, and then the bride will appear ([[Revelation-19#v7|19:7-9]]).

### Footnote 17.2 — done

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v17|Revelation 16:17]]

Or, over, passed, accomplished. This refers to the fact that everything has been accomplished for God’s judgment and for God’s expression, God’s testimony.

## Footnotes for verse 18

### Footnote 18.1 — earthquake

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v18|Revelation 16:18]]

This earthquake is the same as that associated with the seventh trumpet ([[Revelation-11#v19|11:19]]). See [note 19²](<Revelation-11.fn#Footnote 19.2 — earthquake>) in [[Revelation-11|Revelation 11]].

## Footnotes for verse 19

### Footnote 19.1 — great

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v19|Revelation 16:19]]

The great city is the city of Jerusalem. See [note 8¹](<Revelation-11.fn#Footnote 8.1 — great>) in [[Revelation-11|Revelation 11]].

### Footnote 19.2 — Babylon

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v19|Revelation 16:19]]

The religious, mysterious Babylon mentioned in [[Revelation-14#v8|14:8]] will be destroyed at the beginning of the great tribulation (see [note 8¹](<Revelation-14.fn#Footnote 8.1 — Babylon>) in [[Revelation-14|Revelation 14]]); therefore, Babylon the Great here, which will be destroyed immediately after the war at Armageddon at the end of the great tribulation, must be the material Babylon, the city of Rome. The Babylon in [[Revelation-14#v8|14:8]] corresponds with the one in [[Revelation-17|Revelation 17]], while the Babylon here corresponds with the one in [[Revelation-18|Revelation 18]]. The sketches of the two Babylons are recorded in [[Revelation-14#v8|14:8]] and this verse; the details are given in [[Revelation-17|Revelation 17]] and [[Revelation-18|18]].

### Footnote 19.3 — wine

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v19|Revelation 16:19]]

Rome gave the wine of the fury of her fornication to the nations ([[Revelation-18#v3|18:3]]). Now God in His vengeance gives her the wine of the fury of His wrath.

## Footnotes for verse 21

### Footnote 21.1 — talent

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v21|Revelation 16:21]]

A talent equals about one hundred pounds.

### Footnote 21.2 — blasphemed

**Verse:** [[Revelation-16#v21|Revelation 16:21]]

This proves that the people have no intention of repenting; rather, they will oppose God to the end.
