---
Author: Paul
---
## v1

[[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 1.1 — What|What]] then shall we say that Abraham our [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 1.2 — forefather|forefather]] according to the flesh has found?

## v2

For if Abraham was justified out of [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 2.1 — works|works]], he has something to boast in, but not before God.

## v3

For what does the Scripture say? “And Abraham [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 3.1 — believed|believed]] God, and it was accounted to him as righteousness.”

## v4

Now to the [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 4.1 — one|one]] who works, his [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 4.2 — wages|wages]] are not accounted according to grace, but according to what is due.

## v5

But to the one who does not work, but believes on Him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is accounted as righteousness.

## v6

Even as David also speaks blessing on the man to whom God accounts righteousness apart from works:

## v7

“Blessed are they whose lawlessnesses have been forgiven, and whose sins have been covered over.

## v8

Blessed is the man to whom the Lord shall by no means account sin.”

## v9

Is this blessing then upon the circumcision only, or also upon the uncircumcision? For we say, Faith was accounted to Abraham as righteousness.

## v10

How then was it accounted? While he was in circumcision or in uncircumcision? Not in circumcision, but in uncircumcision.

## v11

And he received the [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 11.1 — sign|sign]] of circumcision, a seal of the [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 11.2 — righteousness|righteousness]] of the faith which he had while in uncircumcision, that he might be the father of all those in uncircumcision who believe, that righteousness might be accounted to them also,

## v12

And the father of circumcision to those who not only are of the circumcision, but who also [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 12.1 — walk|walk]] in the steps of that faith of our father Abraham, which he had in uncircumcision.

## v13

For it was not through the law that the promise was made to Abraham or to his seed that he would be the [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 13.1 — heir|heir]] of the world, but through the [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 13.2 — righteousness|righteousness]] of faith.

## v14

For if those of the law are heirs, faith has been made void and the promise has been annulled;

## v15

For the law works out wrath; but where there is no law, neither is there transgression.

## v16

Therefore the inheritance is out of faith that it might be according to grace, so that the promise may be certain to all the seed, not to that which is of the law only, but also to that which is of the faith of Abraham, who is the [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 16.1 — father|father]] of us all

## v17

(As it is written, “I have appointed you a father of many nations”) in the sight of God whom he [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 17.1 — believed|believed]], who [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 17.2 — gives|gives]] life to the dead and [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 17.3 — calls|calls]] the things not being as being.

## v18

He beyond hope believed [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 18.1 — in|in]] hope in order that he might become the father of many nations, according to that which was spoken, “So shall your seed be.”

## v19

And not weakening in his faith, he considered his own body as [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 19.1 — already|already]] dead, being about a hundred years old, as well as the deadening of Sarah’s womb;

## v20

But with regard to the promise of God, he did not doubt in unbelief, but was empowered by faith, giving glory to God

## v21

And being fully persuaded that what He had promised He was able also to do.

## v22

Therefore also it was accounted to him as righteousness.

## v23

Now it was not written for his sake only that it was accounted to him,

## v24

But for ours also to whom it is to be accounted, who believe on [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 24.1 — Him|Him]] who has raised Jesus our Lord from the dead,

## v25

Who was delivered for our offenses and was [[Romans-04.fn#Footnote 25.1 — raised|raised]] for our justification.
