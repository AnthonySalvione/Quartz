## Footnotes for verse 15

### Footnote 15.1 — resurrection

**Verse:** [[Acts-24#v15|Acts 24:15]]

The resurrection of the righteous will occur before the millennium at the Lord’s coming back ([[1 Corinthians-15#v23|1 Cor. 15:23]]; [[1 Thessalonians-04#v16|1 Thes. 4:16]]). This will be the resurrection of life ([[John-05#v28|John 5:28-29a]] and notes; [[Daniel-12#v2|Dan. 12:2a]]) and the resurrection of reward ([[Luke-14#v14|Luke 14:14]]), which includes the first, or the best, resurrection ([[Revelation-20#v4|Rev. 20:4-6]] and [[Revelation-20.fn#Footnote 5.2 — first|notes 5²]] and [[Revelation-20.fn#Footnote 6.2 — first|6²]]), the out-resurrection ([[Philippians-03#v11|Phil. 3:11]] and [note 2](<Philippians-03.fn#Footnote 11.2 — out>)). The resurrection of the unrighteous will occur after the millennium ([[Revelation-20#v5|Rev. 20:5]]). This will be the resurrection of judgment ([[John-05#v29|John 5:29b]] and notes) and of shame and everlasting contempt ([[Daniel-12#v2|Dan. 12:2b]]), and will be for the judgment of eternal perdition upon the unrighteous ([[Revelation-20#v12|Rev. 20:12-15]] and [[Revelation-20.fn#Footnote 12.1 — standing|note 12¹]]). It was concerning this judgment that the apostle warned the unrighteous Felix in [[Acts-24#v25|v. 25]] (see [[Acts-24.fn#Footnote 25.2 — righteousness|note 2]] there).

## Footnotes for verse 24

### Footnote 24.1 — Drusilla

**Verse:** [[Acts-24#v24|Acts 24:24]]

A daughter of King Herod Agrippa. She was persuaded by Felix, who became enamored of her, to forsake her husband and marry him. This showed the intemperance and corruption of Felix, a Roman politician. See [note 1³](<Acts-23.fn#Footnote 1.3 — conscience>) in [[Acts-23|Acts 23]].

## Footnotes for verse 25

### Footnote 25.1 — reasoning

**Verse:** [[Acts-24#v25|Acts 24:25]]

Lit., saying thoroughly, discussing (in argument or exhortation), disputing. The same as in [[Acts-17#v2|17:2]]; [[Acts-18#v4|18:4]], [[Acts-18#v19|19]].

### Footnote 25.2 — righteousness

**Verse:** [[Acts-24#v25|Acts 24:25]]

Realizing Felix’s unrighteousness ([[Acts-24#v26|vv. 26-27]]) and intemperance (see [note 24¹](<Acts-24.fn#Footnote 24.1 — Drusilla>)), the apostle reasoned with Felix regarding righteousness and self-control, the control of passions and desires, especially, here, sexual desires. The coming judgment is related to the resurrection of the unrighteous, which the apostle preached in [[Acts-24#v15|v. 15]] (see [note 25¹](<Acts-24.fn#Footnote 25.1 — reasoning>) there). The apostle reasoned with Felix regarding the coming judgment also; this was a warning to Felix. Through this, Felix became afraid. See notes [42¹](<Acts-10.fn#Footnote 42.1 — Judge>) in [[Acts-10|Acts 10]] and [31¹](<Acts-17.fn#Footnote 31.1 — day>) in [[Acts-17|Acts 17]].

## Footnotes for verse 27

### Footnote 27.1 — two

**Verse:** [[Acts-24#v27|Acts 24:27]]

Luke does not disclose what the apostle did during these two years. He might have used the time to be with the Lord for His move on earth. If so, this might have influenced the Epistles he wrote during the time of his appeal in Rome—Colossians, Ephesians, and Philippians—which are the most mysterious, most profound, and richest in the divine revelation. The supply that they have brought to the church throughout the generations cannot be fully told.

### Footnote 27.2 — Festus

**Verse:** [[Acts-24#v27|Acts 24:27]]

Felix’s successor as the governor of Judea.

### Footnote 27.3 — gain

**Verse:** [[Acts-24#v27|Acts 24:27]]

Lit., grant favor to; i.e., grant favor to gain favor. This again showed the corruption of Roman politics.
