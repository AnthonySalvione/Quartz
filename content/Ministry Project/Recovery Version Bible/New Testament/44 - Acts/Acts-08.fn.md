# Acts-08 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Acts-08|Acts 8]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — church

**Verse:** [[Acts-08#v1|Acts 8:1]]

This was the first church established in a locality (see [note 11¹](<Acts-05.fn#Footnote 11.1 — church>) in [[Acts-05|Acts 5]]) within the jurisdiction of a city, the city of Jerusalem. It was a local church in its locality, as indicated by the Lord in [[Matthew-18#v17|Matt. 18:17]]. It was not the universal church, as revealed by the Lord in [[Matthew-16#v18|Matt. 16:18]], but only a part of the universal church, which is the Body of Christ ([[Ephesians-01#v22|Eph. 1:22-23]]). The record concerning this matter (the establishing of the church in its locality) is consistent throughout the New Testament ([[Acts-13#v1|13:1]]; [[Acts-14#v23|14:23]]; [[Romans-16#v1|Rom. 16:1]]; [[1 Corinthians-01#v2|1 Cor. 1:2]]; [[2 Corinthians-08#v1|2 Cor. 8:1]]; [[Galatians-01#v2|Gal. 1:2]]; [[Revelation-01#v4|Rev. 1:4]], [[Revelation-01#v11|11]]).

## Footnotes for verse 16

### Footnote 16.1 — not

**Verse:** [[Acts-08#v16|Acts 8:16]]

This does not mean that the believers in Samaria did not receive the Holy Spirit within them essentially when they believed in the Lord. According to the teaching of the New Testament in [[Ephesians-01#v13|Eph. 1:13]] and [[Galatians-03#v2|Gal 3:2]], they must have received the Holy Spirit essentially when they believed for their regeneration ([[John-03#v6|John 3:6]], [[John-03#v36|36]]). But they had not yet received the Spirit economically to identify them with the Body of Christ. The reason the Holy Spirit did not fall upon them outwardly and economically was so that the apostles, through whom the practical establishing of the church had been initiated in Jerusalem, might come to bring them into identification with the Body of Christ. This case is different from that of the ones in the house of Cornelius, who, when they believed in the Lord, received the Holy Spirit both essentially within them for regeneration and economically upon them for baptism into the Body of Christ ([[1 Corinthians-12#v13|1 Cor. 12:13]]) and identification with the Body of Christ. That was because the gospel was preached then directly by Peter, who played the main role in the initiation of the practical establishing of the church.

### Footnote 16.2 — into

**Verse:** [[Acts-08#v16|Acts 8:16]]

Not in the name but into the name. The name denotes the person. To be baptized into the name of the Lord Jesus is to be baptized into the person of the Lord, to be identified with the crucified, resurrected, and ascended Christ, to be put into an organic union with the living Lord. See notes [38³](<Acts-02.fn#Footnote 38.3 — upon>) in [[Acts-02|Acts 2]] and [19⁴](<Matthew-28.fn#Footnote 19.4 — into>) in [[Matthew-28|Matt. 28]]. 

In [[Matthew-28#v19|Matt. 28:19]] the Lord charged the disciples to baptize the believers into the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. But later, in practice, the believers were baptized into the name of the Lord Jesus, here and in [[Acts-19#v5|19:5]], and into Christ, in [[Romans-06#v3|Rom. 6:3]] and [[Galatians-03#v27|Gal 3:27]]. This indicates that (1) to be baptized into the name of the Lord Jesus is equivalent to being baptized into the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, because the Lord Jesus is the Triune God, the embodiment of God Himself ([[Colossians-02#v9|Col. 2:9]]), and (2) to be baptized into the name of the Triune God or into the name of the Lord Jesus is equivalent to being baptized into the person of Christ.

### Footnote 16.3 — name

**Verse:** [[Acts-08#v16|Acts 8:16]]

Since the Samaritan believers had been baptized into the name of the Lord Jesus, that is, into the Lord Himself, they surely must have received the Spirit of life essentially within them that they might be not only born of the Lord but also joined to the Lord ([[1 Corinthians-06#v17|1 Cor. 6:17]]), although they had not received the Spirit of power outwardly and economically.

## Footnotes for verse 17

### Footnote 17.1 — laid

**Verse:** [[Acts-08#v17|Acts 8:17]]

See [note 14⁴](<1 Timothy-04.fn#Footnote 14.4 — laying>) in [[1 Timothy-04|1 Tim. 4]]. Peter and John were sent to Samaria not only to confirm the preaching of the gospel by Philip, one of the seven appointed to serve tables, but also to bring the church in Samaria, composed of Samaritans, with whom the Jews had no dealings, into identification with the Body of Christ by laying their hands on them. The Holy Spirit honored this laying on of hands and fell upon the Samaritans, signifying their identification with the Body of Christ. In this way the Samaritan believers received the Holy Spirit economically in addition to their having received the Holy Spirit essentially when they believed in the Lord Jesus.

## Footnotes for verse 20

### Footnote 20.1 — destruction

**Verse:** [[Acts-08#v20|Acts 8:20]]

Not eternal perdition but a punishment, as in [[Hebrews-10#v39|Heb. 10:39]] (see [[Hebrews-10.fn#Footnote 39.2 — ruin|note 2]] there) and [[Matthew-07#v13|Matt 7:13]] (see [[Matthew-07.fn#Footnote 13.3 — destruction|note 3]] there). Simon had believed in the gospel and had been baptized ([[Acts-08#v13|v. 13]]); hence, he must have experienced salvation in its initial stage, but he had not been saved from his wicked thought and act concerning money. Therefore, he needed to repent of this wickedness that he might receive the Lord’s forgiveness; otherwise, he would go with his silver into punishment.

## Footnotes for verse 27

### Footnote 27.1 — Ethiopian

**Verse:** [[Acts-08#v27|Acts 8:27]]

Ethiopia is Cush ([[Isaiah-18#v1|Isa. 18:1]]), the land of the descendants of Cush, the son of Ham ([[Genesis-10#v6|Gen. 10:6]]). The gospel had spread from the racially pure Jews to the racially mixed Samaritans through Philip and Peter and John ([[Acts-08#v5|vv. 5-25]]). Now the angel of the Lord directed Philip to contact an utter Gentile from Ethiopia. Through this the gospel spread southward to Africa.

### Footnote 27.2 — worship

**Verse:** [[Acts-08#v27|Acts 8:27]]

Proving that the Ethiopian eunuch was seeking God (cf. [[Acts-17#v26|17:26-27]]).

## Footnotes for verse 29

### Footnote 29.1 — Spirit

**Verse:** [[Acts-08#v29|Acts 8:29]]

The mentioning of the Spirit here, as in [[Acts-08#v39|v. 39]]; [[Acts-10#v19|10:19]]; [[Acts-13#v2|13:2]]; and [[Acts-16#v6|16:6-7]], indicates that in this book the Lord’s move in spreading His kingdom through the preaching of the gospel was by the Spirit’s leading and directing, not by man’s device and schedule. Hence, this move was not the act of man but the act of the Spirit. This is altogether different from today’s degraded practice.

## Footnotes for verse 36

### Footnote 36.1 — water

**Verse:** [[Acts-08#v36|Acts 8:36]]

This indicates that Philip preached water baptism to the eunuch. In this gospel case, water baptism was particularly emphasized, but no mention was made of Spirit baptism. This should give us strong instruction that we must pay attention to water baptism, which signifies the believers’ identification with Christ’s death and resurrection ([[Romans-06#v3|Rom. 6:3-5]]; [[Colossians-02#v12|Col. 2:12]]), as well as to Spirit baptism. Spirit baptism produces the reality of the believers’ union with Christ in life essentially and in power economically, whereas water baptism is the believers’ affirmation of the Spirit’s reality. Both are needed (cf. [[Acts-10#v47|10:47]]), and neither can replace the other. All believers in Christ should properly have both, just as the children of Israel were baptized in the cloud (signifying the Spirit) and in the sea (signifying water)—[[1 Corinthians-10#v2|1 Cor. 10:2]]. See notes [8¹](<Mark-01.fn#Footnote 8.1 — water>) in [[Mark-01|Mark 1]], [5²](<John-03.fn#Footnote 5.2 — water>) in [[John-03|John 3]], and [6¹](<1 John-05.fn#Footnote 6.1 — He>) in [[1 John-05|1 John 5]].
