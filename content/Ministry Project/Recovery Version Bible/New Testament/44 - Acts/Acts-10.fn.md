# Acts-10 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Acts-10|Acts 10]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — Italian

**Verse:** [[Acts-10#v1|Acts 10:1]]

Here the Lord took a further step in His evangelistic work. By this He reached another utter Gentile, a man of Italy of the Roman Empire in Europe. Thus the door of the gospel was opened to all the Gentiles. It was difficult for the Jewish apostles and disciples, with their Jewish background and habit, to come near the Gentiles ([[Acts-10#v28|v. 28]]). Hence, this was an extraordinary move, needing the participation of an angel of God ([[Acts-10#v3|v. 3]]), just as when Philip approached the Ethiopian, a man from Africa, in [[Acts-08#v26|8:26]]. In the two cases the Spirit spoke to Philip and to Peter, respectively, in a particular way ([[Acts-08#v29|8:29]]; [[Acts-10#v19|10:19]]).

### Footnote 1.2 — cohort

**Verse:** [[Acts-10#v1|Acts 10:1]]

One of ten divisions of an ancient Roman legion. It was composed of six hundred men.

## Footnotes for verse 4

### Footnote 4.1 — memorial

**Verse:** [[Acts-10#v4|Acts 10:4]]

Though Cornelius was part of fallen mankind, sinful and condemned before God like all others, God accepted his prayers and his alms, whereas He rejected Cain’s ([[Genesis-04#v3|Gen. 4:3]], [[Genesis-04#v5|5]]). This must have been because of the fact that God, based on Christ’s eternal redemption and in view of Cornelius’s believing in Christ in the ensuing days, forgave him ([[Acts-10#v43|v. 43]]) according to His foreknowledge.

### Footnote 10.3 — trance

**Verse:** [[Acts-10#v10|Acts 10:10]]

The Greek word means being put out of its place, referring to a state in which a man senses that he passes out of himself and from which he comes to himself ([note 11¹](<Acts-12.fn#Footnote 11.1 — came>) in [[Acts-12|Acts 12]]), as in a dream but without sleep. It differs from a vision, as in [[Acts-10#v3|vv. 3]], [[Acts-10#v17|17]], and 19, in which definite objects are visible to human eyes. However, in this ecstasy, or trance, Peter saw a vision ([[Acts-11#v5|11:5]]).

## Footnotes for verse 44

### Footnote 44.1 — fell

**Verse:** [[Acts-10#v44|Acts 10:44]]

Outwardly and economically (see [note 8²](<Acts-01.fn#Footnote 8.2 — upon>) in [[Acts-01|Acts 1]]). In the case of the house of Cornelius, the Holy Spirit’s entering into the believers essentially for life and falling upon them economically for power occurred simultaneously when they believed in the Lord. However, only His falling upon them economically is noted here, because it was outward and could be realized by others through their speaking in tongues and magnifying God ([[Acts-10#v46|v. 46]]). His entering into them occurred silently and invisibly. They received both aspects of the Holy Spirit directly from Christ the Head, without any mediatorial channel, before they were baptized in water by other members of the Body of Christ. This indicates emphatically that the spreading of the gospel of the kingdom of God to the Gentiles was of the Lord, and that the Head of the Body baptized the Gentile believers into His Body directly, without the laying on of hands of any member of His Body, in contrast to the cases of the Samaritan believers and Saul of Tarsus ([[Acts-08#v17|8:17]]; [[Acts-09#v17|9:17]]).

### Footnote 44.2 — hearing

**Verse:** [[Acts-10#v44|Acts 10:44]]

This included their believing into the Lord ([[Acts-10#v43|v. 43]]; [[John-05#v24|John 5:24]]; [[Romans-10#v14|Rom. 10:14]]; [[Ephesians-01#v13|Eph. 1:13]]).

## Footnotes for verse 46

### Footnote 46.1 — speaking

**Verse:** [[Acts-10#v46|Acts 10:46]]

Speaking in tongues was not the unique issue of receiving the Holy Spirit economically, because in this case the magnifying, that is, the praising, of God was also one of its issues, as was prophesying in the case of the twelve believers in Ephesus ([[Acts-19#v6|19:6]]). Hence, speaking in tongues is not the only evidence of receiving the Holy Spirit economically; neither is it the necessary evidence, because there is at least one case of the receiving of the Holy Spirit economically, the case of the Samaritan believers ([[Acts-08#v15|8:15-17]]), which does not mention speaking in tongues. In the case of Saul of Tarsus ([[Acts-09#v17|9:17]]) concerning this matter, there again is no mention of speaking in tongues, although he told us later, in [[1 Corinthians-14#v18|1 Cor. 14:18]], that he spoke in tongues.

### Footnote 46.2 — magnifying

**Verse:** [[Acts-10#v46|Acts 10:46]]

The same expression as in [[Luke-01#v46|Luke 1:46]].

## Footnotes for verse 47

### Footnote 47.1 — as

**Verse:** [[Acts-10#v47|Acts 10:47]]

Like the early apostles and the Jewish believers on the day of Pentecost ([[Acts-02#v4|2:4]]), the Gentile believers in the house of Cornelius received the Holy Spirit economically directly from the ascended Head. Only these two instances in the New Testament are considered the baptism in the Holy Spirit ([[Acts-01#v5|1:5]]; [[Acts-11#v15|11:15-16]]). By these two steps the Head of the Body baptized all His believers, both Jewish and Gentile, once for all into His one Body ([[1 Corinthians-12#v13|1 Cor. 12:13]]). Hence, the baptism in the Spirit is an accomplished fact carried out by Christ in His ascension, on the day of Pentecost and in the house of Cornelius. None of the other cases—the Samaritan believers in [[Acts-08|Acts 8]], Saul of Tarsus in [[Acts-09|Acts 9]], and the twelve Ephesian believers in [[Acts-19|Acts 19]]—are considered the baptism in the Holy Spirit according to the revelation of the New Testament. These other cases were merely the believers’ experiences of the once-for-all-accomplished baptism in the Holy Spirit. 

In this book, concerning the believers’ receiving of the Holy Spirit economically, that is, the Holy Spirit’s falling upon them, only five cases are mentioned. Two of them are for the accomplishing of the baptism in the Holy Spirit. These are the instances that occurred on the day of Pentecost and in the house of Cornelius. The other three—the cases of the Samaritan believers, Saul of Tarsus, and the twelve believers in Ephesus—are considered extraordinary, in that some members of the Body of Christ were needed to identify those believers with the Body by the laying on of hands. Besides these five cases, in many cases of conversion, such as the three thousand ([[Acts-02#v41|2:41]]), the five thousand ([[Acts-04#v4|4:4]]), the Ethiopian eunuch ([[Acts-08#v36|8:36]], [[Acts-08#v38|38-39a]]), the many who believed in Antioch ([[Acts-11#v20|11:20-21]], [[Acts-11#v24|24]]), the many instances in [[Acts-13|Acts 13]] and 14 under Paul’s preaching ministry, Lydia in Philippi ([[Acts-16#v14|16:14-15]]), the jailer in Philippi ([[Acts-16#v33|16:33]]), the believers in Thessalonica ([[Acts-17#v4|17:4]]), the believers in Berea ([[Acts-17#v10|17:10-12]]), the believers in Athens ([[Acts-17#v34|17:34]]), the ruler of the synagogue and many other believers in Corinth ([[Acts-18#v8|18:8]]), and the believers in Ephesus ([[Acts-19#v18|19:18-19]]), there is no mention of the believers’ receiving of the Holy Spirit economically—the Holy Spirit’s falling upon the believers—because in all these cases the believers were brought into the Body of Christ in a normal way through their believing and had no particular need for a member of the Body of Christ to bring them into identification with the Body by the laying on of hands. According to the principle of God’s New Testament economy, they all must have received the Holy Spirit essentially for life and economically for power in a normal way through their believing into Christ.
