# Acts-03 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Acts-03|Acts 3]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — temple

**Verse:** [[Acts-03#v1|Acts 3:1]]

See [note 46¹](<Acts-02.fn#Footnote 46.1 — temple>) in [[Acts-02|Acts 2]]. It was not only the early believers who were not clear concerning God’s New Testament economy in relation to the Judaic temple; even the early apostles did not have a clear vision concerning God’s abandoning of the Judaic things. Hence, even after God poured out the Spirit upon them on the day of Pentecost to initiate a new dispensation, they still would not separate themselves from the Judaic temple. At the initial stage God tolerated their ignorance in this matter. But this led to a mixture of the church with Judaism, which was not condemned by the early church in Jerusalem (cf. [[Acts-21#v20|21:20-26]]). Eventually, the temple was destroyed by Titus with his Roman army in A.D. 70, as prophesied by the Lord in [[Matthew-23#v38|Matt. 23:38]] and [[Matthew-24#v2|24:2]]. That destruction cleared up the religious mixture.

## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — Silver

**Verse:** [[Acts-03#v6|Acts 3:6]]

Peter did not possess silver and gold, but St. Peter’s Cathedral in Rome was constructed with a superabundance of gold. He did not have silver and gold, but he had the name, the person, of Jesus Christ. He was poor in silver and gold but rich in Christ. The Roman Church is filled with gold but not with the person of Christ. She is rich in gold but poor in Christ.

## Footnotes for verse 18

### Footnote 18.1 — announced

**Verse:** [[Acts-03#v18|Acts 3:18]]

First, the redeeming death of Christ was determined by God in eternity ([[Acts-02#v23|2:23]]) and announced beforehand through the prophets in the Old Testament time. This proves again that Christ’s death was not a historical accident but an act planned by God according to the purpose of His good pleasure and announced beforehand through the prophets.

## Footnotes for verse 20

### Footnote 20.1 — refreshing

**Verse:** [[Acts-03#v20|Acts 3:20]]

Lit., cooling, reviving; hence, relieving, refreshing. The seasons of refreshing denote a time of revival of all things with joy and rest, referring to the times of restoration of all things in [[Acts-03#v21|v. 21]], which will be brought in by the coming of the Messiah in His glory, as taught and prophesied by the Savior in [[Matthew-19#v28|Matt. 19:28]] (see [note 20¹](<Acts-03.fn#Footnote 20.1 — refreshing>) there). It seems that Peter’s word skips over the church age and goes directly from the time of Pentecost to the millennium. This may indicate that Peter did not have a clear vision concerning the church age in God’s New Testament economy. The entire New Testament reveals that before the seasons of refreshing, the church occupies a considerable period of time in God’s dispensation.

### Footnote 20.2 — Lord

## Footnotes for verse 26

### Footnote 26.1 — Him

**Verse:** [[Acts-03#v26|Acts 3:26]]

God sent back the ascended Christ first to the Jews by pouring out His Spirit on the day of Pentecost. Hence, the very Spirit whom God poured out is the very Christ whom God raised and exalted to the heavens. When the apostles preached and ministered this Christ, the Spirit was ministered to people.
