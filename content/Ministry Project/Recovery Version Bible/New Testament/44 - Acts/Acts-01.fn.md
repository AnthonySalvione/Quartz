## Footnotes for verse 3

### Footnote 3.1 — presented

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v3|Acts 1:3]]

This was to train the disciples to practice and enjoy the Lord’s invisible presence. See [note 26³](<John-20.fn#Footnote 26.3 — came>) in [[John-20|John 20]].

### Footnote 3.2 — appearing

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v3|Acts 1:3]]

The resurrected Christ dwelt in the disciples, because He had breathed Himself as the Spirit into them on the day of His resurrection ([[John-20#v22|John 20:22]]). His appearing does not mean that He had ever left them; it simply means that He made His presence visible to them, training them to realize and enjoy His invisible presence all the time.

### Footnote 3.3 — forty

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v3|Acts 1:3]]

Forty days is a period of trial and testing (see [note 2¹](<Matthew-04.fn#Footnote 2.1 — forty>) in [[Matthew-04|Matt. 4]]).

### Footnote 3.4 — kingdom

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v3|Acts 1:3]]

This proves that the kingdom of God would be the main subject of the apostles’ preaching in their commission that was to come after Pentecost ([[Acts-08#v12|8:12]]; [[Acts-14#v22|14:22]]; [[Acts-19#v8|19:8]]; [[Acts-20#v25|20:25]]; [[Acts-28#v23|28:23]], [[Acts-28#v31|31]]). It is not a material kingdom visible to human sight but a kingdom of the divine life. It is the spreading of Christ as life to His believers to form a realm in which God rules in His life. See notes [15¹](<Mark-01.fn#Footnote 15.1 — kingdom>) in [[Mark-01|Mark 1]], [26¹](<Mark-04.fn#Footnote 26.1 — kingdom>) in [[Mark-04|Mark 4]], and [43²](<Luke-04.fn#Footnote 43.2 — kingdom>) in [[Luke-04|Luke 4]].

## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — receive

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v8|Acts 1:8]]

This is to be baptized in the Holy Spirit ([[Acts-01#v5|v. 5]]) for the fulfillment of the promise of the Father ([[Acts-01#v4|v. 4]]).

### Footnote 8.2 — upon

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v8|Acts 1:8]]

Different from in you ([[John-14#v17|John 14:17]]). The Holy Spirit was breathed into the disciples on the day of the Lord’s resurrection ([[John-20#v22|John 20:22]]) to be the Spirit of life ([[Romans-08#v2|Rom. 8:2]]) to them essentially. The same Holy Spirit would come upon the disciples on the day of Pentecost to be the Spirit of power to them economically. See [note 49³](<Luke-24.fn#Footnote 49.3 — put>) in [[Luke-24|Luke 24]].

### Footnote 8.3 — witnesses

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v8|Acts 1:8]]

Lit., martyrs; those who bear a living testimony of the resurrected and ascended Christ in life, differing from preachers who merely preach doctrines in letters. In His incarnation Christ carried out His ministry on the earth by Himself, as recorded in the Gospels, to sow Himself as the seed of the kingdom of God only in the Jewish land. In His ascension He would carry out His ministry in the heavens through these martyrs, in His resurrection life and with His ascension power and authority, as recorded in the Acts, to spread Himself as the development of the kingdom of God from Jerusalem, as a beginning, unto the uttermost part of the earth, as the consummation of His ministry in the New Testament. All the apostles and disciples in the Acts were His martyrs, His witnesses, of this kind (reference [[Acts-01#v8|8c]]). See notes [11³](<Acts-23.fn#Footnote 11.3 — solemnly>) in [[Acts-23|Acts 23]] and [16¹](<Acts-26.fn#Footnote 16.1 — witness>) in [[Acts-26|Acts 26]].

## Footnotes for verse 11

### Footnote 11.1 — Men

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v11|Acts 1:11]]

Lit., Men, Galileans. More dignified and solemn than simply “Galileans.”

### Footnote 11.2 — heaven

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v11|Acts 1:11]]

Luke’s Gospel ends with the Lord’s ascending into heaven ([[Luke-24#v51|Luke 24:51]]), and his Acts begins with it. His Gospel is a narrative of the ministry of the incarnated Jesus on earth; his Acts is a record of the continuing ministry of the resurrected and ascended Christ in heaven, carried out through His believers on earth. In the Gospels His ministry on earth, carried out by Himself, only sowed Himself as the seed of the kingdom of God into His believers, with no church being built up yet. In the Acts His ministry in heaven, carried out through His believers in His resurrection and ascension, spread Him as the development of the kingdom of God for the building up of the church ([[Matthew-16#v18|Matt. 16:18]]) throughout the entire world to constitute His Body, which is His fullness ([[Ephesians-01#v23|Eph. 1:23]]) for His expression, and which is even the fullness of God ([[Ephesians-03#v19|Eph. 3:19]]) for God’s expression.

### Footnote 11.3 — come

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v11|Acts 1:11]]

The Lord’s ascension points to His coming back. Between these two events is the dispensation of grace that He, as the pneumatic Christ, the life-giving Spirit ([[1 Corinthians-15#v45|1 Cor. 15:45]]), might apply His all-inclusive redemption to God’s chosen people for their full salvation, that He might produce and build up the church as His Body for the establishing of the kingdom of God on earth.

### Footnote 11.4 — same

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v11|Acts 1:11]]

Christ ascended into heaven from Mount Olivet ([[Acts-01#v12|v. 12]]), being taken up by a cloud, in a way that was visible to human sight. He will return to the same mount ([[Zechariah-14#v4|Zech. 14:4]]), coming on a cloud ([[Matthew-24#v30|Matt. 24:30]]), in the same visible way.

### Footnote 11.5 — beheld

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v11|Acts 1:11]]

This vision of Christ’s ascension into heaven strengthened the disciples’ faith in Him and in what He had done for them through His death and resurrection. It broadened their view of God’s heavenly economy, which had brought them into cooperation with Christ’s ministry in the heavens for the carrying out of God’s New Testament economy on the earth. The believers should have such a vision concerning Christ’s ascension.

## Footnotes for verse 12

### Footnote 12.1 — Jerusalem

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v12|Acts 1:12]]

The disciples returned to Jerusalem to keep the Lord’s words in [[Luke-24#v49|Luke 24:49]] and [[Luke-01#v4|1:4]] of this book, that they might receive the Spirit of power economically as promised by the Father. They were all Galileans ([[Acts-01#v11|v. 11]]). For them to stay in Jerusalem, especially under the Jewish leaders’ threatening, meant that they were risking their lives.

### Footnote 12.2 — Sabbath

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v12|Acts 1:12]]

According to Jewish tradition, a Sabbath day’s journey equaled about three-quarters of a mile.

## Footnotes for verse 14

### Footnote 14.1 — continued

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v14|Acts 1:14]]

Before the Lord’s death the disciples had no interest in praying for spiritual things ([[Luke-22#v40|Luke 22:40]], [[Luke-22#v45|45-46]]); rather, they contended among themselves as to which of them was considered to be greatest ([[Luke-22#v24|Luke 22:24]]). But after the Lord’s resurrection and ascension, their spiritual condition changed radically. They did not contend among themselves but were burdened to continue steadfastly with one accord in prayer, even before the day of Pentecost, when they would receive the outpoured Spirit of power economically ([[Acts-02|Acts 2]]). This is a strong sign and proof that they had received the indwelling Spirit of life essentially on the day of the Lord’s resurrection ([[John-20#v22|John 20:22]]). This is also evidence that they had been strengthened in God’s New Testament economy by the vision of the Lord’s ascension.

### Footnote 14.2 — with

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v14|Acts 1:14]]

Or, with one mind.

### Footnote 14.3 — prayer

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v14|Acts 1:14]]

The disciples might have prayed to be clothed with the Spirit of power, the promise of the Father, for which the Lord had charged them to remain in Jerusalem ([[Luke-24#v49|Luke 24:49]]; [[Acts-01#v4|Acts 1:4]]), and might also have prayed for the commission given to them by the Lord in [[Luke-24#v47|Luke 24:47-48]] and [[Luke-01#v8|1:8]] of this book to bear His testimony to the uttermost part of the earth. 

God wanted to pour out His Spirit for the carrying out of His New Testament economy and had promised to do it. Yet He still needed His chosen people to pray for this. As God in heaven, He needs men on earth to cooperate with Him for the carrying out of His plan. The one hundred twenty disciples’ praying for ten days met this need of God’s.

### Footnote 14.4 — Mary

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v14|Acts 1:14]]

Here Mary is mentioned for the last time in the New Testament.

## Footnotes for verse 15

### Footnote 15.1 — said

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v15|Acts 1:15]]

Before the Lord’s death Peter often spoke nonsensically ([[Matthew-16#v22|Matt. 16:22-23]]; [[Matthew-17#v24|17:24-26]]; [[Matthew-26#v33|26:33-35]]). This too is proof that the disciples, before they received the Spirit of power economically on the day of Pentecost, had received the Spirit of life essentially on the day of the Lord’s resurrection.

## Footnotes for verse 22

### Footnote 22.1 — resurrection

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v22|Acts 1:22]]

The Lord’s resurrection was the focus of the apostles’ testimony. It points back to His incarnation, humanity, human living on the earth, and God-ordained death ([[Acts-02#v23|2:23]]), and points forward to His ascension, ministry and administration in heaven, and coming back. Thus, the apostles’ testimony of Jesus Christ, the Lord of all, was all-inclusive, as depicted in the whole book of Acts. They preached and ministered the all-inclusive Christ as He is revealed in the entire Scripture.

## Footnotes for verse 26

### Footnote 26.1 — lots

**Verse:** [[Acts-01#v26|Acts 1:26]]

After the Lord’s ascension and before the day of Pentecost the apostles were in a transitional period, as shown by the way they sought the Lord’s guidance. They had received the indwelling Spirit on the day of the Lord’s resurrection ([[John-20#v22|John 20:22]]) and had been trained by the Lord for forty days before His ascension to practice and become accustomed to His invisible presence ([[Acts-01#v3|v. 3]]). Yet it was still difficult for them to drop the old, traditional way of seeking God’s leading by casting lots ([[Leviticus-16#v8|Lev. 16:8]]; [[Joshua-14#v2|Josh. 14:2]]; [[1 Samuel-14#v41|1 Sam. 14:41]]; [[Nehemiah-10#v34|Neh. 10:34]]; [[Nehemiah-11#v1|11:1]]; [[Proverbs-16#v33|Prov. 16:33]]). They were still not accustomed to the leading and guidance of the indwelling Spirit ([[Romans-08#v14|Rom. 8:14]]), unlike the apostle Paul later, in [[Acts-16#v6|16:6-8]]. They were still in the initial stage of God’s New Testament economy before the day of Pentecost.
