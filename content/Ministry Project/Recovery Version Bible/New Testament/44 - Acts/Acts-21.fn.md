# Acts-21 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Acts-21|Acts 21]]

## Footnotes for verse 4

### Footnote 4.1 — told

**Verse:** [[Acts-21#v4|Acts 21:4]]

In [[Acts-20#v23|20:23]] the Holy Spirit made known to Paul that bonds and afflictions awaited him in Jerusalem (see [note 4¹](<Acts-21.fn#Footnote 4.1 — told>) there). Now the Spirit took a further step to tell him, through some members of the Body of Christ, not to go to Jerusalem. Since Paul practiced the Body life, he should have taken this word and obeyed it as a word from the Head.

## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — house

**Verse:** [[Acts-21#v8|Acts 21:8]]

Wherever Paul went, he visited the brothers and stayed with them ([[Acts-21#v4|vv. 4]], [[Acts-21#v7|7]]). He was actually practicing the Body life of the church, living according to what he taught concerning the Body of Christ.

## Footnotes for verse 11

### Footnote 11.1 — says

**Verse:** [[Acts-21#v11|Acts 21:11]]

Again, the Holy Spirit told Paul beforehand, indirectly through a member of the Body of Christ, what would befall Paul in Jerusalem (see [note 23¹](<Acts-20.fn#Footnote 23.1 — Except>) in [[Acts-20|Acts 20]]). This was again a warning in the nature of a prophecy, not a charge. It was again the Head speaking through His Body (see [note 4¹](<Acts-21.fn#Footnote 4.1 — told>)). Since Paul practiced the Body life, he should have listened to this speaking.

**Verse:** [[Acts-21#v21|Acts 21:21]]

## Footnotes for verse 24

### Footnote 24.1 — purified

**Verse:** [[Acts-21#v24|Acts 21:24]]

To be purified with the Nazarites was to become a Nazarite with them, joining them in the fulfilling of their vow. The same word is used in the Septuagint in [[Numbers-06#v3|Num. 6:3]] in describing the Nazarite’s duties. To take the Nazarite vow was a purification before God.

### Footnote 24.2 — expenses

**Verse:** [[Acts-21#v24|Acts 21:24]]

The cost of the offerings, which a Nazarite had to pay for the completion of his purification ([[Numbers-06#v13|Num. 6:13-17]]). This was very expensive for the poor Nazarites. It was a custom among the Jews, and was considered proof of great piety, that a rich person would pay for the poor the expenses of the offerings.

### Footnote 24.3 — shave

**Verse:** [[Acts-21#v24|Acts 21:24]]

To be done at the completion of the Nazarite vow ([[Numbers-06#v18|Num. 6:18]]). This shaving differed from the shearing in [[Acts-18#v18|18:18]] (see [note 24¹](<Acts-21.fn#Footnote 24.1 — purified>) there), which was for a private vow.

## Footnotes for verse 26

### Footnote 26.1 — being

**Verse:** [[Acts-21#v26|Acts 21:26]]

I.e., participating in their Nazarite vow (see [note 24¹](<Acts-21.fn#Footnote 24.1 — purified>)). To do this Paul had to enter into the temple and remain there with the Nazarites until the completion of the seven days of the vow; then the priest would offer the offerings for each one of them, including him. Surely he was clear that such a practice was of the outdated dispensation, which, according to the principle of his teaching in the New Testament ministry, should be repudiated in God’s New Testament economy. Yet he went through with it, probably because of his Jewish background, which had also been manifested earlier in his private vow in [[Acts-18#v18|18:18]], and probably because he was practicing his word in [[1 Corinthians-09#v20|1 Cor. 9:20]]. However, his toleration jeopardized God’s New Testament economy; this God would not tolerate. At this point he must have felt that he was in a predicament, and he must have been deeply troubled, longing to be delivered from it. Just at the time when their vow was to be concluded, God allowed an uproar to rise up against him, and what they intended to accomplish was blown away ([[Acts-21#v27|v. 27]]). Moreover, by God’s sovereignty Paul was rescued out of his predicament. 

The mixing of Judaic practices with God’s New Testament economy was not only erroneous in relation to God’s dispensation but also abominable in the eyes of God. This gross mixture was terminated by Him a mere ten years or so later with the destruction of Jerusalem and the temple, the center of Judaism, through Titus and his Roman army. This rescued and absolutely separated the church from the devastation of Judaism. 

God might have tolerated Paul’s carrying out of a private vow in [[Acts-18#v18|18:18]], but He would not allow Paul, a vessel chosen by Him not only for the completing of His New Testament revelation ([[Colossians-01#v25|Col. 1:25]]) but also for the carrying out of His New Testament economy ([[Ephesians-03#v2|Eph. 3:2]], [[Ephesians-03#v7|7-8]]), to participate in the Nazarite vow, a strict Judaic practice. In going to Jerusalem, Paul’s intention might have been to clear up the Judaic influence on the church there (see [note 21¹](<Acts-19.fn#Footnote 21.1 — purposed>), par. 2, in [[Acts-19|Acts 19]]), but God knew that the church there was incurable. Hence, in His sovereignty God allowed Paul to be arrested by the Jews and imprisoned by the Romans that he might write his last eight Epistles (see [note 11¹](<Acts-25.fn#Footnote 11.1 — appeal>) in [[Acts-25|Acts 25]]), which completed the divine revelation ([[Colossians-01#v25|Col. 1:25]]) and gave the church a clearer and deeper view concerning God’s New Testament economy ([[Ephesians-03#v3|Eph. 3:3-4]]). Thus, God left the Judaism-influenced church in Jerusalem to remain as it was until the devastating mixture was terminated with the destruction of Jerusalem. For Paul to write his last eight Epistles to complete God’s New Testament revelation was far more important and necessary than for him to accomplish some outward works for the church.

### Footnote 28.2 — people

**Verse:** [[Acts-21#v28|Acts 21:28]]

God’s New Testament teaching according to His New Testament economy was indeed against the Jews, who opposed God’s New Testament economy ([[Matthew-21#v41|Matt. 21:41]], [[Matthew-21#v43|43-45]]; [[Matthew-22#v7|22:7]]; [[Matthew-23#v32|23:32-36]]; [[Acts-07#v51|Acts 7:51]]; [[Acts-13#v40|13:40-41]]), against the law of dead letters ([[Romans-03#v20|Rom. 3:20]], [[Romans-03#v28|28]]; [[Romans-06#v14|6:14]]; [[Romans-07#v4|7:4]], [[Romans-07#v6|6]]; [[Galatians-02#v19|Gal. 2:19]], [[Galatians-02#v21|21]]; [[Galatians-05#v4|5:4]]), and against the holy place, the temple ([[Matthew-23#v38|Matt. 23:38]] and [note 1](<Matthew-23.fn#Footnote 38.1 — house>); [[Acts-24#v2|24:2]]; [[Acts-07#v48|Acts 7:48]]). Since Paul’s ministry was to carry out God’s New Testament economy, it could not please the Jews, who were possessed and usurped by Satan, the enemy of God, and were instigated by him to oppose and ravage God’s New Testament move. Hence, it offended the Jews and was opposed to the law and the temple, for which they were zealous, and it stirred up their jealousy and hatred to the uttermost, so that they made a plot ([[Acts-20#v3|20:3]]) to do away with Paul ([[Acts-21#v31|vv. 31]], [[Acts-21#v36|36]]).
