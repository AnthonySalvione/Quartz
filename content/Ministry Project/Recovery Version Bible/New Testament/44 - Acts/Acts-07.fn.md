# Acts-07 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Acts-07|Acts 7]]

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — Men

**Verse:** [[Acts-07#v2|Acts 7:2]]

More dignified and solemn than simply “brothers and fathers.”

### Footnote 2.2 — glory

**Verse:** [[Acts-07#v2|Acts 7:2]]

It might have been visible glory (cf. [[Acts-07#v55|v. 55]]), as when the cloud and the fire appeared to Israel ([[Exodus-16#v10|Exo. 16:10]]; [[Exodus-24#v16|24:16-17]]; [[Leviticus-09#v23|Lev. 9:23]]; [[Numbers-14#v10|Num. 14:10]]; [[Numbers-16#v19|16:19]]; [[Numbers-20#v6|20:6]]; [[Deuteronomy-05#v24|Deut. 5:24]]) and filled the tabernacle and the temple ([[Exodus-40#v35|Exo. 40:35]]; [[1 Kings-08#v11|1 Kings 8:11]]). It was the God of such glory who appeared to Abraham and called him. His glory was a great attraction to him. It separated (sanctified) him from the world unto God ([[Exodus-29#v43|Exo. 29:43]]) and was a great encouragement and strength that enabled him to follow God ([[Genesis-12#v1|Gen. 12:1]], [[Genesis-12#v4|4]]). In the same principle, God calls the New Testament believers by His invisible glory ([[2 Peter-01#v3|2 Pet. 1:3]]).

## Footnotes for verse 14

### Footnote 14.1 — seventy-five

**Verse:** [[Acts-07#v14|Acts 7:14]]

Compare with seventy in [[Genesis-46#v27|Gen. 46:27]] and [[Exodus-01#v5|Exo 1:5]]. Stephen quoted this number from the Septuagint, which mentions five additional descendants of Joseph in [[Genesis-46#v20|Gen. 46:20]]. Hence, the number he mentioned of the house of Jacob who came to Egypt was seventy-five instead of seventy.

## Footnotes for verse 20

### Footnote 20.1 — Moses

**Verse:** [[Acts-07#v20|Acts 7:20]]

From [[Acts-07#v20|v. 20]] through [[Acts-07#v44|v. 44]] Stephen purposely gave a long narration in the most positive way concerning Moses. He did this to vindicate himself before his opposers, who had accused him of blaspheming Moses ([[Acts-06#v11|6:11]]).

### Footnote 20.2 — lovely

**Verse:** [[Acts-07#v20|Acts 7:20]]

Lit., fair to God. A Hebraism denoting fair in the sight of God; hence, exceedingly fair.

## Footnotes for verse 48

### Footnote 48.1 — Most

**Verse:** [[Acts-07#v48|Acts 7:48]]

Stephen called God “the God of glory” ([[Acts-07#v2|v. 2]]) and “the Most High” to vindicate himself before his opposers, who had accused him of blaspheming God ([[Acts-06#v11|6:11]]).

### Footnote 48.2 — not

**Verse:** [[Acts-07#v48|Acts 7:48]]

Implying that God would abandon the material temple of the Old Testament and initiate a new dispensation, that His people might worship Him in the spirit ([[John-04#v24|John 4:24]]), in which is God’s spiritual habitation, the church ([[Ephesians-02#v22|Eph. 2:22]]).

## Footnotes for verse 49

### Footnote 49.1 — What

**Verse:** [[Acts-07#v49|Acts 7:49]]

This quotation from [[Isaiah-66#v1|Isa. 66:1-2]] indicates that the Lord was seeking a spiritual habitation in man’s spirit. This is proved by the unquoted part of [[Isaiah-66#v2|Isa 66:2]] that says, “But to this kind of man will I look, to him who is poor and of a contrite spirit.”

## Footnotes for verse 51

### Footnote 51.1 — oppose

**Verse:** [[Acts-07#v51|Acts 7:51]]

A very strong expression.

### Footnote 51.2 — Spirit

**Verse:** [[Acts-07#v51|Acts 7:51]]

Since Stephen was full of the Spirit ([[Acts-07#v55|v. 55]]) and was one with the Lord the Spirit ([[1 Corinthians-06#v17|1 Cor. 6:17]]), to oppose him was to oppose the Holy Spirit. Hence, the Lord indicated to Saul, one of Stephen’s persecutors ([[Acts-07#v58|v. 58]]; [[Acts-08#v1|8:1]]), that he was persecuting Him ([[Acts-09#v4|9:4]]).

## Footnotes for verse 53

### Footnote 53.1 — ordinances

**Verse:** [[Acts-07#v53|Acts 7:53]]

The Greek word is a noun; the verb form of the same word is used in [[Galatians-03#v19|Gal. 3:19]] and is rendered ordained there. The law of God was ordained through angels and became ordinances of angels.
