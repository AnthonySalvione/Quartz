---
Author: Luke
---
## v1

And Paul, looking intently at the [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 1.1 — Sanhedrin|Sanhedrin]], said, [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 1.2 — Men|Men]], brothers, I have conducted myself in all good [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 1.3 — conscience|conscience]] before God until this day.

## v2

And the high priest Ananias commanded those who stood by him to strike him on the mouth.

## v3

Then Paul [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 3.1 — said|said]] to him, God is going to strike you, you whitewashed wall. And do you sit to [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 3.2 — judge|judge]] me according to the law, and contrary to the law you order me to be struck?

## v4

And those standing by said, Do you revile the high priest of God?

## v5

And Paul said, I did not know, brothers, that he is the high priest; for it is written, “You shall not speak evilly of the ruler of your people.”

## v6

And Paul, knowing that one part was [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 6.1 — Sadducees|Sadducees]] and the other [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 6.2 — Pharisees|Pharisees]], [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 6.3 — cried|cried]] out in the Sanhedrin, [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 6.4 — Men|Men]], brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees; concerning the hope and resurrection of the dead I am being [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 6.5 — judged|judged]]!

## v7

And when he spoke this, a dissension took place between the Pharisees and Sadducees; and the multitude was divided.

## v8

For the Sadducees say that there is no resurrection, neither angel nor spirit; but the Pharisees confess both.

## v9

And there was a great outcry; and some of the scribes of the Pharisees’ party rose up and contended, saying, We find nothing evil in this man. And what if a spirit has spoken to him, or an angel?

## v10

And when much dissension had taken place, the [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 10.1 — commander|commander]], fearing that Paul might be torn to pieces by them, [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 10.2 — ordered|ordered]] the detachment of soldiers to go down and seize him from their midst and bring him into the barracks.

## v11

But in the night [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 11.1 — following|following]], the Lord [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 11.2 — stood|stood]] by him and said, Take courage, for as you have [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 11.3 — solemnly|solemnly]] testified to the things concerning Me in Jerusalem, so also you must [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 11.4 — testify|testify]] in [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 11.5 — Rome|Rome]].

## v12

And when it became day, the Jews formed a [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 12.1 — plot|plot]] and [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 12.2 — put|put]] themselves under a curse, saying that they would neither eat nor drink until they had killed Paul.

## v13

And there were more than forty who swore to this conspiracy.

## v14

They came to the chief priests and the elders and said, [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 14.1 — We|We]] have bound ourselves with a curse to taste nothing until we kill Paul.

## v15

Now therefore you, with the Sanhedrin, notify the commander to bring him down to you, as though you were going to [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 15.1 — determine|determine]] more accurately the things concerning him; and we, before he draws near, are ready to do away with him.

## v16

But the son of Paul’s sister [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 16.1 — heard|heard]] of the ambush, and he came and entered into the barracks and reported it to Paul.

## v17

And Paul called one of the centurions to him and said, Bring this young man to the commander, for he has something to report to him.

## v18

So he took him and brought him to the commander and said, The prisoner Paul called me to him and asked me to bring this young man to you, because he has something to tell you.

## v19

And the commander took him by the hand and, withdrawing privately, inquired, What is it that you have to report to me?

## v20

And he said, The Jews have agreed to ask you to bring Paul down tomorrow to the Sanhedrin, as though they were going to inquire somewhat more accurately concerning him.

## v21

So you should not be persuaded by them, for more than forty of their men are lying in ambush for him; they have put themselves under a curse neither to eat nor to drink until they have done away with him. And now they are ready, awaiting the promise from you.

## v22

So the commander dismissed the young man, charging him, Do not tell anyone that you have informed me of these things.

## v23

And he called to him a certain two of the centurions and said, Prepare two hundred soldiers to go as far as Caesarea, as well as seventy horsemen and two hundred [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 23.1 — spearmen|spearmen]], by the [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 23.2 — third|third]] hour of the night;

## v24

And they were also to provide mounts to put Paul on and bring him safely to [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 24.1 — Felix|Felix]] the governor.

## v25

And he wrote a letter having this form:

## v26

Claudius Lysias, to his Excellency, Governor Felix, rejoice.

## v27

This man, having been seized by the Jews and about to be done away with by them, I came upon with my detachment of soldiers and rescued, when I learned that he was a Roman.

## v28

And intending to ascertain the cause for which they were accusing him, I brought him down to their Sanhedrin,

## v29

And found him to be accused concerning questions of their law, but having nothing worthy of death or of bonds laid to his charge.

## v30

And when it was disclosed to me that there was a plot against the man, I sent him at once to you, charging the accusers also to speak the things against him before you.

## v31

So the soldiers, according to what they had been directed, took up Paul and brought him by night to [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 31.1 — Antipatris|Antipatris]].

## v32

And on the next day, leaving the horsemen to depart with him, they returned to the barracks.

## v33

And when they entered into Caesarea and handed over the letter to the governor, they presented Paul also to him.

## v34

And when he had read it, he asked from what province he was; and learning that he was from Cilicia,

## v35

He said, I will hear your case fully when your accusers also arrive. And he ordered him to be guarded in Herod’s [[Acts-23.fn#Footnote 35.1 — praetorium|praetorium]].
