# Luke-13 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Luke-13|Luke 13]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — Now

**Verse:** [[Luke-13#v1|Luke 13:1]]

Now indicates that [[Luke-13#v1|vv. 1-9]], which deal further with the matter of repentance, continue the last verses of [[Luke-12|Luke 12]]. The Lord used the two incidents in [[Luke-12#v1|vv. 1-5]] to remind the Jews that now was the time for them to repent and that if they did not, they would all perish like the victims of those two incidents.

## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — And

**Verse:** [[Luke-13#v6|Luke 13:6]]

And indicates that [[Luke-13#v6|vv. 6-9]] are a continuation of the foregoing verses concerning repentance.

### Footnote 6.2 — this

**Verse:** [[Luke-13#v6|Luke 13:6]]

This parable indicates that God as the owner came in the Son to seek fruit from the Jewish people, who were likened to a fig tree (see [note 19¹](<Matthew-21.fn#Footnote 19.1 — fig>) in [[Matthew-21|Matt. 21]]) planted in God’s promised land, the vineyard (cf. [[Matthew-21#v33|Matt. 21:33]] and [note 1](<Matthew-21.fn#Footnote 33.1 — householder>)). He had been seeking fruit for three years ([[Luke-13#v7|v. 7]]) but had not found any. He wanted to cut the Jews down, but God the Son as the vinedresser prayed for them, asking God the Father to tolerate them until the Son died for them (dug the ground around the fig tree) and gave them fertilizer (threw on manure), at which point, He hoped, they would repent and produce fruit. Otherwise, they would be cut down. [[Luke-13#v29|Verses 29-32]] and 42-52 in [[Luke-11|Luke 11]], two sections that unveil the Jewish people as an evil generation, confirm this interpretation.

### Footnote 11.2 — bent

**Verse:** [[Luke-13#v11|Luke 13:11]]

Lit., bending together. This signifies a person’s being oppressed by a demon to an extreme, so that the person is bent toward the satanic world only and is unable to stand erect to look into the heavens.

## Footnotes for verse 14

### Footnote 14.1 — indignant

**Verse:** [[Luke-13#v14|Luke 13:14]]

Satan used not only the evil spirit to possess the woman but also the religious ruler to oppose her being released by the Lord. Religion is much used by the usurper to keep God’s chosen people under his oppression.

## Footnotes for verse 16

### Footnote 16.1 — daughter

**Verse:** [[Luke-13#v16|Luke 13:16]]

### Footnote 16.2 — Satan

**Verse:** [[Luke-13#v16|Luke 13:16]]

The demon’s possessing of people is Satan’s binding of them. Hence, the casting out of a demon is the defeat of Satan ([[Matthew-12#v29|Matt. 12:29]] and [note 4](<Matthew-12.fn#Footnote 29.4 — binds>)).

### Footnote 16.3 — Sabbath

**Verse:** [[Luke-13#v16|Luke 13:16]]

The Sabbath was ordained by God so that man could rest ([[Genesis-02#v3|Gen. 2:3]]), not so that man would remain under bondage.

## Footnotes for verse 30

### Footnote 30.1 — last

**Verse:** [[Luke-13#v30|Luke 13:30]]

Referring to the saved Gentiles, who will receive the Savior before some of the saved Jews and will participate in the kingdom of God in the millennium ([[Luke-13#v29|v. 29]]).

### Footnote 30.2 — first

**Verse:** [[Luke-13#v30|Luke 13:30]]

Referring to the Jews who will believe in the Lord after the Gentiles believe ([[Romans-11#v25|Rom. 11:25-26]]). The word in this verse is applied in another sense in [[Matthew-19#v30|Matt. 19:30]]; [[Matthew-20#v16|20:16]]; and [[Mark-10#v31|Mark 10:31]] (see [note 16¹](<Matthew-20.fn#Footnote 16.1 — last>) in [[Matthew-20|Matt. 20]]).

## Footnotes for verse 32

### Footnote 32.1 — today

**Verse:** [[Luke-13#v32|Luke 13:32]]

This indicates that the Lord had a set schedule to carry out His ministry, to end His course, and to reach His goal through His death and resurrection, and that no one, not even Herod, could prevent Him from accomplishing it.

### Footnote 32.2 — am

**Verse:** [[Luke-13#v32|Luke 13:32]]

Or, end My course, reach My goal.

## Footnotes for verse 33

### Footnote 33.1 — must

**Verse:** [[Luke-13#v33|Luke 13:33]]

The threat ([[Luke-13#v31|v. 31]]) did not prevent the Lord from journeying to Jerusalem to accomplish His redemptive death. Rather, He was bold to proceed there ([[Mark-10#v33|Mark 10:33]]) to reach the goal of His entire ministry.

## Footnotes for verse 35

### Footnote 35.1 — house

**Verse:** [[Luke-13#v35|Luke 13:35]]

This house (singular in Greek) was God’s house, God’s temple. Because the Jews rejected the Lord, that is, God’s coming in the Son, the Lord rejected the temple, considering it the house of the Jews themselves and leaving it to them that it might suffer the coming destruction and become desolate.
