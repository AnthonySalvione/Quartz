## Footnotes for verse 7

### Footnote 7.1 — bore

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v7|Luke 2:7]]

Luke presents a Man-Savior. After the narration of the conception of the Man-Savior, Luke gives us in this chapter an excellent record of (1) His genuine human birth, which was according to the law of God’s creation of man, that He might be a Man-Savior for the salvation of man; (2) His physical circumcision, carried out according to the law of God’s ordination ([[Luke-02#v21|vv. 21-24]]), that He might be the legal seed of Abraham ([[Genesis-17#v9|Gen. 17:9-14]]), the One in whom God’s promise to Abraham—“In you all the families of the earth will be blessed” ([[Genesis-12#v3|Gen. 12:3]])—as the gospel announced to him ([[Galatians-03#v8|Gal. 3:8]]), could be fulfilled for all the Gentiles ([[Galatians-03#v14|Gal. 3:14]]); (3) His physical growth, which was according to the law of human life ([[Luke-02#v40|v. 40]]), that He might be a perfect man to express God for the fulfillment of God’s redemptive plan; and (4) His proper boyhood, during which He grew in His interest concerning the things of God, which interest was related to His deity as the Son of God the Father ([[Luke-02#v40|vv. 40-52]]), that He might have the full measure of wisdom, stature, and favor with God and men. 

The record here concerning the Savior’s birth and youth is very different from that in [[Matthew-02|Matt. 2]]. What Matthew recorded of events that occurred at the Savior’s birth and during His youth constitutes striking evidence of Christ’s legitimate kingship. Luke also recorded the Savior’s birth and youth, but what he recorded were events of a different category, events that offer strong proof of Jesus’ genuine humanity. The two records cover only two of the different aspects of the Savior’s wonderful status. See [note 2¹](<Matthew-02.fn#Footnote 2.1 — King>) in [[Matthew-02|Matt. 2]].

## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — shepherds

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v8|Luke 2:8]]

Their work in shepherding the flock (which provided not only food for man but also offerings to God) and their diligence in keeping the night watches qualified them to be the first to receive the good news of the wonderful birth of the Savior, which was announced by the angel.

### Footnote 12.1 — sign

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v12|Luke 2:12]]

### Footnote 12.2 — baby

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v12|Luke 2:12]]

This little baby is called the “Mighty God” in the prophecy concerning the Man-Savior ([[Isaiah-09#v6|Isa. 9:6]]).

## Footnotes for verse 34

### Footnote 34.1 — appointed

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v34|Luke 2:34]]

The Man-Savior was appointed by God to be a test to the children of Israel, so that many of them would be stumbled by Him and many raised up by Him ([[Romans-09#v33|Rom. 9:33]]).

### Footnote 34.2 — sign

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v34|Luke 2:34]]

The Man-Savior is also a sign, a token, spoken against, contradicted, and opposed by the people who take sides with His enemy, so that the reasonings of many hearts may be revealed. In Simeon’s word the Man-Savior was revealed as the consolation of Israel, the salvation of God, a light to the Gentiles, the glory of Israel, a test to Israel, and a sign opposed.

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v35|Luke 2:35]]

What is within man’s heart is easily revealed by his attitude toward Christ.

## Footnotes for verse 39

### Footnote 39.1 — Nazareth

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v39|Luke 2:39]]

The Man-Savior was born in Bethlehem, the city of David, and remained there only briefly; but He was raised in Nazareth, a despised city, which was in Galilee, a despised region. This was arranged by the sovereignty of God in order to make Him a despised person. Being despised was another characteristic of the Savior’s human life.

## Footnotes for verse 40

### Footnote 40.1 — grew

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v40|Luke 2:40]]

Grew in stature ([[Luke-02#v52|v. 52]]) and became strong in spirit (cf. [[Luke-01#v80|1:80]]).

### Footnote 40.2 — wisdom

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v40|Luke 2:40]]

The wisdom of the Savior’s deity ([[Colossians-02#v2|Col. 2:2-3]]) was revealed in proportion to the measure of His bodily growth. So in [[Luke-02#v52|v. 52]].

### Footnote 40.3 — grace

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v40|Luke 2:40]]

As a man, even Jesus needed the grace of God for His human life. He was filled with the wisdom of His deity and needed the grace of God in His humanity.

## Footnotes for verse 42

### Footnote 42.1 — twelve

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v42|Luke 2:42]]

At the age of twelve, a boy was called by the Jews “son of the law” and first incurred legal obligation (Alford). The number twelve signifies eternal perfection in God’s administration. Hence, twelve years old indicates that what the Lord did here was altogether related to God’s administration.

### Footnote 42.2 — custom

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v42|Luke 2:42]]

Ordained by God that one might be legally a male in Israel ([[Deuteronomy-16#v16|Deut. 16:16]]).

## Footnotes for verse 52

### Footnote 52.1 — stature

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v52|Luke 2:52]]

The Greek word denotes not only stature, as in [[Luke-19#v3|19:3]], but also age (see [note 27¹](<Matthew-06.fn#Footnote 27.1 — his>) in [[Matthew-06|Matt. 6]]).

### Footnote 52.2 — grace

**Verse:** [[Luke-02#v52|Luke 2:52]]

In grace before God because He was growing in the expression of God according to God’s desire; in grace before men because He was growing in the divine attributes manifested in the human virtues, which were gracious to men. He was growing as a God-man before God and men.
