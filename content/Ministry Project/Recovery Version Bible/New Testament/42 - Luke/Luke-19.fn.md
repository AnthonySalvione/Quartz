Or, fig-mulberry. Its leaves are like those of the mulberry, and its fruit like that of the fig tree.

## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — half

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v8|Luke 19:8]]

Once a sinner receives the Savior, the issue of his dynamic salvation is that he deals with material possessions and clears his past sinful life.

### Footnote 8.2 — taken

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v8|Luke 19:8]]

The same Greek word as in [[Luke-03#v14|3:14]]. It is a mild way of saying “extorted.” The tax collectors would put an excessive value on property or income, or increase the tax of those unable to pay, and would then practice usury.

### Footnote 8.3 — four

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v8|Luke 19:8]]

This was according to the law’s requirements for restoration ([[Exodus-22#v1|Exo. 22:1]]; [[2 Samuel-12#v6|2 Sam. 12:6]]).

## Footnotes for verse 9

### Footnote 9.1 — also

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v9|Luke 19:9]]

However evil this tax collector was, he nevertheless was a son of Abraham, a chosen heir of God’s promised inheritance ([[Galatians-03#v7|Gal. 3:7]], [[Galatians-03#v29|29]]).

## Footnotes for verse 10

### Footnote 10.1 — seek

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v10|Luke 19:10]]

This indicates that the Savior’s coming to Jericho was not accidental but purposeful; He came to seek this unique lost sinner, just as He sought the sinful woman in Samaria ([[John-04#v4|John 4:4]]).

## Footnotes for verse 11

### Footnote 11.1 — further

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v11|Luke 19:11]]

Spiritually, this further parable is a continuation of the preceding case of salvation. It depicts how the saved ones should serve the Lord that they may inherit the coming kingdom.

### Footnote 11.2 — appear

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v11|Luke 19:11]]

Or, be shown forth, be made to appear.

## Footnotes for verse 12

### Footnote 12.1 — certain

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v12|Luke 19:12]]

Signifying the Savior, who is of the highest status—the God-man, both honorable in His deity and noble in His humanity.

### Footnote 12.2 — went

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v12|Luke 19:12]]

Signifying the Savior’s going to heaven after His death and resurrection ([[Luke-24#v51|24:51]]; [[1 Peter-03#v22|1 Pet. 3:22]]).

### Footnote 12.3 — return

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v12|Luke 19:12]]

Signifying the Savior’s coming back with the kingdom ([[Daniel-07#v13|Dan. 7:13-14]]; [[Revelation-11#v15|Rev. 11:15]]; [[2 TImothy-04#v1|2 Tim. 4:1]]).

## Footnotes for verse 13

### Footnote 13.1 — ten

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v13|Luke 19:13]]

In the parable in [[Matthew-25#v14|Matt. 25:14-30]], the slaves are given a varying number of talents according to their individual ability; here the parable emphasizes the common portion given equally to each slave based on the common salvation. However, the point of both parables is the same: the faithfulness of the slaves will determine their portion as their reward in the coming kingdom.

### Footnote 13.2 — minas

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v13|Luke 19:13]]

A mina is equal to one hundred drachmas, or one hundred days’ wages.

### Footnote 13.3 — until

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v13|Luke 19:13]]

Lit., while I am coming.

## Footnotes for verse 14

### Footnote 14.2 — We

## Footnotes for verse 20

### Footnote 20.1 — And

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v20|Luke 19:20]]

For [[Luke-19#v20|vv. 20-26]], see notes in [[Matthew-25#v24|Matt. 25:24-29]].

### Footnote 20.2 — kept

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v20|Luke 19:20]]

Signifying that the unfaithful believers keep their salvation in an idle way instead of using it productively. To keep the Lord’s salvation laid away is to not make use of it. Such idleness toward the Lord will cause the unfaithful believers to be condemned and to suffer loss.

## Footnotes for verse 28

### Footnote 28.1 — going

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v28|Luke 19:28]]

In the preceding verse the Savior finished His ministry. Now the time came when He needed to go up to Jerusalem to present Himself to the death ordained by God for the accomplishing of God’s eternal redemption (see notes [1¹](<Mark-10.fn#Footnote 1.1 — borders>) and [33¹](<Mark-10.fn#Footnote 33.1 — delivered>) in [[Mark-10|Mark 10]]). Although He knew that the leaders of Judaism were seeking the opportunity to kill Him, He still went straight ahead boldly.

## Footnotes for verse 44

### Footnote 44.1 — level

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v44|Luke 19:44]]

This was fulfilled in A.D. 70 through the Roman prince Titus with his army.

### Footnote 44.2 — visitation

**Verse:** [[Luke-19#v44|Luke 19:44]]

The Savior’s first coming, when He visited them in grace in the acceptable year of the Lord ([[Luke-02#v10|2:10-14]]; [[Luke-04#v18|4:18-22]]). To miss the Lord’s visitation of grace and thereby lose the opportunity to repent and be saved will cause a person to be judged and to regret forever.
