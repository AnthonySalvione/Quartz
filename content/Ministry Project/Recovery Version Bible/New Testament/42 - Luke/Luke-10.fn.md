## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — son

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v6|Luke 10:6]]

The Lord sent the disciples to preach the gospel by going to every city and place and visiting people in their homes. This may serve to confirm our preaching of the gospel today by knocking on doors and visiting people from door to door. The Lord sent the disciples as lambs entering into the midst of wolves ([[Luke-10#v3|v. 3]]). However, among the wolves there were sons of peace, that is, sheep that the Lord was concerned about ([[Matthew-09#v36|Matt. 9:36]]) and the harvest that He desired to reap ([[Luke-10#v2|v. 2]]; [[Matthew-09#v37|Matt. 9:37-38]]). Because these sheep and the harvest, both of which belonged to the Lord, were scattered and mixed with the wolves, it was necessary for the Lord’s disciples to go into the midst of the wolves to seek them out house by house and to reap them. It is the same today. Preaching the gospel by going out to every place to visit people in their homes is ordained by God. It is absolutely different from today’s religious way of holding large gatherings to carry out gospel campaigns.

## Footnotes for verse 18

### Footnote 18.1 — fall

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v18|Luke 10:18]]

At the time of his rebellion against God, before the creation of man, Satan was judged and sentenced to be cast into Sheol (Hades), to the recesses (the deepest part) of the pit ([[Isaiah-14#v15|Isa. 14:15]]; [[Ezekiel-28#v17|Ezek. 28:17]]). Then God began to execute the sentence He had imposed on him. He has executed, and will execute, this sentence on different occasions and to different degrees, such as through the seventy disciples in this chapter, through Christ on the cross ([[Hebrews-02#v14|Heb. 2:14]]; [[John-12#v31|John 12:31]]), through the man-child and Michael before the great tribulation, when Satan will be cast down to the earth ([[Revelation-12#v5|Rev. 12:5]], [[Revelation-12#v7|7-10]], [[Revelation-12#v13|13]]), and through the angel before the millennium, when Satan will be cast into the abyss, the bottomless pit ([[Revelation-20#v1|Rev. 20:1-3]]). Finally, Satan will be cast into the lake of fire to suffer the punishment of eternal fire after the millennium ([[Revelation-20#v10|Rev. 20:10]]) for eternity.

## Footnotes for verse 30

### Footnote 30.1 — certain

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v30|Luke 10:30]]

This is one of the parables narrated only by Luke. It conveys the principle of high morality in the Savior’s full salvation. The Savior intended the “certain man” to be a figure of the self-justified lawyer ([[Luke-10#v29|v. 29]]) as a sinner who had fallen from the foundation of peace (Jerusalem) to the condition of curse (Jericho).

### Footnote 30.2 — down

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v30|Luke 10:30]]

Indicating a fall from the city of the foundation of peace to the city of curse.

### Footnote 30.3 — Jerusalem

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v30|Luke 10:30]]

Jerusalem means foundation of peace (cf. [[Hebrews-07#v2|Heb. 7:2]]); Jericho was a city of curse ([[Joshua-06#v26|Josh. 6:26]]; [[1 Kings-16#v34|1 Kings 16:34]]).

### Footnote 30.4 — robbers

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v30|Luke 10:30]]

Signifying the legalistic teachers of the Judaic law ([[John-10#v1|John 10:1]]), who used the law ([[1 Corinthians-15#v56|1 Cor. 15:56]]) to rob lawkeepers such as the self-justified lawyer.

### Footnote 30.5 — stripped

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v30|Luke 10:30]]

Signifying the stripping caused by the Judaistic teachers’ misuse of the law.

### Footnote 30.6 — beaten

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v30|Luke 10:30]]

Lit., laid blows upon; signifying the killing by the law ([[Romans-07#v9|Rom. 7:9-10]]).

### Footnote 30.7 — leaving

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v30|Luke 10:30]]

Signifying that the Judaistic teachers left the lawkeeper in a dead condition ([[Romans-07#v11|Rom. 7:11]], [[Romans-07#v13|13]]).

## Footnotes for verse 33

### Footnote 33.1 — Samaritan

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v33|Luke 10:33]]

Signifying the Man-Savior, who apparently was a layman of low estate. He was despised and slandered as a low and mean Samaritan ([[John-08#v48|John 8:48]]; [[John-04#v9|4:9]] and [note 1](<John-04.fn#Footnote 9.1 — Samaritans>)) by the self-exalted and self-justified Pharisees, including the one to whom He was speaking here ([[Luke-10#v25|vv. 25]], [[Luke-10#v29|29]]). Such a Man-Savior, in His lost-one-seeking and sinner-saving ministry journey ([[Luke-19#v10|19:10]]), came down to the place where the wounded victim of the Judaistic robbers lay in his miserable and dying condition. When He saw him, He was moved with compassion in His humanity with His divinity and rendered him tender healing and saving care, fully meeting his urgent need ([[Luke-10#v34|vv. 34-35]]).

## Footnotes for verse 34

### Footnote 34.1 — bound

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v34|Luke 10:34]]

In [[Luke-10#v34|vv. 34-35]] all the aspects of the good Samaritan’s care for the dying one portray the Man-Savior in His merciful, tender, and bountiful care, in His humanity with His divinity, for a sinner condemned under law, showing to the uttermost His high standard of morality in His saving grace: (1) He bound up his wounds—healing him; (2) He poured oil and wine on his wounds—giving him the Holy Spirit and the divine life ([[Matthew-09#v17|Matt. 9:17]] and [note 1](<Matthew-09.fn#Footnote 17.1 — new>); [[John-02#v9|John 2:9]] and [note 1](<John-02.fn#Footnote 9.1 — water>)); (3) He placed him on his own beast (a donkey)—carrying him by lowly means in a lowly way ([[Zechariah-09#v9|Zech. 9:9]]); (4) He brought him to an inn—bringing him to the church; (5) He took care of him—taking care of him through the church; (6) He paid the inn for him—blessing the church on his behalf; (7) He said that he would repay at his return whatever the inn spent—declaring that whatever the church spends in this age on one who is saved by the Lord will be repaid at the Savior’s coming back.

### Footnote 36.1 — become

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v36|Luke 10:36]]

The self-justified lawyer thought that he could love another as his neighbor ([[Luke-10#v29|v. 29]]), not knowing, under the blindness of self-justification, that he himself needed a neighbor, the Man-Savior, to love him.

## Footnotes for verse 37

### Footnote 37.1 — The

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v37|Luke 10:37]]

Or, The one who dealt mercifully with him. The self-justified one was helped to know that he needed a loving neighbor (such as the good Samaritan, who was a figure of the Man-Savior) to love him, not a neighbor for him to love. The Savior intended to unveil to him through this story (1) that he was condemned to death under the law and was unable to take care of himself, much less love others, and (2) that the Man-Savior was the One who would love him and render him full salvation.

## Footnotes for verse 39

### Footnote 39.1 — Mary

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v39|Luke 10:39]]

Gk. Maria for Heb. Miriam, meaning their rebellion ([[Numbers-12#v1|Num. 12:1]], [[Numbers-12#v10|10-15]]). The meaning of Martha and Mary conveys the thought of rebellion, perhaps pointing to the natural life of Martha and Mary. The Lord’s salvation can transform the rebellious into the submissive, as portrayed in this story. One like the rebellious Miriam in the Old Testament becomes one like the submissive Mary in the New Testament.

## Footnotes for verse 42

### Footnote 42.1 — good

**Verse:** [[Luke-10#v42|Luke 10:42]]

The Lord prefers that His saved ones who love Him listen to Him ([[Luke-10#v39|v. 39]]) that they may know His desire, rather than do things for Him without knowing His will (cf. [[1 Samuel-15#v22|1 Sam. 15:22]]; [[Ecclesiastes-05#v1|Eccl. 5:1]]). 

It is quite significant that this story of Martha and Mary immediately follows the parable of the good Samaritan. The parable shows the compassion and love of the Savior, who is a man and becomes the sinners’ neighbor; the story of Martha and Mary unveils the desire and preference of the Lord, who is God and becomes the believers’ Master. The Savior gives us His compassion and love so that we can be saved by Him; the Lord expresses His desire and preference so that we can serve Him. After receiving salvation from the Savior, we should render service to the Lord. For our salvation we need to realize the Savior’s compassion and love; for our service we need to know the Lord’s desire and preference.
