## v1

[[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 1.1 — And|And]] He said also to the disciples, There was a certain rich man who had a [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 1.2 — steward|steward]], and this one was accused to him of squandering his possessions.

## v2

And he called him and said to him, What is this I hear concerning you? Render the account of your stewardship, for you can no longer be steward.

## v3

And the steward said within himself, What shall I do, because my master is taking the stewardship away from me? I am not strong enough to [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 3.1 — dig|dig]]; I am ashamed to [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 3.2 — beg|beg]].

## v4

I know what I will do so that when I am removed from the stewardship they may [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 4.1 — receive|receive]] me into their own houses.

## v5

And when he had called to him each one of his master’s debtors, he said to the first, How much do you owe my master?

## v6

And he said, A hundred [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 6.1 — measures|measures]] of oil. And he said to him, Take your bill and sit down quickly and write fifty.

## v7

Then to another he said, And you, how much do you owe? And he said, A hundred [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 7.1 — measures|measures]] of wheat. He said to him, Take your bill and write eighty.

## v8

And the master praised the unrighteous steward because he had acted [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 8.1 — prudently|prudently]]; for the [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 8.2 — sons|sons]] of this age are more prudent in their dealings with their own generation than the [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 8.3 — sons|sons]] of light.

## v9

And I say to you, [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 9.1 — Make|Make]] friends for yourselves by means of the mammon of [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 9.2 — unrighteousness|unrighteousness]], so that when it [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 9.3 — fails|fails]], they may receive you into the [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 9.4 — eternal|eternal]] tabernacles.

## v10

He who is faithful in the [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 10.1 — least|least]] is faithful also in [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 10.1 — least|much]]; and he who is unrighteous in the least is unrighteous also in much.

## v11

If therefore you have not become faithful in the unrighteous mammon, who will entrust to you what is [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 11.1 — true|true]]?

## v12

And if you have not become faithful in that which belongs to [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 12.1 — another|another]], who will give to you that which is [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 12.2 — your|your]] own?

## v13

No household servant can [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 13.1 — serve|serve]] two masters; for either he will hate the one and [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 13.2 — love|love]] the other, or he will hold to one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 13.3 — mammon|mammon]].

## v14

And the Pharisees, being lovers of money, heard all these things and [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 14.1 — were|were]] sneering at Him.

## v15

And He said to them, You are those who justify yourselves in the sight of men, but God knows your hearts; for that which is exalted among men is an [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 15.1 — abomination|abomination]] in the sight of God.

## v16

The [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 16.1 — law|law]] and the prophets were [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 16.2 — until|until]] John; from that time the [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 16.3 — kingdom|kingdom]] of God is proclaimed as the [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 16.4 — gospel|gospel]], and everyone [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 16.5 — forces|forces]] his way into it.

## v17

But it is easier for heaven and earth to pass away than for one [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 17.1 — serif|serif]] of the law to [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 17.2 — fail|fail]].

## v18

Everyone who [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 18.1 — divorces|divorces]] his wife and marries another commits adultery, and he who marries a woman who is divorced from her husband commits adultery.

## v19

Now there was [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 19.1 — a|a]] certain rich man, and he clothed himself in purple and fine linen, making merry every day in splendor.

## v20

And a certain beggar named Lazarus was laid at his gate, covered with sores

## v21

And longing to be satisfied with the things falling from the rich man’s table; moreover, even the dogs came and licked his sores.

## v22

And the beggar died, and he was carried away by the angels into [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 22.1 — Abraham’s|Abraham’s]] bosom; and the rich man also died and was buried.

## v23

And in [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 23.1 — Hades|Hades]] he lifted up his eyes, being in torment, and saw Abraham from afar and Lazarus in his bosom.

## v24

And he called out and said, Father Abraham, have mercy on me and send Lazarus to dip the tip of his finger in water and cool my tongue, because I am in anguish in this flame.

## v25

But Abraham said, Child, remember that in your lifetime you fully received your good things, and Lazarus likewise bad things; but now he is comforted here, and you are in anguish.

## v26

And besides all these things, between us and you a great [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 26.1 — chasm|chasm]] is fixed, so that those wanting to pass from here to you cannot, neither from there to us may any cross over.

## v27

And he said, Then I ask you, Father, to send him to the house of my father —

## v28

For I have five brothers — so that he may solemnly testify to them, lest they also come to this place of torment.

## v29

But Abraham said, They have [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 29.1 — Moses|Moses]] and the prophets; let them hear them.

## v30

But he said, No, Father Abraham; but if someone risen from the dead would go to them, they will repent.

## v31

But he said to him, If they do [[Luke-16.fn#Footnote 31.1 — not|not]] hear Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded if someone rises from the dead.
