### Footnote 1.1 — But

### Footnote 1.2 — last

**Verse:** [[2 Timothy-03#v1|2 Timothy 3:1]]

Denoting the present age ([[2 Peter-03#v3|2 Pet. 3:3]]; [[Jude-01#v18|Jude 18]]), which began with Christ’s first coming ([[1 Peter-01#v20|1 Pet. 1:20]]) and will last until His second appearing. That this period was to be lengthy was not revealed to the apostles ([[Matthew-24#v36|Matt. 24:36]]); they expected the Lord to return in their generation.

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — men

**Verse:** [[2 Timothy-03#v2|2 Timothy 3:2]]

The prophetic picture presented in [[2 Timothy-03#v2|vv. 2-5]] portrays not the evil condition of the non-Christian society but the corrupted situation of the great house mentioned in [[2 Timothy-02#v20|2:20]], which is Christianity in decline. This is proved by the phrase having an outward form of godliness in [[2 Timothy-03#v5|v. 5]]. Non-Christians do not have even the outward form of godliness.

### Footnote 2.2 — lovers

**Verse:** [[2 Timothy-03#v2|2 Timothy 3:2]]

In 1 and 2 Timothy and Titus seven kinds of lovers are mentioned: (1) lovers of self, (2) lovers of money ([[2 Timothy-03#v2|v. 2]]; [[1 Timothy-06#v10|1 Tim. 6:10]]), (3) lovers of pleasure, (4) lovers of God ([[2 Timothy-03#v4|v. 4]]), (5) lovers of good ([[Titus-01#v8|Titus 1:8]]), (6) lovers of husbands, and (7) lovers of children ([[Titus-02#v4|Titus 2:4]]). Also, two kinds of nonlovers are mentioned: (1) nonlovers of good, and (2) nonlovers of God ([[2 Timothy-03#v3|vv. 3-4]]). Whatever one loves, his whole heart, even his entire being, is set on and occupied and possessed by. This is crucial! Whether there would be a day of glory in the church’s victory or grievous days of the church’s decline depends altogether on what kind of lovers we are. History tells us that the root of the church’s decline was the loss of her first love toward the Lord ([[Revelation-02#v4|Rev. 2:4]]). To maintain the victorious standard of the church, we must be lovers of God and lovers of the good that pertains to God’s economy.

## Footnotes for verse 16

### Footnote 16.1 — All

**Verse:** [[2 Timothy-03#v16|2 Timothy 3:16]]

Or, Every Scripture God-breathed is also profitable. To confront the death, corruption, and confusion in the church’s decline, the eternal life, on which [[2 Timothy-01|2 Timothy 1]] is based ([[2 Timothy-01#v1|1:1]], [[2 Timothy-01#v10|10]]), the divine truth, emphasized in [[2 Timothy-02|2 Timothy 2]] ([[2 Timothy-02#v15|2:15]], [[2 Timothy-02#v18|18]], [[2 Timothy-02#v25|25]]), and the Holy Scripture, highly regarded in [[2 Timothy-03|2 Timothy 3]] ([[2 Timothy-03#v14|3:14-17]]), are all needed. The eternal life not only swallows up death but also renders the life supply; the divine truth replaces the vanity of corruption with the reality of all the divine riches; and the Holy Scripture not only dispels the confusion but also furnishes divine light and revelation. Hence, in this book the apostle stressed these three things.

### Footnote 16.2 — God-breathed

**Verse:** [[2 Timothy-03#v16|2 Timothy 3:16]]

This indicates that the Scripture, the word of God, is the breathing out of God. God’s speaking is God’s breathing out. Hence, His word is spirit ([[John-06#v63|John 6:63]]), or breath. Thus, the Scripture is the embodiment of God as the Spirit. The Spirit is therefore the very essence, the substance, of the Scripture, just as phosphorus is the essential substance in matches. We must strike the Spirit of the Scripture with our spirit to catch the divine fire. 

As the embodiment of God the Spirit, the Scripture (God’s word) is also the embodiment of Christ. Christ is God’s living Word ([[Revelation-19#v13|Rev. 19:13]]), and the Scripture is God’s written word ([[Matthew-04#v4|Matt. 4:4]]).
