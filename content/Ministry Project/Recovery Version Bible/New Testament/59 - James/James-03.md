Author: James
---
## v1

Do not become many [[James-03.fn#Footnote 1.1 — teachers|teachers]], my brothers, knowing that we will receive greater [[James-03.fn#Footnote 1.2 — judgment|judgment]].

## v2

For in many things we all stumble. If anyone does not stumble in word, this one is a perfect man, able to bridle the whole body as well.

## v3

Now if we put bridles into the mouths of horses that they would obey us, we direct their whole body as well.

## v4

Behold, the ships also, though they are so great and are driven by rough winds, are directed by a very small rudder wherever the impulse of the pilot wills.

## v5

So also the tongue is a little member yet boasts great things. Behold, how great a forest so little a [[James-03.fn#Footnote 5.1 — fire|fire]] ignites!

## v6

And the tongue is a [[James-03.fn#Footnote 6.1 — fire|fire]]; the very world of unrighteousness, the tongue is set among our members as that which contaminates the whole body and sets on fire the [[James-03.fn#Footnote 6.2 — course|course]] of life and is set on fire by [[James-03.fn#Footnote 6.3 — Gehenna|Gehenna]].

## v7

For every nature, both of beasts and of birds, both of reptiles and of creatures of the sea, is tamed and has been tamed by the human [[James-03.fn#Footnote 7.1 — nature|nature]];

## v8

But the tongue no one among men is able to tame; it is a [[James-03.fn#Footnote 8.1 — restless|restless]] evil, full of deadly poison.

## v9

With it we bless the Lord and Father, and with it we curse men, who have been made according to the likeness of God.

## v10

Out of the same mouth come forth blessing and cursing. These things, my brothers, ought not to be so.

## v11

Does the spring, out of the same [[James-03.fn#Footnote 11.1 — opening|opening]], gush forth the sweet and the bitter?

## v12

Can a fig tree, my brothers, produce olives? Or a vine, figs? Neither can [[James-03.fn#Footnote 12.1 — salt|salt]] water produce sweet.

## v13

Who is wise and understanding among you? Let him show [[James-03.fn#Footnote 13.1 — by|by]] his good manner of life his [[James-03.fn#Footnote 13.2 — works|works]] in [[James-03.fn#Footnote 13.3 — meekness|meekness]] of wisdom.

## v14

But if you have bitter jealousy and selfish ambition in your heart, do not boast and lie against the truth.

## v15

[[James-03.fn#Footnote 15.1 — This|This]] wisdom is not that which descends from above, but is [[James-03.fn#Footnote 15.2 — earthly|earthly]], soulish, demonic.

## v16

For where jealousy and selfish ambition are, there disorder and every worthless practice are.

## v17

But the [[James-03.fn#Footnote 17.1 — wisdom|wisdom]] from above is first pure, then peaceable, [[James-03.fn#Footnote 17.2 — forbearing|forbearing]], [[James-03.fn#Footnote 17.3 — compliant|compliant]], full of mercy and good fruit, impartial, without hypocrisy.

## v18

And the [[James-03.fn#Footnote 18.1 — fruit|fruit]] of righteousness is sown in peace [[James-03.fn#Footnote 18.2 — by|by]] those who make peace.
