# James-05 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[James-05|James 5]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — rich

**Verse:** [[James-05#v1|James 5:1]]

[[James-05#v1|Verses 1-6]] could be considered a parenthetical section, and it might have been addressed, in light of its contents, to the rich class in general among the Jews, since James considered the recipients of his Epistle to be the twelve tribes of the Jews ([[James-01#v1|1:1]]). See notes [1³](<James-01.fn#Footnote 1.3 — twelve>) in [[James-01|James 1]] and [2¹](<James-02.fn#Footnote 2.1 — synagogue>) in [[James-02|James 2]].

## Footnotes for verse 4

### Footnote 4.1 — cries

**Verse:** [[James-05#v4|James 5:4]]

Or, imploring.

### Footnote 4.2 — Lord

**Verse:** [[James-05#v4|James 5:4]]

As in [[Romans-09#v29|Rom. 9:29]], this is equivalent to the divine title in Hebrew, Jehovah-Sabaoth (Jehovah of hosts, Jehovah of armies—[[1 Samuel-01#v3|1 Sam. 1:3]]). Such a title bears a Jewish character.

## Footnotes for verse 5

### Footnote 5.1 — nourished

**Verse:** [[James-05#v5|James 5:5]]

I.e., satiated their gluttonous desire in indulgence, even in a day of slaughter ([[Jeremiah-12#v3|Jer. 12:3]]), i.e., in a day of judgment, when they were to be slaughtered like animals by God’s judgment. This implies that they were in a stupor, unaware of their coming miseries, their miserable destiny ([[James-05#v1|v. 1]]).

## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — righteous

**Verse:** [[James-05#v6|James 5:6]]

The collective singular, with the article used to indicate the class. It does not refer directly to any individual but portrays the death of the Lord Jesus, who is the Righteous ([[Acts-07#v52|Acts 7:52]]; [[Acts-03#v14|3:14]]).

## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — longsuffering

**Verse:** [[James-05#v8|James 5:8]]

See [note 10²](<James-05.fn#Footnote 10.2 — longsuffering>). While we are awaiting with long-suffering the Lord’s coming, He, as the real Farmer ([[Matthew-13#v3|Matt. 13:3]]), is awaiting with patience our maturity in life as the firstfruits and harvest of His field ([[Revelation-14#v4|Rev. 14:4]], [[Revelation-14#v14|14-15]]). Our maturing in life can shorten the period of our long-suffering and His patience.

## Footnotes for verse 9

### Footnote 9.1 — complain

**Verse:** [[James-05#v9|James 5:9]]

Lit., groan.

### Footnote 9.2 — Judge

**Verse:** [[James-05#v9|James 5:9]]

The Lord will come back not only as the Bridegroom to meet the bride ([[Matthew-25#v1|Matt. 25:1]], [[Matthew-25#v6|6]]; [[Revelation-19#v7|Rev. 19:7-8]]) but also as the Judge to judge all peoples, judging first His believers at His judgment seat ([[1 Corinthians-04#v4|1 Cor. 4:4-5]]; [[2 Corinthians-05#v10|2 Cor. 5:10]]). We need to pursue maturity in life in order to meet the Lord and be prepared to be judged by Him.

## Footnotes for verse 10

### Footnote 10.1 — suffering

**Verse:** [[James-05#v10|James 5:10]]

This is a further development of [[James-05#v7|vv. 7-8]], which concern the suffering and long-suffering of the faithful believers.

### Footnote 10.2 — longsuffering

**Verse:** [[James-05#v10|James 5:10]]

The Greek word—in noun form here and in [[Hebrews-06#v12|Heb. 6:12]]; [[Romans-02#v4|Rom. 2:4]]; [[2 TImothy-04#v2|2 Tim. 4:2]]; [[1 Peter-03#v20|1 Pet. 3:20]], and in verb form in [[James-05#v7|vv. 7-8]]—connotes patience toward persons, such as the patience the prophets had toward those who persecuted them.

### Footnote 10.3 — in

**Verse:** [[James-05#v10|James 5:10]]

Speaking in the name of the Lord indicates that the prophets were one with the Lord. Hence, their suffering and long-suffering were with the Lord and for the Lord. The faithful believers’ suffering and long-suffering should be the same.

## Footnotes for verse 11

### Footnote 11.1 — endured

**Verse:** [[James-05#v11|James 5:11]]

The Greek word—in verb form here and in noun form in [[Romans-05#v3|Rom. 5:3]] and [[2 Corinthians-01#v6|2 Cor. 1:6]]—connotes patience toward things, such as the patience that Job had toward the things that afflicted him.

## Footnotes for verse 12

### Footnote 12.1 — above

**Verse:** [[James-05#v12|James 5:12]]

Lit., before.

### Footnote 12.2 — not

**Verse:** [[James-05#v12|James 5:12]]

We should not swear, because we are nothing, and nothing is under our control or up to us ([[Matthew-05#v34|Matt. 5:34-36]]). Swearing displays the acting of our self-will and our forgetting of God. But to let our yes be yes and our no be no is to act according to our divine nature, in the consciousness of the presence of God, denying our self-will and sinful nature.

### Footnote 12.3 — yes

**Verse:** [[James-05#v12|James 5:12]]

See [note 37¹](<Matthew-05.fn#Footnote 37.1 — Yes>) in [[Matthew-05|Matt. 5]].

### Footnote 12.4 — lest

**Verse:** [[James-05#v12|James 5:12]]

Our being genuinely faithful and sincere in our words according to the divine nature, of which we partake, will keep us from God’s judgment (cf. [[Matthew-12#v36|Matt. 12:36]]).

## Footnotes for verse 13

### Footnote 13.1 — pray

**Verse:** [[James-05#v13|James 5:13]]

Praying brings us the strength of the Lord to endure suffering, and singing praise keeps us in the joy of the Lord.

### Footnote 13.2 — sing

**Verse:** [[James-05#v13|James 5:13]]

The Greek word means primarily play on a stringed instrument, and thence, make melody. Hence, it denotes to sing a hymn, to sing praise. Whether we pray or sing praise, we contact God. In any environment and under any circumstance, humble or exalted, grievous or joyful, we need to contact the Lord.

## Footnotes for verse 14

### Footnote 14.1 — ill

**Verse:** [[James-05#v14|James 5:14]]

Ill because of weakness. Weakness leads to illness ([[1 Corinthians-11#v30|1 Cor. 11:30]]), and illness causes more weakness.

### Footnote 14.2 — call

**Verse:** [[James-05#v14|James 5:14]]

To call the elders of the church to pray regarding one’s illness implies (1) that there is no problem between the calling one and the church, represented by the elders; (2) that a normal relationship has been restored between the calling one and the church, if the illness is due to the calling one’s having offended the church (cf. [[1 Corinthians-11#v29|1 Cor. 11:29-32]]); and (3) that the sick one and the elders have thoroughly confessed to one another their sins ([[James-05#v16|v. 16]]). The barriers in the church must be cleared away before the elders can represent the church in praying for the sick ones.

### Footnote 14.3 — anointing

**Verse:** [[James-05#v14|James 5:14]]

Two Greek words are translated anoint: aleipho, the word used here and in [[John-12#v3|John 12:3]], is the common term used for applying oil; chrio means to anoint officially for office, as a priest ([[Acts-10#v38|Acts 10:38]]), king ([[Hebrews-01#v9|Heb. 1:9]]), or prophet ([[Luke-04#v18|Luke 4:18]]). Chrio, related to Christos (Christ), is used for the anointing of the Son by the Father ([[Acts-10#v38|Acts 10:38]]). To anoint with oil signifies to impart the Spirit of life, who has been poured upon the Body of Christ as the anointing oil ([[Psalm-133#v2|Psa. 133:2]]), to the sick member of the Body through the elders as representatives of the church, for the healing of the sick one (cf. [[1 John-05#v16|1 John 5:16]] and notes [3](<1 John-05.fn#Footnote 16.3 — will>) and [4](<1 John-05.fn#Footnote 16.4 — life>)).

### Footnote 14.4 — in

**Verse:** [[James-05#v14|James 5:14]]

In the name of the Lord signifies oneness with the Lord. The elders do not do the anointing alone; rather, by being one with the Lord, they represent both the Body and the Head to do the anointing.

## Footnotes for verse 17

### Footnote 17.1 — earnestly

**Verse:** [[James-05#v17|James 5:17]]

Lit., prayed in prayer. This indicates that a prayer from the Lord was given to Elijah, in which he prayed. He did not pray in his feeling, thought, intention, or mood, or in any kind of motivation, arising from circumstances or situations, to fulfill his own purpose. He prayed in the prayer given to him by the Lord for the accomplishing of His will.

## Footnotes for verse 19

### Footnote 19.1 — led

**Verse:** [[James-05#v19|James 5:19]]

This may imply that the ill one in [[James-05#v14|v. 14]] had been led astray from the truth and needed to be turned back.

## Footnotes for verse 20

### Footnote 20.1 — sinner

**Verse:** [[James-05#v20|James 5:20]]

According to the context of these two verses, this is not an unbelieving sinner but a believing brother who has been led astray from the truth and is turned back from his error to the truth. Hence, the salvation of his soul does not refer to the eternal salvation of the person but to the dispensational salvation of his soul from the suffering of physical death under God’s discipline. James may also have considered all the virtues covered in [[James-05#v7|vv. 7-20]] to be aspects of practical Christian perfection.

### Footnote 20.2 — save

**Verse:** [[James-05#v20|James 5:20]]

See notes [21²](<James-01.fn#Footnote 21.2 — save>) in [[James-01|James 1]], [5⁵](<1 Peter-01.fn#Footnote 5.5 — salvation>) in [[1 Peter-01|1 Pet. 1]], and [39³](<Hebrews-10.fn#Footnote 39.3 — gaining>) in [[Hebrews-10|Heb. 10]].

### Footnote 20.3 — death

**Verse:** [[James-05#v20|James 5:20]]

Not eternal perdition but dispensational discipline by physical death (see [note 16⁵](<1 John-05.fn#Footnote 16.5 — sin>) in [[1 John-05|1 John 5]]). From death here should equal raise—up in [[James-05#v15|v. 15]].

### Footnote 20.4 — cover

**Verse:** [[James-05#v20|James 5:20]]

This is an Old Testament expression ([[Psalm-32#v1|Psa. 32:1]]; [[Psalm-85#v2|85:2]]; [[Proverbs-10#v12|Prov. 10:12]]) used by James to indicate that turning an erring brother back is to cover his sins so that he is not condemned. Cover—sins here should equal sins—forgiven in [[James-05#v15|v. 15]], as in [[Psalm-32#v1|Psa. 32:1]]; [[Psalm-85#v2|85:2]].

### Footnote 20.5 — sins

**Verse:** [[James-05#v20|James 5:20]]

Sins committed by the sinning brother, which brought forth death to him ([[James-01#v15|1:15]]).
