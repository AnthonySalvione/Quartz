---
Author: James
---
## v1

My brothers, do not hold the [[James-02.fn#Footnote 1.1 — faith|faith]] of our Lord Jesus Christ [[James-02.fn#Footnote 1.2 — of|of]] glory with respect of persons.

## v2

For if there comes into your [[James-02.fn#Footnote 2.1 — synagogue|synagogue]] a man with gold rings in splendid clothing, and there also comes in a poor man in filthy clothing,

## v3

And you look upon the one wearing the splendid clothing and say, You sit here in a good place, and to the poor man you say, You stand there, or sit under my footstool;

## v4

Have you not made [[James-02.fn#Footnote 4.1 — distinctions|distinctions]] [[James-02.fn#Footnote 4.2 — among|among]] yourselves and become judges with evil reasonings?

## v5

Listen, my beloved brothers: Did not God choose the poor [[James-02.fn#Footnote 5.1 — in|in]] the world to be rich in faith and heirs of the [[James-02.fn#Footnote 5.2 — kingdom|kingdom]], which He promised to those who [[James-02.fn#Footnote 5.3 — love|love]] Him?

## v6

But you have dishonored the poor. Is it not the rich who oppress you and is it not they who drag you to the [[James-02.fn#Footnote 6.1 — courts|courts]]?

## v7

Is it not they who blaspheme the [[James-02.fn#Footnote 7.1 — honorable|honorable]] name [[James-02.fn#Footnote 7.2 — by|by]] which you are called?

## v8

If indeed you are fulfilling the [[James-02.fn#Footnote 8.1 — royal|royal]] law according to the Scripture, “You shall [[James-02.fn#Footnote 8.2 — love|love]] your neighbor as yourself,” you do well;

## v9

But if you respect persons, you [[James-02.fn#Footnote 9.1 — commit|commit]] sin, being convicted by the law as transgressors.

## v10

For whoever [[James-02.fn#Footnote 10.1 — keeps|keeps]] the whole law yet stumbles in one point has become guilty of all.

## v11

For He who said, Do not commit adultery, also said, Do not murder. Now if you do not commit adultery, but you murder, you have become a transgressor of the law.

## v12

[[James-02.fn#Footnote 12.1 — So|So]] speak and so do as those who are to be judged by the [[James-02.fn#Footnote 12.2 — law|law]] of freedom.

## v13

For the judgment is without mercy to him who has shown no mercy; mercy [[James-02.fn#Footnote 13.1 — triumphs|triumphs]] over judgment.

## v14

What is the profit, my brothers, if anyone says he has faith but does not have works? Can that faith [[James-02.fn#Footnote 14.1 — save|save]] him?

## v15

If a brother or sister [[James-02.fn#Footnote 15.1 — is|is]] without clothing and lacks daily food,

## v16

And any one of you says to them, [[James-02.fn#Footnote 16.1 — Go|Go]] in peace, be warmed and filled, yet you do not give them the [[James-02.fn#Footnote 16.2 — necessities|necessities]] of the body, what is the profit?

## v17

So also [[James-02.fn#Footnote 17.1 — faith|faith]], if it does not have works, is dead [[James-02.fn#Footnote 17.2 — in|in]] itself.

## v18

But someone will say, You have faith, and I have works; show me your faith without the works, and I will show you my faith by my works.

## v19

You believe that God is one. You do well; the demons also believe and shudder.

## v20

But are you willing to know, O vain man, that faith without its works is [[James-02.fn#Footnote 20.1 — useless|useless]]?

## v21

Was not Abraham our father justified by works in that he offered up Isaac his son upon the altar?

## v22

You see that faith worked together with his works, and by these works faith was perfected.

## v23

And the Scripture was fulfilled which says, “And Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him as righteousness”; and he was called the friend of God.

## v24

You see that a man is [[James-02.fn#Footnote 24.1 — justified|justified]] by works and not by faith only.

## v25

And in like manner was not also Rahab the harlot justified by works in that she received the messengers and [[James-02.fn#Footnote 25.1 — sent|sent]] them out by a different way?

## v26

For just as the body without the [[James-02.fn#Footnote 26.1 — spirit|spirit]] is dead, so also faith without works is dead.
