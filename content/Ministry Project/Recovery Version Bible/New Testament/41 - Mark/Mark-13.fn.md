### Footnote 1.2 — one

**Verse:** [[Mark-13#v1|Mark 13:1]]

In His preparation for His redemptive work ([[Mark-11#v15|11:15]]—[[Mark-14#v42|14:42]]), after confronting the opposers ([[Mark-11#v15|11:15]]—[[Mark-12#v37|12:37]]) the Slave-Savior stayed with His followers to prepare them for His death ([[Mark-13#v1|13:1]]—[[Mark-14#v42|14:42]]), an unexpected event that would shock and disappoint them, by (1) telling them the things that would come ([[Mark-13#v2|vv. 2-37]]); (2) enjoying their love, expressed in a feast, and being anointed with costly, pure nard ([[Mark-14#v3|14:3-9]]); (3) instituting His supper ([[1 Corinthians-11#v20|1 Cor. 11:20]]) that they might remember Him ([[Mark-14#v12|14:12-26]]); and (4) warning them that they would stumble and charging them to watch and pray ([[Mark-14#v27|14:27-42]]). Immediately after such a preparation, He was arrested to be crucified ([[Mark-14#v43|14:43]]—[[Mark-15#v28|15:28]]).

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — There

**Verse:** [[Mark-13#v2|Mark 13:2]]

In preparing the disciples for His death, the Slave-Savior in this chapter told them first the things to come, the things that would take place in the world during the church age, from His resurrection to His coming back. Concerning the days to come, He did not leave them in darkness; rather, He told them (1) concerning the destruction of the temple, which would occur in A.D. 70 ([[Mark-13#v1|vv. 1-2]]); (2) concerning the plagues at the beginning of birth pangs, which would begin after His ascension and continue until the great tribulation ([[Mark-13#v3|vv. 3-8]]); (3) concerning the preaching of the gospel and the persecutions in the church age ([[Mark-13#v9|vv. 9-13]]); (4) concerning the great tribulation and His second coming, both of which will take place in the last three and a half years of this age ([[Mark-13#v14|vv. 14-27]]); and (5) concerning watching, praying, and awaiting the Slave-Savior throughout the church age ([[Mark-13#v28|vv. 28-37]]). To the Slave-Savior’s suffering followers, such an enlightening word was like “a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns” ([[2 Peter-01#v19|2 Pet. 1:19]]).
