### Footnote 12.2 — sacrificed

**Verse:** [[Mark-14#v12|Mark 14:12]]

In the Jews’ calendar, which was according to their Scripture, a day began with the evening ([[Genesis-01#v5|Gen. 1:5]]). During the evening of the last Passover day, first the Slave-Savior ate the passover feast with His disciples and instituted His supper for them ([[Mark-14#v12|vv. 12-25]]); then He went with the disciples to the Garden of Gethsemane at the Mount of Olives ([[Mark-14#v26|vv. 26-42]]). He was arrested there and brought to the high priest, where He was judged by the Sanhedrin late in the night ([[Mark-14#v43|vv. 43-72]]). In the morning of the same day, He was delivered to Pilate to be judged by him and was sentenced to death ([[Mark-15#v1|15:1-15]]). Then He was brought to Golgotha and was crucified there at 9:00 a.m., remaining on the cross until 3:00 p.m. ([[Mark-15#v16|15:16-41]]), for the fulfillment of the type of the Passover ([[Exodus-12#v6|Exo. 12:6-11]]; see [note 37¹](<Mark-12.fn#Footnote 37.1 — son>) in [[Mark-12|Mark 12]]).

## Footnotes for verse 20

### Footnote 20.1 — one

**Verse:** [[Mark-14#v20|Mark 14:20]]

After being exposed, Judas left ([[John-13#v21|John 13:21-30]]) before the Slave-Savior’s supper ([[Matthew-26#v20|Matt. 26:20-26]]). He did not participate in the Slave-Savior’s body and blood, because he was not a real believer in Him but a son of perdition ([[John-17#v12|John 17:12]]), considered by the Slave-Savior even a devil ([[John-06#v70|John 6:70-71]]). [[Luke-22#v21|Luke 22:21-23]] seems to indicate that Judas left after the Lord’s supper, which is mentioned in [[Luke-22#v19|Luke 22:19-20]]. However, Mark’s record, like Matthew’s, shows that Judas was identified by the Slave-Savior as His betrayer ([[Mark-14#v18|vv. 18-21]]) before He instituted His supper ([[Mark-14#v22|vv. 22-24]]). Mark’s record is according to historical sequence, whereas Luke’s sequence is according to morality. See [note 16¹](<Matthew-08.fn#Footnote 16.1 — many>), par. 2, in [[Matthew-08|Matt. 8]].

### Footnote 22.2 — eating

**Verse:** [[Mark-14#v22|Mark 14:22]]

This was the eating of the Slave-Savior’s supper, after He and His followers had eaten the passover feast in [[Mark-14#v16|vv. 16-18]]. He initiated this new feast, which was for His believers’ remembrance of Him, to replace the passover feast, the old testament feast for the elect’s remembrance of Jehovah’s salvation ([[Exodus-12#v14|Exo. 12:14]]; [[Exodus-13#v3|13:3]]). This new feast of the new testament is for the remembering of the Slave-Savior through the eating of the bread, which signifies His body given for His believers ([[1 Corinthians-11#v24|1 Cor. 11:24]]), and the drinking of the cup, which signifies His blood shed for their sins ([[Matthew-26#v28|Matt. 26:28]]). The bread denotes life ([[John-06#v35|John 6:35]]), the life of God, the eternal life, and the cup denotes blessing ([[1 Corinthians-10#v16|1 Cor. 10:16]]), which is God Himself as the believers’ portion ([[Psalm-16#v5|Psa. 16:5]]). As sinners, the believers should have had the cup of God’s wrath as their portion ([[Revelation-14#v10|Rev. 14:10]]). But the Slave-Savior drank that cup for them ([[John-18#v11|John 18:11]]), and His salvation became their portion, the cup of salvation ([[Psalm-116#v13|Psa. 116:13]]) that runs over ([[Psalm-23#v5|Psa. 23:5]]), the content of which is God as the believers’ all-inclusive blessing. Such a bread and such a cup are the constituents of the Slave-Savior’s supper, which is a table ([[1 Corinthians-10#v21|1 Cor. 10:21]]), a feast, set up by Him that His believers may remember Him by enjoying Him as such a feast. Thus, as His believers remember Him, they display His redeeming and life-imparting death ([[1 Corinthians-11#v26|1 Cor. 11:26]]—His blood separated from His body declares His death), testifying to the entire universe of His rich and marvelous salvation.

## Footnotes for verse 24

### Footnote 24.1 — covenant

**Verse:** [[Mark-14#v24|Mark 14:24]]

In [[Exodus-24#v3|Exo. 24:3-8]] God made a covenant with redeemed Israel ([[Hebrews-09#v18|Heb. 9:18-21]]), which became the old testament, as a base from which He could deal with His redeemed people in the dispensation of law. The Slave-Savior came to accomplish God’s eternal redemption for God’s chosen people by His death, according to God’s will ([[Hebrews-10#v7|Heb. 10:7]], [[Hebrews-10#v9|9-10]]), and with His blood He instituted a new covenant, a better covenant ([[Hebrews-08#v6|Heb. 8:6-13]]), which after His resurrection became the new testament ([[Hebrews-09#v16|Heb. 9:16-17]]), as a base from which God can be one with His redeemed and regenerated people in the dispensation of grace. This new covenant replaced the old covenant and at the same time changed God’s old dispensation to His new dispensation. The Slave-Savior wanted His followers to know this and to live a life based on this and according to this after His resurrection.

## Footnotes for verse 36

### Footnote 36.1 — You

**Verse:** [[Mark-14#v36|Mark 14:36]]

The Triune God determined in His divine plan in eternity past that the Second of the Divine Trinity should be incarnated and die on the cross to accomplish His eternal redemption for the fulfillment of His eternal purpose ([[Ephesians-01#v7|Eph. 1:7-9]]). Hence, before the foundation of the world, that is, in eternity past ([[1 Peter-01#v19|1 Pet. 1:19-20]]), the Second of the Divine Trinity was ordained to be the Lamb of God ([[John-01#v29|John 1:29]]); and in the eyes of God He was slain as the Lamb of God from the foundation of the world, that is, from the creation of God’s creatures, who became fallen ([[Revelation-13#v8|Rev. 13:8]]). From the time of man’s fall, lambs, sheep, calves, and bulls were used as types for God’s chosen people ([[Genesis-03#v21|Gen. 3:21]]; [[Genesis-04#v4|4:4]]; [[Genesis-08#v20|8:20]]; [[Genesis-22#v13|22:13]]; [[Exodus-12#v3|Exo. 12:3-8]]; [[Leviticus-01#v2|Lev. 1:2]]), pointing to Him who was to come as the real Lamb foreordained by God. In the fullness of time the Triune God sent the Second of the Divine Trinity, the Son of God, to come in incarnation to take a human body ([[Hebrews-10#v5|Heb. 10:5]]) that He might be offered to God on the cross ([[Hebrews-09#v14|Heb. 9:14]]; [[Hebrews-10#v12|10:12]]) to do the will of the Triune God ([[Hebrews-10#v7|Heb. 10:7]]), that is, to replace the sacrifices and offerings, which were types, with Himself in His humanity as the unique sacrifice and offering for the sanctification of God’s chosen people ([[Hebrews-10#v9|Heb. 10:9-10]]). In His prayer here, immediately before His crucifixion, He prepared Himself to take the cup of the cross ([[Matthew-26#v39|Matt. 26:39]], [[Matthew-26#v42|42]]), being willing to do this unique will of the Father for the accomplishing of the eternal plan of the Triune God.
