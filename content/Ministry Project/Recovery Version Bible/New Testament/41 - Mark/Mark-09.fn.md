## Footnotes for verse 39

### Footnote 39.1 — not

**Verse:** [[Mark-09#v39|Mark 9:39]]

This displayed the Slave-Savior’s tolerance, in the practice of the gospel service, toward those believers who were different from the ones closer to Him. In this respect the attitude of the apostle Paul in [[Philippians-01#v16|Phil. 1:16-18]] and that of Moses in [[Numbers-11#v26|Num. 11:26-29]], but not that of the impetuous John, are like His. It is very meaningful that this section, [[Mark-09#v38|vv. 38-50]], continues the previous section, [[Mark-09#v33|vv. 33-37]], in which the Slave-Savior taught His followers humility because they had argued with one another concerning who was greater. In that argument the two sons of thunder, James and John, probably played the leading roles (cf. [[Mark-10#v35|10:35-45]]). Here the same John would not tolerate a differing believer. His impetuous action was probably related to his ambition to be great. It might have been this ambition that caused his intolerance toward the different practice of other believers. This is a basic divisive factor among Christians. The Slave-Savior surely would not go along with John in this matter.

## Footnotes for verse 40

### Footnote 40.1 — he

**Verse:** [[Mark-09#v40|Mark 9:40]]

This word does not contradict that in [[Matthew-12#v30|Matt. 12:30]]. Both issued from the Slave-Savior’s mouth and can be considered maxims. The maxim here speaks of outward conformity in practice and is in regard to people who are not against Him ([[Mark-09#v39|Mark 9:39]]); that in Matthew speaks of the inward unity of purpose and is in regard to people who are against Him ([[Matthew-12#v24|Matt. 12:24]]). To maintain the inward unity, we need to practice the word in Matthew, and as to the outward conformity, we should practice the word here, tolerating believers who differ from us.

## Footnotes for verse 41

### Footnote 41.1 — you

**Verse:** [[Mark-09#v41|Mark 9:41]]

John behaved as one who acted upon others. The Slave-Savior’s wise word here turned him and the other disciples, making them those acted upon by others. This implied that all—whether the disciples or other believers—were under the Lord’s care because all were His. Whether it was their treatment of other believers or other believers’ treatment of them, anything done in the Lord’s name, even the giving of a cup of water, would be rewarded by Him.

### Footnote 41.2 — Christ’s

**Verse:** [[Mark-09#v41|Mark 9:41]]

This indicates that the Slave-Savior recognized that the one forbidden by John was a genuine believer belonging to Him. This should have been a lesson to John.

### Footnote 41.3 — reward

**Verse:** [[Mark-09#v41|Mark 9:41]]

## Footnotes for verse 49

### Footnote 49.1 — salted

**Verse:** [[Mark-09#v49|Mark 9:49]]

I.e., to kill and eliminate the germs of corruption that came in through sin, in order to preserve the sinning believers (cf. [[Leviticus-02#v13|Lev. 2:13]]; [[Ezekiel-43#v24|Ezek. 43:24]]).

### Footnote 49.2 — fire

**Verse:** [[Mark-09#v49|Mark 9:49]]

This is the refining fire ([[Malachi-03#v2|Mal. 3:2]]), the purifying, purging fire, as in [[1 Corinthians-03#v13|1 Cor. 3:13]], [[1 Corinthians-03#v15|15]] (cf. [[Isaiah-33#v14|Isa. 33:14]]), which, as a dispensational punishment in the kingdom age, will purge the believers who commit sin and are unrepentant in this age (see [note 43⁵](<Mark-09.fn#Footnote 43.5 — unquenchable>)). Even in this age God purges the believers through trials as if by fire ([[1 Peter-01#v7|1 Pet. 1:7]]; [[1 Peter-04#v12|4:12]], [[1 Peter-04#v17|17]]). Dispensational punishment by fire in the coming age is in the same principle as God’s chastisement through sufferings as if by fire in this age.

## Footnotes for verse 50

### Footnote 50.1 — Salt

**Verse:** [[Mark-09#v50|Mark 9:50]]

See [note 13²](<Matthew-05.fn#Footnote 13.2 — salt>) in [[Matthew-05|Matt. 5]]. The application of the word here differs from that in [[Matthew-05#v13|Matt. 5:13]] and [[Luke-14#v34|Luke 14:34]]. In Matthew and Luke, salt, portraying the believers’ influence on the world, is for the salting of the corrupted world; salt here, in apposition to the purging fire, is for the salting of sinning believers, even as God’s chastisements are for the sinning believers in this age. Hence, the believers should have salt in themselves that they may be purified not only from sins but also from any divisive element (such as was displayed by the impetuous John in forbidding a differing brother and in arguing for greatness) and may thus be at peace with one another. Such purging salt purges the believers’ speech that they may keep peace with one another ([[Colossians-04#v6|Col. 4:6]]). The Lord’s word here indicates that John’s speech to the brother who differed from him had not been purged. Thus, the entire section, [[Mark-09#v38|vv. 38-50]], presents the Slave-Savior’s teaching concerning the believers’ being tolerant for the sake of unity.
