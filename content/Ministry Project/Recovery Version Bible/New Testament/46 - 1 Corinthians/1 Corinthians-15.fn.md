# 1 Corinthians-15 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[1 Corinthians-15|1 Corinthians 15]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — gospel

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v1|1 Corinthians 15:1]]

The full gospel, including the teachings concerning Christ and the church, as fully disclosed in the book of Romans ([[Romans-01#v1|Rom. 1:1]]; [[Romans-16#v25|16:25]]).

### Footnote 1.2 — stand

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v1|1 Corinthians 15:1]]

We should stand in the full gospel, that is, in the entire New Testament, not just in certain teachings or doctrines.

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — being

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v2|1 Corinthians 15:2]]

Or, in the way of salvation (Conybeare). Having been justified in Christ and regenerated by the Spirit, we are in the process of being saved in the life of Christ ([[Romans-05#v10|Rom. 5:10]]), and will be until we are mature and conformed to Him in full ([[Romans-08#v29|Rom. 8:29]]).

## Footnotes for verse 3

### Footnote 3.1 — Christ

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v3|1 Corinthians 15:3]]

Christ’s death for our sins, His burial for our termination, and His resurrection for our germination with life, which were according to the prophecies of the Old Testament ([[Isaiah-53#v5|Isa. 53:5-8]], [[Isaiah-53#v10|10-12]]; [[Psalm-22#v14|Psa. 22:14-18]]; [[Daniel-09#v26|Dan. 9:26]]; [[Isaiah-53#v9|Isa. 53:9]]; [[Psalm-16#v9|Psa. 16:9-10]]; [[Hosea-06#v2|Hosea 6:2]]), are the basic items among the first things of the gospel. The last of these items is the most vital in the gospel, for it is the positive aspect of the gospel in that it imparts life to us that we may obtain life and live Christ.

### Footnote 3.2 — Scriptures

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v3|1 Corinthians 15:3]]

The Old Testament, that is, the Law and the Prophets.

## Footnotes for verse 5

### Footnote 5.1 — appeared

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v5|1 Corinthians 15:5]]

Or, was seen by. The earlier apostles and disciples were eyewitnesses of Christ’s resurrection ([[Acts-01#v22|Acts 1:22]]), and their preaching stressed their testimony to this ([[Acts-02#v32|Acts 2:32]]; [[Acts-04#v33|4:33]]). They witnessed to the resurrected Christ not only by their teaching but also by their living. They lived with Him by His living in resurrection ([[John-14#v19|John 14:19]]).

## Footnotes for verse 10

### Footnote 10.1 — grace

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v10|1 Corinthians 15:10]]

Grace, mentioned three times in this verse, is the resurrected Christ becoming the life-giving Spirit ([[1 Corinthians-15#v45|v. 45]]) to bring the processed Triune God in resurrection into us to be our life and life supply that we may live in resurrection. Thus, grace is the Triune God becoming life and everything to us. (See notes [17¹](<John-01.fn#Footnote 17.1 — law>) in [[John-01|John 1]] and [21¹](<Galatians-02.fn#Footnote 21.1 — grace>) in [[Galatians-02|Gal. 2]].) It is by this grace that Saul of Tarsus, the foremost of sinners ([[1 Timothy-01#v15|1 Tim. 1:15-16]]), became the foremost apostle, laboring more abundantly than all the apostles. His ministry and living by this grace are an undeniable testimony to Christ’s resurrection.

### Footnote 10.2 — not

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v10|1 Corinthians 15:10]]

Not I but the grace of God equals no longer I—but—Christ in [[Galatians-02#v20|Gal. 2:20]]. The grace that motivated the apostle and operated in him was not some matter or some thing but a living person, the resurrected Christ, the embodiment of God the Father who became the all-inclusive life-giving Spirit, who dwelt in the apostle as his everything.

## Footnotes for verse 12

### Footnote 12.1 — no

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v12|1 Corinthians 15:12]]

In this chapter the apostle dealt with the Corinthians’ heretical saying that there is no resurrection of the dead. The Corinthians were like the Sadducees ([[Matthew-22#v23|Matt. 22:23]]; [[Acts-23#v8|Acts 23:8]]). This was the tenth problem among them. It is the most damaging and destructive to God’s New Testament economy, worse than the heresy of Hymenaeus and Philetus concerning resurrection ([[2 Timothy-02#v17|2 Tim. 2:17-18]]). Resurrection is the life pulse and lifeline of the divine economy. If there were no resurrection, God would be the God of the dead, not of the living ([[Matthew-22#v32|Matt. 22:32]]). If there were no resurrection, Christ would not have been raised from the dead. He would be a dead Savior, not a living One who lives forever ([[Revelation-01#v18|Rev. 1:18]]) and is able to save to the uttermost ([[Hebrews-07#v25|Heb. 7:25]]). If there were no resurrection, there would be no living proof of our being justified by His death ([[Romans-04#v25|Rom. 4:25]] and [note](<Romans-04.fn#Footnote 25.1 — raised>)), no imparting of life ([[John-12#v24|John 12:24]]), no regeneration ([[John-03#v5|John 3:5]]), no renewing ([[Titus-03#v5|Titus 3:5]]), no transformation ([[Romans-12#v2|Rom. 12:2]]; [[2 Corinthians-03#v18|2 Cor. 3:18]]), and no conformity to the image of Christ ([[Romans-08#v29|Rom. 8:29]]). If there were no resurrection, there would be no members of Christ ([[Romans-12#v5|Rom. 12:5]]), no Body of Christ as His fullness ([[Ephesians-01#v20|Eph. 1:20-23]]), and no church as Christ’s bride ([[John-03#v29|John 3:29]]), and therefore no new man ([[Ephesians-02#v15|Eph. 2:15]]; [[Ephesians-04#v24|4:24]]; [[Colossians-03#v10|Col. 3:10-11]]). If there were no resurrection, God’s New Testament economy would altogether collapse and God’s eternal purpose would be nullified.

## Footnotes for verse 17

### Footnote 17.1 — futile

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v17|1 Corinthians 15:17]]

I.e., fruitless, worthless. If Christ had not been resurrected to live in us as our life and everything, our faith in Him would be fruitless, worthless, and without any issue, such as the impartation of life, freedom from sin, victory over Satan, and growth in life.

### Footnote 17.2 — still

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v17|1 Corinthians 15:17]]

Christ’s death saves us from being condemned because of our sins, not from the power of sin. It is His resurrection life that delivers us from the power of sin ([[Romans-08#v2|Rom. 8:2]]). If Christ had not been resurrected, we would still remain in sins and under the power of sin.

### Footnote 20.1 — But

[[1 Corinthians-15#v20|Verses 20-28]] are a parenthetical word verifying the truth of resurrection by setting forth Christ as the firstfruits of resurrection.

### Footnote 20.2 — firstfruits

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v20|1 Corinthians 15:20]]

Christ was the first One raised from among the dead, becoming the firstfruits of resurrection. This was typified by the firstfruits (a sheaf of the firstfruits, including Christ with some of the dead Old Testament saints, was raised at the Lord’s resurrection—[[Matthew-27#v52|Matt. 27:52-53]]) in [[Leviticus-23#v10|Lev. 23:10-11]], which were offered to God on the day after the Sabbath, the day of resurrection ([[Matthew-28#v1|Matt. 28:1]]). Christ as the firstfruits of resurrection is the Firstborn from among the dead that He might be the Head of the Body ([[Colossians-01#v18|Col. 1:18]]; [[Ephesians-01#v20|Eph. 1:20-23]]). Since He, the Head of the Body, has been resurrected, we, the Body, also will be resurrected.

## Footnotes for verse 24

### Footnote 24.1 — end

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v24|1 Corinthians 15:24]]

In the preceding verse the order of resurrection is mentioned: the first, Christ; then those who are Christ’s. Accordingly, in this verse, after then, one would expect a group of people to be named. However, here it does not tell us who, but it says “the end,” because those who will be resurrected at the end in their own order are not in Christ. 

The end here is the end of all the ages and dispensations of the old creation. It is also the end of the millennium before the new heaven and new earth ([[Revelation-21#v1|Rev. 21:1]]). In all the ages and dispensations God, on one hand, has been dealing with His enemy Satan and all the negative things in the universe; on the other hand, He has been accomplishing all things for the fulfillment of His eternal purpose. The last of the ages and dispensations will be the millennium, the kingdom age, after which all God’s dealings and accomplishments will be fully completed. That completion will be the end, the conclusion, of all God’s work. At this end all the dead unbelievers, the unrighteous, will be resurrected unto judgment for eternal perdition ([[John-05#v29|John 5:29]]; [[Revelation-20#v5|Rev. 20:5]], [[Revelation-20#v11|11-15]]). They will be third in order in the resurrection.

### Footnote 24.2 — when

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v24|1 Corinthians 15:24]]

When Christ annuls the satanic authority, subdues all His enemies ([[1 Corinthians-15#v25|v. 25]]), abolishes death ([[1 Corinthians-15#v26|v. 26]]), and delivers up the kingdom to God the Father, that is, when all the negative things are done away with and the entire purpose of God is fulfilled, the old creation will be concluded.

### Footnote 24.3 — delivers

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v24|1 Corinthians 15:24]]

After accomplishing redemption, Christ went to receive the kingdom from the Father ([[Luke-19#v12|Luke 19:12]], [[Luke-19#v15|15]]). Before the millennium He as the Son of Man will have received the kingdom from God, the Ancient of Days, to rule all the nations for one thousand years ([[Daniel-07#v13|Dan. 7:13-14]]; [[Revelation-20#v4|Rev. 20:4]], [[Revelation-20#v6|6]]). At the end of the millennium, after He has defeated Satan, the devil, and his evil angels (as all rule, authority, and power), and even death and Hades, putting all His enemies under His feet ([[1 Corinthians-15#v25|vv. 25-26]]) and casting all of them, including death and Hades, into the lake of fire ([[Revelation-20#v7|Rev. 20:7-10]], [[Revelation-20#v14|14]]), He will deliver the kingdom back to God the Father.

### Footnote 28.2 — Son

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v28|1 Corinthians 15:28]]

This indicates the Son’s absolute subjection and subordination to the Father, which exalts the Father that God the Father may be all in all.

## Footnotes for verse 29

### Footnote 29.1 — Otherwise

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v29|1 Corinthians 15:29]]

Considering [[1 Corinthians-15#v20|vv. 20-28]] a parenthesis, this verse should be connected with [[1 Corinthians-15#v18|v. 18]], and [[1 Corinthians-15#v30|vv. 30-32]] with [[1 Corinthians-15#v19|v. 19]].

### Footnote 29.2 — baptized

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v29|1 Corinthians 15:29]]

This should not have been an official matter generally practiced by the early churches but a personal activity of some individual believers on behalf of dead persons for whom they were concerned, who might have believed in the Lord but not been baptized before they died. They did this in hope of the dead ones’ being resurrected from the dead at the Lord’s coming back ([[1 Thessalonians-04#v16|1 Thes. 4:16]]), since in baptism resurrection is strongly signified ([[Colossians-02#v12|Col. 2:12]]). The apostle used what they did to confirm the truth of resurrection. This does not mean, however, that he sanctioned some believers’ being baptized for the dead.

## Footnotes for verse 31

### Footnote 31.1 — boasting

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v31|1 Corinthians 15:31]]

The Corinthian believers were the fruit of the apostle’s labor, a labor in which he risked his life. In them the apostle could boast of this. By this boasting he protested that daily he died, that is, daily he risked death, faced death, and died to self ([[2 Corinthians-11#v23|2 Cor. 11:23]]; [[2 Corinthians-04#v11|4:11]]; [[2 Corinthians-01#v8|1:8-9]]; [[Romans-08#v36|Rom. 8:36]]).

### Footnote 31.2 — in

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v31|1 Corinthians 15:31]]

At this point, in his word concerning resurrection the apostle used his experience in the Lord’s work to confirm the matter of resurrection, especially the effect of Christ’s resurrection on him. The apostle’s boasting in the Corinthians, who were the fruit of his risking of his life, was in the resurrected Christ, not in himself, because his risking of death in his labor for them was not by himself but by the resurrected Christ.

## Footnotes for verse 34

### Footnote 34.1 — Awake

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v34|1 Corinthians 15:34]]

Or, Cease, righteously, to be drunken. Righteously means being right with God and with man. To say that there is no resurrection offends God and man. This is to commit sin and thereby be unrighteous. Hence, the apostle advised the misled Corinthians to awake soberly from this sin and restore a right relationship with God and man. They were drunken unrighteously in the stupor of the no-resurrection heresy. They needed to cease being in that stupor.

### Footnote 34.2 — are

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v34|1 Corinthians 15:34]]

To be heretical in saying that there is no resurrection is to be ignorant of God, not knowing God’s power and economy ([[Matthew-22#v29|Matt. 22:29-32]]). This is a shame to the believers.

The reality of resurrection is contained and concealed in nature, especially in the plant life. A seed sown into the earth dies and is made alive. This is resurrection. This answered the foolish Corinthians’ first question, “How are the dead raised?” ([[1 Corinthians-15#v35|v. 35]]).

## Footnotes for verse 38

Not the body sown to die ([[1 Corinthians-15#v37|v. 37]]) but the resurrected body given by God, in a different shape and on a higher level. This answered the Corinthians’ foolish question, “With what kind of body do they come?” ([[1 Corinthians-15#v35|v. 35]]).

## Footnotes for verse 39

### Footnote 39.1 — All

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v39|1 Corinthians 15:39]]

In [[1 Corinthians-15#v39|vv. 39-41]] the apostle proved to the foolish Corinthians that God is able to give a body to all resurrected lives, just as He gave a body to all created things: to men and animals on the earth, to birds in the air, and to fish in the water—the earthly bodies with their different glories; and to the sun, the moon, and the stars—heavenly bodies with heavenly glory in varying degrees.

## Footnotes for verse 44

### Footnote 44.1 — soulish

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v44|1 Corinthians 15:44]]

A soulish body is a natural body animated by the soul, a body in which the soul predominates. A spiritual body is a resurrected body saturated by the spirit, a body in which the spirit predominates. When we die, our natural body, being soulish, will be sown, i.e., buried, in corruption, in dishonor, and in weakness. When it is resurrected, it will become spiritual in incorruption, in glory, and in power ([[1 Corinthians-15#v42|vv. 42-43]]).

## Footnotes for verse 45

### Footnote 45.1 — soul

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v45|1 Corinthians 15:45]]

Through creation Adam became a living soul with a soulish body. Through resurrection Christ became a life-giving Spirit with a spiritual body. Adam as a living soul is natural; Christ as a life-giving Spirit is resurrected. First, in incarnation He became flesh for redemption ([[John-01#v14|John 1:14]], [[John-01#v29|29]]); then, in resurrection He became a life-giving Spirit for the imparting of life ([[John-10#v10|John 10:10b]]). Through incarnation He had a soulish body, as Adam had; through resurrection He has a spiritual body. His soulish body has become a spiritual one through resurrection. Now He is a life-giving Spirit in resurrection, with a spiritual body, ready to be received by His believers. When we believe into Him, He enters our spirit, and we are joined to Him as the life-giving Spirit. Hence, we become one spirit with Him ([[1 Corinthians-06#v17|6:17]]). Our spirit is made alive and is resurrected with Him. Eventually, our present soulish body will become a spiritual body in resurrection, just like His ([[1 Corinthians-15#v52|vv. 52-54]]; [[Philippians-03#v21|Phil. 3:21]]).

## Footnotes for verse 47

### Footnote 47.1 — out

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v47|1 Corinthians 15:47]]

Out of the earth denotes the origin of the first man, Adam, and earthy, his nature.

### Footnote 47.2 — second

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v47|1 Corinthians 15:47]]

Christ is not only the last Adam ([[1 Corinthians-15#v45|v. 45]]) but also the second man. The first Adam ([[1 Corinthians-15#v45|v. 45]]) is the beginning of mankind; the last Adam is the ending. As the first man, Adam is the head of the old creation, representing it in creation. As the second man, Christ is the Head of the new creation, representing it in resurrection. In the entire universe there are only two men: the first man, Adam, including all his descendants, and the second man, Christ, comprising all His believers. We believers were included in the first man by birth and became a part of the second man by regeneration. Our believing has transferred us out of the first man into the second. In regard to our being part of the first man, our origin is the earth and our nature is earthy. In regard to our being part of the second man, our origin is God and our nature is heavenly.

### Footnote 47.3 — out

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v47|1 Corinthians 15:47]]

Out of heaven denotes both the divine origin and the heavenly nature of the second man, Christ.

### Footnote 49.1 — image

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v49|1 Corinthians 15:49]]

As a part of Adam, we have borne the earthy man’s image through birth; as a part of Christ, we will bear the heavenly man’s image in resurrection. This indicates that just as in Adam we have been born as those who are earthy, so in Christ we will be resurrected as those who are heavenly. Such a resurrection is our destiny. It is as sure as our birth and should never be questioned.

## Footnotes for verse 50

### Footnote 50.1 — flesh

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v50|1 Corinthians 15:50]]

Flesh and blood are the components of the soulish body, which is corruptible and is not qualified to inherit the kingdom of God, which is incorruptible. Corruption cannot inherit incorruption. Our corruptible body must be resurrected into an incorruptible one that we may be able to inherit the incorruptible kingdom of God in resurrection.

## Footnotes for verse 51

### Footnote 51.1 — mystery

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v51|1 Corinthians 15:51]]

The transfiguration, including the resurrection, of our corruptible body into an incorruptible one ([[Philippians-03#v21|Phil. 3:21]]). This is mysterious to human understanding.

### Footnote 51.2 — sleep

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v51|1 Corinthians 15:51]]

I.e., die ([[1 Corinthians-11#v30|11:30]]; [[John-11#v11|John 11:11-13]]; [[1 Thessalonians-04#v13|1 Thes. 4:13-16]]).

### Footnote 51.3 — changed

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v51|1 Corinthians 15:51]]

I.e., transfigured from corruption, dishonor, and weakness to incorruption, glory, and power ([[1 Corinthians-15#v42|vv. 42-43]]). This is to have the body of our humiliation conformed to the body of Christ’s glory ([[Philippians-03#v21|Phil. 3:21]]).

## Footnotes for verse 53

### Footnote 53.1 — this

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v53|1 Corinthians 15:53]]

Referring to our corruptible and mortal body, which must put on incorruption and immortality either through our being resurrected from the dead or through our being transfigured while still alive. This is mysterious and incomprehensible to the natural sight.

## Footnotes for verse 54

### Footnote 54.1 — when

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v54|1 Corinthians 15:54]]

I.e., when our corrupted and mortal body is resurrected or transfigured from corruption and death into glory and life. Then death will be swallowed up unto victory. This is the consummation of the resurrection we share in God’s economy through the redemption and salvation in Christ. This resurrection begins with the enlivening of our dead spirit and is completed with the transfiguration of our corruptible body. Between these two ends is the process in which our fallen soul is metabolically transformed by the life-giving Spirit, who is the reality of resurrection ([[2 Corinthians-03#v18|2 Cor. 3:18]]).

### Footnote 54.2 — unto

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v54|1 Corinthians 15:54]]

Death is a defeat to man. Through Christ’s salvation in the resurrection life, death will be swallowed up unto victory to us, the beneficiaries of Christ’s resurrection life.

### Footnote 54.3 — victory

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v54|1 Corinthians 15:54]]

This chapter is on resurrection. Christ’s resurrection was His victory over Satan, God’s enemy, over the world, over sin, and over death. After His triumphal ascension to the height ([[Ephesians-04#v8|Eph. 4:8]]) in His resurrection, God subdued all the enemies for Him ([[1 Corinthians-15#v25|v. 25]]). Then, as One who is in resurrection, He will come to the earth with the kingdom of God ([[Daniel-07#v13|Dan. 7:13-14]]) to exercise God’s power and subdue everything on earth. This will continue for a thousand years ([[Revelation-20#v4|Rev. 20:4]], [[Revelation-20#v6|6]]). The last enemy to be abolished is death. The swallowing up of death will result in the ultimate victory of the Lord’s resurrection, that is, in the ultimate and complete victory that He has accomplished in resurrection for us who believe in Him and participate in His resurrection. This is the ultimate issue of His resurrection for His and God’s eternal kingdom and for the eternal enjoyment of His resurrection life in eternity by those who believe in Him.

## Footnotes for verse 55

### Footnote 55.1 — Where

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v55|1 Corinthians 15:55]]

This is the apostle’s triumphant exclamation concerning the victory of resurrection life over death.

## Footnotes for verse 56

### Footnote 56.1 — sting

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v56|1 Corinthians 15:56]]

Death is of the devil ([[Hebrews-02#v14|Heb. 2:14]]), and through sin it stings us to death ([[Romans-05#v12|Rom. 5:12]]). In God’s redemption Christ was made sin for us ([[2 Corinthians-05#v21|2 Cor. 5:21]]) in order that God might condemn sin through Christ’s death ([[Romans-08#v3|Rom. 8:3]]), thus abolishing the sting of death. Then, through Christ’s resurrection death is swallowed up by the resurrection life.

### Footnote 56.2 — power

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v56|1 Corinthians 15:56]]

Sin brings to us a curse and condemnation, both in our conscience and before God, through the law ([[Romans-04#v15|Rom. 4:15]]; [[Romans-05#v13|5:13]], [[Romans-05#v20|20]]; [[Romans-07#v7|7:7-8]]). Hence, the law becomes the power of sin to kill us ([[Romans-07#v10|Rom. 7:10-11]]). Since Christ’s death has fulfilled the law’s requirements on us ([[1 Peter-03#v18|1 Pet. 3:18]]; [[1 Peter-02#v24|2:24]]), the power of sin has been annulled. Through the death of Christ sin has been condemned and the law has been annulled, and through His resurrection death has been swallowed up. Therefore, we must give thanks to God, who gives us such a victory over sin and death through the death and resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ ([[1 Corinthians-15#v57|v. 57]]).

## Footnotes for verse 57

### Footnote 57.1 — victory

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v57|1 Corinthians 15:57]]

This victory over sin and death through Christ’s death and resurrection should not be merely an accomplished fact for our acceptance; it must become our daily experience in life through the resurrected Christ as the life-giving Spirit ([[1 Corinthians-15#v45|v. 45]]), who is one with our spirit ([[1 Corinthians-06#v17|6:17]]). Hence, we should live by and walk according to this mingled spirit. Thus thanks will be given continuously to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.

## Footnotes for verse 58

### Footnote 58.1 — steadfast

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v58|1 Corinthians 15:58]]

To question the truth of resurrection is to be shaken. To be assured and remain in the reality of resurrection is to be steadfast, immovable.

### Footnote 58.2 — work

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v58|1 Corinthians 15:58]]

Not believing in the truth of resurrection causes us to be disappointed concerning our future, thus making us discouraged in the work of the Lord. Faith gives us a strong aspiration to abound in the work of the Lord with the expectation of pleasing the Lord in resurrection at His coming back.

### Footnote 58.3 — labor

**Verse:** [[1 Corinthians-15#v58|1 Corinthians 15:58]]

Not by our natural life and natural ability but by the Lord’s resurrection life and power. Our labor for the Lord in His resurrection life with His resurrection power will never be in vain, but will result in the fulfilling of God’s eternal purpose through the preaching of Christ to sinners, the ministering of life to the saints, and the building up of the church with the experiences of the processed Triune God as gold, silver, and precious stones ([[1 Corinthians-03#v12|3:12]]). This labor will be rewarded by the returning Lord in the day of the resurrection of the righteous ([[1 Corinthians-03#v14|3:14]]; [[Matthew-25#v21|Matt. 25:21]], [[Matthew-25#v23|23]]; [[Luke-14#v14|Luke 14:14]]).
