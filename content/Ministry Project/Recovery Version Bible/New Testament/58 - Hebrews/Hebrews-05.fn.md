# Hebrews-05 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Hebrews-05|Hebrews 5]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — gifts

**Verse:** [[Hebrews-05#v1|Hebrews 5:1]]

Gifts are for God’s pleasure; sacrifices are for our sins.

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — compassion

**Verse:** [[Hebrews-05#v2|Hebrews 5:2]]

The Greek here implies a feeling toward the ignorant and erring that is neither too severe nor too tolerant. To exercise compassion is to be moderate or tender in judging their situation. The thought in this verse is a continuation of the thought in [[Hebrews-04#v15|4:15]]. Though not encompassed with weakness like the high priest taken from among men, Christ as our High Priest was tempted in all respects like us. Hence, being touched with the feeling of our weaknesses, He is able to exercise compassion toward us, the ignorant and erring ones.

## Footnotes for verse 5

### Footnote 5.1 — glorify

**Verse:** [[Hebrews-05#v5|Hebrews 5:5]]

In this verse the word glorify replaces honor used in the foregoing verse. With the high priest taken from among men, there is only honor, a matter of position. With Christ as the High Priest, there is not only honor but also glory, not only the preciousness of His position but also the splendor of His person. See [note 9²](<Hebrews-02.fn#Footnote 9.2 — glory>) in [[Hebrews-02|Hebrews 2]].

### Footnote 5.2 — begotten

**Verse:** [[Hebrews-05#v5|Hebrews 5:5]]

This refers to Christ’s resurrection ([[Acts-13#v33|Acts 13:33]]), which qualifies Him to be our High Priest. For Christ to be our High Priest, He had to partake of our humanity, as mentioned in [[Hebrews-02#v14|2:14-18]], and enter with this humanity into resurrection. In His humanity He can be touched with the feeling of our weaknesses and be merciful to us ([[Hebrews-04#v15|4:15]]; [[Hebrews-02#v17|2:17]]). In resurrection, in His divinity He can do everything for us and be faithful to us ([[Hebrews-07#v24|7:24-25]]; [[Hebrews-02#v17|2:17]]).

## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — You

**Verse:** [[Hebrews-05#v6|Hebrews 5:6]]

This refers to the ascension and enthronement of Christ ([[Psalm-110#v1|Psa. 110:1-4]]), which are in addition to His resurrection and further qualify Him to be our High Priest ([[Hebrews-07#v26|7:26]]).

### Footnote 6.2 — order

**Verse:** [[Hebrews-05#v6|Hebrews 5:6]]

The order of Melchizedek is higher than the order of Aaron. The order of Aaron was for the priesthood that was only in humanity, whereas the order of Melchizedek is for the priesthood that is in both humanity and divinity. This is fully delineated in [[Hebrews-07|Hebrews 7]].

## Footnotes for verse 7

### Footnote 7.1 — out

**Verse:** [[Hebrews-05#v7|Hebrews 5:7]]

Out of death here does not mean that Christ did not enter into death and suffer death. It means that He resurrected out of death. Before He died, Christ prayed for this, and God answered by raising Him from the dead.

## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — obedience

**Verse:** [[Hebrews-05#v8|Hebrews 5:8]]

God ordained that Christ should die, and Christ obeyed ([[Philippians-02#v8|Phil. 2:8]]). He learned this obedience through the suffering of death.

## Footnotes for verse 12

### Footnote 12.1 — rudiments

**Verse:** [[Hebrews-05#v12|Hebrews 5:12]]

Or, primary elements.

### Footnote 12.2 — oracles

**Verse:** [[Hebrews-05#v12|Hebrews 5:12]]

I.e., divine utterances.

### Footnote 12.3 — milk

**Verse:** [[Hebrews-05#v12|Hebrews 5:12]]

Though this verse mentions teachers and teach, it likens the word of God to milk and solid food, which are for nourishment. This corresponds with the Lord’s word in [[Matthew-04#v4|Matt. 4:4]] and the prophet’s word in [[Jeremiah-15#v16|Jer. 15:16]]. Paul and Peter held this same concept concerning the divine word ([[1 Corinthians-03#v2|1 Cor. 3:2]]; [[1 Peter-02#v2|1 Pet. 2:2]]).

## Footnotes for verse 13

### Footnote 13.1 — milk

**Verse:** [[Hebrews-05#v13|Hebrews 5:13]]

Milk here refers to the rudiments of the beginning of the oracles of God mentioned in [[Hebrews-05#v12|v. 12]], whereas the word of righteousness refers to solid food. The good word mentioned in [[Hebrews-06#v5|6:5]] is the word of the beginning of Christ ([[Hebrews-06#v1|6:1]]). But the word of righteousness is deeper than the rudiments of the beginning of the oracles of God because it embodies the deeper thought of God’s justice and righteousness in His dispensational and governmental dealings with His people. This word is more difficult to discriminate than the word of grace ([[Acts-14#v3|Acts 14:3]]; [[Acts-20#v32|20:32]]) and the word of life ([[Philippians-02#v16|Phil. 2:16]]).

## Footnotes for verse 14

### Footnote 14.1 — faculties

**Verse:** [[Hebrews-05#v14|Hebrews 5:14]]

Or, senses; implying powers of perception that depend not only on our mental capability but also on our spiritual apprehension.

### Footnote 14.2 — good

**Verse:** [[Hebrews-05#v14|Hebrews 5:14]]

Good and evil here refers to what is superior in contrast to what is inferior, e.g., the superiority of Christ in contrast to the inferiority of the angels, Moses, and Aaron, or the superiority of the new covenant in contrast to the inferiority of the old covenant. According to the context of this verse, the discriminating mentioned here is similar to discriminating between different foods and has nothing to do with the moral nature of things.
