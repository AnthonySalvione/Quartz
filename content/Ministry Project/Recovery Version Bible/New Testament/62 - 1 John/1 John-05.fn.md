# 1 John-05 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[1 John-05|1 John 5]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — believes

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v1|1 John 5:1]]

The Gnostics and the Cerinthians did not believe that Jesus and the Christ were identical (see notes [22¹](<1 John-02.fn#Footnote 22.1 — denies>) in [[1 John-02|1 John 2]] and [3¹](<1 John-04.fn#Footnote 3.1 — spirit>) in [[1 John-04|1 John 4]]). Hence, they were not the children of God, were not begotten of God. But whoever believes that the man Jesus is the Christ, God incarnate ([[John-01#v1|John 1:1]], [[John-01#v14|14]]; [[John-20#v31|20:31]]), has been begotten of God and has become a child of God ([[John-01#v12|John 1:12-13]]). Such a one loves God the Father, who has begotten him, and also loves the brother, who has been begotten of the same Father. This explains, confirms, and strengthens the word in the preceding verses ([[1 John-04#v20|4:20-21]]).

## Footnotes for verse 4

### Footnote 4.1 — everything

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v4|1 John 5:4]]

Referring to every person who has been begotten of God. Yet such an expression should refer especially to that part, i.e., the spirit of the regenerated person, that has been regenerated with the divine life ([[John-03#v6|John 3:6]]). The regenerated spirit of the regenerated believer does not practice sin ([[1 John-03#v9|3:9]]) and overcomes the world. The believer’s divine birth with the divine life is the basic factor for such victorious living.

### Footnote 4.2 — begotten

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v4|1 John 5:4]]

Both in his Gospel and in this Epistle John stressed the divine birth ([[John-01#v13|John 1:13]]; [[John-03#v3|3:3]], [[John-03#v5|5]]; [[1 John-02#v29|1 John 2:29]] and [note 7](<1 John-02.fn#Footnote 29.7 — begotten>); [[1 John-03#v9|3:9]]; [[1 John-04#v7|4:7]]; [[1 John-05#v1|5:1]], [[1 John-05#v4|4]], [[1 John-05#v18|18]]), through which the divine life is imparted into the believers in Christ ([[John-03#v15|John 3:15-16]], [[John-03#v36|36]]; [[1 John-05#v11|1 John 5:11-12]]). This divine birth, which brings in the divine life, is the basic factor of all the mysteries concerning the divine life, such as the fellowship of the divine life ([[1 John-01#v3|1:3-7]]), the anointing of the Divine Trinity ([[1 John-02#v20|2:20-27]]), the abiding in the Lord ([[1 John-02#v28|2:28]]—[[1 John-03#v24|3:24]]), and the divine living that practices the divine truth ([[1 John-01#v6|1:6]]), the divine will ([[1 John-02#v17|2:17]]), the divine righteousness ([[1 John-02#v29|2:29]]; [[1 John-03#v7|3:7]]), and the divine love ([[1 John-03#v11|3:11]], [[1 John-03#v22|22-23]]; [[1 John-05#v1|5:1-3]]) to express the divine Person ([[1 John-04#v12|4:12]]). The divine birth with the divine life is also the basic factor in this section, [[1 John-05#v4|vv. 4-21]]. It assures the God-begotten believers, giving them confidence in the ability and virtue of the divine life.

### Footnote 4.3 — overcomes

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v4|1 John 5:4]]

Since regenerated believers have the capability of the divine life to overcome the world, the powerful satanic world system, the commandments of God are not heavy or burdensome to them ([[1 John-05#v3|v. 3]]).

### Footnote 4.4 — world

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v4|1 John 5:4]]

See [note 15²](<1 John-02.fn#Footnote 15.2 — world>) in [[1 John-02|1 John 2]].

### Footnote 4.5 — faith

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v4|1 John 5:4]]

The faith that brings us into the organic union with the Triune God and that believes that Jesus is the Son of God ([[1 John-05#v5|v. 5]]) that we may be begotten of God and have His divine life, by which we are enabled to overcome the Satan-organized-and-usurped world.

## Footnotes for verse 5

### Footnote 5.1 — believes

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v5|1 John 5:5]]

Such a believer is one who has been begotten of God and has received the divine life ([[John-01#v12|John 1:12-13]]; [[John-03#v16|3:16]]). The divine life empowers him to overcome the evil world energized by Satan. The Gnostics and the Cerinthians, who were not believers of this kind, remained pitiful victims of the evil satanic system.

## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — He

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v6|1 John 5:6]]

He, Jesus Christ, came as the Son of God that we might be born of God and have the divine life ([[John-10#v10|John 10:10]]; [[John-20#v31|20:31]]). It is in His Son that God gives us eternal life ([[1 John-05#v11|vv. 11-13]]). Jesus, the man of Nazareth, was attested to be the Son of God by the water He went through in His baptism ([[Matthew-03#v16|Matt. 3:16-17]]; [[John-01#v31|John 1:31]]), by the blood He shed on the cross ([[John-19#v31|John 19:31-35]]; [[Matthew-27#v50|Matt. 27:50-54]]), and also by the Spirit He gave not by measure ([[John-01#v32|John 1:32-34]]; [[John-03#v34|3:34]]). By these three God has testified that Jesus is His Son given to us ([[1 John-05#v7|vv. 7-10]]), that in Him we may receive His eternal life by believing into His name ([[1 John-05#v11|vv. 11-13]]; [[John-03#v16|John 3:16]], [[John-03#v36|36]]; [[John-20#v31|20:31]]). The water of baptism terminates people of the old creation by burying them; the blood shed on the cross redeems those whom God has chosen from among the old creation; and the Spirit, who is the truth, the reality in life ([[Romans-08#v2|Rom. 8:2]]), germinates those whom God has redeemed out of the old creation, by regenerating them with the divine life. Thus they are born of God and become His children ([[John-03#v5|John 3:5]], [[John-03#v15|15]]; [[John-01#v12|1:12-13]]) and live a life that practices the truth ([[1 John-01#v6|1:6]]), the will of God ([[1 John-02#v17|2:17]]), the righteousness of God ([[1 John-02#v29|2:29]]), and the love of God ([[1 John-03#v10|3:10-11]]) for His expression.

## Footnotes for verse 16

### Footnote 16.1 — unto

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v16|1 John 5:16]]

Lit., toward.

### Footnote 16.2 — ask

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v16|1 John 5:16]]

This must be a prayer made while we are abiding in fellowship with God.

### Footnote 16.3 — will

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v16|1 John 5:16]]

The subject is still he, the subject of the first predicate shall ask, indicating that the asker will give life to the one for whom he is asking. This does not mean that the asker has life of himself and can give life by himself to others. It means that such an asker, who is abiding in the Lord, who is one with the Lord, and who is asking in one spirit with the Lord ([[1 Corinthians-06#v17|1 Cor. 6:17]]), becomes the means by which God’s life-giving Spirit can give life to the ones for whom he is asking. This is a matter of life-imparting in the fellowship of the divine life. To be one who can give life to others, we must abide in the divine life and walk, live, and have our being in the divine life. In [[James-05#v14|James 5:14-16]] the prayer is for healing; here the prayer is for life-imparting.

### Footnote 16.4 — life

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v16|1 John 5:16]]

Undoubtedly, this refers to spiritual life imparted, through the prayer of the asker, into the one for whom he is asking. However, according to the context, this spiritual life will also rescue the physical body of the one for whom the asker is asking, from the danger of suffering death because of his sinning (cf. [[James-05#v15|James 5:15]]).

### Footnote 16.5 — sin

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v16|1 John 5:16]]

Concerning sin unto death, Bible teachers have different interpretations. Some say that it refers to the sin of the antichrists in denying that Jesus is the Christ ([[1 John-02#v22|2:22]]), which keeps them in death forever. But according to the context of this verse, sin unto death is related to a sinning brother, not to an antichrist or any other unbeliever. Since this section, [[1 John-05#v14|vv. 14-17]], is related to prayer in the fellowship of eternal life (covered in [[1 John-01#v3|1:3]]—[[1 John-02#v11|2:11]]), whatever it deals with must be related to the matter of the fellowship of the divine life. In the fellowship of the divine life there is the governmental dealing of God according to the spiritual condition of each of His children. In God’s governmental dealing, some of His children may be destined to physical death in this age because of a certain sin, and others may be destined to physical death because of other sins. The situation is like that of Ananias and his wife, Sapphira, who were dealt with by physical death because they lied to the Holy Spirit ([[Acts-05#v1|Acts 5:1-11]]), and like that of the Corinthian believers, who were dealt with by the same judgment because they did not discern the Body ([[1 Corinthians-11#v29|1 Cor. 11:29-30]]). This was typified by God’s dealing with the children of Israel in the wilderness ([[1 Corinthians-10#v5|1 Cor. 10:5-11]]). All the Israelites except Caleb and Joshua were judged by God with physical death because of certain sins. God’s governmental dealing is severe. Miriam, Aaron, and even Moses were not spared from this kind of dealing, which came because of certain of their failures ([[Numbers-12#v1|Num. 12:1-15]]; [[Numbers-20#v1|20:1]], [[Numbers-20#v12|12]], [[Numbers-20#v22|22-29]]; [[Deuteronomy-01#v37|Deut. 1:37]]; [[Deuteronomy-03#v26|3:26-27]]; [[Deuteronomy-32#v48|32:48-52]]). The punishment of God’s governmental dealing with His children is not at all related to eternal perdition; it is a dispensational dealing according to the divine government, a dealing that is related to our fellowship with God and with one another. Whether or not a sin is unto death depends on God’s judgment according to the sinning believer’s position and condition in the house of God. In any case, for the children of God to sin is a serious matter. It may be judged by God with physical death in this age! The apostle did not say that we should make request concerning a sin that is unto death.

The basic fact of our divine birth prevents us, the regenerated ones, from practicing sin ([[1 John-03#v9|3:9]] and [note 2](<1 John-03.fn#Footnote 9.2 — not>)), that is, from living in sin ([[Romans-06#v2|Rom. 6:2]]). A regenerated believer (especially his regenerated spirit, which is born of the Spirit of God—[[John-03#v6|John 3:6]]) keeps himself from living in sin, and the evil one does not touch him (especially his regenerated spirit). His divine birth with the divine life in his spirit is the basic factor of such a safeguard (see [note 4¹](<1 John-05.fn#Footnote 4.1 — everything>)).

### Footnote 18.5 — touch

## Footnotes for verse 19

### Footnote 19.1 — of

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v19|1 John 5:19]]

Lit., out of. Since we have been begotten of God, we are out of Him and thus possess His life and partake of His nature. By this we are separated unto God from the satanic world, which lies in the evil one.

### Footnote 19.2 — whole

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v19|1 John 5:19]]

“The whole world” comprises the satanic world system ([[1 John-02#v15|2:15]] and [note 2](<1 John-02.fn#Footnote 15.2 — world>)) and the people of the world, the fallen human race.

### Footnote 19.3 — lies

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v19|1 John 5:19]]

I.e., remains passively in the sphere of the evil one’s influence, under the evil one’s usurpation and manipulation. While the believers are living and moving actively by the life of God, the whole world (and especially the people of the world) is lying passively under the usurping and manipulating hand of Satan, the evil one.

### Footnote 19.4 — evil

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v19|1 John 5:19]]

The Greek word does not refer to an essentially worthless and wicked character, nor does it indicate worthlessness and corruption, degeneracy from original virtue. It refers to one who is pernicious, harmfully evil, one who affects others, influencing them to be evil and vicious. Such an evil one is Satan, the devil, in whom the whole world lies.

### Footnote 21.3 — idols

**Verse:** [[1 John-05#v21|1 John 5:21]]

Referring to the heretical substitutes for the true God that were brought in by the Gnostics and the Cerinthians, as revealed in this Epistle and in John’s Gospel and as referred to in the preceding verse. The idols here also refer to anything that replaces the real God. As genuine children of the genuine God, we should be on the alert to guard ourselves from these heretical substitutes and from all vain replacements for our genuine and real God, with whom we are organically one and who is eternal life to us. This is the aged apostle’s word of warning to all his little children as a conclusion to his Epistle. 

The center of this Epistle’s revelation is the divine fellowship of the divine life, the fellowship between the children of God and their Father God, who is not only the source of the divine life but is also light and love as the source of the enjoyment of the divine life ([[1 John-01#v1|1:1-7]]; [[1 John-04#v8|4:8]], [[1 John-04#v16|16]]). To enjoy the divine life we need to abide in its fellowship according to the divine anointing ([[1 John-02#v12|2:12-28]]; [[1 John-03#v24|3:24]]), based on the divine birth with the divine seed for the development of the divine birth ([[1 John-02#v29|2:29]]—[[1 John-03#v10|3:10]]). This divine birth was carried out by three means: the terminating water, the redeeming blood, and the germinating Spirit ([[1 John-05#v1|vv. 1-13]]). By these we have been born of God to be His children, possessing His divine life and partaking of His divine nature ([[1 John-02#v29|2:29]]—[[1 John-03#v1|3:1]]). He is now indwelling us through His Spirit ([[1 John-03#v24|3:24]]; [[1 John-04#v4|4:4]], [[1 John-04#v13|13]]) to be our life and life supply that we may grow with His divine element unto His likeness at His manifestation ([[1 John-03#v1|3:1-2]]). To abide in the divine fellowship of the divine life, that is, to abide in the Lord ([[1 John-02#v6|2:6]]; [[1 John-03#v6|3:6]]), is to enjoy all His divine riches. By such abiding we walk in the divine light ([[1 John-01#v5|1:5-7]]) and practice the truth, righteousness, love, the will of God, and the commandments of God ([[1 John-01#v6|1:6]]; [[1 John-02#v29|2:29]], [[1 John-02#v5|5]]; [[1 John-03#v10|3:10-11]]; [[1 John-02#v17|2:17]]; [[1 John-05#v2|5:2]]) by the divine life received through the divine birth ([[1 John-02#v29|2:29]]; [[1 John-04#v7|4:7]]). To preserve ourselves in this abiding in the divine fellowship, three main negative things need to be dealt with. The first is sin, which is lawlessness and unrighteousness ([[1 John-01#v7|1:7]]—[[1 John-02#v6|2:6]]; [[1 John-03#v4|3:4-10]]; [[1 John-05#v16|5:16-18]]); the second is the world, which is composed of the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the vainglory of this life ([[1 John-02#v15|2:15-17]]; [[1 John-04#v3|4:3-5]]; [[1 John-05#v4|5:4-5]], [[1 John-05#v19|19]]); and the last is idols, which are the heretical substitutes for the genuine God and the vain replacements for the real God ([[1 John-05#v21|v. 21]]). These three categories of exceedingly evil things are weapons used by the evil one, the devil, to frustrate, harm, and, if possible, even annihilate our abiding in the divine fellowship. The safeguard against his evildoings is our divine birth with the divine life ([[1 John-05#v18|v. 18]]); and, based on the fact that through His death on the cross the Son of God destroyed the works of the devil ([[1 John-03#v8|3:8]]), we overcome him by the word of God that abides in us ([[1 John-02#v14|2:14]]). By virtue of our divine birth we also overcome his evil world by our faith in the Son of God ([[1 John-05#v4|vv. 4-5]]). Moreover, our divine birth with the divine seed that was sown into our inner being enables us not to live habitually in sin ([[1 John-03#v9|3:9]]; [[1 John-05#v18|5:18]]), because Christ has taken away sins through His death in the flesh ([[1 John-03#v5|3:5]]). In case we sin occasionally, we have our Advocate as our propitiatory sacrifice to take care of our case before our Father God ([[1 John-02#v1|2:1-2]]), and His everlasting efficacious blood cleanses us ([[1 John-01#v7|1:7]]). Such a revelation is the basic and substantial element of the apostle’s mending ministry.
