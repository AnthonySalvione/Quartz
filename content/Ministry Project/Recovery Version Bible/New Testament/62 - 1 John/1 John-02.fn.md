# 1 John-02 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[1 John-02|1 John 2]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — little

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v1|1 John 2:1]]

The Greek word for little children was often used by older persons in addressing younger ones. “It is a term of parental affection. It applies to Christians irrespective of growth. Used in [[1 John-02#v12|vv. 12]], [[1 John-02#v28|28]]; [[1 John-03#v7|3:7]], [[1 John-03#v18|18]]; [[1 John-04#v4|4:4]]; [[1 John-05#v21|5:21]]; [[John-13#v33|John 13:33]]; [[Galatians-04#v19|Gal. 4:19]]” (Darby). The aged apostle considered all the recipients of his Epistle his dear little children in the Lord. In [[1 John-02#v13|vv. 13-27]] he classified them into three groups: young children, young men, and fathers. [[1 John-02#v1|Verses 1-12]] and [[1 John-02#v28|28-29]] are addressed to all the recipients in general, and [[1 John-02#v13|vv. 13-27]] to the three groups respectively, according to their growth in the divine life.

### Footnote 1.2 — these

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v1|1 John 2:1]]

The things mentioned in [[1 John-01#v5|1:5-10]] regarding the committing of sin by the children of God, the regenerated believers, who have the divine life and participate in its fellowship ([[1 John-01#v1|1:1-4]]).

### Footnote 1.3 — may

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v1|1 John 2:1]]

This word and if anyone sins in the succeeding sentence indicate that the regenerated believers can still sin. Though they possess the divine life, it is still possible for them to sin if they do not live by the divine life and abide in its fellowship.

### Footnote 1.4 — sins

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v1|1 John 2:1]]

Aorist subjunctive, denoting a single act, not habitual action.

### Footnote 1.5 — Advocate

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v1|1 John 2:1]]

The Greek word refers to one who is called to another’s side to aid him; hence, a helper. It refers also to one who offers legal aid or one who intercedes on behalf of someone else; hence, an advocate, counsel, or intercessor. The word carries the sense of consoling and consolation; hence, a consoler, a comforter. It is used in the Gospel of John (14:16, 26; 15:26; 16:7) for the Spirit of reality as our Comforter within us, the One who takes care of our case or our affairs (see [note 16¹](<John-14.fn#Footnote 16.1 — Comforter>) in [[John-14|John 14]]). It is used here in reference to the Lord Jesus as our Advocate with the Father. When we sin, He, based on the propitiation that He accomplished, takes care of our case by interceding ([[Romans-08#v34|Rom. 8:34]]) and pleading for us.

### Footnote 1.6 — with

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v1|1 John 2:1]]

See [note 2⁴](<1 John-01.fn#Footnote 2.4 — with>) in [[1 John-01|1 John 1]]. The Lord Jesus as our Advocate is living in communion with the Father.

### Footnote 1.7 — Father

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v1|1 John 2:1]]

Here this divine title indicates that our case, which the Lord Jesus as our Advocate undertakes for us, is a family affair, a case between children and the Father. Through regeneration we have been born children of God. After regeneration, if we sin, it is a matter of children sinning against their Father. Our Advocate, who is the sacrifice for our propitiation, undertakes for us to restore our interrupted fellowship with the Father that we may abide in the enjoyment of the divine fellowship.

### Footnote 1.8 — Righteous

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v1|1 John 2:1]]

Our Lord Jesus is the only righteous man among all men. His righteous act ([[Romans-05#v18|Rom. 5:18]]) on the cross fulfilled the righteous requirement of the righteous God for us and all sinners. Only He is qualified to be our Advocate, to care for us in our sinning condition and restore us to a righteous condition that the relationship between us and our Father, who is righteous, may be appeased.

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — propitiation

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v2|1 John 2:2]]

I.e., the sacrifice for propitiation. See [note 25²](<Romans-03.fn#Footnote 25.2 — propitiation>) in [[Romans-03|Rom. 3]]. The Lord Jesus Christ offered Himself to God as a sacrifice for our sins ([[Hebrews-09#v28|Heb. 9:28]]), not only for our redemption but also for the satisfying of God’s demand, thus appeasing the relationship between us and God. Hence, He is the sacrifice for our propitiation before God.

### Footnote 2.2 — whole

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v2|1 John 2:2]]

The Lord Jesus is a sacrifice for propitiation, not only for our sins but also for the whole world. However, this propitiation is conditioned on man’s receiving the Lord by believing in Him. The unbelievers do not experience its efficacy, not because it has any fault but because they do not believe.

## Footnotes for verse 3

### Footnote 3.1 — And

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v3|1 John 2:3]]

[[1 John-02#v1|Verses 1-2]] are a conclusion to the word in [[1 John-01#v5|1:5-10]] regarding our confessing and God’s forgiving of our sins, which interrupt our fellowship with Him. That is the first condition of—the first requirement for—our enjoyment of the fellowship of the divine life. [[1 John-02#v3|Verses 3-11]] deal with the second condition of—the second requirement for—our fellowship with God: that we keep the Lord’s word and love the brothers.

### Footnote 3.2 — know

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v3|1 John 2:3]]

Or, perceive; not doctrinally but experientially by keeping His commandments.

### Footnote 3.3 — know

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v3|1 John 2:3]]

Lit., have come to know Him; denoting that we have begun to know Him and that we continue to know Him until the present time. This refers to our experiential knowledge of God in our daily walk and is related to our intimate fellowship with Him.

### Footnote 3.4 — commandments

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v3|1 John 2:3]]

See [note 34¹](<John-13.fn#Footnote 34.1 — commandment>) in [[John-13|John 13]]. So throughout the book.

## Footnotes for verse 4

### Footnote 4.1 — liar

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v4|1 John 2:4]]

See [note 6⁴](<1 John-01.fn#Footnote 6.4 — lie>) in [[1 John-01|1 John 1]].

### Footnote 4.2 — truth

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v4|1 John 2:4]]

Denoting the revealed reality of God as conveyed in the divine word (see [note 6⁶](<1 John-01.fn#Footnote 6.6 — truth>) in [[1 John-01|1 John 1]]), which reveals that our keeping the Lord’s commandments should follow our knowing Him. If we say that we have known the Lord, yet we are not keeping His commandments, the truth (reality) is not in us, and we become liars.

## Footnotes for verse 5

### Footnote 5.1 — word

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v5|1 John 2:5]]

Word is synonymous with commandments in [[1 John-02#v3|vv. 3-4]]. The word here comprises all the commandments. Commandments emphasizes injunction; word implies spirit and life as a supply to us ([[John-06#v63|John 6:63]]).

### Footnote 5.2 — love

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v5|1 John 2:5]]

The Greek word denotes the love that is higher and nobler than human affection (see notes [7¹](<2 Peter-01.fn#Footnote 7.1 — brotherly>) and [7²](<2 Peter-01.fn#Footnote 7.2 — love>) in [[2 Peter-01|2 Pet. 1]]). Only this word with its verb forms is used in this Epistle for love. Here the love of God denotes our love toward God, which is generated by His love within us. The love of God, the word of the Lord, and God Himself are all related to one another. If we keep the Lord’s word, God’s love has been perfected in us. It is altogether a matter of the divine life, which is God Himself. God’s love is His inward essence, and the Lord’s word supplies us with this divine essence, with which we love the brothers. Hence, when we keep the divine word, the divine love is perfected through the divine life, by which we live.

### Footnote 5.3 — perfected

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v5|1 John 2:5]]

See [note 12⁴](<1 John-04.fn#Footnote 12.4 — perfected>) in [[1 John-04|1 John 4]].

### Footnote 5.4 — in

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v5|1 John 2:5]]

I.e., in the Lord Jesus Christ ([[1 John-02#v1|v. 1]]). This is a strong expression, stressing that we are one with the Lord. Since we are one with the Lord, who is God, the loving essence of God becomes ours. It is supplied to us by the Lord’s word of life for our walk of love that we may enjoy the fellowship of the divine life and abide in the divine light ([[1 John-02#v10|v. 10]]).

To abide in Christ is the continuation of the Christian life. This is our responsibility in our daily walk, a walk that is a copy of Christ’s walk on earth. See [note 27⁸](<1 John-02.fn#Footnote 27.8 — abide>).

### Footnote 6.2 — He

## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — new

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v8|1 John 2:8]]

The commandment regarding brotherly love is both old and new: old, because the believers have had it from the beginning of their Christian life; new, because in their Christian walk it dawns with new light and shines with new enlightenment and fresh power again and again.

### Footnote 8.2 — which

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v8|1 John 2:8]]

This relative pronoun, in the neuter gender, does not refer to commandment, which is in the feminine gender. It should refer to the fact that the old commandment regarding brotherly love is new in the believers’ Christian walk. That the old commandment is new is true in the Lord, since He not only gave it to His believers but also renews it in their daily walk continually. It is true also in the believers, since they not only have received it once for all but also are enlightened and refreshed by it repeatedly.

### Footnote 8.3 — darkness

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v8|1 John 2:8]]

The passing away of the darkness is its vanishing in the shining of the true light. The true light is the light of the Lord’s commandment. Because this light shines, the commandment regarding brotherly love dawns in the darkness, making the old commandment always new and fresh in our entire Christian living.

## Footnotes for verse 9

## Footnotes for verse 13

### Footnote 13.1 — fathers

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v13|1 John 2:13]]

The believers who are mature in the divine life. They are classified by the apostle as the first group among his recipients.

### Footnote 13.2 — know

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v13|1 John 2:13]]

The Greek verb is in the perfect tense, denoting that the state produced continues: you have known; therefore, you know all the time. Such living knowledge is the fruit of the experience of life.

### Footnote 13.3 — Him

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v13|1 John 2:13]]

The eternal, preexisting Christ, who is the Word of life from the beginning ([[1 John-01#v1|1:1]]; [[John-01#v1|John 1:1]]). Knowing in the way of life such an eternal Christ is the characteristic of the mature and experienced fathers, who were not and could not be deceived by the heresies that said that Christ was not eternal as the Father is. Knowing Christ as the eternal One who is from the beginning will cause us to mature and not be deceived by the heresies of modernism.

### Footnote 13.4 — from

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v13|1 John 2:13]]

In the absolute sense. See [note 1²](<1 John-01.fn#Footnote 1.2 — from>), par. 2, in [[1 John-01|1 John 1]].

### Footnote 13.5 — young

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v13|1 John 2:13]]

The believers who are grown up in the divine life. They are classified by the apostle as the second group of his recipients.

### Footnote 13.6 — overcome

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v13|1 John 2:13]]

Overcoming the evil one is the characteristic of the grown-up and strong young believers, who are nourished, strengthened, and sustained by the word of God, which is abiding and operating in them against the devil, the world, and the lust of the world ([[1 John-02#v14|vv. 14b-17]]).

### Footnote 13.7 — evil

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v13|1 John 2:13]]

Satan, the devil. See [note 19⁴](<1 John-05.fn#Footnote 19.4 — evil>) in [[1 John-05|1 John 5]].

### Footnote 13.8 — write

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v13|1 John 2:13]]

The Greek word denotes have written; in other MSS, write. Although have written is more authentic according to a more recent discovery of MSS, write, which is used by the KJV and J. N. Darby’s New Translation, is more logical according to the context. The apostle in this verse addressed his writing to each of the three classes of his recipients, always in the present tense. In the succeeding verses, 14-27, he addressed each of the three classes again, but always in the aorist tense ([[1 John-02#v14|v. 14]], to the fathers and young men; [[1 John-02#v26|v. 26]], cf. [[1 John-02#v18|v. 18]], to the young children).

### Footnote 13.9 — young

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v13|1 John 2:13]]

The believers who have just received the divine life. They are classified by the apostle as the third group of recipients.

### Footnote 13.10 — Father

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v13|1 John 2:13]]

The Father is the source of the divine life; of Him the believers have been reborn ([[John-01#v12|John 1:12-13]]). To know the Father is the initial issue of being regenerated ([[John-17#v3|John 17:3]], [[John-17#v6|6]]). Hence, such an experiential knowledge in the youth of the divine life is the basic qualification of the young children, who are the youngest in John’s classification.

## Footnotes for verse 15

### Footnote 15.1 — Do

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v15|1 John 2:15]]

[[1 John-02#v15|Verses 15-17]] are the development of the word written in [[1 John-02#v13|v. 13]] to the young men.

### Footnote 15.2 — world

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v15|1 John 2:15]]

The Greek word is used for different things, as follows: In [[Matthew-25#v34|Matt. 25:34]]; [[John-17#v5|John 17:5]]; [[Acts-17#v24|Acts 17:24]]; [[Ephesians-01#v4|Eph. 1:4]]; and [[Revelation-13#v8|Rev. 13:8]], it denotes the material universe as a system created by God. In [[John-01#v29|John 1:29]]; [[John-03#v16|3:16]]; and [[Romans-05#v12|Rom. 5:12]], it denotes the fallen human race, corrupted and usurped by Satan to be the components of his evil world system. In [[1 Peter-03#v3|1 Pet. 3:3]] it denotes adorning, ornaments. Here, as in [[John-15#v19|John 15:19]]; [[John-17#v14|17:14]]; and [[James-04#v4|James 4:4]], it denotes an order, a set form, an orderly arrangement, hence, an ordered system (set up by Satan, the adversary of God), not the earth. God created man to live on the earth for the fulfillment of His purpose. But His enemy, Satan, in order to usurp the God-created man, formed an anti-God world system on this earth by systematizing men with religion, culture, education, industry, commerce, entertainment, etc., through men’s fallen nature, in their lusts, pleasures, and pursuits, and even in their indulgence in necessities for their living, such as food, clothing, housing, and transportation (see [note 31²](<John-12.fn#Footnote 31.2 — world>) in [[John-12|John 12]]). The whole of such a satanic system lies in the evil one ([[1 John-05#v19|5:19]]). Not loving such a world is the ground for overcoming the evil one. Loving it just a little gives the evil one the ground to defeat and occupy us.

## Footnotes for verse 22

### Footnote 22.1 — denies

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v22|1 John 2:22]]

This is the heresy of Cerinthus, a first century Syrian heresiarch of Jewish descent educated at Alexandria. His heresy was a mixture of Judaism, Gnosticism, and Christianity. He separated the maker (creator) of the world from God and represented that maker as a subordinate power. He taught adoptionism, saying that Jesus was merely God’s adopted Son and had become the Son of God by being exalted to a status that was not His by birth; thus he denied that Jesus had been conceived by the Holy Spirit. In his heresy he separated the earthly man Jesus, regarded as the son of Joseph and Mary, from the heavenly Christ and taught that after Jesus was baptized, Christ as a dove descended upon Him, and then He announced the unknown Father and did miracles. Further, he taught that at the end of His ministry Christ departed from Jesus, and Jesus suffered death on the cross and rose from the dead, while Christ remained separate as a spiritual being, and, finally, that Christ will rejoin the man Jesus at the coming of the Messianic kingdom of glory.

### Footnote 22.2 — Jesus

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v22|1 John 2:22]]

To confess that Jesus is the Christ is to confess that He is the Son of God ([[Matthew-16#v16|Matt. 16:16]]; [[John-20#v31|John 20:31]]). Hence, to deny that Jesus is the Christ is to deny the Father and the Son. Whoever so denies the divine person of Christ is the antichrist. 

The fact that denying that Jesus is the Christ equals denying the Father and the Son implies the thought that Jesus, Christ, the Father, and the Son are all one. They all are the elements, the ingredients, of the all-inclusive compound indwelling Spirit, who is now anointing the believers all the time ([[1 John-02#v20|vv. 20]], [[1 John-02#v27|27]]). In this anointing, Jesus, Christ, the Father, and the Son are all anointed into our inner being.

## Footnotes for verse 23

### Footnote 23.1 — denies

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v23|1 John 2:23]]

Since the Son and the Father are one ([[John-10#v30|John 10:30]]; [[Isaiah-09#v6|Isa. 9:6]]), to deny the Son is to be without the Father, and to confess the Son is to have the Father. Here to deny the Son is to deny the deity of Christ, to deny that the man Jesus is God. This is a great heresy.

## Footnotes for verse 24

### Footnote 24.1 — that

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v24|1 John 2:24]]

The Word of life, i.e., the Word of the eternal life that the believers heard from the beginning ([[1 John-01#v1|1:1-2]]). Not to deny but to confess that the man Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God ([[1 John-02#v22|v. 22]]), is to let the Word of the eternal life abide in us. In so doing we abide in the Son and in the Father and are not led astray by the heretical teachings concerning Christ’s person ([[1 John-02#v26|v. 26]]). This indicates that the Son and the Father are the eternal life for our regeneration and enjoyment. In this eternal life we have fellowship with God and with one another ([[1 John-01#v2|1:2-3]], [[1 John-01#v6|6-7]]) and have our being in our daily walk ([[1 John-02#v6|v. 6]]; [[1 John-01#v7|1:7]]).

### Footnote 24.2 — from

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v24|1 John 2:24]]

In the relative sense (see [note 1²](<1 John-01.fn#Footnote 1.2 — from>), par. 2, in [[1 John-01|1 John 1]]).

### Footnote 24.3 — abide

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v24|1 John 2:24]]

See [note 27⁸](<1 John-02.fn#Footnote 27.8 — abide>).

## Footnotes for verse 25

### Footnote 25.1 — He

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v25|1 John 2:25]]

The singular pronoun He, referring to both the Son and the Father in the preceding verse, indicates that the Son and the Father are one. As far as our experience of the divine life is concerned, the Son, the Father, Jesus, and Christ are all one. It is not that only the Son, and not the Father, is the eternal life to us. It is that Jesus, being the Christ as the Son and the Father, is the eternal, divine life to us for our portion.

### Footnote 25.2 — promised

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v25|1 John 2:25]]

In the Gospel of John, such as in [[1 John-03#v15|3:15]]; [[1 John-04#v14|4:14]]; 6:40, 47; 10:10; 11:25; and 17:2-3.

### Footnote 25.3 — eternal

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v25|1 John 2:25]]

According to the context of [[1 John-02#v22|vv. 22-25]], the eternal life is just Jesus, Christ, the Son, and the Father; all these are a composition of the eternal life. Hence, the eternal life also is an element of the all-inclusive, compound indwelling Spirit, who moves within us.

## Footnotes for verse 26

### Footnote 26.1 — concerning

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v26|1 John 2:26]]

This indicates that this section of the Word was written to inoculate the believers with the truth of the Divine Trinity against the heresies concerning Christ’s person.

### Footnote 26.2 — lead

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v26|1 John 2:26]]

Or, deceive you. To lead the believers astray is to distract them from the truth concerning Christ’s deity and humanity by means of heretical teachings concerning the mysteries of what Christ is.

## Footnotes for verse 27

### Footnote 27.1 — anointing

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v27|1 John 2:27]]

See [note 20¹](<1 John-02.fn#Footnote 20.1 — anointing>).

### Footnote 27.2 — Him

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v27|1 John 2:27]]

See [note 25¹](<1 John-02.fn#Footnote 25.1 — He>).

### Footnote 27.3 — abides

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v27|1 John 2:27]]

This is the indwelling of the all-inclusive, compound life-giving Spirit ([[Romans-08#v9|Rom. 8:9]], [[Romans-08#v11|11]]).

### Footnote 27.4 — no

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v27|1 John 2:27]]

Concerning the indwelling of the Divine Trinity ([[John-14#v17|John 14:17]], [[John-14#v23|23]]), we do not need anyone to teach us; by the anointing of the all-inclusive, compound Spirit, who is the composition of the Divine Trinity, we know and enjoy the Father, the Son, and the Spirit as our life and life supply.

### Footnote 27.5 — teaches

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v27|1 John 2:27]]

It is not an outward teaching by words but an inward teaching by anointing, through our inner spiritual consciousness. This teaching by anointing adds the divine elements of the Trinity, which are the elements of the anointing compound Spirit, into our inner being. It is like the repeated painting of some article: the paint not only indicates the color, but also by coat upon coat being added, the elements of the paint are added to the thing painted. It is in this way that the Triune God is transfused, infused, and added into all the inward parts of our being that our inner man may grow in the divine life with the divine elements.

### Footnote 27.6 — all

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v27|1 John 2:27]]

According to the context, all things refers to all things concerning the person of Christ, which are related to the Divine Trinity. The teaching of the anointing concerning these things keeps us that we may abide in Him (the Divine Trinity), that is, in the Son and in the Father ([[1 John-02#v24|v. 24]]).

### Footnote 27.7 — true

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v27|1 John 2:27]]

The anointing within us of the compound Spirit as the composition of the Triune God, who is true ([[1 John-05#v20|5:20]]), is a reality, not a falsehood. It can be proven by our actual and practical experience in our Christian life.

### Footnote 27.8 — abide

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v27|1 John 2:27]]

The Greek word means to stay (in a given place, state, relation, or expectancy); hence, to abide, remain, and dwell. To abide in Him is to abide in the Son and in the Father ([[1 John-02#v24|v. 24]]). This is to remain and dwell in the Lord ([[John-15#v4|John 15:4-5]]). It is also to abide in the fellowship of the divine life and to walk in the divine light ([[1 John-01#v2|1:2-3]], [[1 John-01#v6|6-7]]), i.e., abide in the divine light ([[1 John-02#v10|v. 10]]). We should practice this abiding according to the teaching of the all-inclusive anointing, that our fellowship with God ([[1 John-01#v3|1:3]], [[1 John-01#v6|6]]) may be maintained.

## Footnotes for verse 28

### Footnote 28.1 — little

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v28|1 John 2:28]]

See [note 1¹](<1 John-02.fn#Footnote 1.1 — little>). The word that begins in [[1 John-02#v13|v. 13]], written to the three different classes of recipients, ends in [[1 John-02#v27|v. 27]]. [[1 John-02#v28|Verse 28]] turns back to all the recipients. Hence, the address is again to the little children, as in [[1 John-02#v1|vv. 1]] and [[1 John-02#v12|12]].

### Footnote 28.2 — abide

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v28|1 John 2:28]]

The word addressed to the three groups of recipients in [[1 John-02#v13|vv. 13-27]] concludes in the charge to abide in Him as the anointing has taught. In this section, [[1 John-02#v28|2:28]]—[[1 John-03#v24|3:24]], the apostle continued to describe the life that abides in the Lord. The section begins ([[1 John-02#v28|v. 28]]), continues ([[1 John-03#v6|3:6]]), and ends ([[1 John-03#v24|3:24]]) with abide(s) in Him.

### Footnote 28.3 — He

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v28|1 John 2:28]]

Here the pronoun He refers definitely to Christ the Son, who is coming. This, with the preceding clause abide in Him, which is a repetition of the clause in the preceding verse involving the Trinity, indicates that the Son is the embodiment of the Triune God, inseparable from the Father or the Spirit.

### Footnote 28.4 — put

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v28|1 John 2:28]]

This indicates that some believers, those who do not abide in the Lord (i.e., remain in the fellowship of the divine life according to pure faith in Christ’s person) but are led astray by the heretical teachings concerning Christ ([[1 John-02#v26|v. 26]]), will be punished by being put to shame from Him, from His glorious parousia.

### Footnote 28.5 — from

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v28|1 John 2:28]]

See [[Matthew-07#v23|Matt. 7:23]], [note 51¹](<Matthew-24.fn#Footnote 51.1 — cut>) in [[Matthew-24|Matt. 24]], and [[Matthew-22#v13|Matt. 22:13]] and [note 2](<Matthew-22.fn#Footnote 13.2 — cast>).

### Footnote 28.6 — at

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v28|1 John 2:28]]

Lit., in His presence (parousia).

## Footnotes for verse 29

### Footnote 29.1 — know

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v29|1 John 2:29]]

The Greek word denotes perception with a conscious knowledge, a deeper inward seeing. This is for knowing the Lord.

### Footnote 29.2 — He

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v29|1 John 2:29]]

Here He denotes the Triune God—the Father, the Son, and the Spirit—all-inclusively, because it refers to He and Him in the preceding verse, which denote the coming Son; it also refers to Him in this verse, denoting the Father, who has begotten us. This indicates strongly that the Son and the Father are one ([[John-10#v30|John 10:30]]).

### Footnote 29.3 — righteous

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v29|1 John 2:29]]

This refers to the righteous God in [[1 John-01#v9|1:9]] and Jesus Christ the Righteous in [[1 John-02#v1|v. 1]] of this chapter. In this word to all the recipients, beginning from [[1 John-02#v28|v. 28]], the apostle turned his emphasis from the fellowship of the divine life ([[1 John-01#v3|1:3]]—[[1 John-02#v11|2:11]]) and the anointing of the Divine Trinity ([[1 John-02#v12|vv. 12-27]]) to the righteousness of God. The fellowship of the divine life and the anointing of the Divine Trinity should have an issue, that is, the expression of the righteous God.

### Footnote 29.4 — you

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v29|1 John 2:29]]

Or, know that—(imperative).

### Footnote 29.5 — know

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v29|1 John 2:29]]

The Greek word denotes outward, objective knowledge (see notes [55¹](<John-08.fn#Footnote 55.1 — known>) in [[John-08|John 8]], [17²](<John-21.fn#Footnote 17.2 — know>) in [[John-21|John 21]], and [11¹](<Hebrews-08.fn#Footnote 11.1 — Know>) in [[Hebrews-08|Heb. 8]]). This is for knowing man.

### Footnote 29.6 — practices

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v29|1 John 2:29]]

See [note 6⁵](<1 John-01.fn#Footnote 6.5 — practicing>) in [[1 John-01|1 John 1]]. This is not to do righteousness occasionally and purposely as some particular act but to practice righteousness habitually and unintentionally as a common daily living. It is the same in [[1 John-03#v7|3:7]]. This is a spontaneous living that issues from the divine life within us, with which we have been begotten of the righteous God. Hence, it is a living expression of God, who is righteous in all His deeds and acts. It is not just an outward behavior but the manifestation of the inward life; not just an act of purpose but the flow of life from within the divine nature, of which we partake. This is the first condition of the life that abides in the Lord. It is all due to the divine birth, which is indicated by the word has been begotten of Him and the title children of God in the succeeding verse ([[1 John-03#v1|3:1]]).

### Footnote 29.7 — begotten

**Verse:** [[1 John-02#v29|1 John 2:29]]

John’s writings on the mysteries of the eternal divine life stress very much the divine birth ([[1 John-03#v9|3:9]]; [[1 John-04#v7|4:7]]; [[1 John-05#v1|5:1]], [[1 John-05#v4|4]], [[1 John-05#v18|18]]; [[John-01#v12|John 1:12-13]]), which is our regeneration ([[John-03#v3|John 3:3]], [[John-03#v5|5]]). It is the greatest wonder in the entire universe that human beings could be begotten of God and sinners could be made children of God! Through such an amazing divine birth we have received the divine life, which is the eternal life ([[1 John-01#v2|1:2]]), as the divine seed sown into our being ([[1 John-03#v9|3:9]]). Out of this seed all the riches of the divine life grow from within us. It is by this that we abide in the Triune God and live out the divine life in our human living, i.e., live out the life that does not practice sin ([[1 John-03#v9|3:9]]) but practices righteousness ([[1 John-02#v29|2:29]]), loves the brothers ([[1 John-05#v1|5:1]]), overcomes the world ([[1 John-05#v4|5:4]]), and is not touched by the evil one ([[1 John-05#v18|5:18]]).
