# 2 Corinthians-06 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[2 Corinthians-06|2 Corinthians 6]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — And

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v1|2 Corinthians 6:1]]

And here indicates a continuation. In the last part of [[2 Corinthians-05|2 Corinthians 5]] ([[2 Corinthians-05#v16|5:16-21]]) the apostle told us that they, the ministers of the new covenant, had been commissioned with the ministry of reconciliation for the Lord’s new creation. From this verse to the end of [[2 Corinthians-07|2 Corinthians 7]] he continued by telling us how they worked. They worked together with God by a life (not by any gift) that was all-sufficient and all-mature, able to fit all situations, that is, able to endure any kind of treatment, to accept any kind of environment, to work in any kind of condition, and to take any kind of opportunity, for the carrying out of their ministry.

### Footnote 1.2 — together

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v1|2 Corinthians 6:1]]

The apostles not only were commissioned by God with their ministry but also were working together with Him. They were God’s co-workers ([[1 Corinthians-03#v9|1 Cor. 3:9]]).

### Footnote 1.3 — entreat

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v1|2 Corinthians 6:1]]

This is the work of reconciliation, as mentioned in [[2 Corinthians-05#v20|5:20]].

### Footnote 1.4 — grace

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v1|2 Corinthians 6:1]]

See [note 10¹](<1 Corinthians-15.fn#Footnote 10.1 — grace>) in [[1 Corinthians-15|1 Cor. 15]]. The grace of God always brings us back to Him. Not to receive the grace of God in vain, according to the context, means not to remain in any condition that is a distraction from God, but to be brought back to Him.

## Footnotes for verse 4

### Footnote 4.1 — endurance

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v4|2 Corinthians 6:4]]

Endurance is the first qualification for the all-fitting life of the new covenant ministers. This refers not merely to a kind of patience but to the ability to endure suffering in tribulation, whether pressure, oppression, persecution, poverty, or any kind of trial.

### Footnote 4.2 — necessities

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v4|2 Corinthians 6:4]]

The Greek word denotes constraints, urgent needs that press heavily, indicating a shortage of daily necessities such as food, lodging, or clothing.

## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — knowledge

Weapons of righteousness are used for fighting that we may be right with God and men according to the righteousness of God ([[Matthew-06#v33|Matt. 6:33]]; [[Matthew-05#v6|5:6]], [[Matthew-05#v10|10]], [[Matthew-05#v20|20]]).

Weapons on the right hand, such as the sword, are offensive; those on the left hand, such as the shield, are defensive.

## Footnotes for verse 11

### Footnote 11.1 — opened

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v11|2 Corinthians 6:11]]

The apostles, all-mature and all-fitting, as depicted in [[2 Corinthians-06#v3|vv. 3-10]], had their mouths opened and their hearts enlarged to the believers. With an enlarged heart they were able to embrace all believers regardless of their condition, and with an opened mouth they were free to speak to all believers frankly concerning the real situation into which they had been misled. This kind of openness and enlargement is needed to reconcile, to bring back, the misled or distracted believers to God.

## Footnotes for verse 12

### Footnote 12.1 — constricted

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v12|2 Corinthians 6:12]]

The Corinthian believers, being childish ([[2 Corinthians-06#v13|v. 13]]), were constricted, contracted, in their inward parts toward the apostles. All children are very narrow in their affection and easily offended by those who correct them.

### Footnote 12.2 — inward

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v12|2 Corinthians 6:12]]

The same Greek word as for affections in [[2 Corinthians-07#v15|7:15]]. See [note 8¹](<Philippians-01.fn#Footnote 8.1 — inward>) in [[Philippians-01|Phil. 1]].

## Footnotes for verse 13

### Footnote 13.1 — recompense

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v13|2 Corinthians 6:13]]

The apostle wanted the Corinthian believers to give him a recompense—the same kind of enlarged heart, that they might receive him in their love.

### Footnote 13.2 — children

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v13|2 Corinthians 6:13]]

This indicates (1) that the apostle considered the Corinthian believers childish, and (2) that in dealing with them he spoke as a father to his children.

### Footnote 13.3 — be

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v13|2 Corinthians 6:13]]

To be enlarged requires growth and maturity in life, which the Corinthian believers lacked ([[1 Corinthians-03#v1|1 Cor. 3:1]], [[1 Corinthians-03#v6|6]]; [[1 Corinthians-14#v20|14:20]]). The apostle was laboring on them to make up the shortage. According to the context, which is continued from the end of [[2 Corinthians-05|2 Corinthians 5]], to be enlarged by growing and maturing in life is equivalent to being fully reconciled to God. Writing in this way, the apostle was carrying out his ministry of reconciling the believers, who were but halfway in being reconciled to God.

## Footnotes for verse 14

### Footnote 14.1 — Do

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v14|2 Corinthians 6:14]]

This word was spoken by the apostle based on his mouth being opened and his heart being enlarged to the believers in [[2 Corinthians-06#v11|v. 11]].

### Footnote 14.2 — dissimilarly

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v14|2 Corinthians 6:14]]

Dissimilarly means diversely, implying a difference in kind. This refers to [[Deuteronomy-22#v10|Deut. 22:10]], which forbids the yoking together of two dissimilar animals. Believers and unbelievers are diverse peoples. Because of their divine nature and holy standing, the believers should not be yoked together with the unbelievers. This should be applied to all intimate relationships between believers and unbelievers, not only to marriage and business. 

This word indicates that the Corinthian believers were dissimilarly yoked with unbelievers, not separated unto God from worldly people. This means that they were not fully reconciled to God. Hence, the apostle exhorted them not to be dissimilarly yoked with unbelievers but to be separated that they might be fully reconciled, brought back, to God.

### Footnote 14.3 — partnership

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v14|2 Corinthians 6:14]]

The apostle used five illustrations to depict the difference between believers and unbelievers: (1) no partnership, no sharing together, between righteousness and lawlessness; (2) no fellowship, no communion, between light and darkness; (3) no concord, no harmony, between Christ and Belial; (4) no part, no portion, held by a believer with an unbeliever; and (5) no agreement, no consent, between the temple of God and idols. These illustrations also unveil the fact that the believers are righteousness, light, Christ, and the temple of God, and the unbelievers are lawlessness, darkness, Belial (Satan, the devil), and idols.

### Footnote 14.4 — with

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v14|2 Corinthians 6:14]]

Lit., toward.

## Footnotes for verse 17

### Footnote 17.1 — separated

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v17|2 Corinthians 6:17]]

This is to be brought back, to be reconciled, to God in a practical way ([[2 Corinthians-05#v20|5:20]]). To be brought back to God, to be reconciled to God, in a practical way requires us to be separated from all persons, things, and matters that do not belong to God.

### Footnote 17.2 — what

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v17|2 Corinthians 6:17]]

Things that belong to lawlessness, darkness, Belial, and idols, as listed in [[2 Corinthians-06#v14|vv. 14-16]].

### Footnote 17.3 — welcome

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v17|2 Corinthians 6:17]]

This is God’s glad receiving of the believers who are brought back, fully reconciled, to Him.

## Footnotes for verse 18

### Footnote 18.1 — a

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v18|2 Corinthians 6:18]]

God’s being a Father to us and our being sons and daughters to Him are matters of life. They are deeper than His being our God and our being His people ([[2 Corinthians-06#v16|v. 16]]).

### Footnote 18.2 — sons

**Verse:** [[2 Corinthians-06#v18|2 Corinthians 6:18]]

This is the only instance in which the New Testament indicates that God has daughters. For the most part it tells us that the believers are sons of God.
