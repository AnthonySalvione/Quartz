# Philippians-04 — Footnotes

**Chapter:** [[Philippians-04|Philippians 4]]

## Footnotes for verse 1

### Footnote 1.1 — joy

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v1|Philippians 4:1]]

Joy is inward; a crown is manifested outwardly. The believers were the apostle’s inward happiness and outward glory.

### Footnote 1.2 — in

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v1|Philippians 4:1]]

In the way presented by the apostle in the foregoing chapter.

## Footnotes for verse 2

### Footnote 2.1 — think

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v2|Philippians 4:2]]

This indicates that the two sisters were dissenting from each other; they were not of the same mind. Hence, there was the exhortation in this book to strive together with one soul along with the personified gospel ([[Philippians-01#v27|1:27]]), to be joined in soul, thinking the one thing ([[Philippians-02#v2|2:2]]), and to have the same mind to pursue Christ ([[Philippians-03#v14|3:14-15]]).

## Footnotes for verse 3

### Footnote 3.1 — yokefellow

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v3|Philippians 4:3]]

Referring to two oxen pulling a plow; used as a metaphor. Yokefellow refers to being yoked together with another to bear a common burden.

### Footnote 3.2 — assist

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v3|Philippians 4:3]]

Assist them to think the same thing in the Lord, and thereby to be one.

### Footnote 3.3 — contended

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v3|Philippians 4:3]]

Labored with, strove together with, wrestled in company with, as a team of athletes.

### Footnote 3.4 — book

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v3|Philippians 4:3]]

A heavenly record of the redeemed in Christ.

## Footnotes for verse 4

### Footnote 4.1 — Rejoice

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v4|Philippians 4:4]]

Rejoicing affords us the strength for the oneness spoken of in [[Philippians-04#v2|vv. 2]] and [[Philippians-04#v3|3]]. Furthermore, rejoicing in the Lord is the secret of having the excellent virtues listed in [[Philippians-04#v5|vv. 5-9]].

## Footnotes for verse 5

### Footnote 5.1 — Let

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v5|Philippians 4:5]]

The apostle’s charge in [[Philippians-04#v5|vv. 5-9]] should be the outward expression of the inner reality of the experience of Christ spoken of by him in [[Philippians-01|Philippians 1—3]].

### Footnote 5.2 — forbearance

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v5|Philippians 4:5]]

I.e., reasonableness, considerateness, and consideration in dealing with others, not being strict in claiming one’s legal rights. It is in contrast to selfish ambition and vainglory ([[Philippians-02#v3|2:3]]) and to murmurings and reasonings ([[Philippians-02#v14|2:14]]). It is Christ Himself as an excellent virtue lived out of the believers.

### Footnote 5.3 — near

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v5|Philippians 4:5]]

Near in space and time. With respect to space, the Lord is near us, ready to help. With regard to time, the Lord is at hand, coming soon.

## Footnotes for verse 6

### Footnote 6.1 — be

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v6|Philippians 4:6]]

I.e., worry. Anxiety, coming from Satan, is the sum total of human life and disturbs the believers’ life of living Christ; forbearance, coming from God, is the sum total of a life that lives Christ. The two are opposites.

### Footnote 6.2 — prayer

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v6|Philippians 4:6]]

Prayer is general, having worship and fellowship as its essence; petition is special, being for particular needs.

### Footnote 6.3 — with

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v6|Philippians 4:6]]

Not and but with. Both our prayer and our petition should be accompanied by our giving thanks to the Lord.

### Footnote 6.4 — to

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v6|Philippians 4:6]]

The Greek word is often translated with ([[John-01#v1|John 1:1]]; [[Mark-09#v19|Mark 9:19]]; [[2 Corinthians-05#v8|2 Cor. 5:8]]; [[1 John-01#v2|1 John 1:2]]). It denotes motion toward, in the sense of a living union and communion, implying fellowship. Hence, the sense of to God here is in the fellowship with God.

## Footnotes for verse 7

### Footnote 7.1 — peace

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v7|Philippians 4:7]]

The result of practicing fellowship with God in prayer is that we enjoy the peace of God. The peace of God is actually God as peace ([[Philippians-04#v9|v. 9]]) infused into us through our fellowship with Him by prayer, as the counterpoise to troubles and the antidote to anxiety ([[John-16#v33|John 16:33]]).

### Footnote 7.2 — guard

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v7|Philippians 4:7]]

Or, mount guard over. The God of peace patrols before our hearts and thoughts in Christ, keeping us calm and tranquil.

### Footnote 7.3 — hearts

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v7|Philippians 4:7]]

The heart is the source; the thoughts are the issue.

## Footnotes for verse 8

### Footnote 8.1 — true

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v8|Philippians 4:8]]

Truthful ethically. It does not mean true in matter of fact.

### Footnote 8.2 — dignified

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v8|Philippians 4:8]]

I.e., venerable, worthy of reverence, noble, grave; implying the idea of dignity, which inspires and invites reverence.

### Footnote 8.3 — righteous

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v8|Philippians 4:8]]

Right, not just, before God and man.

### Footnote 8.4 — pure

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v8|Philippians 4:8]]

I.e., single in intention and action, without any mixture.

### Footnote 8.5 — lovely

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v8|Philippians 4:8]]

I.e., lovable, agreeable, endearing.

### Footnote 8.6 — well

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v8|Philippians 4:8]]

I.e., of good repute, attractive, winning, gracious.

### Footnote 8.7 — virtue

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v8|Philippians 4:8]]

Meaning excellence, that is, ethical energy exhibited in vigorous action. See notes [3¹¹](<2 Peter-01.fn#Footnote 3.11 — virtue>) and [5⁴](<2 Peter-01.fn#Footnote 5.4 — virtue>) in [[2 Peter-01|2 Pet. 1]].

### Footnote 8.8 — praise

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v8|Philippians 4:8]]

I.e., things worthy of praise, as the companion of virtue. The first six items are categorized under what things are; the last two, under if any. This indicates that the last two items are a summing up of the first six, in all of which are some virtue or excellence and something worthy of praise.

### Footnote 8.9 — take

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v8|Philippians 4:8]]

Think about, ponder, consider.

### Footnote 9.2 — seen

## Footnotes for verse 10

### Footnote 10.1 — blossom

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v10|Philippians 4:10]]

The Greek word means to sprout and bloom, implying that the Philippian believers’ thinking toward the apostle was a matter of life. For some time their thinking for Paul had been dormant. But when Paul wrote this Epistle, their thinking for him had begun to blossom anew, just like a plant’s sprouting and blooming.

## Footnotes for verse 11

### Footnote 11.1 — content

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v11|Philippians 4:11]]

Self-sufficient, satisfied in oneself. This is a word used by the Stoics, who taught that a person should be content in all circumstances. Being content is the opposite of the Epicurean practice referred to in [[Philippians-03#v18|3:18-19]].

## Footnotes for verse 12

### Footnote 12.1 — abased

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v12|Philippians 4:12]]

Humbled, in lowly circumstances.

### Footnote 12.2 — abound

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v12|Philippians 4:12]]

To live in abundance.

### Footnote 12.3 — in

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v12|Philippians 4:12]]

In everything means in each matter; in all things means in all matters. Together, these two phrases encompass all the things in the course of human life. Paul learned the secret of experiencing Christ—to experience Him in everything and in every place.

### Footnote 12.4 — I

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v12|Philippians 4:12]]

Lit., I have been initiated. The metaphor here refers to a person’s being initiated into a secret society with instruction in its rudimentary principles. After Paul was converted to Christ, he was initiated into Christ and the Body of Christ. He then learned the secret of how to take Christ as life, how to live Christ, how to magnify Christ, how to gain Christ, and how to have the church life, all of which things are rudimentary principles.

### Footnote 12.5 — filled

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v12|Philippians 4:12]]

Satisfied.

### Footnote 12.6 — abound

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v12|Philippians 4:12]]

To have abundance, sufficiency, more than enough.

### Footnote 12.7 — lack

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v12|Philippians 4:12]]

To be in want, to suffer privation.

## Footnotes for verse 13

### Footnote 13.1 — in

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v13|Philippians 4:13]]

Paul was a person in Christ ([[2 Corinthians-12#v2|2 Cor. 12:2]]), and he desired to be found in Christ by others. Now he declared that he was able to do all things in Him, the very Christ who empowered him. This is an all-inclusive and concluding word on his experience of Christ. It is the converse of the Lord’s word in [[John-15#v5|John 15:5]] concerning our organic relationship with Him, “Apart from Me you can do nothing.”

### Footnote 13.2 — empowers

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v13|Philippians 4:13]]

The Greek word means makes dynamic inwardly. Christ dwells in us ([[Colossians-01#v27|Col. 1:27]]). He empowers us, makes us dynamic from within, not from without. By such inward empowering Paul was able to do all things in Christ.

## Footnotes for verse 18

### Footnote 18.1 — filled

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v18|Philippians 4:18]]

Fully supplied.

### Footnote 18.2 — things

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v18|Philippians 4:18]]

The material things given as a supply to the apostle.

### Footnote 18.3 — sweet-smelling

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v18|Philippians 4:18]]

The same Greek phrase as in [[Ephesians-05#v2|Eph. 5:2]]. A sweet-smelling odor of sacrifices offered to God ([[Genesis-08#v21|Gen. 8:21]]).

### Footnote 18.4 — sacrifice

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v18|Philippians 4:18]]

The Philippians’ material gift, given to supply the apostle’s need, was considered by him a sacrifice to God, acceptable and well pleasing ([[Hebrews-13#v16|Heb. 13:16]]). Paul indicated in his appreciation that what the Philippians did toward him was done toward God. This implies that he had the assurance that he was one with God and that his work was by God and for God.

## Footnotes for verse 19

### Footnote 19.1 — my

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v19|Philippians 4:19]]

The apostle in his experience had the conviction and assurance that he was one with God and that God was his God. Since he considered the Philippians’ material gift to him a sacrifice to God, he believed with assurance that God, who was one with him and who was his God, would richly repay the Philippians.

### Footnote 19.2 — fill

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v19|Philippians 4:19]]

Fully supply, satisfy to the full.

### Footnote 19.3 — your

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v19|Philippians 4:19]]

Since the Philippians cared for God’s sent one, God would care for their every need.

### Footnote 19.4 — according

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v19|Philippians 4:19]]

God supplies us according to His riches, not according to our needs. His riches exceed our needs.

### Footnote 19.5 — in

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v19|Philippians 4:19]]

In glory modifies fill. Glory is the expression of God; it is God expressed in splendor. God’s rich supply to the believers, who are His children, expresses God and bears the glory of God. The apostle assured the Philippians that God would abundantly supply all their needs in a way that would bring them into His glory.

### Footnote 19.6 — in

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v19|Philippians 4:19]]

In Christ Jesus also modifies fill. Christ, the all-inclusive One, is the base, the element, the sphere, and the channel in and by which God cares for His people according to His riches and in glory. This indicates that even the believers’ giving and receiving of material things are intimately related to the experience of Christ.

## Footnotes for verse 23

### Footnote 23.1 — grace

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v23|Philippians 4:23]]

God in Christ as our supply and enjoyment, conveyed to us and realized through the bountiful supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ ([[Philippians-01#v19|1:19]]). To experience Christ as Paul did, we need this grace.

### Footnote 23.2 — spirit

**Verse:** [[Philippians-04#v23|Philippians 4:23]]

Our regenerated spirit, which is indwelt by the Spirit of Christ. It is in this spirit of ours that we experience and enjoy Christ as Paul did. 

This book, which is concerned with the experience of Christ, points out to us at the beginning, in [[Philippians-01|Philippians 1]], that it is the bountiful supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ that enables us to live Christ, even to magnify Christ under any circumstances, that we may enjoy Him as the uttermost salvation. Here, at the end of the book, we are told that our experience and enjoyment of Christ must take place in our spirit, which has been regenerated to be the dwelling place and vessel of the Triune God. Therefore, this is altogether a matter of the union and mingling of the processed Triune God, who has become the all-inclusive, life-giving Spirit, and the spirit of us, the tripartite men, who are being transformed by such a Spirit. We must live and act in our wonderful spirit by the Triune God as the wonderful Spirit. Only then can we experience and enjoy the Triune God, who was processed through incarnation, crucifixion, resurrection, and ascension, as the inexhaustibly sufficient grace and thus become His fullness, His expression.
