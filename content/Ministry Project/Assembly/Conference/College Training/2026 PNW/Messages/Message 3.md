# Message Three — Heralds and Fishers of Men

## Scripture Reading

1 Tim. 2:7; 2 Tim. 1:10-11; Rom. 10:14-15; Matt. 4:19; Mark 1:17; Luke 5:10

## I. A herald is a proclaimer of the gospel of Christ, an official reporter of God's New Testament economy.

### A. The most important thing about a herald is representation; God is a speaking God and we represent Him in our ability to speak; we can speak, therefore we can be a herald.

### B. In the Old Testament Noah was the first herald and was called a "herald of righteousness" as a protest against the evil generation—2 Pet. 2:5.

#### 1. When God told Noah that He was going to destroy all men with the earth, Noah rose up to preach God's righteousness in opposition to the corruption of his generation, telling the people to get right with God, with others, and with themselves or else God's righteous judgment would come upon them.

#### 2. In order to speak for God, we must be spoken to by Him and we need to learn to understand the word of God—Gen. 6:13-22; Acts 4:10-12, 20; Matt. 11:16-19.

### C. In the New Testament, Paul was "appointed a herald" of the gospel of Christ Jesus; he was commissioned as a herald, an official reporter of God's New Testament economy—1 Tim. 2:7.

#### 1. Today in the New Testament age we need to be heralds, those who proclaim the gospel of Christ; like Paul, we must practice to speak in boldness the mystery of the gospel—Eph. 6:19.

#### 2. It is our faithfulness to the word of the gospel, not our eloquence, that will result in a reward for faithful service to the One who entrusted us with the gospel—1 Cor. 9:16-17; Rom. 1:14; Jer. 20:9; 1 Cor. 4:2.

## II. To be a fisher of men is to bring men out of the world, signified by the sea with its death waters, into the kingdom of the heavens.

### A. The Greek word for *catch alive* in Luke 5:10 is a compound of *living* and *to catch*; the common fishers catch fish unto death, but Peter was called by the Lord to be a fisher of men that he might catch men unto life—Matt. 4:19; Acts 2:38; 11:18.

### B. The Bible uses the illustration of fishing to show us how we can gain these ones; to get sinners saved is like catching fish—Matt. 4:19; Luke 5:9-10.

#### 1. In the view of people in the world, we are students, but in the view of the angels in the kingdom of the heavens, we are not students but fishers of men.

#### 2. As fishers of men, gaining others for Christ's increase, we need patience; we should contact others again and again until they are "caught" and brought out of the world into the kingdom of the heavens.

### C. When Peter was called he was fishing not with a pole but with a net; the saints who are in the schools should not fish with a pole but should cast a net, that is, form a nucleus of burdened students.

#### 1. The only way to carry out a dynamic, powerful, and prevailing work is for the students on each campus to form a nucleus of students who are burdened for their campus.

#### 2. By praying as a nucleus and inviting our friends to it, our friends will gradually be brought to Christ and into the church life.

## Focus

God has commissioned us to be heralds, official reporters of God's New Testament economy who speak what we have seen and heard, and called us to be fishers of men, those who "catch men alive" out of the "sea" where we live and into the boat of the church life.
