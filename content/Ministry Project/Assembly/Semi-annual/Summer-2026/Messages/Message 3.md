## I. The believers are symbolized by the wheat of life, which will be gathered into the Lord's barn—[[Matthew-03#v11|Matt. 3:11-12]]; [[Matthew-13#v24|13:24-30]], [[Matthew-13#v38|38-42]]

That life is simply God Himself to be enjoyed by us—the tree of life. And by receiving, enjoying, and containing this life, who is God Himself, and even by becoming joined to this life, the believers, who are us today, have become the **wheat of life**. We used to be dead items, dead people, right? Dead in sins, dead in the world, but no longer, brothers and sisters! We have life! And today, we are the wheat of life!

Now, in the next point, right away we come to something that *hinders* this life. This life has come into all of us, but after He comes into us, this life faces a big problem. You say, "What is that problem?" Everything about us is that problem. I am a problem to the divine life. All of you are in the same way. This life that God has put within us through our regeneration, through our second birth, is not just to come into us, but God's intention is that this life would be sown as a seed in us, which will grow, develop, spread, and bear fruit. This life should fill us, our entire being, from our spirit to our soul and then to our body, so that we would become men of life. Now, today, in order for us to become the wheat of life in actuality, this life has to spread. This life has to grow within us. But our whole being, as I said, is a hindrance to God's life. Or, you could say, God's life immediately encounters many obstacles in us.

I hope, dear saints, you would right away be considering this matter very carefully. Okay? Let me continue my speaking in point B.

Unhindered means for Christ's life to have room to grow, for this life to have a way to spread within us to fill us. We must experience the breaking of the cross, the killing death of Christ in the all-inclusive Spirit, so that the following obstacles, these four obstacles we will see, which are restrictions to God's life within us, can be dealt with and removed.

Now, the first thing to be re-impressed with again, brothers and sisters, is that this life is not just some kind of package, just some kind of thing. This life is a living person. This life is Christ. Today, the resurrected Christ, who has become the life-giving Spirit—**this is life**. This is life. I know this sounds *so* elementary, *so* basic. We all know this. But I find, even in reading these basic things, brothers and sisters, that I just *forgot* about it. #### 1. Being a Christian means taking care of the living Christ in us and not taking anything other than Christ as our aim; the obstacle to this is in ==not knowing the pathway of life and not taking Christ as our life==—[[Matthew-07#v13|Matt. 7:13-14]]; [[Philippians-03#v8|Phil. 3:8-14]]; [[Colossians-03#v4|Col. 3:4]]; [[Romans-08#v28|Rom. 8:28-29]].

So, do you see? The first problem here that obstructs the life of God is that we don't even know; we are ignorant that the Christian life is not about this or that, but the Christian life is about Christ in **you** and in **me**. It's about taking care of this Christ, this living person. I don't need to say that today, there are many Christians with the wrong concept of what it is to be a Christian. They think that to be a Christian is to be moral, to be ethical, to be good. I wouldn't say that is *wrong*, but that concept is surely *off*. Or, they think that Christians should be zealous, should be industrious in doing the things and the work for God, and so on and so forth. It's not that we don't do that, but the primary thing, the primary **thing**, brothers and sisters, is about this person. This person who is in us. How much are we taking care of Him? If we are not taking care of Him, we could be doing **all those things**, but that life would have *no way* within us.

So, today, you will find Christian ethics, Christian morality, Christian work, Christian endeavoring, but seldom will you find *life*. Even the idea of life, not to mention the growth of that life and the expression of that life. I hope that we have been spared of this ignorance, amen? That's why in the recovery, we strengthen this matter of life so, so much.

#### 2. The ==second obstacle is hypocrisy==; a person's spirituality is not determined by outward appearance but by how he takes care of Christ—[[Matthew-06#v1|Matt. 6:1-6]]; [[Matthew-15#v7|15:7-8]]; [[John-05#v44|John 5:44]]; [[John-12#v42|12:42-43]]; cf. [[Joshua-07#v21|Josh. 7:21]].

So, when we talk about hypocrisy here, we are talking about a certain kind of, shall we say, *false spirituality*. Just because a person looks spiritual, and a person may be moving around in a *pious* way—in other things, in speaking, in a "godly" way—they typically do things slower and more deliberately than others. They are gentle, compassionate, kind, uncontentious, and all of these good things. These are really good things, but all of these things could be, what? Not life. #### 4. The ==fourth obstacle is our natural capabilities==; if these natural capabilities remain unbroken in us, they will become a problem to Christ's life—[[Matthew-02#v14|2:14-15]]; [[Matthew-03#v12|3:12-17]]; [[Jude-01#v19|Jude 19-21]].

It's all about how we take care of this person, Christ, who is life, within us. If we do, brothers and sisters, this life will have a way within us to grow and to spread, and we will become the real wheat of life, expressing this Christ, living out this Christ, and even magnifying this Christ.

## II. The believers are symbolized by the good seed, the sons of the kingdom who are sown by the Lord to grow in His kingdom—[[Matthew-13#v24|Matt. 13:24]], [[Matthew-13#v38|38]]

### A. The seed is the word of God ([[Matthew-03#v4|v. 4]], [[Matthew-13#v19|19]]; [[Luke-08#v11|Luke 8:11]]), Christ Himself ([[1 Peter-01#v23|1 Pet. 1:23]]), sown into us to make us the good seed, the sons of the resurrection ([[Matthew-13#v38|Matt. 13:38]]; [[Luke-20#v36|Luke 20:36-38]]).

So, we know that the seed is the word of God in Matthew 13. A man went forth to sow, to sow the good seed in His field. But eventually, you have, what? The result is that from these good seeds that are sown, some other good seeds come forth, which are the sons of the kingdom. Very interesting. What is sown in the earth is the good seed. What comes out of it is also the good seed. So, in this point, you have the seed being the Word of God, Christ Himself, sown into us to make us the good seed, the sons of resurrection.

So, whenever the Word is sown into us and received by us, that word of life grows. It grows and multiplies, and the result of that multiplication is what? It is many good seeds, who are us, even called the sons of resurrection, the children of the kingdom.

### B. The word of God as a grain of wheat dispenses God as life into us to nourish us; also, the word as fire burns us and many of the things in which we have confidence, and the word as a hammer breaks down our self, our natural life, our flesh, our lusts, and our concepts—[[Jeremiah-23#v28|Jer. 23:28-29]].

Do you see? Right away, we are touching the problems again, the obstacles here again. I am not here trying to make us all, you know, navel-gazing or introspective. That is not my point here. But dear brothers and sisters, we do need to see what is in the way of His life within us, so that we can deal with these things and face them, so that we can then allow the Lord to deal with them or eliminate them.

The Christian, or the saint who grows the quickest, is not the one who receives more Christ. We all received the same Christ. The difference is that some give that Christ the way all the time. That makes the difference. This person is growing faster. The same Christ, the same life. So, even when we receive the Word of God for nourishment, do not forget that this Word also has the function to burn. The Lord will burn many things in us that are not Christ, that actually are a hindrance to Christ. Here it says, "things in which we have confidence." Did you know—I certainly know from experience—that self-confidence is a big problem. A **big problem**. Because you have some ability, some experience, you kind of know the ropes, how to do this or do that. After a while, you acquire a kind of confidence, even in the things of the Lord and the church life. That little confidence there will just **stop the life from growing within you**. Really. The Lord wants us to not have confidence in ourselves, just like Paul. He said, "I am nearing death. All hope is gone. I have no more confidence in myself in that situation," so that he had to trust in God, trust in the Lord, and have confidence in the Lord uniquely. This is the great apostle! This is Paul! And he needed to be placed in that situation of persecution, suffering, and affliction, so that the last bit of confidence in this man would be eliminated.

It sounds *easy*, brothers and sisters, but it's actually not that easy to be a person who has utter and total trust in the Lord and no confidence in ourselves. We cannot forget the Word; it is like a hammer, a big hammer, a hammer to break. Break down what? Break down the self, the natural life, the flesh, the natural lusts, the concepts, all these things. The Lord works this way if we are faithful to open to His Word. Amen? The Word, when it becomes living rhema within us, breaks us, kills us I would even say, hammers us, to what? To break down all these things that are obstacles to life.

### C. As the good seed, we must follow the Lord into the ground to die and bear much fruit in resurrection; to die and experience the cross is to deny and reject our soul-life, our natural life, to live by the divine life—[[John-12#v23|John 12:23-26]], [[John-12#v31|31-32]].

We say, "Why don't we talk more about positive things regarding how to be this good seed?" Well, that is not the need. The need is to see what is **preventing** us from being that good seed. Here, we have to follow the Lord, the first seed, into the ground to die in order to bear fruit in resurrection. And for us, just like the Lord, to die is to experience the cross, to reject and deny our soul-life, and to live by the divine life. Obviously, the Lord lived this way even before He went to the cross. He lived a life under the shadow of the cross to deny Himself, and that is why He is full of life! That is why He can give life abundantly. There is no obstacle in the Lord Jesus to the Father's life in Him. That life could flow freely, because all these things, these obstacles, are not there with Him.

### D. The sowing of the good seed is martyrdom, for "what you sow is not made alive unless it dies" ([[1 Corinthians-15#v36|1 Cor. 15:36]]); we can be martyrs physically, psychologically, or spiritually ([[2 Timothy-04#v6|2 Tim. 4:6]]; [[Matthew-16#v25|Matt. 16:25]]; [[1 Corinthians-16#v12|1 Cor. 16:12]]; [[2 Corinthians-02#v12|2 Cor. 2:12-14]]).

Even the word martyrdom here is used for this quote in [[1 Corinthians-15#v36|1 Corinthians 15:36]]. So, no death, no life. For us to have the experience of the abundance of life, the only way is more death. The only way is more killing of our natural life and soul-life. "Unless it dies." What we sow, what you sow, is not made alive. So today, in a good sense, we are martyrs, physically, psychologically, or spiritually. In this country, it is probably not so likely to be a physical martyr, but surely, in our daily life, with all kinds of circumstances and situations, there is for us the experience of psychological and even spiritual martyrdom.

### E. Christ's love of affection constrains us to live and die to Him and makes us martyrs for Him—[[2 Corinthians-05#v14|2 Cor. 5:14-15]]; [[Romans-08#v35|Rom. 8:35-37]]; [[Romans-14#v7|14:7-9]]; [[Revelation-02#v10|Rev. 2:10]]; [[Revelation-12#v11|12:11]].

There is not one martyr who is *not* a lover of the Lord Jesus. Love, in the end, is what motivates us to go into death, to live to Him, and to die to Him.

### F. The putting to death of Jesus in our environment cooperates with the indwelling Spirit to kill our natural man and release the divine life—[[Romans-08#v2|Rom. 8:2]], [[Romans-08#v13|13]]; [[2 Corinthians-04#v10|2 Cor. 4:10-13]]; [[Jeremiah-48#v11|Jer. 48:11]].

So, it's all over Paul's writings, especially 2 Corinthians, the killing or the putting to death of Jesus in a daily way in our environment. This works with the indwelling Spirit to kill our natural man and release the divine life. Hah. I hope you're not depressed, eh? The fact of the matter is, brothers and sisters, we all wish we had a happy life, right? Joyful, kicking our heels, right? A happy life. Nothing wrong with that. We are thrust into environments, situations, and relationships that are more than difficult, that actually kill us, that we cannot stand or take. All kinds of environments—the "all things" in Romans 8—they are all there. And brothers and sisters, next time, again, in those situations, we should not murmur. We should not complain. We should not blame. We should not question. We should say, "Lord, I need *this*. This killing of Jesus. I take this Spirit who is in me to cooperate with this killing work of my natural man."

Every time, whenever we do this—any time—I tell you, saints, many times this is not just for a day or for a few hours. It is **longer than that**. We wish that these things would just go away. We wish that we could just make a prayer and God would answer and remove that situation. How wonderful that would be! But it seems God is silent. God is hidden. We are left there, in that misery, in that sorrowful situation and state. There comes a time when we finally realize, that's where we belong. That's where God assigned us to be. This is for those who love Him and are called according to His purpose. I don't want anything; I just want that divine life within me to be released. I want that life to grow and to mature, and you need this, this environment. You and I need *this*.

### G. Now, we're talking about us who serve the Lord, who work for the Lord. We also need to learn this lesson, that in all our work and service, nothing should originate from us. #### 1. David loved God, feared God, and cooperated with God in order to let God work; although he had the ability to build the temple of God, he stopped when God's word came to him—[[2 Samuel-07#v18|2 Sam. 7:18]], [[2 Samuel-07#v25|25]], [[2 Samuel-07#v27|27]]; cf. [[Luke-01#v38|Luke 1:38]].

We take the case of David. Actually, the ministry material from which these points come mentions others, but David was particularly a good pattern. David loved, feared, and cooperated with God.

David loved the Lord. David feared the Lord. He looked at his palace and said, "What about God? God needs a home and a dwelling place. I will build Him a house." But a word came from Nathan saying, no, you are not to build that house. He had it all—materials, wisdom—but when the word came from the prophet, **David had the grace to stop**. When the word comes...

You know, brothers and sisters, *stopping* is the hardest thing for a worker. It's hard, but oftentimes, that's what God wants. We think God just wants us to work. Actually, probably more times, God wants us to stop so that He can work.

#### 2. David's stopping established a twofold testimony in the universe: first, all the work in this universe should come from God, not from man; second, all that matters is what God does for man, not what man does for God.

I hope the young people would pay attention to *these utterances*, alright? Everything should come from God and not from man. God doesn't want to share credit with anybody. It's all God and no man. David, "You are not going to build it, but your son will build it. Can you see this, dear brothers and sisters? Do you see how God works? Recently, I've been very, very taken myself by this matter of forgiveness. I've done much study, just for myself, on this matter of forgiveness. We cover some of these things and realize, we do realize, that God's forgiving us is a **great thing**. It's a huge thing. And for us to be forgiven by God is a **great thing**. You know, today we just say, "Oh, I'm a forgiven sinner. Praise the Lord." But the sense, the weightiness of divine forgiveness, I'm afraid we have not been impacted by it and have not experienced it. So we think it's a good and wonderful thing—thank You, Lord—but you just read David in Psalm 51, the way he poured out his prayers of thorough and utter repentance to God. I tell you, that man knows forgiveness. It is that forgiveness of God, that through that forgiveness of God, God did something for David, which is eventually something for Himself.
