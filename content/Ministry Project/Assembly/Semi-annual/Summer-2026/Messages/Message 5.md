## Introduction

I wonder: the stones are strong, but the sheep are weak. So, we have to be strong yet weak, and weak yet strong. Both of these symbols are, to me, very precious and very, very important as believers. We need to see this. And both of these symbols are actually covered quite extensively in the whole Bible: the stones and the sheep. They both have a context of a corporate life. The stones are not just for the stones themselves, but for the building of the spiritual house; and the sheep should always be in the context of the flock, which signifies the church. So, these two symbols truly have particular importance in God’s economy.

## I. The believers are symbolized by living stones, those who are transformed with Christ’s stone nature to be God’s spiritual house—[[1 Peter-02#v4|1 Pet. 2:4-5]]

The significance of the stones, or the living stones, is transformation. Also, building. Stones signify a transformed man. Not the natural man, but the transformed man. Stones are for the building.

Before we go into the rest of this Roman numeral, I’d like to impress you with this line of revelation in the Bible concerning stone. It is very impressive. From Genesis all the way to Revelation, stone plays a crucial role in God’s plan, in God’s eternal purpose.

The first time stone was used or appeared was in [[Genesis-02|Genesis 2]], in the garden of Eden. With the tree of life, there is a river there where it mentions gold, bdellium, and onyx stone. That is the first time the Bible mentions stones, in that context—the garden of Eden with the tree of life and the river flowing out. There were these stones. Then, later on, we know in [[Genesis-28|Genesis 28]], with Jacob running away from his brother Esau, while taking a rest in the wilderness, he took a piece of stone to be his pillow. He rested on the stone. I don’t know how he was able to get rest on a stone, but nevertheless he did. Then God spoke to him. With that speaking, he realized something. He turned over that stone to erect it as a pillar and pour oil on it. He called that stone and that place Bethel, the house of God.

Then you go on to Exodus, with the traveling of the children of Israel in the wilderness. There was a rock, a cleft rock, which flowed out living water to quench the thirst of this traveling people of God. Then, in [[Genesis-28|chapter 28]] of the same book, with the high priest, we are told that upon the ephod, what the high priest was wearing on the shoulder plate, there were onyx stones, and on those stones were inscribed the twelve names of the tribes of Israel. Not only the shoulder plate, but the breastplate too. The high priest was carrying a big load of stones in his ministry. There were so many stones on his shoulders and breast, signifying how many people are borne by God on His shoulder and in His heart.

Also, with the prophets in Isaiah and Zechariah, they both prophesied the coming Christ as the stone: the coming stone, the foundation stone, even the topstone. And in Daniel, particularly in that interpretation of Nebuchadnezzar’s dream, we talked about the stone, the smiting stone, which will strike the whole image of the Roman Empire and topple that empire, and that stone would become a great mountain. That is the Old Testament. You see this development of this matter of the stone when you come to the New Testament.

You see in the New Testament with the conversion of Peter. When he was Simon before, when he met the Lord, the Lord told him that he is Peter, Cephas, meaning a stone. Not only at that time, but when the Lord brought His disciples to Caesarea Philippi, where He asked His disciples, “Who do you say that I am?” And Peter said, “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.” Then the Lord said, “You are Peter, and upon this rock”—Myself—“I will build My church.” So again, Peter got reminded that he was a stone, and Christ also is a stone, a big stone, a rock on which the church was to be built. So, of course, Peter was deeply impressed with this matter of being stones, and he called the believers the living stones in his epistle in chapter 2. We need to come to Christ as the living stone, he tells us, and as living stones, we are building the spiritual house.

We even go to the end of the Bible in Revelation. To the overcomers in the church in Pergamos, the promise to them was that they would be given a piece of white stone. Upon it will be inscribed the Lord’s new name. Then, ultimately, even there in [[Revelation-04|chapter 4 of Revelation]], we see and are shown that the One sitting on the throne, His likeness, was like a jasper stone. Could you imagine the God who sits on the throne was like a jasper stone? Then, you go to the end of the Bible in the New Jerusalem, and you see a wall of jasper and all manner of different precious stones. So eventually, the New Jerusalem, which is just a city, is a city full of living stones.

So, you can see how important this matter of stones is throughout the Bible. God must have stones, precious and living stones. Through being constituted and built up together, God can fulfill through us His eternal economy by making us living stones. I hope you would have this view to realize that, “As a believer, I should not be wishy-washy. I need to be transformed. I need to become solid, weighty, and strong as a stone, transformed as a stone for God’s building.”

### A. We, the believers in Christ, are living stones, like Christ, through regeneration and transformation; we were created of clay ([[Romans-09#v21|Rom. 9:21]]), but at regeneration we received the seed of the divine life ([[1 Peter-01#v23|1 Pet. 1:23-24]]), which by its growing in us transforms us into living stones.

We were all created of clay. We were all clay. Clay is soft, even muddy. If you just wash it with water, it’s very muddy and messy. That’s what we were—we were made of clay. Praise the Lord, at regeneration, when the divine life as the seed came into us, this life began to transform us, the clay man, to become a stone-man. Then, we would be transformed by this life that we received, growing in us and transforming us. Transformation is not just an outward change merely. Transformation is a metabolic process. It is through the impartation of the divine life in us. Then, He begins to add the new element into our being, the divine element being added to the human element to create a change, an eternal change, a constitutional change, to even eventually bring about a change in form. A good example of this is petrified wood.

In Arizona, there is a place called the Petrified Forest, where there are many pieces of wood. They were wood, but after passing through water for millions of years, by depositing these minerals in the wood, over time, they became petrified. If you see the stone, you would never know it was wood before. They were soft wood, but now they have become strong, weighty pieces of stone.

In previous messages, we saw that we need to grow, but I would say, by growing we can be transformed. We cannot change ourselves. We can only be transformed by the growth in life. Our growth in life actually is for our transformation so that we could be fit for God’s building.

#### 1. At Peter’s conversion the Lord gave him a new name—Peter, a stone ([[John-01#v42|John 1:42]]), and when Peter received the revelation concerning Christ, the Lord revealed further that He was the rock—a stone ([[Matthew-16#v16|Matt. 16:16-18]]).

So, those were the two occasions when Peter was spoken to and reminded that he was a stone. That must have left a deep, deep impression with Peter. I think among all the apostles, only Peter wrote about this matter of the stone, because he was directly spoken to by the Lord about this.

#### 2. By these two incidents Peter received the impression that both Christ and His believers are stones for God’s building—[[1 Peter-02#v4|1 Pet. 2:4-5]].

### B. Christ is the living stone, the foundation stone, the cornerstone, and the topstone of God’s building; after being born of God, the believers in Christ are transformed into living precious stones for the building up of the church as the spiritual house of God—[[1 Peter-02#v4|1 Pet. 2:4]]; [[1 Corinthians-03#v11|1 Cor. 3:11]]; [[Isaiah-28#v16|Isa. 28:16]]; [[Acts-04#v11|Acts 4:11]]; [[Zechariah-04#v7|Zech. 4:7]]; [[John-01#v12|John 1:12-13]], [[John-01#v42|42]]; [[1 Peter-02#v5|1 Pet. 2:5-7]]; [[1 Corinthians-03#v12|1 Cor. 3:12]]; [[Matthew-16#v18|Matt. 16:18]].

The goal of God’s economy is to have a building, a spiritual house of God. This is why He dispenses His life into us, to regenerate us, to renew us, to sanctify us, to transform us, so that we could become _buildable_. You cannot build mud. You have to bring all the transformed stones to be built together to become that spiritual house of God.

You know, here in the reference in [[Acts-04|Acts 4]], that is when Peter, in response to the rulers who were persecuting him, told them about this Christ, whom they crucified and who was resurrected. Then, he says, “This is the stone which the builders rejected, whom God has made the head of the corner.” Then, he referred to [[Acts-04#v12|verse 12]], that there is no other salvation under heaven, no other name under heaven by which you can be saved. That name is Jesus Christ, who is our stone-Savior. He is the One who was crucified for us and resurrected for us to bring us salvation. That salvation is not just to deliver you from God’s judgment and to bring you peace and joy. That salvation is to make you a piece of stone for God’s building. Our Savior Christ is a stone-Savior. This is very precious. So, dear saints, we need to know who we are as believers: that we are pieces of stone that need to be transformed for God’s building. We need to become buildable to be built up in the church today, in the Body of Christ, and eventually in the New Jerusalem.

### C. Christ as the stone has seven eyes, which signify the seven Spirits of God, the sevenfold intensified Spirit ([[Zechariah-03#v9|Zech. 3:9]]; [[Zechariah-04#v10|4:10]]); the sevenfold intensified Spirit is also signified by the seven eyes of the Lamb ([[Revelation-05#v6|Rev. 5:6]]) and the seven lamps of fire burning before God’s throne ([[Revelation-04#v5|Rev. 4:5]]).

In [[Zechariah-03#v9|Zechariah 3:9]], we are told there is a piece of stone that has seven eyes. Have you seen a stone like that, having seven eyes? And then, if you compare [[Zechariah-03#v9|Zechariah 3:9]], the stone having seven eyes, with [[Revelation-05|Revelation 5]], the Lamb also has seven eyes! The seven eyes of the stone are the same as the seven eyes of the Lamb in Revelation, and these seven eyes of the Lamb in Revelation are the seven lamps of fire before God’s throne, the seven lamps. Then, these are what? The seven Spirits of God.

Well, this particular point gives us the secret of how we can experience Christ as the stone subjectively. Christ is a stone. We are also stones. But now, how do we actually experience this, not just positionally because God has made us such, but experientially, subjectively? How do we experience this stone? The secret is with the seven eyes. There are seven eyes on this stone, which are the seven eyes of the Lamb. This is the seven eyes of Jehovah, the seven Spirits of God.

#### 1. The lamps are for enlightening, searching, exposing, judging, and burning, and the eyes are for infusing; the seven eyes infuse all that the Lamb-stone is into our being to make us His golden lampstand—[[Zechariah-04#v2|Zech. 4:2-6]], [[Zechariah-04#v11|11-14]].

This is what Zechariah chapters 3 and 4 try to reveal to us: through this stone with seven eyes, these seven eyes, which are the seven lamps of fire, the seven Spirits of God. These seven lamps are for enlightenment, for searching, for exposing, for judging, for burning. If we want to become stones, we need to be burned. We need to be exposed, judged, and enlightened by the seven lamps. In our natural nature, we are all muddy and earthy. Like Paul says in 1 Corinthians 15, we are all earthy. We are the earth; that is where we came from. We are not absolute. We are oftentimes wishy-washy. How can God build us? How can we become buildable and bear the burden for God’s interest? We need the experience of the seven lamps burning us, exposing us, shining into us all that we are naturally. All those natural elements of ours—our wishy-washiness, our looseness, lightness, and naturalness—need to be exposed and shined on.

Like the message from yesterday, we all need the light, the shining of the light. Without light we are in darkness. We don’t know where we are. We are deceived. We are obsessed. Only the light can rescue us. We need the seven lamps. We need to come to the Lord, to the sevenfold intensified Spirit as the seven lamps, to shine intensively into us to show us who we are. At the same time, we also need the infusing eyes. There are seven eyes. These eyes are for infusion, right? When people look at you with the eyes, then there is something being transmitted, transfused from that person to you. In the Bible, I think [[Luke-22|Luke 22]], after Peter denied the Lord three times, it says that Jesus turned and looked at him. I think, if you were Peter in that moment, you just denied the Lord three times, and there the Lord was being judged by people, and the Lord knew what Peter just did, and He turned and looked at Peter. I don’t think for his whole life, Peter would ever forget that look that Jesus had with him. Something was infused, infused into him. We need to be looked at by the Lord. It’s only by His eyes infusing us, infusing all that He is into us, that we can be wrought and constituted with His elements.

This coming point is very precious. It concerns the way for us, which is very practical.
